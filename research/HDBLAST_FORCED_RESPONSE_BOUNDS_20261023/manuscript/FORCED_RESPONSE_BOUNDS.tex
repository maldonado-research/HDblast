\begin{abstract}
For one previously registered quadrupole in a conditional five-dimensional
Einstein--scalar material-shell model, we enclose an evaluated component of the
auxiliary quadratic forced response. Twenty exact-area source cells and a
bound for every retained feedback iteration imply
$1.7\times10^7<\langle W_{yy}\rangle<6.5\times10^{10}$ throughout
$|x-1/4|,|r-5/4|\le10^{-6}$. All 23 bulk amplitudes are retained. The domain
of dependence stays away from the shell; its contributions vanish by causality.
The evaluated point also gives $\|W\|_X>8.8\times10^6$, below the retained
upper bound $5.228\times10^{13}$. A four-term true-operator Neumann expression
has truncation tail below $1035$, but remains unevaluated. The methods are
standard interval-integral and norm estimates applied to the retained
model-specific certificates. The result is an auxiliary, frame/gauge-dependent
response enclosure, not a global waveform, standalone physical constraint
solution, nonlinear spacetime, or physical-force or cosmological-origin claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Retained conditional problem}
The inputs are the published geometric-source construction and its separate
response-attribution calculation~\cite{sources,attribution}, pinned to the
repository commit recorded in the accompanying reproducibility manifest.
We retain their background, first solution, gauge, angular conventions,
regularity assumptions, and inverse estimates. This note does not reprove those
upstream assumptions or replace the actual first solution by its comparison.
The fixed quadrupole is $Y=n_0^2-n_1^2$ on the unit $S^3$, with the retained
actual master and shell-preserving relative-harmonic gauge field. The scaled
coordinates are $x=t/\ell$, $r=z/\ell$, with $\ell=10^{-6}$ and
$dy=-\rho\,dz$. Tensor entries use the retained radially parallel background
frame, whose normal vector is $e_y=\partial_y$.

The fixed finite angular state has 23 amplitudes, retaining all metric blocks,
the scalar, and both transverse-traceless orientations. Its squared norm
$|\cdot|_{\HH}^2$ is the probability-normalized $S^3$ integral of the metric
Frobenius square plus the scalar square. On
\[
 D=\{(x,r):0\le x\le3,\ r\ge0,\ x+r\le8\},
 \qquad
 \norm{V}_X=\max\left\{\sup_D|V|_{\HH}/3,
                         \sup_D|V_x|_{\HH},\sup_D|V_r|_{\HH}\right\},
\]
the accepted reduced equation and complete linear operator are
\begin{align}
 U&=U_{\rm free}+\KK F+\LL G+TU,\\
 TV&=\KK(AV_x+BV_r+D_0V)+\LL(MP_NV|_{r=0}),
 \qquad \norm{T}_{X\to X}\le q=\frac{2109}{10^6}<1.
\end{align}
The source normalization is $F_{\rm metric}=-2\ell^2\rho^2Q_{\rm red}$ and
$F_{\rm scalar}=\ell^2\rho^2Q_\phi$, for second parameter derivatives.
The boundary vector $G$ includes the physical shell rows and normal harmonic
source; $T$ includes the complete coupled Robin matrix. In particular,
\[
 \KK f(x,r)=\frac12\int_0^x\int_{r-x+s}^{r+x-s}
                    f_{\rm ext}(s,v)\,dv\,ds,
 \qquad
 \LL g(x,r)=-\int_0^{\max(x-r,0)}g(s)\,ds,
\]
where the extension is the registered odd/even reflection $P_N-P_D$.
The accepted flat initial wedge and corner compatibility give $G(0)=0$
and the required $C^1$ integral fields with zero Cauchy data.

Set
\[
 S=\KK F+\LL G,\qquad W=(I-T)^{-1}S,
 \qquad U_{\rm hom}=(I-T)^{-1}U_{\rm free}.
\]
Thus $U=U_{\rm hom}+W$. This is an auxiliary decomposition of the reduced
operator problem. The prescribed second initial data may themselves encode
quadratic constraints. Neither summand is asserted separately to satisfy the
complete physical constraint and gauge system.
Throughout, $\lambda(V)=\langle V_{yy}\rangle_{S^3}$ denotes the
probability-sphere mean of the covariant normal-frame component. Its dual
$\HH$ norm is one, attained by the retained constant pure $yy$ tensor.
It commutes with $x,r$ derivatives, and the exact angular projection identifies
$\lambda(F)$ with the constant $yy$ forcing coefficient.

\section{Global norm consequences and an accuracy target}
The sphere mean obeys $|\lambda(W)(x,r)|\le3\norm{W}_X$, because the
value term of the registered norm has weight three. The exact evaluated apex
lower endpoint $L$ therefore yields
\[
 \norm{W}_X\ge L/3>8.8\times10^6.
\]
This is a stronger global norm lower bound than the independent weak-source
witness in Appendix~\ref{app:weak}. It does not evaluate a global coefficient
field or identify a physical energy.
The retained actual source bounds and Green-map estimates give
\[
 F_{\HH}\le17387513156573,\quad G_{\HH}\le3851400,
 \quad\norm{S}_X\le3F_{\HH}+G_{\HH}=52162543321119=:s_*.
\]
Therefore $\norm{W}_X\le s_* /(1-q)<5.228\times10^{13}$.
For $V_N=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}T^jS$,
\[
 (I-T)V_N-S=-T^NS,\qquad
 \norm{W-V_N}_X\le\frac{q^Ns_*}{1-q}.
\]
For $N=4$ this last bound is strictly below $1035$. It excludes numerical
input, integration, and reconstruction errors. $V_4$ here is a true-operator
expression, not a computed coefficient field or numerical residual certificate.
Its Robin defect is $-MP_N(T^3S)|_{r=0}$, so finite partial sums need not
satisfy the exact coupled boundary law. A numerical candidate must account for
the complete integral residual against the true retained inputs.

\section{Independent weak-source lower bound}\label{app:weak}
Write $P=\partial_x^2-\partial_r^2$. The normalized retarded fundamental
solution $\tfrac12\mathbf1_{x\ge|r|}$ gives $P\KK F=F$ in the physical
interior, distributionally. Since $\LL G$ depends on $x-r$ alone,
$P\LL G=0$, also distributionally. These identities require no derivative
estimate for the continuous quadratic source.

Let $\lambda$ be the sphere mean of the covariant $yy$ metric component.
Its dual $\HH$ norm is one: its Riesz vector is the retained constant pure
$yy$ tensor of value one. Consequently
\[
 |\lambda(V_x)|,\ |\lambda(V_r)|\le\norm{V}_X.
\]
The value weight three in $X$ does not enter this derivative estimate.
The exact angular projection identifies the constant $yy$ source coefficient
with $f_0=\lambda(F)$; the other scalar harmonics have zero sphere mean.

The published actual-source report encloses $f_0\ge c_->16\times10^9$
continuously on the square
\[
 R=[1/4,1025/4096]\times[5/4,5121/4096],\qquad h=1/4096.
\]
The exact rational $c_-$ is read from the hash-authenticated report by the
reproducer. The square lies strictly inside $D$ and has $r-x>0$. In fact its
boundary lift vanishes and its backward cones remain at positive depth;
ordinary unreflected integration suffices for the local weak identity.

For any nonnegative $\chi\in C_c^\infty(R^\circ)$, one weak integration
by parts gives
\[
 c_-\int\chi\le\int f_0\chi
 =-\int\lambda(S_x)\chi_x+\int\lambda(S_r)\chi_r
 \le\norm{S}_X\bigl(\norm{\chi_x}_{L^1}+\norm{\chi_r}_{L^1}\bigr).
\]
Take products of smooth one-dimensional plateau cutoffs with compact support
inside the two coordinate intervals. Each has total variation two and mass
$m_\varepsilon\to h$. The product has mass $m_\varepsilon^2$ and summed
first variation $4m_\varepsilon$. Passing to the limit yields
\[
 \norm{S}_X\ge\frac{c_-h}{4}>
 \frac{(16\times10^9)h}{4}=\frac{1953125}{2}.
\]
The limit gives a nonstrict inequality. Strictness of the rounded bound
comes afterwards from the separately verified slack $c_->16\times10^9$.
Since $S=(I-T)W$, the complete operator bound gives
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\quad\norm{W}_X\ge\frac{c_-h}{4(1+q)}
       >\frac{976562500000}{1002109}>974000.\quad}       \label{eq:lower}
\end{equation}
The transfer factor is $1+q$, not the upper-resolvent factor $1-q$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:lower} proves full-state nonvanishing. Coupling prevents
inferring a specified response component from this argument alone.
