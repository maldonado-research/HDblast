# How can particle production change the expansion of a shell?

Three results from the literature bear directly on this experiment. A scalar-dependent matter interaction supplies both stress and a force on the scalar. The gravitational field on a brane also depends on the surrounding bulk. Finally, the same homogeneous expansion history can have a four-dimensional description. These facts determine which quantities the calculation must evolve and which inferences an expansion measurement permits.

## The sources that enter the gravity equations

Mennim and Battye vary a five-dimensional action including a brane matter Lagrangian with explicit dependence on the bulk scalar. The metric variation fixes the jump of extrinsic curvature. The scalar variation fixes the jump of the scalar's normal derivative. Their Eq. (25) gives the associated exchange of energy and momentum between brane matter and the scalar. Eq. (21) retains a projection of the bulk Weyl tensor in the induced Einstein equation. In the HDblast convention the normal points from each interior toward the shell and the matter current is the scalar derivative of the proper matter Hamiltonian per physical volume. With these definitions the homogeneous matter balance reads $\dot\rho+3H(\rho+p)=J\dot\phi$. A scalar-dependent Yukawa coupling contributes to $J$ even at times when the time-dependent parent mass contributes separately to $J$. Both terms have to enter with the sign fixed by the same action. [Mennim and Battye, Eqs. (18), (20)–(25)](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0008192#S2.E18) · [HDblast signed action](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22922928)

Himemoto and Tanaka explicitly consider transfer from a massive bulk scalar to brane matter via a phenomenological dissipation term. The matter continuity equation uses the signed normal energy flux $T_{ab}u^a n^b$. The projected contribution to the expansion also depends on the bulk normal stress $T_{ab}n^a n^b$. Their low-energy, late-time treatment recovers effective four-dimensional reheating when $H\ell$ and $|m|\ell$ are small, with $\ell$ the bulk curvature length. Their system has a different field content and interaction from HDblast. It provides a concrete precedent for comparing particle transfer and geometry separately. [Himemoto and Tanaka, Eqs. (29)–(39)](https://arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0212114#S3.E29) · [Effective four-dimensional field in a related model](https://arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0112027#S4.E61)

Martin, Felder, Frolov, Kofman and Peloso evolve the five-dimensional Einstein–scalar system in a two-dimensional conformal coordinate system with homogeneous spatial sections on the branes. Their code applies junction conditions at the boundaries and requires initial data consistent with both those boundary conditions and the bulk constraints. The authors' branes carry scalar potentials and their example involves two branes. Their formulation motivates the separate checks of bulk evolution, junction forces and constraints here. [BRANECODE, Secs. 3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/0404141#S3)

## Which four-dimensional description can the expansion exclude?

For spatially flat four-dimensional FLRW geometry in Einstein gravity, consider any differentiable history $H(t)$ in physical proper time and a fixed reduced Planck mass $M_4$. The density and pressure of an effective single fluid that reproduces that expansion are

$$
\rho_{\rm eff}=3M_4^2H^2,\qquad
p_{\rm eff}=-M_4^2(2\dot H+3H^2).
$$

Differentiating at fixed $t$ with respect to a model parameter gives $\delta\rho_{\rm eff}=6M_4^2H\,\delta H$ and $\delta p_{\rm eff}=-2M_4^2(\delta\dot H+3H\,\delta H)$. Substitution gives the fluid continuity equation identically. This construction matches the homogeneous background over the interval where the history is given. It supplies neither a fluid Lagrangian nor its sound speed, perturbations or microscopic coupling. A pure radiation fluid is a more restrictive comparison because it must also satisfy $p=\rho/3$. Departure from $H a^2=\mathrm{constant}$ can exclude that restricted comparison while leaving the general effective fluid available. Kunz discusses the corresponding degeneracy of gravitational information about the total stress, including the need for additional component assumptions. [Kunz, *The dark degeneracy*](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0702615)

## Recent calculations relevant to interpreting the result

- In a preprint submitted on 18 November 2026, Benedix, Currens, Giblin and Richards simulate a cosmological first-order phase transition with nonlinear four-dimensional gravity. They construct constrained initial data and follow one- and two-bubble configurations. Their final state includes a component with matter-like behavior associated with oscillating scalar lumps. This supplies an example of a violent field event with an expanding final state for which a pure radiation equation of state requires a separate check. The mechanism, spatial dimensionality and sources differ from the HDblast experiment. The gravitational-wave spectrum they compute belongs to their phase-transition model. [Benedix et al., Secs. III–V](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.24581#S3.SS1)
- Bhusal et al. calculate fermion production by a quartic inflaton and compare the energy obtained from its phase-space distribution with Boltzmann estimates. Some strong-coupling examples can approach energy equality before fragmentation. The authors explicitly omit the fermion backreaction on the inflaton in that estimate. Their example separates a computed particle population from the stronger claim that a radiation era has been dynamically demonstrated. Herring and Boyanovsky give a complementary stress and energy benchmark for a self-consistent Yukawa condensate at large fermion flavor number in Minkowski spacetime. [Bhusal et al., Fig. 7 and discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16203#S3.F7) · [Herring and Boyanovsky, Eq. (III.82)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22465v2#S3.E82)
