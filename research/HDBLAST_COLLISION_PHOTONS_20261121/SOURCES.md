# Photon emission in collisions on the HDblast shell

## Which interaction is being tested?

The benchmark models three equal-mass Dirac flavors. Each carries one of three independent Abelian charges. The collision operator uses a separate neutral vector resonance, with $M_V/m=8300$ and $\Gamma_V/M_V=0.30$. Its accepted two-body scattering law is proportional to $3-2\beta^2+\beta^4\cos^2\theta$ in the pair center-of-momentum frame, with $\beta=\sqrt{1-4m^2/s}$. For one shared mediator, summing over the three allowed final flavors gives one third of the dedicated elastic rate at the same density. An accepted collision already includes that branching factor. The eligible opposite-sign target occupies one sixth of the total comoving particle population on the symmetric history. [Collision kernel and spin sampling](https://github.com/maldonado-research/HDblast/blob/9467ffbc67e9540da6762fecd1c44e5e22c80522/research/HDBLAST_SCATTERING_20261121/live/collide.hpp) · [Shared flavor conversion](https://github.com/maldonado-research/HDblast/blob/9467ffbc67e9540da6762fecd1c44e5e22c80522/research/HDBLAST_AXIAL_CURRENT_20261121/flavor/README.md)

For this local surface Maxwell law the canonically normalized photon coupling is $\alpha=q^2/(4\pi S_0K)=3Q^2(mL)^2/(4\pi S_0K)$. The factor $\sqrt3$ enters when the project defines $Q=q/(\sqrt3\,mL)$. With $K=2.54$, $Q=10^7$, $mL=4\times10^{12}$ and $S_0=1.0415592372880226\times10^{54}$, $\alpha=1.4438220891\times10^{-16}$. The much stronger interaction responsible for the resonance enters the underlying scattering amplitude. The previous dedicated-pole study used this photon coupling to estimate smooth-trajectory radiation. By time 7.05 it obtained $1.48\times10^{-15}$ of the final gas energy on the finest run. That calculation explicitly left radiation in individual collisions open. [Gauge normalization](https://github.com/maldonado-research/HDblast/blob/9467ffbc67e9540da6762fecd1c44e5e22c80522/research/HDBLAST_NEUTRAL_PARENT_DECAY_20261120/consistency/CONSISTENCY.md) · [Smooth-field calculation](https://github.com/maldonado-research/HDblast/tree/9467ffbc67e9540da6762fecd1c44e5e22c80522/research/HDBLAST_ENERGETIC_PARTICLES_20261121/radiation)

## Soft photons from an individual encounter

Use signature $(+,-,-,-)$, future-directed on-shell momenta $p_a$, a photon direction $n=(1,\hat{\boldsymbol n})$ and photon energy $\omega$ in the pair center-of-momentum frame. For each independent photon field separately, set $c_a=\eta_a Q_a$, where $Q_a$ is the particle's signed charge in units of that field's elementary charge and $\eta_a$ is $-1$ for an incoming leg or $+1$ for an outgoing leg. Conservation of each charge gives $\sum_a c_a=0$. The leading external-line amplitude and one-photon phase space give

$$
j^\mu(n)=\sum_a c_a\frac{p_a^\mu}{p_a\cdot n},\qquad
 dN_\gamma=\frac{\alpha}{4\pi^2}[-j(n)^2]\,d\Omega\,\frac{d\omega}{\omega}.
$$

The sign follows from the transverse polarization sum and $n\cdot j=0$. Integrating the angle gives $B=-(\alpha/\pi)\sum_{a,b}c_ac_b L_{ab}$, with the sum over ordered pairs of legs. Here $L_{aa}=1$, $L_{ab}=\operatorname{atanh}\beta_{ab}/\beta_{ab}$ and $\beta_{ab}=\sqrt{1-m_a^2m_b^2/(p_a\cdot p_b)^2}$. One can evaluate the integral in the rest frame of either massive leg. The angular measure combined with the two eikonal denominators has zero net transformation weight. Weinberg derives the relative-velocity formula for massive charged particles. Balsach, Bonocore and Kulesza examine how energy-momentum conservation and the choice of underlying elastic momenta affect finite-energy extensions of the soft expansion. [Weinberg, *Infrared photons and gravitons*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.140.B516) · [Balsach et al., *Soft-photon spectra and the LBK theorem*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11386)

Number the incoming positive and negative particles 1 and 2 and the outgoing positive and negative particles 3 and 4. With equal masses, preserving their flavor gives $B_{\rm same}=4\alpha[L_{12}+L_{13}-L_{14}-1]/\pi$. The result vanishes for an undeflected pair. If the outgoing pair belongs to a different independent gauge field, add the two dipole probabilities to obtain $B_{\rm change}=4\alpha(L_{12}-1)/\pi$. Each dipole is separately conserved. The two photon species in a flavor-changing event are distinguishable. For an angularly symmetric sample of the neutral vector's elastic law at fixed $s$, the interference contribution to $B_{\rm same}$ is odd in $\cos\theta$ and averages to zero. Event-by-event angle and species dependence survive.

A leading-soft photon energy moment between $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ is $B(\omega_2-\omega_1)$, finite as $\omega_1\to0$. The photon count over the same band is $B\log(\omega_2/\omega_1)$ and requires a positive lower energy resolution. For the equal-mass back-to-back dipoles above the angular intensity is even under $\hat{\boldsymbol n}\mapsto-\hat{\boldsymbol n}$. The average emitted three-momentum therefore vanishes in the pair frame for an angularly inclusive center-of-momentum energy band. The shell-frame mean energy multiplies by the center-of-momentum Lorentz factor. Multiplication by the wall radius at emission converts the local energy into the model's conformal energy units. A finite soft-band result alone gives no estimate for all harder photons.

## Finite energy and other channels

For a specified pointlike Dirac coupling to a neutral spin-one mediator, tree-level external-leg emission includes both diagrams on the incoming pair and both on the outgoing pair, with their relative signs and their fermion propagators. The intermediate resonance samples $(p_1+p_2-k)^2$ for initial-state radiation and $(p_1+p_2)^2$ for final-state radiation. Each complete neutral pair of diagrams satisfies a Ward identity. Emission amplitudes interfering between the initial and final pairs require identical photon species. In the pure-vector unpolarized channel the interference is odd under interchange of the two final charges and vanishes for charge-symmetric full integration. Differential measurements can retain it. The BABAR collaboration measured this interference using charge asymmetry, and Czyż and collaborators give an explicit initial- and final-state amplitude construction for radiative muon-pair production. Their visible-photon experiments and propagators are independent of the HDblast choices. [Czyż et al., PHOKHARA](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0404078) · [Complete QED contributions for the charged-fermion channel](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.3610) · [BABAR charge-asymmetry measurement](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04008)

An independent closed-form check is Eq. (5) of Bystritskiy, Kuraev, Fedotovich and Ignatov. With $x=2\omega/\sqrt{s}$ it gives the fully angular-integrated final-state photon spectrum for a point Dirac pair of finite mass. The common neutral-vector propagator at $s$ cancels when the final-state spectrum is divided by the Born rate. The authors also explain why charge-blind integration removes initial–final interference. [Bystritskiy et al., differential pair-mass spectra, Eqs. (5) and (21)](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0505236)

The emitted-energy moment at first order in the photon coupling is infrared finite because its energy weight vanishes on the zero-photon state. Dividing this moment by the Born collision rate is consistent at its leading order. A hard-collinear approximation or a selected set of diagrams should be identified explicitly when used. An exclusive no-emission probability requires a resolution threshold and virtual or resummed corrections. A prediction for other crossed channels or for a different mediator requires the corresponding interaction. For opposite charges of the same flavor, direct annihilation into two photons is another process, of order $\alpha^2$ at Born level. Roman Lee gives its exact massive cross section in Eq. (2) and treats higher-order corrections separately. [Lee, *Electron-positron annihilation to photons at order α³ revisited*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11082)

A passive calculation folds isolated-vacuum-pair emission with particle histories computed using the registered collision and field laws. Photon formation over multiple encounters, screening, absorption, radiation from distinct particles with correlated phases, propagation into the bulk, and changes to the gas or wall expansion require additional inputs. In particular, a produced photon energy does not determine an escaping flux or an equilibrated radiation density. Arnold, Moore and Yaffe explicitly treat the effect of repeated scattering during photon formation for a hot weakly coupled plasma. HDblast's measured nonthermal distributions and specified gauge normalization must enter any quantitative medium calculation on this shell. [Arnold et al., *Photon Emission from Ultrarelativistic Plasmas*](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0109064)
