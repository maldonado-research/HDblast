## 2. Diagnosis of the A1 Y = 3 runs (step 1, already done; numbers in `D1_Y3_DIAGNOSIS.json`)

1. **Friction is not the problem.** The source Yv²/(4HR) falls below 10⁻³ at H₀τ ≈ 8.6 (dc = 10⁻²) / 13.5 (dc = 10⁻⁴) and stays there; R a⁴ is constant to ≈ 10⁻⁵ afterwards.
2. **The late drift of 𝒲a⁴ is a 4th-order discretisation error, amplified by the growing shell lapse of the bounded chart.** After 𝒲a⁴ settles (|d ln 𝒲a⁴/d ln a| < 0.05 at H₀τ ≈ 13), it drifts upwards; the drift at dz_fine = 10⁻³ is 14–23 times that at 5×10⁻⁴ (measured order 3.8–4.5; 4.00 at H₀τ ≈ 21.7), and it grows as the shell lapse rises from ≈ 10 to ≈ 35. The Weyl transport identity, whose source terms are all ∝ v ≈ 10⁻⁷ there, fails in that window (p95 residual 0.3), so the drift is not physical.
3. **The registered plateau window is physically tight at Y = 3.** Between 𝒲a⁴ settling and the turnaround (H = 0, caused by the tuned negative residual vacuum ≈ −3.5×10⁻⁴ H₀²) there are only ≈ 0.57–0.64 e-folds, so the registered 0.5 e-fold window must end at ln a ≈ 7.45 (H₀τ ≈ 22, lapse ≈ 23), where the drift is 70% (dz 10⁻³) and 4.4% (dz 5×10⁻⁴). The coarse run can therefore never show a plateau and the fine one is borderline.
4. The "non-finite" stop occurs after the turnaround and is irrelevant; the A1 "H₀τ 31–45" is the reliable end (near-shell constraint residual > 0.05).

**Remedy (method change, labelled; physics and A1 rule unchanged):** a finer near-shell resolution in the late phase, obtained by a restart (section 4), plus a supplementary Richardson extrapolation. Because the drift is generated next to the shell (development check above), L is reduced from 16 to 10 for the new runs (shell exact for T < 20; no run is expected to need T > 16).

## 4. Classification and reliability (A1 rule unchanged)

- **Per run:** the A1 rule (A1 REGISTRATION §3), applied by the unchanged function `a1_analyze.classify` (copied verbatim): plateau = first time with H > 0 and both 𝒲a⁴ and Ra⁴ varying by ≤ 5% over the preceding Δ ln a = 0.5; PASS-conservative: plateau, Ω_r ≥ 0.9, |r| ≤ 0.1; **PASS-combined: additionally |r| ≤ 0.03**; FAIL-Weyl: plateau with |r| > 0.1; FAIL-no-radiation-era: recollapse (H/H₀ < −0.05) before any time with Ω_r ≥ 0.9 and |r| ≤ 0.1, or plateau with Ω_r < 0.5; INCONCLUSIVE otherwise. Reliable end of a run: last record before the near-shell constraint residual exceeds 0.05.
- **Reliability (A1 criterion, unchanged):** the two spacings give the same class and agree on r at the classification time to max(20% relative, 0.02 absolute) (recollapse: H = 0 times within 0.05 H₀⁻¹), and the finer run's near-shell Hamiltonian and momentum residuals stay < 0.05 up to the classification time.
- **Primary pair:** (10⁻³, 5×10⁻⁴). **Secondary pair (method change, labelled):** if the primary pair is not reliable, and the 10⁻³-versus-5×10⁻⁴ difference of 𝒲a⁴ at the 5×10⁻⁴ run's last reliable record (or at its plateau time if it has one) is consistent with 4th-order convergence (measured order between 3 and 5), a **restart run** is made: the 5×10⁻⁴ run's saved full-field state at the last saved T at which its shell lapse is ≤ 3 is transferred by quintic-spline interpolation to a dz_fine = 2.5×10⁻⁴ grid (dz_coarse 10⁻³, L = 10) and evolved on. Its time series (parent's records before the restart, its own after) is a "hybrid 2.5×10⁻⁴ run". The pair (5×10⁻⁴, hybrid 2.5×10⁻⁴) is then assessed with the identical criterion and its reliable class counts. The class reported for (Y, dc) is that of the primary pair if reliable, else of the secondary pair if reliable, else UNRELIABLE.
- **Supplementary, not decisive:** Richardson-extrapolated series X_RE = X_f + (X_f − X_c)/(2^p − 1) on the finest pair, with the plateau rule applied, for p = 3.5, 4, 4.5 (spread = systematic uncertainty).

## 5. Outcomes and their meaning

- **Main question:** does any Y give r ≤ 0.03 with Ω_r ≥ 0.9 at plateau? **YES** if at least one Y has a reliable PASS-combined at dc = 10⁻² **and** (if run) at dc = 10⁻⁴; "YES (single seed)" if the dc = 10⁻⁴ repeat was not completed; **NO** if every Y in the scan with a reliable class is PASS-conservative or FAIL and no Y is PASS-combined; **INCONCLUSIVE** if the only Y values that could reach r ≤ 0.03 have UNRELIABLE classes.
- Reported for each Y: class, r, Ω_r, Ω_vac at the plateau (finest reliable run), the Richardson values, and the minimum r over Y.
- **Meaning.** YES: within the friction closure, a large enough friction coefficient puts the released energy into brane radiation rather than the bulk Weyl mode, and the dark-radiation constraint at the combined CMB+BBN+BAO level (snippet-level threshold, A1 §2) can be met at δ = 0.1. It does **not** remove the d\* fine tuning, the eventual recollapse caused by the negative residual vacuum, the phenomenological nature of Y, or the open δ = 10⁻³ case. NO: the friction closure cannot reach the combined bound at δ = 0.1 for the scanned Y. A run with no plateau because the turnaround comes less than 0.5 e-folds after the Weyl term settles is INCONCLUSIVE under the A1 rule; this is reported as such, together with the values at the time of maximal Ω_r, and is not counted as a pass.
- **Trend:** r(Y) fitted as a power law a Y^(−p) over reliable plateaus (dc = 10⁻²), compared with the fraction of the energy released by the roll-off that goes into R (reported: 1/(1 + r) at the plateau, and the a⁴-weighted integrals ∫Yv² a⁴ dτ and ∫(Weyl source) a⁴ dτ).

## 6. Controls (fixed now)

- **C5 (Weyl identity, strengthened):** on the finest run of each reported (Y, dc), up to the classification time: correct V2-form identity median relative residual ≤ 10⁻³. Wrong-factor controls (4H → 3H; Yv dropped from the scalar junction in the identity) must be ≥ 10× worse over all records. **R-controls** (R dropped from n·A; R → 3R; R → −R) are evaluated on the records where the R term's weight in the identity, |(2/3)R w v|/(6 × scale), is ≥ 10⁻³, and must be ≥ 10× worse there (with ≥ 20 such records). Reason: over all records the R term is ≤ 0.5% of the identity's scale and typically ~10⁻⁵, i.e. at the numerical floor, so no R-based control can be ≥ 10× worse in an all-record median (the A1 failure); this is stated as the explanation if the windowed control also fails.
- **C6 (radiation ledger):** median ≤ 10⁻⁴ (as A1).
- **C10 (restart validation):** the 10⁻³ run of Y = 3 also saves states; a restart from it onto a 5×10⁻⁴ grid at the same restart rule is compared with the full 5×10⁻⁴ run: the late 𝒲a⁴ drift of the restarted run must be within a factor 2 of the full 5×10⁻⁴ run's drift (and ≥ 5× below the 10⁻³ run's) at the plateau-relevant times (H₀τ = 18–24).
- **C11 (L independence):** new Y = 3 runs (L = 10) against the A1 Y = 3 runs (L = 16): 𝒲a⁴ and H agree to ≤ 10⁻³ relative up to H₀τ = 20 at equal spacing.
- **C12 (Y = 1 reproduction):** not run (budget); the Y = 1 A1 PASS is quoted.

**Y = 5: time step reduced (method change; physics and rule unchanged).** The Y = 5 pre-run blew up after 14 steps (T = 0.007, shell lapse |B_b| > 40), before anything physical happened. Cause (`d2_friction_stiffness.py` → `D2_FRICTION_STIFFNESS.json`): the friction term in the shell condition n·∂φ = −(σ′ + Yv)/2 is a damping boundary term whose semi-discrete eigenvalue grows as Y/dz (|λ|dz ≈ 5.0 at Y = 3, 9.8 at Y = 5 in the model problem with the solver's Hermite ghost); explicit RK4 needs |λ|Δt ≤ 2.785, so with Δt = 0.5 dz Y = 3 is just stable (2.5) and Y = 5 is not (4.9). For Y = 5 only, all runs (pre-run and both spacings, and any restart) use `--cfl 0.25` (|λ|Δt ≈ 2.4). Y ≤ 3 keep cfl = 0.5 as in A1. The failed Y = 5 attempts at cfl 0.5 (pre-run and any main-queue attempts, which take x_c = ∞ from the failed pre-run and blow up at once) are moved to `runs/unstable_cfl0.5/` and not used.
