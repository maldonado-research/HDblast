# Preparing a field at the top of a closed universe

Research record, 19 November 2026. The question is whether quantum fluctuations of the four-dimensional shell field can remain small during the long classical delay required for radiation to catch the parent field. The literature supplies the fluctuation dynamics, finite-interval concentration theorem and gravitational constraints. The calculations in this release apply them to two particular potentials, field resolutions and waiting times.

## Which modes exist at the bounce?

Write the unit three-sphere harmonics as $Y_{n\alpha}$ with $n=0,1,\ldots$. The eigenvalue of $-\nabla^2$ is $n(n+2)$ and the multiplicity is $(n+1)^2$. For harmonics with unit integral of $|Y|^2$, the sum of $|Y_{n\alpha}|^2$ across the multiplet is $(n+1)^2/(2\pi^2)$ at every point. Habib, Molina-París and Mottola use the label $k=n+1$. Their equations also show where the scale factor and Hubble friction enter the minimally coupled scalar equation. A finite band or a specified spatial filter gives a well-defined field variance. Full local stress-energy requires an ultraviolet state and renormalization. [Habib et al., 1999 preprint, Eqs. 18–23 and §§IV–V](https://arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/9906120#S2.E18)

At the exact stationary scalar background, $\dot{\bar\phi}=V'(\bar\phi)=0$ throughout the closed de Sitter solution. A change of time coordinate alters $\delta\phi$ by a term proportional to $\dot{\bar\phi}$, which vanishes here. The linear matter source for a metric perturbation also vanishes after absorbing the background potential into the cosmological constant. The degree-one scalar field on this fixed background is therefore a valid first-order field fluctuation. The homogeneous mode is a separate coordinate. On a rolling scalar background the lapse and shift enforce constraints, and the scalar-derived traceless metric harmonic vanishes at $n=1$. The usual reduced inhomogeneous curvature action applies for $n\ge2$. A formula derived by dividing by $n(n+2)-3$ cannot decide the dipole sector. Noh, Hwang and Barrow exhibit physical exceptional modes in closed fluid models. The material system, gauge conditions and harmonic convention matter. [Kravchenko, v5, 17 September 2026, Appendix B.1, Eqs. 72–76](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18522v5#A2.SS1) · [Noh, Hwang and Barrow, 2020, §§III–IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.14108#S3)

A further restriction appears on compact Cauchy slices with Killing symmetries. Extending linear Einstein–matter perturbations to a family of exact solutions can require the total quadratic charges for the Killing fields to vanish. Matter and gravitational perturbations both contribute. Losic and Unruh illustrate the constraint in a massless scalar example on global de Sitter. Its precise specialization to the present tachyonic branches and a state of gravity remains open. Thus a kinematic covariance or projector bound describes the effective field Hilbert space. Attaining a mathematical optimum in the full gravitational theory requires a physical state, including the global constraints. [Losic and Unruh, 2006, Eq. 54 and discussion](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0604122) · [De Vuyst et al., §§2.4.1 and 2.5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19931#S2.SS4.SSS1)

## How should the initial fluctuation be specified?

An inverted oscillator has no normalizable lowest-energy oscillator state. Epstein and Moschella construct positive quantizations for general negative mass squared which break de Sitter symmetry and identify exceptional mass values with a separate discrete gauge construction. Consequently a finite-time width, a reference frequency and a spatial filter are physical preparation assumptions. In a different expanding constant-roll setup, Tomberg derives the stochastic noise from the mode at the coarse-graining scale $k=\sigma_c aH$. The value $H/(2\pi)$ is recovered in the slow-roll limit. The exit-time distribution also uses specified absorbing and reflecting boundaries. Both the geometry and mode state would have to change to turn that calculation into a closed-bounce preparation prediction. [Epstein and Moschella, 2014, §§1 and 7–8](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.3319) · [Tomberg, June 2026 preprint, Eqs. 29–30](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09690#S3.E29)

Easson studies a separate single-scalar positive-curvature solution joining a bounce to inflation. A contracting-branch adiabatic state is imposed only when the canonical frequency squared is positive and varies sufficiently slowly. The low harmonics monitored at the bounce have a separate role from the high modes associated with an observable pivot. Kravchenko's two-field calculation also distinguishes a finite-time spectator fluctuation from late-time density isocurvature, and explicitly limits its demonstrated bounce to a finite interval in v5. Different preparations and potentials can produce regular perturbations, so results on a steep archived maximum apply to the two branches studied here. [Easson, JCAP 08 (2026) 040, §V and Appendix B](https://doi.org/10.1088/1475-7516/2026/08/040) · [Kravchenko, v5, §9](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18522v5#S9)

## Can tunnelling prepare the stationary solution?

For one specific test, continue the exact four-dimensional stationary scalar to the complete round Euclidean four-sphere of curvature scale $H_*$. The scalar fluctuation eigenvalues in units of $H_*^2$ are $L(L+3)-\beta$ with $\beta=|V''|/H_*^2$. Each eigenvalue occurs $(L+1)(L+2)(2L+3)/6$ times. Here the archived masses give $\beta\simeq207.255$ and $140.966$. On the positive-minimum branch the negative levels span $L=0,\ldots,12$, with **3,185** negative scalar directions. On the negative-minimum branch they span $L=0,\ldots,10$, with **1,716**. These are four-sphere scalar Hessian counts. The Lorentzian spatial three-sphere has a different operator and degeneracies. [Archived branch masses](../prior/hilltop/HILLTOP.json) · [Markkanen, Rajantie and Stopyra, 2018, Eqs. 130–132](https://arxiv.org/html/1809.06923#S4.E130)

Both exceed the single negative mode required in the usual false-vacuum interpretation of a homogeneous Hawking–Moss saddle. Substituting the potential height into its exponential cannot supply a launch probability here. Another Euclidean saddle, a chosen contour for a no-boundary wave function, or an incoming quantum state would be a new preparation prescription. A decay rate would also require a specified parent vacuum, boundary conditions, saddle and fluctuation determinant. The count includes the scalar sector on the full sphere and assigns no tunnelling rate. [Rajantie and Stopyra, Phys. Rev. ## What does a two-time probability mean?

Landau and Pollak obtained the sharp allowed concentrations of a wave function and its Fourier transform in finite intervals. For a quadratic Hamiltonian, the classical linear transfer $Q_T=AQ_0+Bv_0$ makes the two field measurements a scaled Fourier pair up to unitary quadratic phases when $B\ne0$. If $[Q_0,v_0]=i\kappa$, then $[Q_0,Q_T]=i\kappa B$. For intervals of full widths $w_0,w_T$ the squared norm of the product of their projectors is the largest prolate concentration eigenvalue $\lambda_0(w_0w_T/(4|\kappa B|))$. Lin et al. give this normalization for position and momentum in Eq. 29. The present contribution is using the transfer and waiting times of the closed hilltops. [Landau and Pollak, Bell System Technical Journal 40 (1961), 65–84, §§I–III](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~saito/data/ONR15/PSWF-II.pdf) · [Janjan and Kheirandish, 2023, Eq. 53](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19052#S3.E53) · [Lin et al., v2, 2 July 2026, Eqs. 27–30](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04484v2#A1.E29)

The unmeasured marginals obey $p_0+p_T\le1+\sqrt{\lambda_0}$. An ideal first measurement projects onto its interval, and the subsequent joint success probability is bounded by $\lambda_0$. Continuous residence in a window, or an unmonitored trajectory that reheats, requires its own probabilities and dynamics. Halliwell already treated successive position samplings, including the distinction between measured sequences and probabilities assigned to decoherent histories. [Halliwell, Phys. Rev. D 48 (1993) 2739](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.48.2739)

## What could an observation constrain?

ACT DR6 constrains a late-time curvature parameter, and separately reconstructs primordial *curvature* power in wavenumber bins. For its combination of Planck and ACT primary spectra, CMB lensing and BAO, the curvature extension of the baseline model gives $\Omega_k=0.0019\pm0.0015$. Using this information here would require a predicted expansion through reheating and later epochs, a present curvature radius, and the map from the prepared field modes to metric and matter perturbations. Nonlinear tachyonic simulations in other potentials demonstrate fragmentation, while the calculations here stop at a fluctuation scale or a specified homogeneous displacement. Neither the time of domain formation nor a CMB likelihood is measured in this release. [ACT DR6, 2025, §§IV.2 and VII.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14454#S7.SS1) · [Felder et al., 2001, §II and figures](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/0012142#S2)
