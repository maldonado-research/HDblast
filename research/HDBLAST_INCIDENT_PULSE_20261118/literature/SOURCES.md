# Matching a scalar pulse to a quiet exterior

A compact scalar profile can change the gravitational data outside its support. The B1 question is whether the metric and its required derivatives return to the prescribed background at the second cut while the scalar field carries an incident disturbance. The following primary studies identify which parts of that question have precedents.

**Local gravitational gluing.** Chruściel, Isenberg and Pollack construct smooth vacuum initial data by joining neighborhoods of two points, leaving the original data exactly unchanged outside designated regions. Their Theorem 1.1 assumes the absence of local Killing initial data in each gluing region. In vacuum these are lapse–shift pairs in the kernel of the formal adjoint of the linearized constraint operator. Pairing a compactly supported correction with such a kernel element gives a compatibility condition by integration by parts. Their construction covers spatial dimension at least three. Their separate matter theorem preserves an energy condition and requires strictness at the joining points. A prescribed scalar action, a shell junction and a chosen pulse require their own analysis. A small singular value of a restricted B1 shooting matrix alone does not identify a spacetime symmetry. [Chruściel, Isenberg and Pollack, *Initial data engineering*, Theorems 1.1 and 1.4, v2, 16 June 2005](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0403066v2)

**Scalar stress enters both constraints.** In the conventions of Choquet-Bruhat, Isenberg and Pollack, the Hamiltonian source contains scalar momentum squared, spatial gradient squared and twice the potential. The momentum source is minus the scalar momentum times its spatial gradient. This explains why adjusting a directional pulse affects more than one matching condition. Their conformal analysis treats closed spatial manifolds, with the general existence discussion restricted to constant mean curvature. The HDblast problem includes a timelike wall. [Choquet-Bruhat, Isenberg and Pollack, *The constraint equations for the Einstein-scalar field system on compact manifolds*, Eqs. 4–5 and §4.1, v2, 11 December 2006](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0610045v2)

**A recent shooting experiment.** Lee varied a scalar profile with more adjustable coefficients than endpoint conditions, continued solution branches and rechecked residuals on successively refined grids. This enabled numerical matching of flat initial data to an extremal Reissner–Nordström horizon along an outgoing null hypersurface. That construction uses a spherically symmetric charged field coupled to Einstein–Maxwell gravity. Its transferable features are the treatment of endpoint residuals, independent admissibility conditions and refinement checks. The B1 pulse lies on a spacelike slice and has a different field content. [Lee, *Flexible numerical characteristic gluing to extremal Reissner–Nordström black holes*, §§2–3 and Appendix B, v1, 18 September 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22518v1)

**Wave response at a brane.** Cardoso and colleagues derive a boundary condition relating the normal derivative of a bulk scalar to a separate field on a de Sitter brane in five-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime. Their time-domain evolution checks resonant behavior predicted by spectral analysis in a linear model. Deng and colleagues evolve scalar–gravitational wave packets about a static five-dimensional thick brane. Their delayed echoes occur for split-brane configurations with two effective potential barriers. For B1, the arrival of a disturbance, the shell’s field response and the transfer of energy to material particles are distinct quantities to measure. [Cardoso et al., Eqs. 10–11 and §V, v2, 15 March 2007](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0612202v2) · [Deng et al., §§2–3.2, v1, 28 August 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.20937v1)
