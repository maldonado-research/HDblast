# Independent evolution of the parent stress and force

The original closed-field shell supplies its own scalar correlations. A scalar prepared in the instantaneous vacuum at bulk time $T=0.70$ evolves to a state with nonzero field–momentum covariance by $T=1.11$. In the fine simulation the latter slice has proper clock $s=H_0\tau=1.30371703$. Changing this covariance at that instant preserves the initial density, pressure and scalar force, yet changes how those sources develop. We calculate this effect on each of four recorded geometries with an independent proper-time propagator. [Original coupled walls](https://github.com/maldonado-research/HDblast/tree/838834ff58748b559a14726a3a16b8716051be35/research/HDBLAST_SOURCE_WORK_20261118/coupled) · [Initial-source derivation](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24356875)

## Four preparations with the same initial sources

Write $X=\langle\chi_q^2\rangle$, $P=\langle\Pi_q^2\rangle$ and $C=\langle\{\chi_q,\Pi_q\}/2\rangle$, with $\Pi_q=a^3 d\chi_q/ds$. These denote the covariance of each real oscillator. Define $d\nu=q^2dq/(2\pi^2)$, $k=q/a$, $E^2=k^2+G^2(\phi-0.5)^2$, $G=100$. The native scalar sources use the finite-band instantaneous ground-state subtraction with $0<q<8$. Differences of the four preparations on the same wall cancel this common reference.

Three preparations retain $X,P$ at each momentum and replace the evolved $C$ by $C$, $0$ or $-C$. They have identical occupation in the instantaneous basis, density, pressure and force. The reflected covariance has the same uncertainty determinant as the original. Setting $C$ to zero gives a permissible mixed Gaussian covariance since $XP\ge XP-C^2$. These comparisons specify the post-intervention state. Producing it with a physical apparatus would require a model for that apparatus.

A fourth comparison uses the same canonical shear at every momentum, $\Pi\mapsto\Pi+\kappa\chi$. This transformation leaves $X$ fixed and produces $P'=P+2\kappa C+\kappa^2 X$, $C'=C+\kappa X$. Choosing

$$
\kappa=-2\frac{\int C\,d\nu}{\int X\,d\nu}
$$

gives $\int(P'-P)d\nu=0$. Thus mean density, pressure and scalar force again match. Individual mode occupations can change. The shear preserves each mode's uncertainty determinant and is generated by a spatially local quadratic impulse for the modeled field. The coefficient is chosen using the incoming state. Its zero integrated change in parent energy applies at the instant of the impulse.

On the fine mesh the reconstructed nonzero $C_q$ ranges from 0.003080 to 0.016987 at the 24 momentum nodes. The shear has $\kappa=-0.31674092$ and changes the occupation of individual modes by up to 0.000257. All four preparations start with $\rho/H_0^4=0.03287835$, $p/H_0^4=1.906075$ and $j/H_0^4=8.158928$ in the native scalar-force convention. The native finite-difference history reproduces these values within $5.0\times10^{-6}$ absolute on that mesh. The native junctions see equal one-point sources at the intervention, including the pre-existing scalar contribution.

## Initial rates and energy balance

Compare each preparation against the case with $C=0$. Their initial $X$ values are identical per mode, and their $P$ integrals are equal. Let $D_q$ be their initial canonical covariance difference $C_q-C_{q,\rm erased}$. Hamilton's equations give $dX/ds=2C/a^3$ and $dP/ds=-2a^3E^2C$. Differentiating the native sources gives, on this slice,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta j' &= \frac{2 G^2(\phi-0.5)}{a^3}\int D_q\,d\nu,\\
\Delta p' &= -\frac{2}{a^3}\int\left[m^2+\frac{2k^2}{3}\right]D_q\,d\nu,\\
\Delta\rho' &=0,\qquad
\Delta\rho''=\phi'\Delta j'-3h\Delta p',\qquad h=d\log(a)/ds .
\end{aligned}
$$

The density identity follows by differentiating $\rho'+3h(\rho+p)=j\phi'$ on a common wall. The results below are one-sided proper-time derivatives, with densities and force expressed in $H_0^4$ units.

| Fine mesh, relative to erased | $\Delta j'$ | $\Delta p'$ | $\Delta\rho''$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Retained | −287.38865 | −63.41478 | −119.09091 |
| Reflected | +287.38865 | +63.41478 | +119.09091 |
| Common shear | +287.38865 | +65.17867 | +114.75031 |

The rates of the pressure and force distinguish the retained state from its reflection as soon as evolution resumes. Correspondingly, time derivatives of the metric and scalar normal-derivative conditions that involve $p'$ and $j'$ can change at the operation. Continuity of the sources at the fork supplies matching to zeroth order. A preparation required to be smooth in time would need its finite-duration history.

## Propagation on a common geometry

We use cubic Hermite interpolation of $\log a$ and $\phi$ against measured shell proper time, with their stored derivatives. The complex mode evolves from the earlier slice in the physical field and its proper velocity by

$$
\chi_q''+3h\chi_q'+E^2\chi_q=0 .
$$

A separate pair of real fundamental solutions transports each fork covariance to the readout. This avoids using the native covariance equations to integrate these sources. The fork gets no particles or external background energy from the audit. After the fork each comparison propagates freely on the selected archived wall. The work check integrates $a^3j\phi'$ and $3a^3hp$ with a separate composite Simpson quadrature and compares their difference to the change of $a^3\rho$. The normalized work residual is below $9\times10^{-9}$ across all 32 fixed-wall evolutions at baseline resolution. The largest change in any final $\rho,p,j$ after increasing from 24 to 48 momenta and 2,049 to 4,097 work samples is below $6\times10^{-7}$ in these units. This refinement tests the independent replay on each fixed input geometry. The bulk meshes represent separate numerical solutions.

For a fixed geometry every source is linear in the propagated covariance up to the common subtraction. The erased source therefore equals the arithmetic midpoint of the retained and reflected sources, at every later time. This gives a reference for testing a fully coupled calculation in which each preparation develops its own geometry. The matching state and source curvatures in this appendix describe the parent. An emitted-fermion observable requires its separate interaction calculation.
