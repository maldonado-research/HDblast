# Scalar covariance, feedback and the shell junctions

A scalar population can have the same initial density, pressure and scalar force in two preparations while retaining different field–momentum correlations. Two published calculations identify what must be propagated to tell them apart.

## Evolution of the source

Eltzner and Gottschalk evolve the three equal-time covariances of a scalar field and its canonical momentum in a homogeneous, spatially flat four-dimensional cosmology. Their Eq. (6) gives $\dot G_{\chi\chi}=2G_{(\chi\Pi)}/a^3$. Two initial states with equal $G_{\chi\chi}$ and opposite mixed covariances therefore acquire different field variances immediately. Their Eq. (7) conserves $G_{\chi\chi}G_{\Pi\Pi}-G_{(\chi\Pi)}^2$ for each free mode. This gives a check on propagation even when the oscillator frequency varies. The pure-state value $1/4$ in Eq. (8) applies to the full canonical covariance. A finite excitation contribution obtained by subtracting a reference covariance can have another determinant. Their semiclassical Einstein equation uses a renormalized absolute stress. Computing the finite contribution of prepared excitations on the shell leaves the choice of absolute vacuum stress as separate input. [Eltzner and Gottschalk, Eqs. (6)–(10)](https://arxiv.org/html/1003.3630#S2.E6)

A 2026 preprint by Lozanov, Pritchard and Sasaki studies a scalar mass crossing during four-dimensional inflation. Particle production changes the field variance that feeds back on the rolling inflaton. Their Eq. (6) describes the resulting change in the inflaton potential. The late-time approximation in Eqs. (26)–(27) uses the produced occupation $|\beta_k|^2$. The earlier equal-time covariance equation explains why the mixed covariance matters when comparing preparations *through* a crossing. Demonstrating variance feedback on a particular HDblast trajectory would test that trajectory and its junctions. The inflationary example already realizes mass-crossing feedback without an extra dimension. [Lozanov, Pritchard and Sasaki, revised September 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26920v2)

## What a continuous variance record can determine

Gottschalk and Siemssen express spatial coincidence limits of the three covariances as even momentum moments and derive the adjacent-moment evolution $\partial_\eta M_n=A M_n+B M_{n+1}$ in Sec. 4.1. This supplies a direct precedent for the hierarchy used here. On a common, sufficiently smooth background, with the two initial diagonal covariances matched mode by mode, the time derivatives of the variance difference at preparation successively determine the moments of the remaining mixed covariance. For an integrable signed difference on a compact momentum band, polynomials in $q^2$ are dense among continuous functions of $q$ there. The full sequence consequently fixes that difference almost everywhere. This is the classical compact moment argument applied to a restricted inverse problem. Finite time samples and measurement noise leave conditioning and resolution as separate questions. For comparison, multimode optical tomography uses correlations at pairs of time delays and a projection onto the well-resolved singular modes. [Gottschalk and Siemssen, Sec. 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1809.03812#S4.SS1) · [Hausdorff, *Summationsmethoden und Momentfolgen I*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01378337) · [Hubenschmid and Burkard, Sec. II](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10483#S2)

## What must enter the wall evolution

Maeda and Wands derive the exchange relation for conformally coupled brane matter by combining the Codazzi equation, the metric junction and the scalar jump. Their derivation also exhibits a bulk normal-derivative datum in the induced scalar equations. A homogeneous brane conservation equation by itself leaves that datum undetermined. HDblast has a different scalar coupling, $m^2(\phi)\chi^2/2$. Its own signed action gives $j=(\partial_\phi m^2)\langle\chi^2\rangle/2$ and $\dot\rho+3H(\rho+p)=j\dot\phi$. Comparing the work integral against the density change tests the matter calculation. Testing gravitational feedback also requires that the computed density, pressure and force enter the bulk boundary equations. [Maeda and Wands, Sec. II, especially Eqs. (11), (15) and (26)](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0008188) · [HDblast signed matter action and junctions](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22922928)
