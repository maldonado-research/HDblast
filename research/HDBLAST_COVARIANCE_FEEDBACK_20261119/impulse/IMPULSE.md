# A spatially uniform preparation with zero net work at the fork

At the common pre-pulse fork let $X_q=\langle\chi_q^2\rangle$, $P_q=\langle\Pi_q^2\rangle$ and $C_q=\langle\{\chi_q,\Pi_q\}/2\rangle$ for the canonical real oscillator, with $\Pi=a^3\dot\chi$ and $d\nu=q^2dq/(2\pi^2)$. A uniform quadratic impulse shifts every momentum by the same coefficient times its own field, $\Pi_q\mapsto\Pi_q+\kappa\chi_q$. It can be generated by a spatially homogeneous mass-squared pulse for the band-limited field. The map has determinant one. It preserves each mode's uncertainty determinant.

The transformed moments are $X_q$, $P_q+2\kappa C_q+\kappa^2X_q$, and $C_q+\kappa X_q$. The force is unchanged because it reads only $X_q$. The change in physical density equals the change in pressure,

$$
\Delta\rho=\Delta p=a^{-6}\left(\kappa\int C_q\,d\nu+\frac{\kappa^2}{2}\int X_q\,d\nu\right).
$$

When the initial integrated $C$ is nonzero there is exactly one nonzero spatially uniform shear that preserves both sources, $\kappa_*=-2\int C_q\,d\nu/\int X_q\,d\nu$. The ideal impulse reverses the integrated $C$, hence its contribution to the initial time derivative of the force. Within this one-parameter family half that shear extracts the largest energy density. For the fine fork the full neutral shear is −0.316741 in canonical simulation units. The largest extractable density is $0.0082043\,H_0^4$, about 25% of the initial density above the model's subtracted vacuum. The neutral shear changes the per-mode occupation by up to 0.000257. Preparing this state requires an external control, whose origin the simulation does not specify. [Source and numerical values](FINITE.json)

## Can a pulse of finite duration approach the ideal operation?

For a separate oscillator check, freeze the scale factor and mass at their fork values and apply a rectangular positive mass-squared increment $U=-\kappa/(a^3t)$ for a proper duration $t$. We propagate each $(\chi,\Pi)$ through the resulting oscillator exactly. The external comoving work at the two switching edges is $W=a^3 U\int(X_{\rm in}-X_{\rm out})\,d\nu/2$. It equals the change in free comoving energy. At each duration we choose the nonzero branch that restores the integrated field variance, so the net work and the change in scalar force both vanish. Individual modes can exchange energy with this pulse.

| Duration in $H_0^{-1}$ | Integrated shear | Relative cross-covariance error vs ideal |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.010000 | −0.402534 | 25.22% |
| 0.002500 | −0.339148 | 6.14% |
| 0.000625 | −0.322402 | 1.52% |

The error is the weighted root-mean-square difference from the ideal final $C$ divided by the size of the ideal change. The finite-duration control has a residual pressure change. At the shortest duration it is $0.000532\,H_0^4$ relative to the initial pressure, and the pulse requires $U=773.0\,H_0^2$. These controls hold geometry and baseline mass fixed. The 5D evolution uses the ideal shear as initial data. Extending the pulse to ultraviolet modes or sourcing it dynamically would require a new model. [Exact oscillator propagation](finite_pulse.py)
