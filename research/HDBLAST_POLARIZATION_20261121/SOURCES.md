# Sources for the two-polarization study

The preceding HDblast calculation supplies the shell histories, outer electromagnetic source, charged-particle birth law and comparison with a moving thermal stream. Its integral over emitted energy fixes the normalization for this edition. Every inherited numerical input in the capsule can be compared with its checksum at the revision tested by the preceding public replay. O. O. Sobol, E. V. Gorbar, M. Kamarpour and S. I. Vilchinskii derive the energy exchanged between a scalar and an electromagnetic field with a scalar-dependent kinetic coefficient. Their equations (2)–(6) also retain a charged current. The scalar loses or gains energy according to the difference of electric and magnetic field strengths. Their four-dimensional inflationary setting provides a check on the form of the energy exchange used here. The radial five-dimensional evolution and the daughter thresholds in this capsule follow the HDblast equations. [Influence of backreaction of electric fields and Schwinger effect on inflationary magnetogenesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.09851)

Aviv David and Robert Brandenberger follow coupled scalar and gauge-field modes in a 2026 study of a pseudoscalar after recombination. They evaluate how energy extraction limits electromagnetic amplification. The interaction and cosmological epoch differ from this capsule. The shared physical question is how long an evolving field can supply a growing mode. [Study of scalar and gauge-field backreaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20793)

Taiki Jikei and Takanobu Amano estimate the distance ions need to scatter into different directions in electron–ion shocks. Their result depends on the growth of magnetic structure and electron heating. This provides one example of how to quantify a mechanism for redistributing momentum. The present calculations use charged daughters on an expanding shell and supply no measured plasma scattering timescale. [Saturation Level of Ion Weibel Instability and Isotropization Length Scale in Electron-Ion Weibel-Mediated Shocks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00462)
