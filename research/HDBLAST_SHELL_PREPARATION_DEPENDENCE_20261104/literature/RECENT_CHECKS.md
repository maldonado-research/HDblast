## Primary passages actually read

1. **2501.05036, section 5.5**, [Constant Potential Solutions](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05036#S5.SS5): the paper explicitly says it needs three initial conditions, radius, radial velocity and scalar value at a fixed time. Constant-potential shift symmetry removes the scalar value and time-translation symmetry removes one more parameter, so its nontrivial solutions have one parameter. Section 5.5.1 imposes a time-symmetric initial radius and derives the acceleration in equation (5.34). This is an exact finite-dimensional initial-data dependence result in its own minisuperspace model. It is not a theorem about the homogeneous freedom in a second constraint completion.

   **Section 7**, [Conclusions](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05036#S7), states that the scalar sits on the brane while the bulk is pure gravity; it describes the effective Liouville action at small brane curvature and the constant-potential cosmological solution classes. These facts prevent conflating its brane-local scalar with a scalar propagating through the bulk.

2. **2609.11643, section 2.1**, [Basic description](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11643#S2.SS1): action (2.1) and Neumann condition (2.3) define a scalar localized on a two-dimensional EOW brane in AdS3. **Section 1 and the cosmological/no-boundary discussion** explain that an arbitrary initial finite-size geometry is replaced by smooth Euclidean closure; the authors compute semiclassical on-shell weights, while leaving normalization and contour questions open. **The concluding stability discussion** states that linearized Neumann conditions produce an oscillatory equation for the physical brane perturbation and no exponentially growing mode within the approximation. This is linear stability, not second-order constrained-data robustness or nonlinear stability.

## Consequence for the preparation question

These recent papers are useful context but do not supply the sought quantitative theorem in the inspected primary passages. The closest genuine connection is the distinction between **freely supplied classical initial data** (2501.05036) and **a proposed state/regularity prescription selecting histories** (2609.11643). Importing that selection prescription into the five-dimensional bulk-scalar problem would require a separate model derivation.

For the fixed-first-order shell problem, ordinary differentiation of a smooth solution map explains why changing only the second-order initial datum changes the second-order solution by a homogeneous linearized evolution, and why a smooth observable then has an affine dependence on that datum. That structural machinery is not made new by a recent braneworld citation. A rigorously bounded, gauge-invariant target volume/expansion response for the specified compact constraint-compatible family is a model-specific application requiring its own nonzero functional or rank certificate. Neither these recent papers nor general initial-data dependence certifies its slope or a sign reversal.
