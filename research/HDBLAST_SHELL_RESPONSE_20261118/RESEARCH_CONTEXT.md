# How angular symmetry and observer motion enter the response

## Angular selection and the shell boundary

Kodama, Ishibashi and Seto treat perturbations of a warped product with maximally symmetric spatial sections. At linear order different angular eigenvalues decouple. For a scalar-type perturbation, the orbit-space metric components are multiples of a scalar harmonic, the mixed components involve its gradient, and the spatial components combine its trace and trace-free Hessian. Their scalar spectrum on a unit three-sphere gives eigenvalue 8 at degree two. The constant scalar harmonic is exceptional because its derived vector and trace-free tensor vanish. Their brane junction equations also carry angular information. Adapting the decomposition requires checking the actual gauge, complete initial data, boundary operators and uniqueness in the model being studied. Their single master equation (160) uses a vacuum constant-curvature specialization and excludes the exceptional harmonics in that form. See §§II.3, II.3.3, III.3.3 and IV.2.3, especially Eqs. (38)–(46), (121)–(124), and (160). [Kodama, Ishibashi and Seto, Phys. Rev. D 62, 064022 (2000), arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0004160v3)

Lindblom, Taylor and Zhang give an independent normalization reference. Their index *k* labels total harmonic degree on the three-sphere, while *ℓ* labels harmonics on its two-sphere angular sections. For radius R the scalar eigenvalue is −k(k+2)/R². Their orthonormal functions use the volume integral divided by R³. Here a round-sphere *mean* uses division by 2π²R³, which matters when reporting a quadrupole projection. See §2, Eqs. (1) and (9). [Lindblom, Taylor and Zhang (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10714-017-2303-y)

## Moving cuts and measured scalars

Mars derives the first and second derivatives of a tensor pulled back along a varying embedding. In the scalar application, the second derivative contains two copies of the first field derivative transported by the first embedding variation. When the background scalar is constant throughout the fixed shell, its derivatives along the shell vanish. This explains why some observer-acceleration terms cancel in that observable while the transport of the first field derivative survives. A normalized integral also varies through its induced density. Heinesen, Mourier and Buchert separate field evolution, measure expansion and boundary transport in a time derivative of a relativistic average. Their parameter is foliation time. The model’s amplitude derivative requires a separate differentiation at fixed observer time. [Mars, §3, Lemma 1 (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1088/0264-9381/22/16/013) · [Heinesen, Mourier and Buchert, §3.1, Eqs. (8)–(10) (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6382/ab0618)

## What a quadratic source can establish

Brizuela and colleagues exhibit quadratic sources in the second-order polar equations of a spherical perfect fluid and separate the equations by allowed multipoles. Kwon and colleagues give a recent worked example of projecting a quadratic source after imposing its second-order Hamiltonian and momentum constraints. Their neutron-star demonstration uses a static quadrupolar response. Its numerical coupling coefficients depend on stellar matter, exterior matching and the imposed tide. Both studies motivate computing the second-order source and constraints together. The five-dimensional shell’s quadratic scalar monopole still requires its own solution. [Brizuela et al., §IV.2, Eqs. (97)–(103), Phys. Rev. D 82, 104039 (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.82.104039) · [Kwon et al., §III.3, Eqs. (125)–(132), preprint v1, 13 October 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18952v1)

Béchaz and colleagues give an observational comparison. Their geodesic light-cone construction describes photons on the past light cone and a two-dimensional cut at fixed observation variables. The shell construction uses a three-dimensional spacelike cut carried by timelike observers. A comparison to luminosity distance would additionally require a map to light propagation, sources and measurement. See §3.1, Eqs. (3.78)–(3.81). [Béchaz et al., JCAP 03 (2026) 075](https://doi.org/10.1088/1475-7516/2026/03/075)

Deng and colleagues solve for perturbations of a five-dimensional Einstein–scalar thick brane with Poincaré-invariant slices. Their split-brane simulations exhibit position-dependent echoes. The effective potentials, boundary geometry and observation points differ from those of the regular de Sitter shell used here. Their waveforms illustrate how model-dependent an extra-dimensional signature can be. [Deng et al., §4, JHEP 01 (2026) 066](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP01(2026)066)
