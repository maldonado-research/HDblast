## Initial-state admissibility and actual state accuracy

The inherited [Lindig, *Not all adiabatic vacua are physical states*, `hep-th/9808133`](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9808133), [Baacke–Kevlishvili, *Initial time singularities and admissible initial states for a system of coupled scalar fields*, `0910.1128`](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.1128), and [Junker–Schrohe, *Adiabatic vacuum states on general spacetime manifolds: Definition, construction, and physical properties*, `math-ph/0109010`](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0109010) already identify the immediate distinction: stress regularity, finite adiabatic order, and the Hadamard condition are separate properties. The October 1 review specifically rejects the old general assertion that every adiabatic state is Hadamard. None of these articles was newly retrieved here, so their titles and implications retain the prior reading provenance.

The present project initialization is the inherited BD target: first-order perturbations vanish before the compact source begins at t=-5, and the values of U and W at a=-4.5 are fixed by retarded Duhamel propagation over the source prehistory. It is not initialized by a new finite-time WKB prescription. The following WKB observation is general context for alternative state models. For a declared WKB prescription, amplitude and derivative belong to the same initial data. In the conventional ansatz `v=(2W)^(-1/2) exp(-i integral W dt)`, differentiation gives `v'=(-iW-W'/(2W))v`. Omitting the derivative term changes the state except in the applicable special cases. The frequency, derivative order, initial slice, low-momentum completion, and phase convention must therefore be specified together. A finite-time instantaneous vacuum and a transported state from an earlier preparation are different targets. A Hadamard condition still permits many states and does not choose one of them.

In the project's unchanged first-order finite-band model, exact normalization of both compared directions can establish `c(k)=0`; it does not establish a small oscillatory state difference `A(k)`. The October 7 theorem already shows that wrong homogeneous initial amplitudes can satisfy the same Ward identities. The analytic BD target is already specified; its prehistory values need independently justified enclosures, followed by a comparison with the retained input values and derivatives, converted into the theorem's `chi(k)` and `sigma(k)`. The new prospective BD-prehistory candidate addresses target enclosures at nine registered probes and analytic spectral/source bounds; it does not yet enclose the old stored binary80 state error. For real-source quadrature atoms, the linear normalization combination c can vanish regardless of the quadrature amplitudes, so normalization cannot bound prehistory quadrature error. Stored binary values can be enclosed as exact represented inputs without proving their closeness to the intended continuum state. Finite samples cannot certify the unsampled spectrum, the infrared completion, or the ultraviolet tail.
