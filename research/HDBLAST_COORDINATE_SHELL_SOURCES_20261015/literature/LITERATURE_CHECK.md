## Two numerical methods with explicit limits

**Jan Bouwe van den Berg and Maxime Breden, [A simple rigorous integrator for semilinear parabolic PDEs, arXiv:2601.05146v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05146v1).** The canonical arXiv history independently confirms 8 January 2026. Primary passages from §2.4, Theorem 2.9, give a product-Banach-space fixed-point criterion: computable defect bounds \(Y\), derivative bounds \(Z\), derivative-variation bounds \(W\), and explicit positive vectors \(r,\eta\) verify a self-map and strict contraction. The conclusion is a unique fixed point in the stated product box. The paper attributes this abstract criterion to an earlier theorem; no priority claim is made here. Its implementation paragraph reports Matlab/INTLAB interval arithmetic.

Equation (1) concerns parabolic equations on a torus; Remark 1.1 discusses symmetry reductions to homogeneous Dirichlet/Neumann conditions. Its PDE-specific spectral and smoothing estimates do not cover the present hyperbolic Robin shell. Only the abstract criterion is a proposed validation framework. The retrieved HTML carries a separate August 24, 2026 title-page date although its version header and canonical record identify v1; that date is not treated as an independently established revision.

**Xi Chen and Kuan Xu, [Fast spectral methods for Volterra integral equations, arXiv:2610.20206v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20206v1).** The direct canonical submission history confirms 14 October 2026, 13:50:09 UTC; the high identifier sequence alone is not used as date evidence. Primary §3, equations (15)–(18), applies the affine map \(s=(x+1)t/2+(x-1)/2\) to write an integral over \(-1\le s\le x\) as one over \(-1\le t\le1\). This respects the causal triangle and avoids extending the kernel outside it. The inspected text constructs Chebyshev approximations to smooth linear Volterra operators.

This is a possible approximation engine after deriving the actual coupled kernel and checking its regularity. The retrieved passages supply no interval enclosure, HDB residual constant, or derivative-observation bound. Smooth-kernel approximation and numerical accuracy cannot be substituted for those missing bounds.

## Proposed next use for full U2

For a known first solution, the second-order coefficient problem is linear in its unknown response. If its complete bulk/Robin/constraint reconstruction is represented by an affine map on a specified product space, the generic criterion above reduces to bounds of the form
\[
 Y+Zr\le r,\qquad Z\eta<\eta,\qquad r,\eta>0.
\]
Here \(Y\) must enclose the continuous defect of a reconstruction using the actual bulk and both boundary sources, coefficient uncertainties and initial/corner errors. \(Z\) must bound the actual coupled map. This is a proposed HDB construction, not a result already proved by the cited parabolic paper.
