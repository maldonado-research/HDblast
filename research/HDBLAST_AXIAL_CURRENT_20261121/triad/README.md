# Can three directional particle populations look like one hot gas?

An equal mixture of six narrow beams, along both signs of three perpendicular axes, has exactly the same pressure in all directions. A detector counting where the particles go can still see six concentrations. This calculation asks whether that distinction survives collisions and the changing electric field in the shell simulation. It uses the directions of the particles crossing a single wall-time slice. A detector can perform the count without identifying a particle's charge, species or energy.

At wall time 7.05 the three principal pressures of the dedicated-pole gas were 32.86%, 32.86% and 34.28% of its energy density in its energy rest frame. The lower bound on angular separation from every single classical thermal gas was **10.15 percentage points** to two decimal places, even allowing arbitrary bulk motion and temperature. Weighting by energy gave a **27.39-point** lower bound. The dedicated run averaged about 9.5 encounters per particle by this time. Across four samplings the number-weighted bound was 8.54–10.37 points. These are finite-model calculations. [Particle calculation](../live/) · [All measured moments and counting tests](TRIAD.json)

## What is being pooled?

Each of three equal-mass charged species couples to its own vector field, with mutually perpendicular primary field directions on the shell. The original equal-amplitude convention assigns each primary field one third of the collective squared amplitude. Dedicated poles keep both products in their incoming species. The shared pole allows an opposite-sign pair to emerge in any of the three species. The shared fixed-species control uses its own matched reduced inclusive cross section. The simulations with cyclic symmetry follow both signs of one representative species and obtain the other two by cyclic permutations of all momentum coordinates. A conversion updates the outgoing momenta in the new field's local basis. These rotated copies have a common numerical realization. [Three-field convention](https://github.com/maldonado-research/HDblast/blob/6a62a0798713dbe28d0d670376896893cd8d79a1/research/HDBLAST_KINETIC_TEST_20261121/driven/ORIENTATION.md) · [Collision rules](../flavor/)

Summing the three wall-frame stress tensors makes their diagonal spatial components equal. The off-diagonal components all equal the original mean of the three distinct off-diagonal entries and can be nonzero. We compute the principal pressures in the energy rest frame of the full summed tensor. A fixed common roll of 135 degrees around each species' primary axis gives (.3332, .3334, .3334) at 7.05 for the dedicated sample. Choosing a common roll separately at an individual time can make the pressures exactly equal in these samples. These are kinematic orientation tests. The natural cyclic configuration in the table uses zero added roll and includes the orientations used in the simulations. Changing the imposed transverse orientation can change the evolution of an interacting shared-pole gas.

## Angular bounds

Let $P$ be the measured distribution of unit momentum directions in the orthonormal frame of the wall. The thermal comparator is a single Maxwell–Jüttner distribution on the same equal-time slice, with arbitrary positive temperature and bulk velocity. Conditional on momentum magnitude, its density relative to uniform solid angle is $k\exp(k\mu)/\sinh k$, where $\mu$ is the direction cosine against the bulk velocity and $k=\Gamma v |p|/T$ in units with $c=1$. Its continuous limit at $k=0$ is one. Number weighting, positive energy weighting and conditioning on an energy interval all preserve the angular distribution as a mixture over $k$.

* For cap half-angle $a<45^\circ$, let $p_+$ and $p_-$ be the representative species' fractions within $a$ of its positive and negative longitudinal axes, pooling both charges. Write $b=p_++p_-$ and $d=\min(p_+,p_-)$. The corresponding six caps are disjoint. Dropping the one, two or three poles closest to the test velocity retains measured fractions at least $(2b+d)/3$, $(b+2d)/3$ or $d$. The worst-case bounds follow by counting the signs left from each axis. All three results allow independent rolls of the species around their respective primary axes.
* The six pole cosines against any unit velocity direction, in descending order, are bounded above entry by entry by $1,1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt3,0,0,-1/\sqrt3$. For a pole with cosine upper bound $h$, every direction inside its cap has cosine at most $u=\cos[\max(0,\arccos h-a)]$. With $s=\max(0,u)$, Jensen's inequality on $[2s-1,1]$ gives $\sinh(k)/k\ge(1-s)e^{ks}$. The target cap probability is at most $(1-\cos a)/[2(1-s)]$. Sum this ceiling over the retained caps and subtract it from the observed floor. Maximizing the gap over the nine recorded cap angles and three retained counts gives the thermal distance bounds. The stationary isotropic ceiling over all six caps is $3(1-\cos a)$.
* For **any axially symmetric angular distribution** about any chosen line, discard the two caps on the coordinate axis nearest that line. The remaining four caps have centers at least 45 degrees from either end of the line. On any latitude ring, the fraction in one retained cap is at most $\arcsin(\sqrt{2}\sin a)/\pi$. This follows by maximizing its azimuthal width on rings about the chosen line. The four-cap observed floor is $2b/3$. At 6.67 the species-changing run gives a count-distance lower bound of .257 to this larger target class. The ring witness can lose sensitivity as the beams broaden.

## A fourth-order angular check

The cubic harmonic $H(n)=\tfrac52(\sum_i n_i^4-3/5)$ has mean zero under a uniform sky and one for six ideal coordinate beams. The natural dedicated three-species mixture has mean .625 at 6.67 and .271 at 7.05. The code also calculates the exact range over independent transverse rolls by writing the azimuthal term as $\tfrac58\operatorname{Re}[e^{4i\psi}\langle(n_x+i n_y)^4\rangle]$ plus an angle-independent term.

A rotating gas can have a fourth moment, so a nonzero $\langle H\rangle$ alone does not exclude it. Let $v(n)$ be the nine-component unit vector of normalized degree-four spherical harmonics, with $v(n)\cdot v(u)=P_4(n\cdot u)$. Then $m=\langle v\rangle_P$ and $f(u)=m\cdot v(u)$. Every distribution axially symmetric about $u$ has its moment vector parallel to $v(u)$. The squared norm is $\|m\|^2=9\langle f^2\rangle_\Omega$, where the angular average uses the uniform sphere. The cubic projection of $f$ is $cH$, with $c=7\langle H\rangle_P/12$. Thus $\max|f|\le M=|c|+3\sqrt{\langle(f-cH)^2\rangle_\Omega}$ by the reproducing kernel and Cauchy–Schwarz. Since the diameter of $v(n)$ is $\sqrt{20/7}$, the angular TV distance from every axially symmetric law is at least $\sqrt{\max(0,\|m\|^2-M^2)}/\sqrt{20/7}$. Five Gauss–Legendre nodes and nine uniform azimuths integrate the squared harmonics exactly up to rounding.

At 7.05 this fourth-order check still yields count gaps of .068 for the dedicated run, .060 with bulk return and .077 for the species-changing shared run. It constrains an unknown preferred line even for arbitrary angular profiles about that line. These bounds use the natural cyclic orientations. A separate run with individually evolved species can apply the same fourth-order test directly to its measured global directions. A superposition of independently moving gases can have more than one preferred line and lies outside this comparator.

## Reproduction and limits

Run the enclosing archive replay, or pass the generated arrays to the diagnostic.

```sh
python -I -B triad/diagnose.py --snapshots OUT/live/snapshots \
  --cross-snapshots OUT/live/cross-snapshots --shared-snapshots OUT/flavor/snapshots \
  --return-snapshots OUT/return/snapshots --output OUT/triad/TRIAD.json
```

The JSON retains every supplied seed, time and control, all cap choices, number- and energy-weighted moments and conditional diagnostics in four equal-count energy bins. Checks compare the analytic ceilings to 3,402 numerical thermal cap integrals and 11,529 latitude-ring intersections, test pencil and cubic harmonic controls, and reproduce the pressure eigenvalues by explicitly assembling and diagonalizing three rotated stress tensors. Multiplying an ensemble into three cyclic copies does not triple its number of independent samples. The distance bounds concern the recorded populations. The runs sample simplified pulse, geometry, species and source histories, and finite refinements do not give a statistical confidence interval for the continuum. Their angular signatures are predictions inside this model, without a mapping to an observed sky. [Implementation](diagnose.py) · [Results](TRIAD.json)
