# Sources and normalization for the current and angular tests

## HDblast inputs

The November 21 polarization and collision studies fix the outgoing convention, incident pulse, charged daughter birth law, vector-pole cross section and early shell history used here. An earlier three-flavor replay already measured the axial particle current and tested rotated species. A separate earlier calculation coupled the axial particle stress to the bulk and expanding shell for a dedicated resonance. Those calculations establish the comparison cases for adding a polarized pulse and following the full vector current. The project defines the electric amplitude $X$ by the *total* energy of three equal fields, $e_E=KX^2/2$. Each flavor sees amplitude $X/\sqrt{3}$ and the reduced force parameter is $Q=q/(\sqrt{3}\,mL)$. If each flavor has one third of the total comoving rest $D$ and the same rotated charge-velocity distribution, the summed work on the particles implies collective conformal current $J=QD\langle s v\rangle$. Here $s=\pm1$ and the velocity is expressed in the species-aligned basis. The density of eligible targets for a binary collision with the opposite charge of a given flavor instead contains $D/6$. The factor of six counts physical target populations. The public equations use these normalizations for different observables. ## Independent physics references

C. Armendáriz-Picón showed how three equal mutually perpendicular background vector fields can have an isotropic *summed* stress. The paper also distinguishes background isotropy from the behavior of perturbations. Tomoaki Murata and Tsutomu Kobayashi constructed orthogonal vector-field configurations on positively curved cosmological slices and showed explicitly how the spatial curvature enters their field equations. These different cosmological models support the use of a symmetry check. The particle current and pulse in this study follow the HDblast equations. [Armendáriz-Picón, *Could dark energy be vector-like?*](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0405267) · [Murata and Kobayashi, *Dynamics of inflation with mutually orthogonal vector fields in a closed universe*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07199)

The energy-momentum tensor contains momentum moments through rank two. A fourth-order angular measurement can retain information about beams even when the eigenvalues of their summed pressure coincide. Caio V. P. de Brito and Gabriel S. Denicol explicitly retain rank-three and rank-four irreducible moments in a relativistic Boltzmann calculation. Their hydrodynamic closure and the angular weighting used here are distinct. D. Bazow and collaborators supply a complementary analytic example where a spatially homogeneous and isotropic expanding gas still has a nonthermal energy spectrum. [de Brito and Denicol, *Third-order relativistic dissipative fluid dynamics from the method of moments*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09097) · [Bazow et al., *Analytic Solution of the Boltzmann Equation in an Expanding System*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.07834)

Samuel E. Gralla and Morifumi Mizuno analyze how charged pairs take energy from an electric field, including the field's subsequent evolution and an explicit energy-conservation check in section III.4. Their example is quantum electrodynamics in one spatial dimension. In a 2026 quantum-kinetic study, I. A. Aleksandrov and collaborators compute the induced current, momentum-resolved yields and energy-momentum tensor for a prescribed field of arbitrary polarization. Taken together these papers illustrate two different physical questions, measuring the current and evolving the field that supplied its energy. HDblast assumes a classical two-body decay source and an expanding three-dimensional shell in a five-dimensional model. Neither paper supplies its boundary forcing or collision rate. [Gralla and Mizuno, *Schwinger effect with backreaction in 1+1D massive QED with a strong external field*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23464) · [Aleksandrov et al., *Vacuum polarization and pair production in time-dependent electric fields*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00873)
