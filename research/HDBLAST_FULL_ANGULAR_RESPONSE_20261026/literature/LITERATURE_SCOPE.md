# Focused primary-source check and interpretation limits

Checked26 October2026. This is a bounded literature check, not an exhaustive
search or an originality assessment. Direct arXiv API records were read;
the two PDFs identified below were retrieved and their gauge/observable
discussions inspected. Source metadata and hashes are retained separately.
Downloaded article bodies and full abstracts are excluded from this capsule.

| Primary source | Relevance and limit |
| --- | --- |
| [De Luca, Franciolini, Kehagias and Riotto, *On the Gauge Invariance of Cosmological Gravitational Waves*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.09689) | Their discussion separates production, propagation and measurement, and ties the choice of second-order tensor variable to a specified measurement. It supports defining an observable before interpreting our registered-gauge TT coefficient as a signal. Their cosmological construction is not a theorem for this shell model. |
| [Domènech and Sasaki, *Approximate gauge independence of the induced gravitational wave spectrum*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14016) | Their subhorizon/inactive-source gauge comparisons and active-source example show why a tensor coefficient alone is insufficient for a wave interpretation. Our small exterior source cone has neither a derived late-time radiation limit nor a detector observable, so their spectral conclusion is not imported. |
| [Inomata and Terada, *Gauge Independence of Induced Gravitational Waves*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.00785) | The abstract concerns late-time induced waves in radiation domination and distinguishes their observable limit from gauge-dependent tensor perturbations. That radiation-era setting does not identify an observed wave in this five-dimensional Einstein–scalar shell calculation. Only the abstract was inspected in this check. |
| [*Gauge dependence of scalar-induced gravitational waves from isocurvature perturbations: Analytical results*, arXiv:2510.07252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07252) | The recent abstract discusses gauge-sensitive intermediate kernels and a late-time projection in a radiation-era isocurvature problem. It is relevant methodological context; its conclusions and equations have not been independently audited or transferred to our model. |
