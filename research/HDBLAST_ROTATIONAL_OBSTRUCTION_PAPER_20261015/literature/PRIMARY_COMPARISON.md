# Focused primary-source comparison for the proposed shell paper

Prepared 15 October 2026 (America/Los_Angeles). This is a bounded scientific reassessment, not a novelty certificate or prediction of acceptance. This comparison concerns the shell-obstruction research and its focused paper draft.

## Assessment

The defensible present positioning is **a technical application of established linearization-obstruction and Hamiltonian-boundary methods to a specified physical Einstein–scalar interface**, with an explicit nonintegrable perturbation and a finite-dimensional classification. The existing evidence does not establish a new general Noether theorem, a new mechanism of linearization instability, or a nonlinear existence theorem. Changing spacetime dimension alone supplies little conceptual novelty.

Two close general treatments missing from the old manuscript deserve direct citations: De Vuyst, Eccles, Höhn and Kirklin (2025), which gives the covariant quadratic constraint and boundary-charge analysis, and Khavkine (2015), which constructs obstruction classes for a broad class of smooth nonlinear PDEs. Their presence strengthens the mathematical context but narrows the claim of originality.

A finite-dimensional commuting-matrix singularity calculation is a useful clarification of the allowed test states. Its underlying moment-map geometry is standard. It should not carry the paper's significance claim.

## Contribution paragraph suitable for a revised draft

> Quadratic linearization obstructions and their relation to background symmetries are classical, and their covariant constraint and boundary-charge formulations are available in general form. We apply this framework to two Einstein–scalar regions with a common induced metric and scalar, two outward gravitational boundary terms, and one scalar-dependent shell tension. We evaluate the physical interface contribution explicitly and give a direct ADM proof that an arbitrary second-order correction, including shell tilt, cannot cancel the rotational quadratic constraint at the specified symmetric background. We then construct a smooth compactly supported linear perturbation for which that constraint is nonzero and classify its vanishing on a fixed-profile quadrupole family. The result is a model-specific obstruction and worked interface calculation; it is not a general sufficiency theorem for nonlinear continuation.

This paragraph is conditional on the accompanying proof audit confirming every action, trace, support and reconstruction hypothesis. No claim that an identical application has never appeared is established by the search.

## The direct ADM result and its significance

The strongest accessible central result is the following restricted statement. At the chosen time-symmetric background, integrate the exact bulk momentum constraints and the mixed Israel residual against a common cut-tangent rotation. Retain the total boost-angle term. Its first variation on an arbitrary second-order correction is an integral of a rotation derivative against the invariant background cut density and hence vanishes on the closed cut. The quadratic corner term vanishes because the selected first-order perturbation vanishes near the shell. The remaining bulk Hessian can be calculated and shown nonzero for an actual smooth linearized perturbation.

This proof usefully answers a specific interface concern: a forbidden first-order state cannot be rescued simply by allowing a previously omitted second-order shell correction. However, it is an explicit realization of the established charge mechanism. A paper can have value as a carefully documented application without claiming a new general framework. Its likely significance depends on whether specialists consider this physical-shell realization and explicit admissible witness useful, which internal algebra checks cannot decide.

The proof should separate three statements: (i) the exact ADM/interface identity; (ii) the vanishing of its first derivative for arbitrary allowed corrections; (iii) the construction and nonzero charge of an actual first-order solution. The initial-data obstruction already applies before shell contact. Making shell-contact evolution a separate corollary reduces the main theorem's dependencies.

## Matrix geometry: what is standard

Let `V=Sym₀(4,R)` and identify `T*V` with `V×V` using the trace pairing. The cotangent lift of orthogonal conjugation has momentum pairing

`μ_M(A,B)=tr(B[M,A])=tr([A,B]M)`

up to the chosen symplectic sign. Thus the six rotational zero-charge equations reduce to the familiar zero momentum level `[A,B]=0` once the physical radial prefactor is known to be nonzero. Simultaneous diagonalization, the rank loss at repeated joint eigenvalues and the simple-spectrum parametrization are elementary manifestations of this geometry. The physical identification and normalization require the gravitational calculation; the matrix commutator does not become new mathematics because it appears there.

Arms–Marsden–Moncrief (1981) is a direct historical comparison for quadratic local models of singular momentum levels. Its author-hosted abstract and canonical metadata were verified; the complete original could not be fetched, so no numbered theorem is imported here. Their 1982 paper's author-hosted abstract describes conical singularities under compact constant-mean-curvature Cauchy hypotheses and coupled Einstein–Yang–Mills results. These do not establish a shell PDE theorem. A constructive joint-block tangent-cone lemma can be included with its own proof, labelled an elementary specialization, without an unsupported priority claim.
