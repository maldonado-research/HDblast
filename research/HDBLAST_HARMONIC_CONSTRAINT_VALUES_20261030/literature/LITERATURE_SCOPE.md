# Focused primary-source comparison

One focused web search and targeted passages from two primary sources were
retrieved on 30 October 2026. This is a bounded comparison, not an exhaustive
literature search or an assessment establishing originality.

1. Jeffrey Winicour, *Disembodied boundary data for Einstein's equations*,
   [arXiv:0909.1989v2](https://arxiv.org/html/0909.1989v2).
   The paper develops a harmonic formulation of a vacuum Einstein initial
   boundary value problem with a hierarchy of Sommerfeld boundary conditions.
   Sections III–IV distinguish the reduced evolution equations from the
   harmonic constraints and build constraint preservation into compatible
   initial and boundary data. Equations (26)–(30) give the relevant constraint
   boundary rows and reduction. This supports the methodological distinction
   used here; it is not a proof for this model's five-dimensional Einstein–
   scalar system with a physical shell and its mixed boundary conditions.

2. Stefano Muzzolon, Michael Dumbser, Olindo Zanotti and Elena Gaburro,
   *High order numerical discretizations of the Einstein–Euler equations in
   the Generalized Harmonic formulation*,
   [arXiv:2512.24121v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24121v2), version dated
   11 May 2026 in the retrieved source.
   The paper studies CWENO finite differences and ADER discontinuous Galerkin
   schemes, including preservation of known stationary equilibria. Equations
   (9) and (11) separately define monitored Einstein and gauge constraints.
   This is useful numerical-method context for future full evolutions. The
   reported numerical tests do not supply the rigorous derivative bounds,
   physical shell conditions or cosmological evidence missing from HDBLAST.

The present checkpoint contributes a model-specific finite-dimensional
harmonic-covector map and interval bounds for a registered finite update.
Linearized harmonic-gauge formulas, subsidiary constraint propagation and
constraint-preserving boundary methods already belong to established
relativity. No general new gauge theorem or novelty claim is inferred from
the computations. Neither cited paper establishes a higher-dimensional
origin of the Big Bang.
