## Historical numbers are not headline constraints

The original demonstrations contain synthetic inputs. The A1 `REALDATA_APPROX` stage used approximate summary anchors rather than authenticated posterior ingestion. Several translated small-excess bounds lie outside the approximation's valid regime. The detailed account retains these numbers for history; they must not be quoted as measured FTL limits.

The label “Einstein-allowed” means satisfying an author-defined numerical condition. It does not establish physical consistency against every relevant theory requirement or provide a probability that FTL is true.

The later supplied conversation reports nonlinear bias in classifier PCI, duplicated rows crossing folds, overlooked synthetic metadata, and ringdown transformations being checked but not applied. Stable cross-validation was therefore rejected as an accuracy certificate.

A reported synthetic truth case produced classifier medians around 0.540–0.976 against a known overlap of 0.515672. These numbers are source-reported, not rerun for this publication. They show why estimator stability and absence of leakage do not settle accuracy.

## Specific unresolved issues

1. **Physical mechanism:** no complete FTL-sector action, fields, interactions, stability proof, microcausal prescription, or production model is supplied.
2. **Diagnostic calibration:** the x-channel proportionality and unit Einstein ball are proposed. Their physical/statistical justification is unresolved.
3. **Radiation convention:** the archived spectrum-to-DeltaNeff relation is underspecified when Omega_gw means a critical-density fraction. Epoch, bounds, tails, and photon conversion need closure.
4. **Population:** the beta distribution controls the ceiling and is an assumed population, not an inferred hidden-sector measurement.
5. **Notation:** Q historically denotes both occupancy and first positive strength. Separate symbols are required for a faithful public explanation.
6. **Dependence:** marginal PTA/ringdown uncertainties do not specify their joint pairing. Shared data and assumptions also affect independence claims.
7. **PCI accuracy:** true Bayes probabilities yield an exact identity; fitted classifier outputs need justified accuracy control.
8. **Ringdown harmonization:** one common scalar, null, sign, units, and applied transformation must be justified before pooling.
9. **Private proofs and code:** exact Appell-F1, Brier, Makarov-DKW, overlap/Gaussian notes and implementation are unavailable.
10. **Source closure:** selected posterior bytes, compatible priors, schemas, TOA disjointness, frequency grids, permissions, and replay artifacts remain incomplete in the supplied snapshot.

## What could fail?

The SR geometry can fail if its implementation or frame convention is incorrect. A proposed hidden-sector model can fail if its dynamics violate the audit's assumptions or other physical requirements. A frozen parameter point can be excluded if a justified analysis shows it outside a defined allowance.

A reported finite speed bound can fail when the severity ceiling never intersects the allowance, when an estimator is inaccurate, when data provenance is insufficient, or when the statistical decision rule is not justified. Those failures reject a calculation or specified benchmark; they do not prove universal impossibility of all FTL proposals.

A result surviving the audit still needs comparison with alternative explanations. Ordinary astrophysical source models, noise, waveform modeling, cosmological assumptions, and shared priors can explain apparent agreement or tension without FTL.
