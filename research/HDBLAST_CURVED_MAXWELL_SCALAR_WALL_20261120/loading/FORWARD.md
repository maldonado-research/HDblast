# A pulse launched from a separate surface

A pulse specified on a timelike emitting surface reaches a self-gravitating wall, deposits energy in an initially empty scalar field and allows a later bounce in this effective model. We follow the wave outward from its emitting surface and the reflected wave back. The experiment produces two different post-bounce outcomes. With the previous decay law the extra wall radiation fluid exceeds 90% of wall matter for about 0.606 e-folds of expansion. With that decay turned off, a separately chosen scalar–Maxwell interaction can put over 90% into the actual three coherent wall gauge fields for about 0.121 e-folds. Both primary solutions subsequently begin contracting. All percentages exclude the tuned background tension. Neither case determines a thermal spectrum.

## What is prescribed

Each of two identical five-dimensional interiors contains a collar extending a fixed inward proper distance $d$ from the closed wall to a source tube. Use $ds^2=dx^2-N(t,x)^2dt^2+r(t,x)^2d\Omega_3^2$, with wall at $x=0$, tube at $x=d$ and $N(t,0)=1$. In the primary run $d=0.002$ in AdS lengths. Before emission both the Maxwell field and the extra wall matter vanish. At $t=0$ the wall has $a=2$, $H=0$ and each interior has mass $m=4$. Its vacuum prehistory on that branch has $a^2=4-t^2$.

Write $u=r_t/N$, $E=A_t/(Nr)$, $Z=A_x/r$, $B=2A/r^2$ for the three equal normalized modes. The inward-coordinate characteristic $I=(E+Z)/2$ travels toward smaller $x$. At the tube, set $I=\sqrt{D/w}\,s(\tau_d/w)$ with $d\tau_d=N(t,d)dt$. Here $D=0.123$, $w=0.001$ and $s$ is the registered smooth pulse, normalized by $2\int s^2=1$. Emission ends at tube proper time 0.00397839. The tube then supplies zero incoming wave and lets the returning characteristic $O=(E-Z)/2$ leave freely. At the wall $I$ is determined by propagation. The sheet current fixes $O=X-I$, where $X=A_t/a$ at the wall follows its kinetic equation. This gives no adjustable wave amplitude at the wall during the run. The source that powers the tube and its interior below the collar are outside the calculation. [Wall material, source shape and normalization](loading.py)

## Equations and independent checks

At each wall time integrate the radial constraints from $r=a$, $r_x=-a(1+e)$, $u=aH$, $N=1$, $N_x=-(1-2e-3p)$. Here $e,p$ are the extra wall density and pressure in tension units. The local spherical mass is $m=r^2(1+r^2+u^2-r_x^2)$ and the bulk energy density is $\rho=(E^2+Z^2+B^2)/2$. In the bulk the radial equations are

$$r_{xx}=r+m/r^3-2r\rho,\qquad u_x=-2r E Z,\qquad
N_{xx}/N=1-3m/r^4+5B^2+E^2-Z^2.$$

The first equation is the timelike Einstein constraint and the second is the mixed constraint. To check the last one independently, the three-sphere stress is $P_\Omega=(E^2-Z^2+B^2)/6$ and $P_x=(E^2+Z^2-B^2)/2$. The angular Einstein equation gives $N_{xx}/N=1-3m/r^4+4(\rho-P_x)+6P_\Omega$. The evolution of the orbit-space Maxwell field follows directly from $\partial_t(r^2E)=\partial_x(Nr^2 Z)-4NA/r$, $\partial_t A=NrE$ and $\partial_t(r Z)=\partial_x(NrE)$. The brane evolves from its local Israel, scalar and Maxwell equations. All constraints and all wave and matter variables continue to evolve until the stopping event. The ordinary-branch normal, the positive masses, the lapse and the positive norm $r_x^2-u^2$ are checked across the collar.

The replay uses piecewise cubic polynomials on four-node Lobatto elements with upwind characteristic fluxes. It advances in time with fourth-order Runge–Kutta and integrates the radial constraints with fourth-order steps using the element polynomial at each midpoint. The finest models use 64 elements until $t=0.08$ and six thereafter. The independent 12-element comparison retaining its original mesh all the way through the first turnaround changes the fluid interval by less than $2\times10^{-10}$ e-folds relative to coarsening that same mesh. The zero-input run reproduces the analytic vacuum wall radius. The time series includes the source pulse, the first bounce, the first expansion maximum and early contraction.

## Comparing the two exits

| Wall law after the scalar reaches its exit | Bounce radius | Radius where the component reaches 90% | First maximum radius | Expanding interval above 90% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Scalar decay to an ideal radiation fluid | 1.880948 | 4.98654 | 9.14225 | 0.60617 e-folds |
| Scalar transfer back to the Maxwell sheet | 1.880948 | 8.63007 | 9.74401 | 0.12140 e-folds |

In the second run the fluid decay rate is zero throughout. Add $K_{\rm late}S((\psi-0.36)/0.02)$ to the positive gauge coefficient $K(\psi)$, where $S$ is the smooth step used in the falling potential. Use its derivative in the scalar equation. The wall magnetic amplitude at the start of the exit is 0.00501486. Equating $K B^2/2$ to the plateau height 0.111 at that instant gives a reference coefficient of about 8,827. The numerical example uses $K_{\rm late}=13,200$, selected in a survey around that scale. Its final $K=13,200.04$ is about 13 million times the initial 0.001. This strong, deliberately shaped response is a model input. At 9,000 or 16,000 the peak fraction falls below 90%. For 22,000 the scalar reaches only about 0.36237 before the wall reaches the reporting radius of 16. The source dose also matters. With the fluid law, raising the separation to 0.003 at the original dose gives a bounce but no exit by radius 16. Raising the dose to 0.124 at that separation restores the exit.

At peak in the selected Maxwell run the three wall fields contain 91.974% of the extra surface energy. The electric and magnetic densities are 0.0040802 and 0.0007257 respectively. Their isotropic sum has $p_{\rm EM}=e_{\rm EM}/3$ even though the fields are coherent. No separate fluid is present. The energy transfer can be checked without using the peak fraction. With dots denoting wall time,

$$ \frac{d(a^4 e_{\rm EM})}{dt}=a^4\left[2(I^2-O^2)+\tfrac12\dot K(B^2-X^2)\right]. $$

At peak the integrated bulk contribution is 1.78720 and net scalar transfer is 41.53620, giving 43.32340 in comoving sheet units. The added late coupling contributes 43.39170 before including the earlier transfer out of the sheet. This comoving balance includes the scale-factor weighting. The source proper energy has different units of normalization. The two tubes together emit 0.97804 in the study's proper three-volume normalization. They receive back 0.05657 by the end of the Maxwell run, including 0.00815 after tube proper time 0.1. In the fluid run the returned energy is approximately 0.04844, with 0.0000143 after that time.

On the finest forward mesh the minima of the bulk mass, lapse, areal radius and $r_x^2-u^2$ are respectively 0.1001, 0.99778, 1.87674 and 3.9800. The peak Friedmann residual is below $6\times10^{-11}$, the largest comoving sheet balance error below $4\times10^{-10}$ and the largest tube-mass balance discrepancy below $1.8\times10^{-6}$ over each primary run. The replay compares the tube radius to an independent time integral of $Nu$ and also differences the radius in time. The integrated discrepancy is below $9\times10^{-11}$. The radial integral of $A_x=rZ$ has residual below $9\times10^{-8}$. Its more sensitive pointwise derivative error peaks at 0.0316, about 0.18% of the largest $|rZ|$. Changing from 48 to 64 early elements shifts the fluid and coherent intervals by about $1.9\times10^{-5}$ and $3.3\times10^{-5}$ e-folds. These are numerical convergence tests for a thin, spherically symmetric collar. They leave the stability to unequal fields, the generation of the input wave and the physical origin of the chosen exit interaction open.
