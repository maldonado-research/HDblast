# Distance at fixed observed redshift

The observable is the angular-diameter distance of a source selected on the observer’s light cone by its measured frequency ratio and direction. The underlying geometry, the emitter and observer velocities, and the redshift selection all enter. The literature below supports these ingredients individually. The HDblast calculation supplies the shell solution, the initial data for the clocks and the resulting directional coefficients.

## Conformal geometry and clocks

Visser gives the exact redshift in a conformally FLRW spacetime. With physical metric $g=\Omega^2\hat g$, FLRW scale factor $a$ in $\hat g$, and photon direction $\hat k$ along propagation, the frequency ratio factors into $a_o\Omega_o/(a_s\Omega_s)$ times the source-to-observer ratio of $\gamma(1-\hat k\cdot\boldsymbol\beta)$. Velocities here are local peculiar velocities relative to the reference cosmological observers. Keeping the endpoints at rest in conformal coordinates sets both Doppler factors to one. A spatial gradient of the conformal factor can accelerate those coordinate-rest worldlines away from free fall. [Visser, §4, equations 4.1–4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1502.02758v3#S4.E2)

Ivanov, Liberati, Viel and Visser give the conformal laws for affinely parametrized rays, frequency and luminosity distance. With identical endpoint events and conformally rescaled four-velocities, an observer-vertex distance obeys $D_A=\Omega_s\hat D_A$ because the source cross-sectional area scales by $\Omega_s^2$ and the observed solid angle is invariant. One also obtains this relation from their equations 49–50 and Etherington reciprocity. Their quantity called “area distance” in equation 51 has its solid angle at the source, so its factor is $\Omega_o$. Selecting a source by redshift additionally shifts the emitting event along the ray. [Ivanov et al., §3.1, equations 46–51](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.06550v3#S3.E46)

## Fixed redshift, boosts and local validity

Kaiser and Hudson derive a fixed-redshift angular-size perturbation for small peculiar velocities in a spatially flat FLRW background. Two distinct terms enter their equation 6. A shift in emission position follows from the redshift perturbation, and aberration changes the angle at the moving observer. With direction $\mathbf n$ from observer to source and $c=1$, the velocity part is $\delta z/(1+z)=\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf v_s-\mathbf v_o)$. The coefficient $1/(\mathcal H\chi)$ multiplying this perturbation in the angular-size response makes a fractional-distance prediction sensitive to peculiar velocities at short distance. The perturbation in angular-diameter distance for a fixed proper ruler has the opposite sign to the angular-size perturbation. The FLRW coefficient assumes that background's redshift gradient. [Kaiser and Hudson, §3, equations 3–6](https://arxiv.org/html/1411.6339v2#S3.E6)

Maartens and collaborators treat a receiver boosted relative to the emitting matter. Aberration gives $\widetilde D_A=\Gamma_o D_A$ at the same events with sky directions identified by the boost. At zero distance the boosted receiver measures the limiting redshift $\widetilde z_o=\Gamma_o^{-1}-1$. A shared smooth observer–emitter congruence instead has unit frequency ratio at coincidence. Their equation 73 independently gives the usual affine vertex coefficient $-R_{kk}/12$ in $D_A$ after taking a square root. [Maartens et al., equations 59 and 65 and §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09875v3#S4.E59) · [Equation 73](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09875v3#S4.E73)

Koksbang and Heinesen explicitly assume local invertibility to exchange affine and redshift derivatives. Macpherson and Heinesen’s expansion around the vertex, for a smooth congruence, gives leading luminosity distance $z/\mathfrak H_o$ and requires checking its range of convergence in the spacetime at hand. A negative redshift on a locally monotonic branch is compatible with a positive angular-diameter distance. A vanishing redshift gradient invalidates the ordinary implicit-derivative formula. An explicit example of multiple distances at one redshift occurs on selected future light cones near shell crossing in Krasiński’s Lemaître–Tolman solution. Those matter-shell crossings are distinct from the material brane boundary in HDblast. [Koksbang and Heinesen, equations 3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05836v2#S0.E3) · [Macpherson and Heinesen, §2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01095v3#S2.E2) · [Krasiński, §§IX–XI](https://arxiv.org/html/1407.2862v3#S9)

## What observations currently measure

Ye and collaborators forecast a Doppler-magnification dipole by cross-correlating observed galaxy number-count fluctuations with convergence estimated from galaxy sizes. The velocity projection, redshift bin, intrinsic size scatter and survey selection enter the statistic. Its dipole is in the orientation of *pairs* of galaxies. The shell calculation instead predicts a quadrupole in individual lines of sight from a specified receiver. Wu and Xia use DESI DR1 to measure the modulation of spectroscopic redshifts across four tracer samples over $0.1<z<2.1$, using mocks to assess the survey window. This observable can constrain observer motion. Comparing either statistic to the shell would require a physical population of emitters, a prediction in the survey’s redshift range and a prescription for converting angular sizes into distances. [Ye et al., §2, equations 3 and 5–7](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17858v2#S2.E3) · [DESI DR1 redshift-dipole analysis, data and estimation](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30914v2#S2)

Distance variations can also occur in four dimensions. For example, Heinesen and Clifton compare modified optical focusing at fixed FLRW redshift evolution with modified large-scale expansion, and obtain distinct consistency tests by combining probes. For higher-dimensional propagation, Lin, Yu and Gong explicitly distinguish photons confined to a brane from gravitons permitted to traverse their assumed bulk. Their signal is a relative arrival time under their propagation assumptions. A brane photon distance alone probes its intrinsic metric and chosen clocks. Any inference about an extra dimension needs further physical input. [Heinesen and Clifton, §II](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07244v2#S2) · [Lin, Yu and Gong, §III](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.17549v2#S3)
