# Which earlier results fix the emitters?

The preceding light-cone calculation held physical affine length fixed and normalized the photon frequency at reception. Its last section gives the general endpoint correction for a source selected by observed redshift. The calibration issue additionally gives the unperturbed comoving blueshift (about −2.405 × 10⁻¹⁴) and a sufficient upper bound on the anisotropic redshift response (8.99 × 10⁻³⁹ per unit registered amplitude) for the affine signal to retain its sign. Both calculations leave the perturbed emitter velocity and its frequency shift to be evaluated. Those requirements apply to the specified local geometry, time window and receiver. A physical congruence is already available. On the initial quiet cut, every label on the round three-sphere carries the future-directed unit normal to that cut within the shell. Its worldline follows the intrinsic timelike geodesic equation. The initial metric agrees exactly with the background in the collar at every admitted amplitude, fixing the initial velocity without a fitted peculiar motion. The observer theorem embeds these worldlines on a common cylinder extending through the optical interval. The first-variation calculation supplies zero initial position and covariant velocity variations and a forced Volterra integral for the resulting motion. Assigning emitters to this congruence gives one defined emitter model. A population of astronomical sources would require independent evidence for that assignment.
