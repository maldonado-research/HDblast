## Actionable comparison

1. A proved smooth ambient master operator is enough for an ordinary **linear** local Cauchy theorem. Choose a causally convex globally hyperbolic ambient neighborhood crossing the background cone and a smooth spacelike Cauchy slice. Give compactly supported smooth master data there. Smooth lower-order coefficients are allowed; no sign condition on their potential is needed for local existence. State the patch and domain of dependence, rather than treating a singular de Sitter radial foliation as the ambient Cauchy geometry.
2. The missing physical step is a differential reconstruction identity: smooth master solutions must produce smooth ambient metric/scalar perturbations satisfying every linearized Einstein–scalar equation and constraint. Gauge invariance of a divided variable, an inverse-square cancellation, and scalar wave existence separately do not prove this. Exceptional scalar harmonics and the constant-scalar `p=0` limit need their own statements. A scalar-flat limit cannot be justified by formulas that divide by `p`.
3. A **nonlinear** cone-crossing Einstein–scalar theorem is an additional result. It requires constraint-satisfying spacelike Einstein–scalar data, a quasilinear harmonic reduction, propagation of gauge/Einstein constraints, and local geometric uniqueness. A linear master packet does not establish its nonlinear integrability. Under nonlinear perturbation, crossing the fixed background cone and crossing a null hypersurface of the new metric are different claims.
4. Establish bulk local existence before the support reaches a shell. The later shell problem needs the actual variational boundary system, trace/bending variables, normal conventions, compatibility at initial corners, and a constraint-preserving energy estimate for the coupled bulk–boundary system. None of the sources read here supplies the current five-dimensional doubled Israel shell theorem.
5. The archive’s conformal coordinates and full-system harmonic architecture are reusable after checking their coefficients on the current branch. Its negative-stationary-point transfer obstruction is not: the bulk polynomial is identical, but the positive stationary comparator has different potential jets and an integer degree-fourteen reference transfer. ## Linear Cauchy existence and characteristic limits

**Bär–Ginoux–Pfäffle, _Wave Equations on Lorentzian Manifolds and Quantization_, arXiv:0806.1036v1.** Actually read Theorems 3.2.11–3.2.12 and their proofs, printed pp.85–86 (PDF pp.91–92), plus the adjacent fundamental-solution statement p.87. For a normally hyperbolic operator on a vector bundle over a globally hyperbolic Lorentzian manifold, compactly supported smooth source and smooth spacelike Cauchy data give a unique smooth solution, causal support, and continuous dependence. The manifold, operator, and data are already smooth in the theorem; it does not validate a coordinate-dependent scalar reconstruction or a timelike shell boundary. The result is linear, although the vector-bundle formulation permits a full linearized metric–scalar system.

**Bär–Tagne Wafo, _Initial value problems for wave equations on manifolds_, arXiv:1408.4995v2, Math. Phys. Anal. Geom. 18, 7 (2015), DOI 10.1007/s11040-015-9176-7.** Actually read the geometric/finite-energy setup, §§1.1–1.2 and §§1.6–1.8, Theorem 13/Corollaries 14–20, and Theorems 22–23/Example 24 (selected PDF pp.3–4,7–12,16–22). Theorem 13, Eq.(13), gives the finite-energy solution/data/source isomorphism with data in `H_c^k ⊕ H_c^(k−1)` and source in `L²_loc,sc(H^(k−1))`. Corollary 14 gives homogeneous wellposedness, Corollary 15 density of smooth solutions, and Remark 16 finite propagation speed. Corollary 18 makes homogeneous finite-energy solution spaces independent of the smooth Cauchy temporal function; arbitrary finite-energy sections need not be invariant under changing that function.

Their Theorem 23 requires a characteristic partial Cauchy hypersurface whose causal future is **past compact**, with trace data in the specified `H_c^1` space. Example 24 explicitly shows uniqueness can fail without past compactness. This is a useful later Goursat comparator, not permission to prescribe sixteen arbitrary Einstein–scalar profiles on a cone. Einstein null constraints and admissible traces remain additional requirements.

## Harmonic Einstein–scalar reduction and constraints

**Lindblad–Rodnianski, _Global stability of Minkowski space-time in harmonic gauge_, arXiv:math/0411109v2.** Selectively read pp.1–2,8–9,19–20, including Eqs.(1.1)–(1.4), (2.1)–(2.5), and Proposition 3.1/Eq.(3.7). It provides an explicit massless Einstein–scalar reduced quasilinear wave system and states the converse under compatible initial gauge and Einstein constraints: the reduced solution is a full Einstein–scalar solution, with wave gauge propagated. Its global theorem concerns small asymptotically flat data in four spacetime dimensions and a massless scalar. It must not be transferred as a global stability result for the five-dimensional curved potential/shell background. Adding the current smooth scalar potential is a local lower-order extension that must be derived with the current normalization; it is not covered by that paper’s massless global theorem. Only these sections were read, not its complete global proof.

**Choquet-Bruhat–Isenberg–Pollack, _The constraint equations for the Einstein-scalar field system on compact manifolds_, arXiv:gr-qc/0610045v2, DOI 10.1088/0264-9381/24/4/004.** Selectively read pp.1–5 and the cited local-existence reference on p.21. Their general `(n+1)`-dimensional smooth scalar-potential formulation gives field equations (2)–(3) and Hamiltonian/momentum constraints (4)–(5), and p.3 explicitly makes spacetime local existence conditional on solving those constraints. The conformal-method existence classification is not blanket solvability for arbitrary scalar/potential data; the authors explicitly list unresolved subclasses. Its compact global setting does not construct the desired current local perturbed data. Foures-Bruhat’s 1952 theorem is cited by this source but was not downloaded or independently read in this review.

**Kreiss–Reula–Sarbach–Winicour, _Well-posed initial-boundary value problem for the harmonic Einstein equations using energy estimates_, arXiv:0707.4188v2, DOI 10.1088/0264-9381/24/23/017.** Actually read §3, pp.7–9, and §4/Appendix 1, pp.10–13. Eqs.(11)–(12) display the frozen-coefficient metric wave system and harmonic constraints. Both the constraints and their initial normal derivatives vanish, with homogeneous compatible boundary constraint data, to obtain propagation. Theorem 3 assumes the selected triangular boundary hierarchy and real negative eigenvalues of matrices (14); Appendix 1 explains smooth compatible quasilinear iteration and estimates stable against lower-order perturbations. The discussion explicitly allows generalized harmonic forcing without changing principal part. These Sommerfeld-type boundary conditions are useful methods and counterchecks, not the current shell’s physical junction conditions. The earlier gr-qc/0602051 download was an identity-discovery comparator, not this exact-title paper; do not misidentify the two articles.

## Boundary kinetic sign and recent timelike-boundary results

**Zahn, _Generalized Wentzell boundary conditions and quantum field theory_, arXiv:1512.05512v3, Ann. Henri Poincaré 19, 163–187 (2018), DOI 10.1007/s00023-017-0629-3.** Actually read pp.2–6,9–11: action (1), inward-normal equations (2)–(3), bulk-plus-boundary symplectic form (5), Hilbert inner product (6), domain (7), Proposition 1, smooth compatibility hierarchy (14), and Propositions 2–4. Positive `c` multiplies both boundary kinetic energy and the boundary Hilbert weight. The local energy inequality (16) includes energy on the timelike boundary, and the proof cancels the bulk normal flux against boundary energy exchange. The theorem is for a free scalar on a flat half-space with the stated compatibility conditions; a strip is discussed separately, but this is not a gravitational shell. Remark 6 describes difficulties for negative `c` and loss of this Hilbert-space energy argument; it does not prove every negative-kinetic system has no local solution. A local existence theorem and a positive energy theorem should remain distinct.

**Dappiaggi–Ferreira–Juárez-Aubry, _Mode solutions for a Klein-Gordon field in anti-de Sitter spacetime with dynamical boundary conditions of Wentzell type_, arXiv:1802.00283v3, Phys. Rev. D 97, 085022 (2018), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.97.085022.** Actually read §§II–III, PDF pp.3–7, including regular equations (8)–(9), bound-state relation (19), singular generalized traces (24)–(25), and relation (33). Their conformally transformed AdS problem is a free field; singular traces and boundary mass ranges are source-specific. Under their equation conventions and nonnegative boundary squared mass, nonnegative `c` has no negative radial eigenvalues in the cases analyzed; negative `c` has parameter-dependent numbers of bound states, so “negative boundary sign always implies a bound state” is false even in this comparator. There is an apparent printed sign mismatch between action (7) and equations (8) under the stated `z>0` inward-normal convention: use the explicit evolution equations/spectral comparator and Zahn’s consistent action, rather than silently importing (7) as the current kinetic convention.
