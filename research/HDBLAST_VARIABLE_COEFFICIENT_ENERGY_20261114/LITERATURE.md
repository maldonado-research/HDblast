# Method sources for the variable-coefficient mixed-wave estimate

Sources checked on 14 November 2026 UTC. Boundary-preserving tangential
commutation followed by equation-based normal recovery is an established
method. This capsule evaluates its application to the retained
scalar-principal tensor system. External qualitative constants do not
supply its numerical energy, source or recovery bounds. This focused
review makes no novelty, nonlinear existence or physical-support claim.

## Application-specific checks

Positive `B^zz` makes the shell noncharacteristic for the second-order
wave operator. A first-order reduction can nevertheless have zero normal
eigenvalues; a characteristic boundary matrix and a null/Goursat
hypersurface are different notions. The fixed componentwise D/N split
also creates no spatial D/N junction along a single component.

Time and natural sphere rotations preserve the fixed boundary split and
commute with the radial derivative. They preserve the homogeneous rows,
while differentiating the general-state principal coefficients. Every
coefficient commutator and tensor direction slot must therefore be
retained. The predecessor's cross-coefficient boundary traces are checked
for the actual mixed flux. Their vanishing at the boundary does not
remove the corresponding mixed terms in the interior.

Higher normal derivatives are recovered by differentiating the
unisolated interior equation, then dividing its highest-normal term by
positive `B^zz`. Derivatives of coefficients enter the explicit lower
unknown-order commutators. No normal differentiation of the boundary
conditions is performed. The word-to-covariant norm comparison, temporal
source hierarchy, product allocations and each recovery stage remain
part of this evaluated application.

These frozen a priori estimates do not themselves show that nonlinear
iterates remain in the coefficient ball, converge, possess continuous
top mixed regularity or have uniform parameter and observer bounds.
Those are separate analytic interfaces.
