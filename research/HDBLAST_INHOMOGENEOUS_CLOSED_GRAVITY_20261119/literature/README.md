# Related work and the choice of test

We searched Firecrawl’s scientific paper index for nonlinear Einstein–scalar simulations, closed bounces, and inhomogeneous starts to inflation, including work indexed in 2025–2026. We checked passages in the relevant articles and preprints. The comparison concerns initial conditions, symmetry, gravity and matter content. Reproducing an expanding patch for a particular field profile would also be possible in a purely four-dimensional scalar theory.

- Goldwirth and Piran solved the full Einstein equations in a closed, spherically symmetric setting with scalar and radiation sources in 1990. Brady, Baumgarte and Clough revisit the importance of boundary and initial conditions in 2026 using a different exterior. Our contribution is the constrained evolution, growth comparison and numerical checks for the two specific HDblast potentials. [Goldwirth and Piran](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.64.2852) · [Brady et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06441)
- In a November 2026 preprint, Benedix, Currens, Giblin and Richards simulate first-order phase transitions with fully nonlinear gravity, a scalar and a relativistic radiation fluid. Section III specifies Hamiltonian and momentum constraints before initializing bubbles. Their work gives an example of which additional degrees of freedom are needed for a radiation question. Here the initial profile is one smooth harmonic on a closed sphere and only one scalar field is evolved. The phase-transition results do not assign an energy or temperature to this field. [Preprint, computational strategy and initial data](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.24581#S3)
- Shterenberg and collaborators investigate spatial gradients in plateau inflation. Their tests use Weyl and Chern–Pontryagin curvature invariants normalized with the initial mean curvature. Our normal congruence starts with exactly zero expansion, so the corresponding normalization diverges there. We compute electric Weyl curvature using the potential’s positive curvature scale and compare two independent curvature formulas. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24997) · [Initial tidal curvature](../tidal/TIDAL.md)
- Launay, Rigopoulos and Shellard impose perturbative quantum-motivated initial fluctuations and numerically evolve inhomogeneous inflation in a family of geodesic gauges. Their initial constraints and choice of slicing make explicit the information needed to connect a field preparation to a dynamical spacetime. Our classical runs use specified signed amplitudes, with no probabilities assigned to the seeds. [Paper, equations 3–7](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06783#S2)
- East and collaborators evolve nonlinear perturbations of inflationary initial data with a periodic spatial domain and initial expansion. Falciano, Lilley and Peter treat perturbations through a positive-curvature scalar bounce. The tests here combine a closed, momentarily static slice with explicitly solved nonlinear spatial constraints and a short spherical evolution. These differences in topology, potential and initial expansion matter when comparing outcomes. [East et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05143) · [Falciano et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.1196)
- The previous HDblast edition compared small quantum fluctuations with the long waiting time needed for radiation equality in tuned homogeneous models. Its classical nonlinear check held the metric fixed. We use its same fitted potentials, zonal harmonic, units, sign pairs and common time 0.12, so the changes attributable to dynamical gravity can be measured. The source ledger pins the input files to the public repository. Its [portfolio review](https://github.com/maldonado-research/HDblast/blob/86087cf3937d94e5fdfb5752c7f27fb91661d5f1/research/HDBLAST_CLOSED_HILLTOP_PREPARATION_20261119/literature/README.md) records the original study folder inputs and the scope of the author’s other research projects. [Parent edition](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24378057) · [Input checksums](../SOURCES.json)
