# Focused literature assessment: massive de Sitter common-reference stress and variance

Reviewed 2026-10-02 UTC. This round extends the existing eight-primary-paper SMOOTH_FRW review; it does not replace those checks or claim an exhaustive survey. Seven additional primary PDFs were downloaded, version-pinned, hashed, and read at the passages below. The de Sitter section of the previously checked PSAR paper was also read. Article bodies and extracted text are excluded from this public payload. Exact URLs, UTC retrieval times, SHA-256 hashes, passages, and access limits are in the companion metadata.

## Directly actionable findings

1. A massive Euclidean S4 determinant supplies a precise benchmark for a positive-mass, maximally analytic de Sitter state. For a real minimally coupled scalar with x=m²>0, the eigenvalues and multiplicities are λ_l=H²l(l+3)+x and d_l=(l+1)(l+2)(2l+3)/6. The volume is 8π²/(3H⁴). The standard determinant includes l=0, so its unsubtracted homogeneous-mode contribution to Q is 3H⁴/(8π²x). Deleting that mode computes a different observable; the massless endpoint cannot be reached by merely setting x=0 in this state.
2. Different finite conventions must be translated at the action level. The literature explicitly allows local trace terms beyond the mass times variance and curvature anomaly. A scale derivative or conservation identity does not independently identify a numerically constructed pair with the Lorentzian Bunch–Davies expectation values. Compare a separately constructed BD mode result with the same finite reference prescription.
3. The two recent scalar effective-potential papers are useful new analytic benchmarks. Their field-independent integration constants can depend on H. Those constants may be irrelevant for scalar field derivatives but matter for metric variation and absolute vacuum stress. The potential formulas therefore cannot be inserted unchanged as the complete gravitational effective action.
4. Keep the same mass convention during dimensional continuation. A dimension-dependent de Sitter index multiplies a UV pole and produces finite local curvature terms. One recent paper displays this correction explicitly. Its intermediate derivative formulas also show an apparent sign inconsistency; use the spectral definition and an independent gamma/tadpole derivation, rather than copying the printed intermediate sign.
5. The newly read 2026 nonlocal and effective-field-theory papers concern interacting or massless correlation observables. They motivate later work on memory and infrared matching, but supply no replacement for the present free massive one-point stress/variance construction or a source-consuming shell.

## New primary passages

**Lucas Vicente García-Consuegra and Arttu Rajantie, Scalar effective potentials in de Sitter spacetime, [2511.23076v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23076v2), version dated 15 May 2026.** Printed pp.10–12, Eqs.28–41 derive the standard S4 determinant, spectrum, dimensional continuation, and integrated polygamma expression. At fixed physical mass, Eq.38 gives ν_d=ν−3ε/(4ν)+O(ε²), with d=4−ε and ν²=9/4−V''/H². The p.12 footnote explicitly explains the finite +6ν² term induced in the potential and its absorption into the nonminimal coupling for their calculation. This directly supports preserving finite local terms when translating schemes.

Printed pp.18–20, Eqs.61–71 define a *constraint* effective potential by fixing the sphere average; its determinant removes the constant l=0 mode. The authors explicitly distinguish this from the standard potential and note that a direct Lorentzian expression for the constrained average is unclear. Use their standard determinant for the present positive-mass free BD benchmark; do not import the constrained construction as a new subtraction convention.

A local algebra audit, confirmed by a separate internal reader, found an apparent sign inconsistency in the displayed intermediate equations: Eq.33 is a positive sum of log[A_l−ν_d²], while the first line of Eq.36 writes a positive sum for its ν_d² derivative. Direct differentiation requires a minus sign. The UV pole in Eq.37 likewise differentiates with the opposite sign from the pole in Eq.39. PDF pages 11–12 were visually inspected to exclude text-extraction error. This is a reason to check formulas against the defining determinant and another primary source, not evidence against the determinant construction or the paper's physical conclusions. The exact common-reference stress benchmark must be established independently.

**Alfio Bonanno, Sergio Luigi Cacciatori and Ugo Moschella, Effective potentials for de Sitter and anti de Sitter quantum fields, [2603.02140v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02140v1), 2 March 2026.** Printed p.6 Eq.2.22 relates the mass derivative of the potential to one half of the coincident Euclidean propagator. Printed pp.9–10 Eqs.3.25–3.32 give the meromorphic gamma-function tadpole and its four-dimensional digamma expansion. With unit sphere radius and their principal-series parameter u, Eq.3.25 is

T_d(u) = (4π)^(-d/2) Γ(1−d/2) Γ((d−1)/2+iu) Γ((d−1)/2−iu) / [Γ(1/2+iu)Γ(1/2−iu)].

Their Eq.3.29, at d=4−2ε and fixed u, has pole −(u²+1/4)[1/ε+1−γ+log(4π)]/(16π²) and finite digamma contribution (u²+1/4)[ψ(−1/2+iu)+ψ(−1/2−iu)]/(16π²). The digamma pair equals ψ(3/2+iu)+ψ(3/2−iu) by recurrence; the reciprocal terms cancel pairwise. Restore H and the renormalization scale and translate fixed-u versus fixed-physical-mass continuation before comparison. Their u²=x/H²−9/4 is the negative of the usual squared Hankel index. Printed p.10 was visually checked. Their explicit higher-loop renormalized applications are principally three dimensional, and their integrated scalar potential does not establish the full common-reference gravitational stress.

**Alan Chodos and András Kaiser, Zeta function regularization in de Sitter space: the Minkowski limit, [hep-th/9609072v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9609072v2), 11 September 1996.** Printed pp.3–4, Eqs.2.4–2.12 specify the Euclidean sphere spectrum, multiplicities, and dimensionless zeta function. Specializing their general spectrum to D=4 gives the spectrum stated above. Their explicit proof of the analytically continued Minkowski limit is for D=2. The general spectral definition is useful; cite neither a D=4 flat-limit proof nor a Lorentzian stress-matching proof as established by this paper. Their field/action normalization must also be translated to the real-scalar one-half determinant.

**Antonio Ferreiro and Francisco Torrenti, Ultraviolet-regularized power spectrum without infrared distortions in cosmological spacetimes, [2212.01078v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01078v2), 2023.** This was inherited-only in the prior review and is primary-checked here. Printed p.4 Eqs.20–32 separates the variance prescription from finite gravitational matching. Eq.25 contains the mass/derivative variance term, an anomaly, and arbitrary a+bR+c□R. In the paper's (+---) conventions Eq.26 gives T_an=(R_ab R^ab−R²/3)/(2880π²)−(ξ−1/6)²R²/(32π²). Eq.29 includes an explicit −m²R/(288π²) finite term in its chosen trace; Eq.21 shows the corresponding local variance shift. These formulas demonstrate why a trace relation imported into a different finite scheme needs the matched local action. Printed p.5 Eqs.33–38 gives the minimal de Sitter Hankel modes and power spectra; its massless infrared limitation is explicit. This prescription does not equal the proposed fixed-r subtraction.

**Tommi Markkanen and Arttu Rajantie, Massive scalar field evolution in de Sitter, [1607.00334v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00334v3), 26 January 2017.** Printed pp.9–12, Eqs.3.16–3.22 show how a sharp momentum cutoff generates boundary terms in ρ+p and how combined mode-by-mode adiabatic subtraction restores the covariant de Sitter equation of state. The integrated BD stress has p=−ρ, although individual mode contributions can look particle-like. The authors then use differences from the BD equilibrium state, which removes the absolute equilibrium stress. Their difference prescription cannot fix the absolute finite vacuum energy required here, but their mode/boundary analysis supports an independent ρ+p diagnostic and warns against equating a particle-number component with total stress.

## Recent adjacent methods, with narrower scope

**Will Cerne and Teruaki Suyama, Nonlocal Corrections to Scalar Field Effective Action in de Sitter spacetime, [2601.22644v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22644v1), 30 January 2026.** Printed pp.7–8, Sec.IIIB–C, Eqs.50–55 subtract local UV terms from an in-in memory kernel and display a finite causal equation for a test scalar at one loop through second interaction order. The reduction of memory to a local drift coefficient additionally assumes a slowly varying field. Printed pp.12–13 reiterates the test-field restriction. This is a possible later interacting/backreaction-method lead; its stochastic noise and interacting variance correction are different observables from the present free-field Q and one-point stress.

**Martin Beneke, Patrick Hager and Andrea F. Sanfilippo, Renormalisation and matching of massless scalar correlation functions in Soft de Sitter Effective Theory, [2603.09438v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09438v2), 22 July 2026.** Printed pp.18–21, Sec.3.5 and Eqs.3.48–3.58 explain dimensional plus analytic regulation and matching of an initial-condition functional. A common infrared regulator in the full theory and EFT is required for cancellation of their infrared divergences; ultraviolet finite conventions can instead be compensated by matching coefficients. This is a distinct massless interacting EFT construction, not a formula for the present massive de Sitter pair. No stress or shell conclusion is imported.

## Implication for this round

Keep x>0; preserve the full sphere spectrum; derive Q and metric stress from the same declared finite action with reference r held fixed during metric variation; translate local terms before comparing digamma and mode formulas; independently test the Lorentzian BD pair. An exact homogeneous calculation can establish a useful common-scheme benchmark. It does not establish a nonsmooth-history quantum state, dynamical shell energy transfer, thermal radiation, a hot Big Bang, novelty, or prize-level significance.
