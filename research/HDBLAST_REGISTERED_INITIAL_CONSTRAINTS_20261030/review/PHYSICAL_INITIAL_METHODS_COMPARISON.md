## Nonlinear scalar constraints and perturbative initial completion

Y. Choquet-Bruhat, J. Isenberg and D. Pollack, *The constraint equations for
the Einstein-scalar field system on compact manifolds*,
[arXiv:gr-qc/0610045v2](https://arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0610045v2), revised
11 December 2006. The primary HTML body was retrieved. Sections 2, 4.3 and
the assumptions of Theorems 1 and 2 were inspected.

Their equations (4) and (5) impose the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints
on physical spatial metric, extrinsic curvature, scalar value and normalized
scalar velocity. Section 2 then converts these nonlinear constraints into a
conformal scalar/vector elliptic system. The main classification assumes a
closed spatial manifold and constant mean curvature; the existence cases also
depend on conformal-data signs. This is a construction of nonlinear initial
data under stated hypotheses. It does not identify HDBLAST's compact packet,
material shell or its chosen second differentiated initial prescription.

The Hamiltonian structure agrees with the HDBLAST physical-Cauchy formula.
There is an explicit convention issue: their equation (5) writes
`div K - d tr K = -pi d psi`, whereas HDBLAST's retained future-normal
convention writes `div K - d tr K = +Pi d phi`. Momentum formulas cannot be
imported without a consistent extrinsic-curvature/unit-normal convention
conversion. A source being written in canonical momentum densities instead
of covariant K and unit-normal Pi would introduce a different nonlinear
variable conversion and therefore a different quadratic second jet.

The useful comparison is between the full nonlinear constraint equations and
their second parameter derivatives. An exact cancellation of registered
second-order coefficients binds a perturbative initial jet to those equations.
It does not prove a family of nonlinear constraint solutions, or an evolving
Einstein-scalar spacetime with a shell, without the corresponding remainder
and existence arguments.

P. Castillon and C. Nguyen-The, *Spherically symmetric solutions to the
Einstein-scalar field conformal constraint equations*,
[arXiv:2602.20099v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20099v2), revised
10 March 2026. The primary HTML body was retrieved; the introduction,
equations (1.1)-(1.4), and Main Theorem 1 were inspected.

This recent work solves or classifies conformal constraints in radial settings
on harmonic manifolds, with specific conclusions on the round sphere,
Euclidean space and hyperbolic space. Its Main Theorem 1 assumes a radial
data set on a space form, zero freely specified TT tensor, and
`rho_scalar * psi_prime = 0`, in addition to its positivity/nondegeneracy
conditions. Here the paper's `rho_scalar` is scalar-velocity data, not
HDBLAST's background radius. The paper's physical momentum equation (1.1b)
uses the positive scalar source sign. The HDBLAST quadrupole is angularly
nonradial and its physical shell is not one of those conformal boundary
settings. The radial reduction therefore cannot be substituted for the
current quadrupole/shell proof. It provides useful future comparison cases,
not a theorem covering this packet.

## Harmonic constraints need their own initial and boundary argument

H.-O. Kreiss and J. Winicour, *Problems which are well-posed in a generalized
sense with applications to the Einstein equations*,
[arXiv:gr-qc/0602051v3](https://arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0602051v3), revised
6 June 2006. The primary HTML body was retrieved; Sections 3 and 4 and the
relevant hypotheses in the appendix were inspected.

The paper proves estimates for constraint-preserving, hierarchically coupled
Sommerfeld conditions for the harmonic Einstein equations. Its linearized
half-space construction and curved-coefficient discussion require the stated
boundary-symbol conditions, smooth compatible data and a genuine homogeneous
constraint boundary problem. A reduced wave equation having a solution is
not by itself the argument that its harmonic covector vanishes. These are
vacuum harmonic Einstein boundary constructions; a five-dimensional
Einstein-scalar material-shell junction law has not been identified with
those Sommerfeld boundary operators in this audit.

J. Seiler, B. Szilagyi, D. Pollney and L. Rezzolla,
*Constraint-preserving boundary treatment for a harmonic formulation of the
Einstein equations*,
[arXiv:0802.3341v2](https://arxiv.org/html/0802.3341v2), revised 11 July 2008.
The primary HTML body was retrieved; Sections 2.1 and 3.2 and the conclusions
were inspected. The arXiv revision record was checked separately; an HTML
rendering date was not used as the publication date.

Their equation (9) explicitly requires both the harmonic constraint and its
initial time derivative to vanish. Equations (10) and the boundary construction
explain why homogeneous subsidiary propagation and appropriate boundary data
are also needed. They implement a first-order-in-time, second-order-in-space
vacuum harmonic system with constraint-preserving Sommerfeld conditions;
their energy argument assumes the boundary is in the linearized regime, and
they also report nonlinear numerical tests. Such tests and their boundary law
do not prove HDBLAST's physical scalar-shell Codazzi/Robin identities.

For the present registration audit, the practical chain is precise: bind the

and subsidiary-uniqueness inputs. An initial exact binding audit discharges

all of its evolution or boundary proofs with one initial cancellation.
