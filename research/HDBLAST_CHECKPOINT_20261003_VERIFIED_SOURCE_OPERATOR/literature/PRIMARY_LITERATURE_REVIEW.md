The immediately useful result is an explicit contract for converting a high-precision integral into a rigorous enclosure. Arb/FLINT supplies enclosing arithmetic and a validated integration algorithm, but the user's integrand implementation, analytic-domain checks, all omitted pieces, and serialization must preserve that contract. A precision setting or a quadrature agreement does not establish it.

## Requirements supported by the primary numerical sources

1. **Enclose the declared target.** Construct retained native values as their exact represented rational/binary values, or state and enclose a different intended real input. `arb_set_d` preserves an already rounded binary64 value; it does not attach the error between that value and its decimal spelling. The enclosing guarantee applies to inputs actually provided.
2. **Verify analytic domains when requested.** `acb_calc_integrate` calls an integrand with `order=1` to bound it on a complex neighborhood. Its callback must reject a domain on which holomorphicity fails, not just evaluate a plausible value on the real line. Bounded branch cuts require explicit handling. Correct ball operations do not automatically make an arbitrary callback valid.
3. **Inspect the final enclosure.** The library documentation warns that cumulative error can exceed requested absolute or relative goals. The final radius and all independently bounded additions must meet the registered acceptance condition. Nonfinite output, resource exhaustion, no convergence and a finite but overly wide enclosure need separate preserved outcomes.
4. **Keep phase structure explicit.** The existing next-study proposal's analytic phase integration with a proved source-polynomial/Taylor remainder is compatible with these guarantees. On the real line, `|exp(i omega s)|=1`, so a uniform source remainder `R` over length `L` contributes at most `L R` to that moment. By contrast, a complex box with imaginary half-width `h` permits a carrier magnitude up to `exp(|omega| h)`, which can inflate a generic complex-domain bound. These are elementary bounds, not newly discovered mathematics or a demonstrated implementation speedup.
5. **Do not cross the bump's singularities silently.** The registered source contract is `B(z)=exp(-z^2/(1-z^2))` for `|z|<1`, zero elsewhere, with `z=eta+4`; `L=-1/eta`. Central `[-4.5,-3.5]` is within the analytic interior, but integration boxes must avoid `eta=-5,-3,0`. The flat compact-support endpoints are not holomorphic continuations of the interior formula. A later whole-support certificate needs explicitly split or separately bounded endpoint pieces.
6. **Propagate every error term.** Forced moments, mode contractions, finite-cutoff contacts, source work, momentum weights and output endpoints need enclosing arithmetic or proved remainder bounds. An integral enclosure on an approximate mode function certifies that approximate integrand unless the trajectory error is enclosed as well. Signed cancellations and invariant defects cannot replace an absolute error budget.

For reference, the released FLINT documentation gives the local Gaussian bound

`|I - sum(w_k f(x_k))| <= 64 M / (15 (rho-1) rho^(2n-1))`

on `[-1,1]` when `f` is holomorphic in the specified ellipse, `|f| <= M` there, and `rho>1`. The exact ellipse, affine interval mapping, enclosing `M`, nodes/weights and rounding are part of the premise; plugging a sampled maximum into this formula does not prove the error bound. The old Taylor routine instead uses a Cauchy bound on a surrounding circle and requires singularities farther away than its outer radius.

## Later physical leads

Mottola's conservation derivation supports retaining contact and state terms when the project eventually constructs a full retarded metric response. The thick-brane paper supplies a useful model-building checklist: specify the localized matter action and profile, derive its normalized overlap/coupling, compare its decay rate with bulk leakage, calculate the initial excitation abundance and establish thermalization. In the inspected trace-coupling example, ideal conformal radiation has vanishing linear trace coupling, so energy transfer cannot be inferred merely from an expanding brane or radiation-like stress term. Its coefficients and temperature estimate are not HDBLAST predictions.

These leads do not establish reheating, physical source-energy normalization, observational accessibility, a higher-dimensional cause of the Big Bang, or external mathematical novelty. The immediate priority remains a narrow, reviewed enclosure method and its fabricated controls before any new registered physical evaluation.
