## Recent primary passages and exact applicability limits

**New August 2026 scalar formula.** Andreas Chatziafratis, Claudio Giorgi, Alain Miranville and Federico Zullo, [On generalised d'Alembert-type integral representations for damped wave equations on the quarter-plane, arXiv:2608.06355v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06355v1). The canonical history confirms submission on 6 August 2026; the PDF separately prints 27 July 2025, which is not treated as an arXiv revision. Primary problem (1.1), assumptions (1.2), Theorems 1–3 and §8/Theorem 5 treat a scalar constant-coefficient forced damped wave with prescribed **Dirichlet** boundary value. The text separately checks corner regularity, boundary traces and uniqueness in its stated decay/integrability class. It offers a prototype and benchmarking source, not the coupled variable-coefficient Dirichlet/Neumann theorem needed here. Its analytic verification of a formula is not an interval numerical error certificate. PDF equation extraction was imperfect; no detailed kernel formula is imported.

**A 2026 revision does not upgrade displacement error to derivative error.** Zhaonan Dong, Lorenzo Mascotto and Zuodong Wang, [A priori and a posteriori error estimates of a C0-in-time method for the wave equation in second order formulation, arXiv:2411.03264v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03264v3), revised 3 May 2026. Primary Theorem 2.1 gives explicit discrete energy stability; §3 and Proposition 3.6 give a semidiscrete-in-time a posteriori bound for displacement in L∞(time;L²(space)). The latter assumes the additional Laplacian regularity in (42); Proposition 3.1 supplies sufficient convex-domain/data hypotheses. The displayed model has homogeneous Dirichlet conditions and explicitly leaves inhomogeneous Neumann complications outside its analysis. The discrete stability theorem must not be relabeled as a validated error bound for gauge derivatives.

**Another explicit reconstruction precedent.** Sergio Gómez, [A variational approach to the analysis of the continuous space-time FEM for the wave equation, arXiv:2501.11494v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11494v2), revised 17 July 2025. Primary §5, assumptions (5.1) and Theorem 5.1, bounds displacement in C0(time;L²(space)) for a semidiscrete-in-time scheme using postprocessing. This section assumes homogeneous Dirichlet data, constant positive wave speed and the stated initial/source regularity; Assumption 1 requires elliptic H² regularity. It supplies explicit reconstruction methodology, not a spatially fully discrete or pointwise derivative certificate for this gauge system. No 2026 arXiv revision was found on its canonical record.

**Energy-estimator follow-up, access limited.** The primary author publication list identifies Théophile Chaumont-Frelet and Alexandre Ern, [Damped energy-norm a posteriori error estimates for fully discrete approximations of the wave equation using C2-reconstructions](https://doi.org/10.1051/m2an/2025027), ESAIM M2AN 59 (2025), 1937–1972. Its HAL and publisher body routes were inaccessible. Its theorem hypotheses and norm have therefore not been audited or imported. A secondary search-result date suggesting 2026 is not used to redate this paper.

## Direct local construction completed after the literature check

The actual system has one fixed angular representation, λ=8, with raw contravariant amplitudes U=(f,b,d), zero initial values/velocities and shell rows f=0, b_y=d_y=0. Here f is the radial gauge amplitude; r denotes the scaled optical spatial coordinate, not an amplitude. The optical coordinate dy=−ρ dz makes the common principal part a scalar unit-speed wave operator; the variable warp and de Sitter terms are linear lower-order couplings. Since ρ is nonzero, the two Neumann rows are equivalently b_z=d_z=0. The [operator proof](../operator/GAUGE_OPERATOR_AND_CONSTRUCTION.md) derives these identities for the actual system.

With x=t/ℓ, r=z/ℓ and ℓ=10⁻⁶, the construction works on 0≤x≤3, r≥0, x+r≤8. For p=U_x+U_r and m=U_x−U_r, the shell gives m=Jp with J=diag(−1,+1,+1). Integrating along the resulting characteristic paths yields a Volterra operator for the actual coupled system. The companion physical half-line Dirichlet/Neumann kernels give the same solution without assuming a smooth parity extension of coefficients or source. The following points were proposed requirements in the initial literature assessment; the linked local proofs now address them:

- The causal triangle and authenticated whole-collar background/source bounds keep every predecessor path in the verified region. No artificial inner boundary or extra reflection is introduced.
- The contraction uses the actual matrix couplings and the mixed physical boundary rows. The evaluated global contraction bound is q₃<0.011412.
- The [regularity proof](../regularity/MIXED_WAVE_REGULARITY.md) retains one-sided shell traces and uses the flat initial packet collar for corner compatibility. It proves convergence of time-differentiated iterates and recovers radial derivatives from the equation, giving classical C3 regularity up to the physical shell.
- The [defect certificate](../solver/GAUGE_WAVE_SOLUTION_AND_DEFECT.md) encloses the continuous comparator residual against the actual master and boundary derivative bounds. A nonnegative matrix resolvent controls the actual solution error in a declared weighted C1 norm. Its comparator satisfies the zero Cauchy and mixed boundary rows exactly; this is stronger than checking a collocation grid residual.
- The evaluated bounds control raw amplitudes and scaled first derivatives. The analytic C3 result did not by itself evaluate numerical C2/C3 envelopes; the [separate higher-norm certificate](../higher-norms/README.md) now supplies them for raw-amplitude coordinate partials. A covariant norm for the transformed metric and bounds for later quadratic sources remain outside these certificates. Displacement L∞L² estimates in the cited papers are still insufficient substitutes for those quantities.

Combining the exact operator identity with the constructed classical solution gives C1[h_wave]=0 under the local proofs' premises. The gauge vector is tangent to the shell, with free tangential boundary values; the Neumann rows do not set b or d to zero. This establishes the first coordinate gauge wave on the audited finite triangle. It does not evaluate the physical second response, certify a force, or complete the later transformation of the metric–scalar pair and its quadratic sources, second evolution, or nonlinear remainder.

No general external mixed-boundary theorem matching all these coefficients and the desired derivative norm was verified in this bounded search. That statement limits the literature comparison; it does not negate the direct conditional construction now proved locally.
