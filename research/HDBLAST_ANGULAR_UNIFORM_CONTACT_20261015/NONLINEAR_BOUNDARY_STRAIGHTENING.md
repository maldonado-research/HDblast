## 1. The exact state-space boundary flow

Use the sixteen state variables
\(q_D=(g_{ya})\), \(q_N=(N,h_{ab},\phi)\), with
\(N=\sqrt{g_{yy}}>0\). In a neighborhood of the background this is an
invertible pointwise change from the metric/scalar variables, including
off the boundary. At the boundary the exact rows are

\[
 q_D=0,\qquad \partial_y q_N=f(t,\Omega,q_N),
\]

\[
 f_N=\tfrac23\sigma(\phi)N^2-a_bN^3\operatorname{tr}_{h}h_{0,b},
 \quad f_h=\tfrac13\sigma(\phi)Nh,
 \quad f_\phi=-\tfrac12\sigma'(\phi)N.
 \tag{1}
\]

The function is smooth on a state neighborhood where \(N>0\) and \(h\)
is nondegenerate of the required Lorentzian signature. Let
\(U=q-q_0\), with the known background subtracted in the full collar.
Since \(\partial_y=-\rho_b^{-1}\partial_z\) at the boundary, define

\[
 \mathcal B(t,\Omega,u)
   =-\rho_b\,[f(t,\Omega,q_{0,b}+u)-f(t,\Omega,q_{0,b})].
 \tag{2}
\]

Then the exact perturbed boundary equation is

\[
 -U_{N,z}+\mathcal B(t,\Omega,U_N)=0,
 \qquad \mathcal B(t,\Omega,0)=0.
 \tag{3}
\]

The minus sign in (2) is essential. The derivative of \(\mathcal B\) at
zero is the inward Robin matrix expressed in the chosen lapse variables;
passing from \(\delta N\) to \(\delta g_{yy}=2\delta N\) gives the matrix
in the linear note.

Let \(\Phi_s(t,\Omega,v)\) be the local flow in state space generated by
\(\mathcal B(t,\Omega,\cdot)\). Choose a fixed collar function
\(\theta(z)=z\) near zero, compactly supported with sufficiently small
amplitude that the flow exists for every state in a fixed smaller
neighborhood. Set

\[
 U_D=V_D,\qquad U_N=\Psi(z,t,\Omega,V_N)
 :=\Phi_{\theta(z)}(t,\Omega,V_N).
 \tag{4}
\]

This is a smooth pointwise state diffeomorphism, equal to the identity away
from the collar. It fixes the zero perturbation. At \(z=0\),

\[
 \Psi(0,t,\Omega,v)=v,\quad D_v\Psi(0,t,\Omega,v)=I,
 \quad\Psi_z(0,t,\Omega,v)=\mathcal B(t,\Omega,v).
\]

Using the chain rule in (3) therefore gives exactly

\[
 V_D|_0=0,\qquad V_{N,z}|_0=0.
 \tag{5}
\]

No nonlinear Robin remainder is discarded. This is the nonlinear version
of the matrix exponential; linearizing the state flow yields the linear
collar conjugation.

## 2. What happens to the quasilinear principal symbol

The reduced Einstein and scalar equations have a common scalar metric wave
principal symbol, after their invertible row normalizations. Both changes
of dependent variables, first \(g_{yy}\leftrightarrow N\) and then (4),
are pointwise maps with invertible Jacobian. Consequently the transformed
system has the form

\[
 g^{\mu\nu}(x,V)\partial_\mu\partial_\nu V
       =\mathcal F(x,V,\partial V),
 \tag{6}
\]

with \(\mathcal F\) smooth in its arguments on the fixed state neighborhood.
The second derivatives of the point transformation multiply products of
first derivatives and produce lower-order terms. Equation (6) has the
actual metric as its common principal coefficient, not the frozen metric.
The zero state is a solution because the background solves the full equations
and the point transformation fixes it.

The coordinate Dirichlet condition implies at the boundary

\[
 g_{za}=0,\qquad g^{za}=0,
 \quad a=t,\Omega^1,\Omega^2,\Omega^3.
 \tag{7}
\]

This fact must be preserved in every frozen-coefficient iterate. If it is,
the principal boundary energy flux for a commuted field \(W\) is a scalar
multiple of \(\langle W_t,W_z\rangle\). Time and angular derivatives of
(5) give zero Dirichlet time traces and zero Neumann normal traces, so the
flux again vanishes. Tangential/time differentiation does not reintroduce
nonlinear boundary forcing.

Near the background, \(dt\) remains timelike, the shell normal remains
spacelike, and the spatial quadratic form used by the standard \(\partial_t\)
wave energy remains positive. The latter is an explicit near-background
condition; timelikeness of \(dt\) alone in arbitrary coordinates would not
justify using that particular elementary energy.

## 3. A plausible quasilinear theorem, and what still needs proof

A natural target in four spatial dimensions is initial regularity
\(H^m\times H^{m-1}\) with, conservatively, \(m\ge6\), exact corner
compatibility to the required orders, and data in a sufficiently small
state neighborhood of the background. The target solution class is

\[
 V\in C([0,T_0];H^m)\cap C^1([0,T_0];H^{m-1}),
 \qquad \partial_t^jV\in C([0,T_0];H^{m-j})\ (j\le m),
\]

with the top time continuity modified in the usual way if rougher forcing
is allowed. Homogeneous nonlinear equations suffice for the intended use.

The actual remaining analytic steps are these:

1. Establish variable-principal-coefficient estimates for (6), with (7),
   whose constants depend on controlled coefficient norms and the fixed
   timelike/spacelike margins. The frozen-background theorem alone is not
   this result.
2. Prove tame high-order estimates using time/angular commutation and
   normal recovery. At order \(m-1\), commutators contain derivatives of
   \(g^{\mu\nu}(V)\) multiplying second derivatives of \(V\). For
   \(m\ge6\), Sobolev multiplication in four spatial dimensions has room
   to control these terms, but the complete estimate must still be written.
3. Construct iterates satisfying homogeneous (5), including initial corner
   jets. Merely freezing arbitrary trial coefficients does not automatically
   preserve all higher corner identities. Initialize a compatible jet
   extension and verify the recursive identities in the chosen iteration.
4. Obtain uniform existence time and convergence, for example by contraction
   one derivative below the uniform high norm. State continuous dependence
   and uniqueness in the resulting solution class.
5. Prove the usual continuation statement while coefficient norms, signature
   and boundary timelikeness stay controlled. To reach a specified contact
   time, establish a lifespan covering it; local existence at time zero
   alone does not do that.

The exact boundary flow eliminates a real nonlinear boundary difficulty and
suggests that this program is feasible. It is not a substitute for completing
these five steps. No general quasilinear theorem is imported here without
matching its boundary, coefficient and compatibility assumptions.

## 4. Constraints and exact data remain separate requirements

For a sufficiently regular exact reduced solution satisfying the actual
physical shell laws, the exact subsidiary system is

\[
 (\Box_g+\operatorname{Ric}_g)C=0,\qquad C_n|_b=0,
 \qquad (\nabla_n C)_a-K_a{}^bC_b=0.
 \tag{8}
\]

The Robin law follows from Codazzi and the scalar junction, including the
field dependence of \(\sigma\). It is homogeneous in \(C\). Once the
variable-coefficient analogue of the energy uniqueness proof is in place,
zero subsidiary Cauchy data imply \(C=0\). Exact physical Cauchy constraints
and exact initial harmonic completion must supply these data.

The prior checkpoint already supplies an exact localized initial-data family
for the selected aligned reflection-invariant quadrupole, under its stated
background, support and weighted-inverse hypotheses. Specifically,
`publish-exact-precontact-20261014/research/HDBLAST_EXACT_LOCALIZED_PRECONTACT_20261014/EXACT_LOCALIZED_INITIAL_DATA_AND_PRECONTACT.md`
proves, for each fixed \(k\ge12\), a parameter-smooth exact constraint family
with metric in \(H^{k+2}\), momentum in \(H^{k+1}\), prescribed scalar
sectors, and corrections supported in a fixed closed annulus away from the
shell and vertex. Its tangent is the selected datum. The family equals the
background on an open shell neighborhood, so all finite-order shell corner
conditions can be inherited after a local initial harmonic gauge completion.
That earlier construction also gives an exact short precontact evolution
with \(C^2\) parameter dependence in a lower, piecewise spacetime \(C^4\)
norm. These achievements are retained as conditional inputs here.

The initial-family theorem therefore need not be reproved merely to attempt
the mixed boundary evolution. What remains is to carry that family, with
its exact initial harmonic completion, through the coupled quasilinear
boundary construction above; prove a uniform lifespan reaching the intended
contact interval; and track parameter differentiability with explicitly
reserved Sobolev losses. The precontact Cauchy estimates do not alone prove
these statements after a signal reaches the shell.

The prior fixed-\(k\) family is not a common spatial \(C^\infty\) family on
one parameter interval. Its selected second initial correction also need
not equal the particular second correction used in a different frozen
Taylor construction. No identification of those second coefficients is
being made here.

Thus the established advance here is the exact nonlinear boundary reduction
and a specified next proof target built on the existing exact initial and
short precontact families. An exact nonlinear shell-contact family for the
selected perturbation remains unproved in these artifacts.
