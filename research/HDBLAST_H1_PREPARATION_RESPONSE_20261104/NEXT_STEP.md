The new theorem supplies a weaker geometric route to a signed coefficient bound.
It does not select initial data. The next continuation should examine one explicit
physical preparation process and determine whether its image fits the theorem.

1. Specify an operational initial-section and shell-anchor rule. Scalar labels
   within an already supplied section do not themselves choose that section.
2. Derive the canonical initial data through second order from a source, state or
   variational preparation rule. Verify the fixed first packet, compact metric-mean
   freedom, weighted zero moment and fixed other canonical sectors. If additional
   sectors occur, derive the corresponding coupled estimate rather than assuming
   the current theorem covers them.
3. Derive a calibrated, preparation-sensitive bound that controls `R_H1`. A bound
   on a quadratic statistic of the fixed first tangent cannot do this. The tilted
   estimating energy in this checkpoint is not automatically a physical Hamiltonian.
   Any physical coercivity, units and budget require their own derivation.
4. Address the explicitly retained continuous operator-norm and development premises
   where possible. Byte authentication and exact finite arithmetic do not discharge
   these analytic assumptions.
5. Establish uniform higher norms and nonlinear remainder bounds for a physically
   justified class before claiming a common finite perturbation window. A fixed H1
   budget still permits arbitrarily large pointwise derivatives and higher norms.
6. Extend to a registered proper-time history, matter/radiation interactions, an
   energy ledger and a physical scale before cosmological or observational claims.
