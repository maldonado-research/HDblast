# Physical energy, squeezing and a microscopic decay test

**Which energy leaves the oscillator?** For the model tested here, freeze the physical Hamiltonian at one instant. A Gaussian attenuation channel with covariance equation $\dot V|_{\rm bath}=-\Gamma(V-V_*)$ assigns physical oscillator power $-\Gamma(\langle H\rangle-\langle H\rangle_*)$. Starting in the physical ground state, the oscillator gains energy if its target is squeezed relative to that ground state. A simultaneous positive daughter credit then requires an identified energy source. The sign follows from this channel, its target and its physical Hamiltonian. It does not determine the actual decay rate of a cosmological field.

**Passivity and squeezing.** Brown, Friis and Huber give conditions for Gaussian passivity and the minimum energy accessible with Gaussian unitaries. For one centered Gaussian oscillator, express the covariance in its normalized physical quadratures. Unsqueezing it to a multiple of the identity leaves its symplectic eigenvalue fixed and minimizes its energy. Manzano gives a specific microscopic repeated-interaction model in which a squeezed reservoir can supply work. Its interaction conserves total bare energy and an additional quadrature observable. Equation (8) gives both balances, and the first law separates heat and squeezing work. In the corresponding resonant beam-splitter construction, a fresh uncorrelated ancilla with covariance $V_*$ gives $V_{\rm out}=\eta V+(1-\eta)V_*$, with $\eta$ the transmissivity. The exchange conserves the two oscillators’ total bare energy. A squeezed ancilla can therefore heat a ground-state oscillator while losing its own stored energy. Realizing such a channel would identify a supplying reservoir. The construction alone gives no emitted fermion energy. [Brown, Friis and Huber, Theorem 1 and steps P1–P4, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04977v2) · [Manzano, Sections II–III, v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07448v3)

**Driven jump operators.** Dann, Levy and Kosloff derive an open-system equation from a specified oscillator–bath coupling. Their nonadiabatic jump operators follow the driven system propagator. The adiabatic limit recovers the instantaneous Hamiltonian transition frequencies. The derivation assumes weak coupling and time-scale separation involving the bath correlation time. Choosing a preferred vacuum definition on the HDblast wall supplies neither the interaction nor these scale checks. [Dann, Levy and Kosloff, Eqs. (16), (22) and (25), v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.10689v4)

**A direct daughter-field calculation.** Boyanovsky and Herring derive a two-time self-energy for a massive scalar Yukawa-coupled to massless fermions on a post-inflationary background. Equations (III.6)–(III.9) express the scalar amplitude’s memory integral in terms of fermion-pair matrix elements. They find finite-time dressing and redshift-dependent survival. Their Eq. (VI.28) compares against a single instantaneous Lorentz factor applied to the whole elapsed time. HDblast integrates its assigned rate through time, so their lifetime bias cannot be carried over. A microscopic extension could use the measured wall geometry, the time-dependent scalar mass, an initial scalar two-point function and specified daughter vacuum, and then compute the daughter stress and interaction energy. Their published calculation addresses a single prepared massive particle within an adiabatic expansion. It does not supply that answer through the HDblast mass crossing. [Boyanovsky and Herring, Sections III and VI, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12343v2)

**Switching control.** Louko and Satz calculate the finite-time response of a smoothly switched particle detector in a four-dimensional curved spacetime with a Hadamard field state. For matched switching, Eq. (3.17) cancels the state-independent switching terms and retains a difference of two-time correlators. Abrupt switching gives logarithmic sensitivity in the individual integrated detector response. This detector result motivates varying the interaction turn-on and comparing histories with the same smooth switch. Its logarithmic law applies to their detector observable. For an explicitly prescribed coupling $H_{\rm int}=f(s)V$, the term $\dot f\langle V\rangle$ belongs in the energy balance of an HDblast daughter calculation. [Louko and Satz, Eqs. (3.17), (7.1)–(7.2), v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.5671v3)
