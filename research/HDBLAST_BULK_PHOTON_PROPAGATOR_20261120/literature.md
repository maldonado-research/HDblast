# When can a photon stay near a five-dimensional wall?

The tuned HDblast geometry has an infinite proper throat. The added scalar weight $G=(1-\phi^2)^2$ makes the norm of its constant Maxwell mode finite. In conformal distance the wave potential decreases as $20.91/z^2$, so excitations with arbitrarily small mass are also available. This study computes how strongly a wall signal couples to them. The separately registered detuned de Sitter shell has different asymptotics and a vector continuum threshold of $H_{\rm shell}/2$. [Earlier project calculation](https://github.com/maldonado-research/HDblast/blob/dbdf3fe1e62981d3b5a92081a0dc9eb14029e21e/research/HDBLAST_GAUGE_KINETIC_FIRST_DESCENT_20261119/origin/ORIGIN.md)

- Freitas, Alencar and Landim (2020) explicitly derive the factor $G(y)e^{-2A(y)}\xi_0(y)^2$ in the tangential Maxwell stress of a warped geometry. With a constant zero mode, independence of this factor from the extra coordinate selects $G\propto e^{2A}$. This paper is a direct antecedent for the weight used in our second comparison. Alencar and colleagues (2026) also impose a local trace relation between tangential and bulk stress. A general Maxwell configuration has a radial pressure proportional to $F^2$ in the separated zero-mode ansatz. For the restricted null Maxwell wave this invariant, and hence the associated radial pressure and scalar source, vanish. The present application compares the two weights quantitatively on the tuned HDblast leaves. [Freitas et al., Eq. 62 and following paragraph](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.01267#S5.Ex10) · [Alencar et al., local conditions 119–121](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05190#S3.E120)
- Ohta and Sakai (2010) construct five-dimensional domain walls with a position-dependent gauge kinetic coefficient. Their examples have a massless vector, a gap to higher states, and a four-dimensional Coulomb force for wall sources in the regime they analyze. Arai, Blaschke, Eto and Kawaguchi (April 2026) explicitly trap gauge fields using the scalar multiplets of an SU(5) wall model. These examples show where a localization law can enter a microscopic action. Whether their field content or masses carry over to HDblast requires its own derivation. [Ohta and Sakai](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.4078) · [Arai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22311)
- Herrera-Aguilar, Rojas and Santos-Rodríguez (2014) find an exactly soluble vector wave potential for a thick de Sitter brane supported by a tachyon field. Its potential tends to $H^2/4$, with a localized photon and a continuum beginning at mass $H/2$. The role of the asymptotic potential is a direct cross-check on the *detuned* HDblast calculation. [Eqs. 18–23](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0999)
- Lu, Guo, Wei and Fu (2024) show how a specified curvature-dependent kinetic coefficient changes the spectrum in a warped thick brane. In their flat-sliced five-dimensional vector example, the critical choice $t_2=b/4$ gives a positive asymptotic wave-potential plateau and can support additional discrete states below the continuum. Their strongly decaying coefficient provides a concrete alternative to the finite-power throat weight used here. [Vector example, Eqs. 89–95](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.11688v3)
- Ruser, Durrer, Vonlanthen and Wittwer (2009) include the velocity term in the normal boundary condition when calculating graviton creation by a moving brane in anti-de Sitter space. This is a methodological precedent for retaining the wall velocity in the Maxwell calculation. Their tensor field and cosmological boundary conditions differ from this transverse classical-wave experiment. Here the reflecting surface moves in a prescribed bulk metric. The normal derivative includes the wall velocity. A self-gravitating wall would also require solving for the metric and matter on both sides. [Moving-boundary equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.3683)
- Kirpichnikov (2013) studies the wall-to-wall photon propagator in models with bulk charged fermions. In his five-dimensional gapped example the one-loop correction has an infrared divergence. His result shows why the charged sector needs a separate consistency test. Our free Maxwell response predicts no charged-particle production or thermalization. [One-loop calculation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.2866)
