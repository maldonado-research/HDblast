# How prior work bears on the calculations

## Preparing a scalar field through the bounce

The time resolution is part of the state specification. Olbermann minimized the free scalar energy density measured by a comoving observer and weighted by the square of a smooth test function in proper time. Within homogeneous isotropic quasifree states on Robertson–Walker spacetime this gives an explicit minimizer for the positive oscillators. He proved that the resulting states have the Hadamard short-distance structure used in local stress renormalization. Martín-Benito, Neves and Olmedo subsequently used low-energy states for perturbations through a loop-quantum-cosmology bounce and compared sampling intervals. HDblast uses a classical positively curved Einstein–scalar background and varies the width of its own physical-density measurement. Its numerical stress differences depend on that choice. [Olbermann, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.2986) · [Martín-Benito, Neves and Olmedo, §§III–V](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.103.123524)

The homogeneous harmonic of a massless minimally coupled scalar on a compact slice needs separate canonical data. Kirsten and Garriga treat it as a free-particle degree of freedom and discuss a formal de Sitter-invariant zero-momentum state and the observables definable in it. The zero-momentum eigenfunction is nonnormalizable on the real field line. The present closed de Sitter comparison uses explicit regular modes for $n\ge1$ and quantifies how a normalizable homogeneous packet's momentum variance contributes to the energy budget. [Kirsten and Garriga, §§III–IV](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.48.567) · [Exact closed de Sitter calculation](checks/STATE_CHECK.md)

## Spatial variation and the scope of a finite calculation

Them and Brum constructed Hadamard low-energy states on compact spatial slices by smearing over a spacetime slab. Equations (7)–(9) assume $h_{ij}(t,\mathbf{x})=c(t)^2h_{ij}(\mathbf{x})$, so the spatial eigenfunctions can be kept fixed while the common scale evolves. Banerjee and Niedermaier proved an anisotropic Hadamard result for spatially homogeneous Bianchi I, including an all-orders short-wavelength expansion. The HDblast first-passage geometries have spatially varying shape evolution and the daughter has a varying kinetic weight. The calculation here minimizes the proper-volume-averaged energy in one coupled angular and radial sector. Establishing a Hadamard state for all its modes would require an argument covering that geometry and field operator. [Them and Brum, §§2–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.3174) · [Banerjee and Niedermaier, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11388v2) · [Finite-sector calculation](patch/PATCH.md)

## Electromagnetic waves on round and uneven slices

The source-free Maxwell action in four dimensions is invariant under a local conformal rescaling. A round closed Friedmann universe is conformal to a static Einstein cylinder, where the transverse oscillators keep fixed conformal frequencies. This gives an exact zero-mixing control for the conformal photon vacuum. On an uneven evolving geometry, the tensor structure of the metric perturbation matters. Garani, Redi and Tesi derived particle-production formulas for perturbations around a conformally flat background, including massless gauge fields. They compare effective-action and Bogoliubov calculations. The HDblast readout compares with a specified instantaneous oscillator basis in a finite interval on a compact slice. Translating that occupation into a late-time photon abundance would require specifying and propagating the later geometry and state. [Garani, Redi and Tesi, §§2–4, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12249v2) · [Compact-sphere electromagnetic spectrum](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.84.104025) · [HDblast transverse Maxwell calculation](maxwell/MAXWELL.md)

A June 2026 preprint by Redi and Tesi gives a further criterion for stochastic sources active at subhorizon scales. In that setting pair production samples the timelike part of the source's unequal-time stress correlation, while gravitational-wave emission samples its lightlike part. The distinction explains why an estimate of geometric distortion or a gravitational-wave amplitude alone cannot set a photon yield. Applying their formula would require the corresponding source correlation and their perturbative regime. [Redi and Tesi, §§2–3, v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28297v1)

## A prepared photon gas and its gravity

Bezerra, Mostepanenko, Mota and Romero computed thermal electromagnetic energies and pressures in a closed universe, including the separate vacuum contribution. In their equation (19), photon energies on a round three-sphere of radius $a$ are $j/a$ with multiplicity $2(j^2-1)$, $j\ge2$, in natural units. Their equation (39) gives the thermal contribution to pressure. Subtracting the same conformal vacuum yields the discrete thermal excess used here. In an expanding round geometry its energy density scales as $a^{-4}$ and its pressure is one third of its density. The occupations are prescribed as initial conditions. Production and thermalization require separate physical mechanisms. [Bezerra et al., §§II–III](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.84.104025)

Ellis and Maartens give the classical positive-curvature Friedmann and acceleration equations for a scalar field plus a separately conserved barotropic fluid. Setting $p=\rho/3$ and initial scalar velocity to zero in their acceleration equation gives $\ddot a/a=(8\pi G/3)(V-\rho_\gamma)$. This explains how enough initial radiation can suppress acceleration at a candidate turning point. The calibrated potential slope and the subsequent radiation fraction have to be obtained from the actual evolution. A surface with zero initial acceleration need not remain static when the potential has a slope. [Ellis and Maartens, §II, equations (3)–(6)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211082)
