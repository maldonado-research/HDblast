### Particle response to a matched pulse

The published positive and negative pulses in the quiet B1 model reach the shell without reaching the phenomenological production event at φ*=0.5. We will test the dynamics of a separate free quantum probe on those already known pulse and quiet histories. Take a conformally coupled spectator scalar in the induced flat Friedmann geometry with m/H₀=100|φ−0.5|, identical specified Gaussian initial state, a(0)=1 and proper clock s=H₀τ. Compare pulse signs and quiet at s=0.15, with a pre-arrival control. Report the dependence of any inferred occupation on how the final positive-frequency mode is chosen. Compute the response of a specified switched detector as an additional comparison.

We will report a finite comoving band with 5 ≤ k/H₀ ≤ 18. Its initial physical frequencies fit below the retained B1 gravitational scale, and the frequencies throughout the interval must be checked. Wider-band results are a distinct continuum sensitivity calculation. As a stationary past/future control, apply the observed perturbation of the scalar mass to an explicitly prescribed oscillator with a smooth switch and test the resulting in/out amplitudes against independent solutions. Its switching protocol defines a different experiment. Instantaneous quanta or detector clicks on prescribed backgrounds do not by themselves determine deposited heat or a thermal state.

Separately we will investigate whether initial data prepared on the *rolling* B1 solution can alter an actual particle event at its original mass crossing while retaining both local gravitational constraints, the quiet comparison away from the perturbation and the original couplings. Any successful restart test will report changes relative to that rolling control, field matching and numerical limitations. The known rolling control already crosses the threshold.
