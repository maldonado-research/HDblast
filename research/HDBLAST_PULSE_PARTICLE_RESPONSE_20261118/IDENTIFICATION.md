# Which observation would distinguish the bulk origin?

Consider any one of the three measured homogeneous histories. Give a four-dimensional spatially flat Friedmann geometry the same proper-time scale factor $a(s)$ as the shell and give a four-dimensional scalar the same prescribed mass $m(s)$. Prepare the same Gaussian state, curvature coupling and units. Then the mode equation, including its initial data, is identical for every comoving wave number. Uniqueness of the linear equation makes the mode functions identical for all times covered. Mode occupations measured in the same basis, field variances and the Wightman function all agree. Every switched detector with the same clock, coupling and spectral filter therefore has the same predicted probability. This conclusion also applies separately to a family of externally controlled pulse histories if the four-dimensional backgrounds and mass drives are supplied for each experiment. The equality is stronger than agreement on one integrated particle count. For conformal curvature coupling the canonical oscillator sees only $k^2+a^2m^2$ as a function of conformal time. A mode transfer alone cannot recover $a$ and $m$ separately. A detector with a measured proper clock and independently calibrated coupling tests additional information because $ds=a\,d\eta$ and $\chi=v/a$. Even with such a detector, the four-dimensional construction above still agrees whenever both functions match. The construction takes $a$ and $m$ as prescribed inputs. It gives no gravitational source, energy budget or explanation for their correlations with an independently launched disturbance. Those dynamical requirements are where a comparison of origin hypotheses can gain leverage. [Earlier project study of four-dimensional alternatives](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24352632)

A future energy-transfer test would evolve the source of $m(\phi)$ alongside the field stress. A consistent renormalization must use the same subtraction conventions for the field energy and the force on the source. For a spatially homogeneous external mass, the energy gained by the field obeys $\dot\rho_\chi+3H(\rho_\chi+p_\chi)=\tfrac12\dot m^2\langle\chi^2\rangle_{\rm ren}$, where $\dot m^2$ denotes $d(m^2)/d\tau$. Energy passed on to daughter fields must also be included. This supplies an explicit conservation test for a proposed origin model. A freely evolved probe and a phenomenological decay ledger can be compared on the same histories but do not, by themselves, solve that coupled system.
