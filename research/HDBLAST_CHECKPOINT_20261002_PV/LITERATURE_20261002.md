# Targeted literature update, 2 October 2026

This is a focused review of renormalization methods useful for the next HDBLAST source calculation, not an exhaustive search of the web. Canonical metadata and relevant in-body passages for the five papers below were retrieved using Firecrawl in this session. The Consensus query did not complete; it supplies no citation or result.

1. **Antonio Ferreiro, Samuel Monin and Francisco Torrenti**, [Physical scale adiabatic regularization in cosmological spacetimes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08986). Submitted 2023; inspected version updated February 2024. Section III, especially Eqs. 48–53, introduces separate scales at different adiabatic orders and constructs conserved stress subtraction while protecting amplified infrared spectra. Section IV, Eqs. 54–55 and surrounding discussion, explicitly fixes renormalization conditions and relates prescription changes to gravitational couplings. The physical mass is fixed in its free-field setup; its formulas are not a completed scalar-dependent HDBLAST source or brane matching calculation.

2. **Antonio Ferreiro and Francisco Torrenti**, [Ultraviolet-regularized power spectrum without infrared distortions in cosmological spacetimes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01078). Submitted 2022; inspected version updated March 2023. Section II, Eqs. 20–22, relates the modified field-square subtraction to local geometric ambiguities; Section III discusses gravitational matching and a trace relation (Eq. 29). This supports controlling ultraviolet subtraction without assuming that an infrared spectral change is a physical prediction. Its main observable is the two-point function for a free field in FLRW, not the present time-dependent scalar mass and source pair.

3. **Antonio Ferreiro and Jose Navarro-Salas**, [Running couplings from adiabatic regularization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05564). Submitted 2018; inspected version updated March 2019. The construction treats m²-mu² as second adiabatic order while keeping an arbitrary reference scale. Eqs. 15–20 identify the finite local stress changes and gravitational terms; Section V explains that scale changes must be accompanied by matched couplings. Its explicit charged-scalar/electromagnetic examples are not identical to a real scalar with x(phi), so source and counterterms are derived here from the declared action instead of transplanted.

4. **Han Lai and Atsuhisa Ota**, [Ultraviolet Structure of Real-time Gravitational Wave Linear Response in a Resonant Scalar Field](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16296), June 2026. This is directly relevant to the pressure issue. Section 5, Eqs. 5.11–5.15, explicitly varies a time-dependent coefficient of R and obtains zero energy-density contribution but nonzero pressure on Minkowski space. Their oscillatory-mass toy model and dimensional regularization differ from our tanh/PV benchmark. Sections 4.3–5 also find a mismatch between linear-response and tadpole counterterms beyond leading adiabatic order for their fixed off-shell background; the paper argues that a covariant, on-shell completion should resolve it. That expectation is not a completed backreaction result. It reinforces the need to derive the shell and matter responses from one common action.

5. **Anamitra Paul and Sonia Paban**, [Stress-Energy Tensor of a Scalar Field on a Product Spacetime with a Time-Dependent Compact Dimension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12444), 2026, [JHEP 09 (2026) 188](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP09(2026)188). Section 3 distinguishes continuum ultraviolet subtraction from discrete compact-dimension sums, preserving topology-dependent Casimir terms. Its approximate WKB prescription is checked in massless conformal limits; general equivalence with point splitting is not demonstrated. The introduction and Section 6 defer cosmological implications and identify missing matter contributions. This is a useful future extra-dimensional vacuum-polarization lead, but its FLRW×S1 topology and adiabatic approximation differ from a dynamical HDBLAST shell and from a nonadiabatic zero crossing.

## Implications for this checkpoint

A positive fixed reference scale is compatible with a local covariant renormalization framework, but its finite terms are a matching choice. It does not by itself determine an observed cosmological constant or validate the hypothesis. Our numerical benchmark uses covariant Pauli–Villars matching and derives both stress and the scalar source from one prescription.

The papers motivate the later FRW extension. They do not establish our particular pressure coefficient, exact pulse state or numerical result; those are derived and checked explicitly in THEORY_AND_MATCHING.md and the executable controls. In particular, one must vary F(x)R before setting the metric flat. A zero value of R in the background does not imply zero pressure variation.

The previously verified [Molina-París–Anderson–Ramsey scalar-work analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9908037) and [Markkanen–Tranberg finite-counterterm construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.0180) remain relevant. Their scope and limitations are recorded in the [1 October review](https://github.com/maldonado-research/HDblast/blob/research/20261001-quantum-source/research/HDBLAST_CHECKPOINT_20261001_QFT/LITERATURE_AND_RENORMALIZATION.md).

No observational support, external novelty or new cosmological solution follows from this literature update.

The pressure-counterterm and trace derivations in this checkpoint were completed before the targeted June 2026 paper was retrieved. Agreement is a consistency check against existing work, not a claim of priority. The search covered methods and selected 2025–2026 papers; it cannot establish the absence of other relevant work.
