# A compatibility condition across independent source preparations

A nonlinear conformal metric and scalar readout can reproduce the scalar Gauss identity through quadratic order. There is a further test if the *same* theory is required to obey that identity on an open family of independent preparations. The distinction between one prepared solution and an open set of local field values and gradients is essential.

Take at least three real, canonically normalized Einstein-frame scalar fields with positive kinetic metric $\delta_{ij}$. Use smooth algebraic readouts $h_J=L(\psi)^2g_E$, $\Phi=f(\psi)$ with $L(0)=1$, $L>0$ and $f(0)=\phi_b$. Put $b_i=\partial_i\log L(0)$ and $d_i=\partial_i f(0)$. The gravitational coupling is $\kappa_4>0$. The matter probes have negligible stress. On shell, the Jordan-frame residual $\mathcal C=R(h_J)-|D\Phi|_J^2-F(\Phi)$ has gradient part
$$
 L^{-2}\sum_{ij} A_{ij}(\psi)\,\partial_a\psi_i\,\partial^a\psi_j,
 \qquad A_{ij}=\kappa_4\delta_{ij}-6\,L_{,ij}/L-f_{,i}f_{,j}.
$$
Indeed $\Box\log L+|D\log L|^2=\Box L/L$, and the Klein–Gordon equations eliminate $\Box_E\psi_i$ in favor of potential derivatives. The remaining terms only depend on the field values. Work in a local orthonormal frame; a freely prescribed spacelike gradient suffices to test the quadratic form. At fixed field values, subtracting the zero-gradient case removes the other terms. Polarization recovers the off-diagonal entries of $A$.

If the Gauss identity is to hold through quadratic order for arbitrary independent field values and gradients about zero, $A(0)=0$. A cubic-order match for all those preparations additionally needs $\partial_k A_{ij}(0)=0$. These conditions are necessary even if the potential and the algebraic part of the residual are tuned. Smoothness makes the third derivative tensor of $L$ symmetric. Choose orthonormal species-space vectors $X,Z$ perpendicular to $d$. From $A(0)=0$ obtain $6L_{,XX}(0)=\kappa_4$ and $6L_{,ZX}(0)=0$. Differentiate $A$ and antisymmetrize to get
$$
 \partial_Z A_{XX}(0)-\partial_X A_{ZX}(0)=\kappa_4\, b(Z).
$$
The third derivatives of $L$ cancel. Every remaining term from differentiating $f_{,i}f_{,j}$ has a factor $d(X)$ or $d(Z)$. The derivative of the external $L^{-2}$ factor likewise drops out because $A(0)=0$. Thus at least one of these two independent gradient-response coefficients has magnitude at least $\kappa_4 |b(Z)|/2$.

When $\dim\ker d\ge 2$ and $d\ne0$, take $Z$ parallel to the component of $b$ perpendicular to $d$ and $X$ perpendicular to both. A cubic-order match for all independent preparations therefore requires $b$ to be proportional to $d$. The conclusion holds on any three-species subspace used as independent controls. In the linear four-dimensional metric replica, $b_j/d_j=-K/[6(M_j^2+4/\rho_b^2)]$. These ratios differ at distinct masses with nonzero detector overlap. The compact-collar construction has simple eigenvalues and nonvanishing endpoint projections, so three such species exist. Noncollinearity already follows on that finite subspace. No choice of third derivatives of the same smooth algebraic readouts removes the discrepancy across this family. More generally, suppose the linear part of the Gauss law also holds for independent excitations in a four-dimensional model of this class. In a mass eigenbasis it requires $-(2R_0+6M_j^2)b_j=K d_j$ separately. If cubic compatibility forces $b$ proportional to $d$ and $K\ne0$, every excited species with $d_j\ne0$ must have the same mass squared. Their linear combination then solves one constant-mass Klein–Gordon equation, which fails the two registered scalar waveform tests. For one or two finite species at arbitrary masses, a finite solution sum is analytic in this clock and cannot be zero on an open interval and nonzero later. These conclusions use the demand for an identity on independent preparations when there are at least three species. They do not address models allowed to correlate the available initial conditions from the outset.

The independent local fields and gradients are a prospective set of controls. This proof does not construct them from the existing shell's single radial packet or identify them with an astronomical measurement. It gives no cubic mismatch for that particular trajectory. Quantitative calibration requires the actual canonical couplings and the ability to vary the indicated directions. Noncanonical field-space geometry, additional stress, higher-derivative dynamics or a metric depending on field gradients would require deriving their own coefficient tensor.
