# Opposite null Ricci signs at one shell observer

Consider the material observer whose original sphere label is $n_*=(0,0,1,0)$ in the registered $S^3\subset\mathbb R^4$. At this label $Y=n_1^2-n_2^2=0$ and $DY=0$. Take reference unit tangent vectors along increasing $n_1$ and $n_2$. At zero amplitude the sphere Hessian of $Y$ on those directions is respectively $+2$ and $-2$. For example, along the first great circle $Y(s)=\sin^2 s$, and along the second $Y(s)=-\sin^2 s$. The third tangent direction, along $n_4$, has zero Hessian. Thus this observer has zero linear scalar amplitude at its own label, although the *cut's* scalar quadrupole is nonzero.

Let $u_\epsilon$ be the observer's unit timelike tangent. Transport the two sphere label tangents to its actual proper cut using the registered observer map, and orthonormalize them with the induced cut metric, starting with the first. These spatial vectors are perpendicular to $u_\epsilon$ by Gauss orthogonality of the normal flow. The two resulting $k_{i,\epsilon}=u_\epsilon+e_{i,\epsilon}$ are true intrinsic null directions with $|h_\epsilon(u_\epsilon,k_{i,\epsilon})|=1$. Either consistent orientation of time gives the same signs below. This is a curvature measurement at each event on a timelike observer's history. It does not average along a null geodesic.

Write $\mathcal R_{i,\epsilon}=\operatorname{Ric}[h_\epsilon](k_{i,\epsilon},k_{i,\epsilon})$. The contracted quantity equals $J[h_\epsilon](k_{i,\epsilon},k_{i,\epsilon})$ with $J=\operatorname{Ric}-Rh/4$ because $k_{i,\epsilon}$ is null in the **actual** metric. The de Sitter background has $J_0=0$, so perturbations of the null vector and changes of event labels drop out of its first variation. It would in general be incorrect to vary Ricci alone and hold a background null direction fixed. At $n_*$, the $qq$ and $qi$ components of the Hessian of $UY$ vanish because $Y$ and $DY$ both vanish there. Its spatial unit components have the factor $(\rho_b\cosh q)^{-2}$. The linear Ricci formula therefore gives
$$
 \mathcal R'_{1,0}(x)=-\frac{4U(\ell x)}{\rho_b^2\cosh^2(\ell x)},\qquad
 \mathcal R'_{2,0}(x)=+\frac{4U(\ell x)}{\rho_b^2\cosh^2(\ell x)} .
$$
All first derivatives here are with respect to the canonical signed perturbation parameter. Multiplying the two derivatives by the common positive model normalization $\mu\rho_b^4/(4\ell)$ gives opposite coefficients $\mp M(x)\operatorname{sech}^2(\ell x)$. Since $1-(\ell X)^2\le\operatorname{sech}^2(\ell x)\le1$ on $0\le x\le X=25/16$, the authenticated pointwise bounds imply strictly positive absolute margins throughout the two windows.

For all sufficiently small positive nonzero parameter values, the *actual* $\mathcal R_{1,\epsilon}$ is negative and $\mathcal R_{2,\epsilon}$ positive throughout both windows. For all sufficiently small negative parameter values the signs exchange. To pass from the derivative to this statement, the prior physical parameter theorem gives differentiability into a norm controlling two spacetime derivatives, and the common observer theorem specifically gives the moved curvature, label table and cut metric $C^2$ in the parameter into joint $C^3$. The frame construction is smooth near the nondegenerate background frame. ## What this tests in four-dimensional gravity

For an intrinsic four-dimensional Einstein metric with positive Newton constant $G_4$ and any cosmological constant, contraction with its own null vector gives $\operatorname{Ric}(k,k)=8\pi G_4 T(k,k)$. Any exact local isometry preserves that value, with a mapped null direction. Consequently no such metric sourced by matter satisfying the pointwise null energy condition in every null direction can reproduce the predicted *intrinsic metric* in this region for the sufficiently small nonzero parameters just described. This argument does not require common source/detector couplings, independent priors on field masses or energy, or a smooth dependence of the competing matter on the perturbation parameter. Canonical minimally coupled scalar fields with positive kinetic form obey the condition since their null stress is a sum of squares. A different theory allowing negative effective null stress or modified gravitational field equations remains possible. A collection of probe fields that only reproduces the linear scalar is still a valid explanation for that scalar alone.

The higher-dimensional Einstein–scalar bulk has $\operatorname{Ric}^{(5)}(k,k)=(k\phi)^2\ge0$ for bulk-null $k$. The Gauss relation includes the normal electric Weyl term in the intrinsic null projection. In the model conventions it reads $\operatorname{Ric}^{(4)}(k,k)=\tfrac23(k\Phi)^2-E^{(5)}(k,k)$. Thus intrinsic defocusing is compatible with the bulk null energy condition. The positive curvature sign in the other null direction, negative Ricci convergence in one local direction, and scalar signal are all predictions of a local prescribed packet. They imply no singularity theorem violation, global bounce, or observed event at the Big Bang.
