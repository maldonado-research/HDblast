# Which curvature information reaches a brane observer?

**The projected field equation and its trace have substantial precedents.** Shiromizu, Maeda and Sasaki derived the induced Einstein equation from the Gauss relation. The electric projection of the bulk Weyl tensor contributes to the induced Ricci tensor but its trace vanishes. Maeda and Wands included a canonical bulk scalar and a scalar-dependent tension. With no additional brane matter, tracing their Eqs. (20)–(22) gives $R_4=\kappa_5^2(D\Phi)^2+4\Lambda_4(\Phi)$. In their notation $\Lambda_4=\kappa_5^2[\Lambda_5+\kappa_5^2\lambda^2/6-\lambda_{,\Phi}^2/8]/2$. Substitution of $\phi=\kappa_5\Phi$, $U=\kappa_5^2\Lambda_5$ and $\sigma=\kappa_5^2\lambda$ gives the shell trace function $(D\phi)^2+2U+\sigma^2/3-\sigma_{,\phi}^2/4$. Reversing the normal changes the signs of both first-order junction conditions consistently. The registered packet and its calculated coefficients supply the particular waveform to which the trace identity is applied here. [Shiromizu et al., Eqs. (3), (8), (9)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.62.024012) · [Maeda and Wands, Eqs. (17)–(22)](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0008188v2#S2.E22)

**Bulk and intrinsic null convergence can have different signs.** Kraus gives an explicit family of moving, positively tensioned walls in five-dimensional anti-de Sitter black-hole geometries. For two identical sides his Eqs. (14)–(18) give $H^2+k/a^2=C+\mu/a^4$ with constant $C$. A hyperbolic spatial section ($k=-1$) admits $-L^2/4<\mu<0$, where $L$ is the bulk curvature radius. On an expanding segment, differentiation gives $R^{(4)}_{ab}l^al^b=-2(\dot H-k/a^2)=4\mu/a^4<0$ for a null vector normalized by $l^\tau=1$. The vacuum bulk has $R^{(5)}_{AB}N^AN^B=0$ for every null $N$ and the wall tension has zero tangential null contraction. This example follows directly from the published homogeneous wall equations. Its dark-radiation term and hyperbolic geometry differ from the local quadrupolar scalar packet. [Kraus, §§2–2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/9910149v2#S2.E18)

**The physical metric matters for a null-convergence test.** Chatterjee, Easson and Parikh start with an Einstein-frame canonical scalar and perform a regular field-dependent conformal transformation. Equations (7)–(10) display a second-derivative contribution to the Jordan-frame stress and explain how the transformed energy condition differs from a bound on the ordinary Jordan-metric null Ricci contraction. At linear order about a constant scalar, a Hessian term can have either sign even when the canonical scalar's Einstein-frame null stress starts at quadratic order. Physical conclusions require specifying the metric that transports clocks and light along with its matter couplings. [Chatterjee et al., §2](https://doi.org/10.1088/0264-9381/30/23/235031)

**Recent gravity tests illustrate two distinct observables.** Radhakrishnan and colleagues directly derive the source-free massive wave equation for the Ricci scalar perturbation in four-dimensional metric $f(R)$ gravity on de Sitter space. This gives an explicit competing equation for the shell Ricci waveform. Imam and Lagos analyzed GW170817 detector strain with an added scalar breathing polarization and electromagnetic information on the binary, including the polarization angle from the gamma-ray-burst afterglow. Their Bayesian evidence favored the GR template over their extended template. Their measured combination of strain polarizations is specific to a binary source, transfer through spacetime and a detector network. Applying such data to the shell would require a source-to-strain prediction and independent identification of the observed metric. [Metric curvature equation, Eqs. (35)–(37)](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01028v1#S2.E37) · [GW170817 analysis, §§II–IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24526v2#S2)
