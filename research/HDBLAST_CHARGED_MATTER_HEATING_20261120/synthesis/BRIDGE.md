# Can the calculated quantum pairs supply the classical carriers?

The successful fluid calculation specifies a cold mass density and a charge-to-mass ratio. The quantum calculation starts in vacuum and counts excitations in a specified range of modes. Equating their populations requires an energy scale and a choice of particle species. The following check makes those choices explicit. ## Put the two energies in the same units

Let $L$ be the bulk curvature radius in metres, $\sigma$ the wall tension as energy per proper three-volume, and $S_0=\sigma L^4/(\hbar c)$. It measures the classical action in units of a quantum of action. Put $\mu=mcL/\hbar$ for a particle of mass $m$. The one-species gauge phase involves $qA/\sqrt3$ because the archived electromagnetic amplitude describes three equal gauge fields. The classical force parameter for a species charged along one of them is

$$Q=\frac{q}{\sqrt3\,\mu}.$$

A closed wall has volume $2\pi^2(aL)^3$. A quantum with conformal frequency $\omega$ has energy $\hbar c\omega/(aL)$ in the free instantaneous basis. For a band whose conformal excitation energy $\mathcal E=\sum\omega N$ includes both charge signs, its energy density in tension units is $\mathcal E/(2\pi^2a^4 S_0)$. At the neutral wall's late potential zero the exact modes $0\le n\le32$ at $q=1$ have $\mathcal E=0.05109$. In that finite-time basis this is $5.97\times10^{-5}/S_0$ of the coherent electromagnetic energy, for **one** complex species. The electric field is still present there. An asymptotic particle count requires a specified readout, and an all-frequency quantum stress needs a renormalization prescription. [Quantum experiment](../quantum/PAIRS.md)

For a total initial cold rest density $D_i$ in tension units, the prescribed number of individual carriers is $N_i=2\pi^2a_i^3 D_i S_0/\mu$. The factor already includes every species and both signs when $D_i$ is their total. Set $q=1$ as in one quantum test and match the fluid's primary $Q=10^8$. This gives $\mu=5.77\times10^{-9}$ and $N_i=2.74\times10^6 S_0$ at $a_i=2$ and $D_i=10^{-4}$. The two externally smoothed quantum readouts give around 0.0098 expected individual quanta **across three hypothetical independent species** in the exact low band. These are counts at different times with different preparations. Shorter waves add pairs, and mechanisms that produce additional matter need their own calculation. The study supplies the cold precursor independently. [Coupled gas](../coupled/COUPLED.md)

## A consistency condition for one proposed species

An additional constraint comes from the finite radius of the sphere. For a conformally coupled scalar at the initial stationary wall with zero gauge field, its lowest free mode has energy $\hbar c\sqrt{\mu^2+a_i^{-2}}/L$. The ratio to its rest energy is $\sqrt{1+(a_i\mu)^{-2}}$. At the mass inferred above, this ratio is about **87 million**. Treating quanta of that field as the initial pressureless carriers would require explaining their very different energy per carrier.

For scale, allow the lowest mode to cost up to **10% more** than rest energy. This illustrative tolerance requires $\mu\ge 1/[a_i\sqrt{1.1^2-1}]=1.091$. Keeping $Q=10^8$ then requires $q\ge1.89\times10^8$ in the phase convention of this calculation. On a locally static wall where its own kinetic term dominates the electric response, $\alpha=q^2/(4\pi S_0K)$. Requiring $\alpha<1$ for that charged species at the initial $K=0.001$ would further require $S_0>2.84\times10^{18}$ at the minimum mass. Neither $S_0$ nor the microscopic charge is measured here. Bulk propagation can modify the charge response at long wavelengths, and the initial precursors in the fluid experiment are neutral. These conditions apply to the proposed common conformal-scalar identification. A different species, production epoch or microscopic mechanism would change them.
