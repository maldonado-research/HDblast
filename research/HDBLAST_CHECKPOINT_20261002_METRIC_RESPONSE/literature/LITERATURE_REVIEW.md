# Focused sources for homogeneous semiclassical metric response

Reviewed 2 October 2026 UTC. This bounded methods audit follows the matched
minimal stress checkpoint at `b807a4d549a40bc78b66122cb5719987091d2ce7`.
It refreshed public bibliographic and version records, reread metric-specific
passages in three hash-verified inherited primary PDFs, and downloaded and
read selected passages in two additional primary PDFs. Eight one-page
INSPIRE searches screened titles; selected nearby abstracts were read.
Article bodies, extracted text, and page renders are outside the public
payload. This is an internal AI-assisted source review, not an external
referee report or an exhaustive survey.

The actionable result is a stricter specification for the missing metric
response. A scalar mass-source response on a prescribed scale factor does
not supply the response to changing that metric. The latter requires the
explicit metric dependence of the stress operator, local terms from the
same finite action, the declared state, and a linearized conservation
identity that includes the baseline stress and connection variations.

## Primary passage checks

**Paul R. Anderson, Carmen Molina-París and Emil Mottola,
Cosmological Horizon Modes and Linear Response in de Sitter Spacetime,
[0907.0823v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.0823v2), 22 October 2009;
[Phys. Rev. D 80, 084005](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.80.084005).**
Printed pp.4–6, Eqs.1.2–1.3, were reread: the metric-induced stress response
contains a retarded stress-tensor commutator and local contacts, with
independent changes in state treated separately. First and second
variations use the same renormalized effective action and state. The
validity criterion concerns bounded gauge-invariant solutions of the
coupled linearized equations, rather than a stationary Hessian or a
scalar-only susceptibility.

Additional passages read here are printed pp.19–21, Eqs.3.13–3.22, and
pp.33–34, Eqs.6.1–6.9. The reduction to the time-time component and trace
uses a self-consistent **total** background equation before invoking
conservation. It is not a license to impose the same reduced homogeneous
identity on a nonzero matter stress alone. Their gauge-invariant curvature
perturbation is useful for designing a coordinate-transformation control.
Their explicit anomaly and horizon-mode results concern conformal matter;
the massive minimal reference with plane-wave canonical modes is a
different stress operator and theory. No state-mode coefficient or
stability conclusion is transferred.

**Guillem Pérez-Nadal, Albert Roura and Enric Verdaguer,
Stability of de Sitter spacetime under isotropic perturbations in
semiclassical gravity,
[0712.2282v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.2282v1), 14 December 2007;
[Phys. Rev. D 77, 124033](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.77.124033).**
Printed pp.3–4, Eqs.1–7, and pp.8–9, Eqs.32–40, were reread. Rescaling the
field turns the homogeneous geometry into a quadratic canonical-mode
source, while the preparation geometry fixes the initial state. The
one-sided finite-part kernel combines its singular integral and local
term before taking the regulator limit. The initial state and subsequent
geometry must be matched through the required derivatives to avoid
spurious initial-time singularities.

Additional passages read here are printed pp.11–12, Eqs.48–51. Their
semiclassical Friedmann equation contains higher derivatives and a state
history. Selecting the perturbative low-energy branch requires the
explicit hierarchy `l_p H << 1`; the initial conditions are constrained
by the state preparation. Their expansion in a massless nonconformal
coupling is not a massive-minimal general kernel, and their stable
isotropic solution does not establish stability of the HDBLAST shell.

**Markus B. Fröb, Demetrios B. Papadopoulos, Albert Roura and Enric
Verdaguer, Nonperturbative semiclassical stability of de Sitter spacetime
for small metric deviations,
[1301.5261v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.5261v1), 22 January 2013;
[Phys. Rev. D 87, 064019](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.87.064019).**
This primary PDF was newly downloaded for this audit. Its arXiv stamp and
version history identify the 2013 version; the served PDF also carries an
April 2022 dateline, recorded separately in the metadata. Printed pp.3–4,
Sec.II.B and Eqs.3–4, describe order reduction within a specified
effective-field-theory expansion. Printed p.4 explicitly cautions that
integro-differential equations can make order reduction ambiguous and
that reducing time and spatial derivatives inconsistently can break
covariance. The page was visually inspected. Printed pp.7–8,
Secs.IV.C–V.A and Eqs.25–32, explain why their nonlocal terms drop out at
the retained order and why their constrained scalar and vector solutions
are pure gauge in the conformal-matter model. That disappearance is
model- and order-dependent; it does not justify discarding the nonlocal
massive-minimal response or an inconvenient mode in a large-correction
branch. Their exact solutions solve the order-reduced equations, not
untruncated quantum gravity.

**Giordano Cintia, Gravitational Backreaction in de Sitter: A Canonical
ADM Approach,
[2609.03008v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03008v1), 2 September 2026.**
This is the new recent primary source selected here. Printed pp.7–8,
Eqs.3–12, display the lapse and shift constraints alongside spatial metric
evolution. In particular the initial expansion rate is fixed by the
Hamiltonian constraint, rather than supplied as an independent condition.
Printed pp.12–15, Eqs.36–56 and Sec.3.5, explain that the hard-cutoff
background equations initially violate their Bianchi relations. Their
specific nongeometric counterterms restore those relations; printed p.13
was visually inspected. A fixed physical cutoff becomes a time-dependent
comoving upper limit, so differentiation generates boundary terms.

Two limitations matter for use here. First, their massless minimal scalar
and quantized tensor modes, hard-cutoff prescription and background
equations differ from our already-subtracted massive minimal scalar
response at a fixed comoving numerical regulator. Their coefficients and
preferred cutoff cannot be inserted into our finite action. Second,
printed p.16, Eqs.61–62, explicitly leaves the possible finite effect of
cutoff-surface terms for future investigation. This audit does not certify
their regulator construction as a complete renormalized metric benchmark.
Printed pp.17–18, Eqs.63–70, show how a gauge condition imposed after a
background/fluctuation split changes the slicing of the metric one-point
function. These passages support checking the constraint and physical
curvature, rather than interpreting every change of a coordinate scale
factor as a physical secular instability.

**Han Lai and Atsuhisa Ota, Ultraviolet Structure of Real-time
Gravitational Wave Linear Response in a Resonant Scalar Field,
[2606.16296v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16296v1), 15 June 2026.**
Printed pp.16–17, Eqs.5.15–5.22, were reread in the hash-verified inherited
PDF. The example shows that a time-dependent curvature counterterm can
contribute to pressure while its density contribution vanishes. The
authors compare the stress tadpole and tensor response in an off-shell
resonant toy background, then identify a covariant dynamical completion
as necessary to relate them consistently. Their tensor coefficient is
not a contact coefficient for the present homogeneous massive minimal
problem. An external mass treated covariantly as a scalar source retains
a sourced Ward identity; its source work must remain explicit. An
off-shell prescribed-metric matter calculation is also not a solution of
the full Einstein or Israel equations.

## Consequences for the next benchmark

1. Fix the common finite action and retain its positive reference during
   metric variation. Changing a subtraction convention after evaluating
   a response changes the benchmark rather than repairs it.
2. Derive the mode forcing from the perturbed minimally coupled canonical
   equation, and derive stress variations from the physical operator.
   Reusing a variance memory kernel can be useful only after this mapping
   and all operator/contact variations have been established.
3. Evaluate density and pressure independently of the Ward ledger. Check
   the linearized matter Ward identity including baseline and connection
   terms. Conservation of a fully coupled solution additionally requires
   the dynamical source and gravitational constraints.
4. Declare the metric parametrization, time slicing and state preparation.
   Test a pure-coordinate perturbation with the corresponding operator,
   state and regulator transformations. At a finite numerical band, use
   its actual reference terms before taking the removed-cutoff limit.
5. Separate the `x=2 H^2` plane-wave calibration from the actual shifted
   stationary root. A passed special-point metric benchmark would not
   establish that root's propagator, coupled evolution or stability.

These are proposed controls and scope conditions, not results of a new
metric experiment. New physical integrations need their own prior public
registration, independent route, grids, tail budgets and gates.
