# What existing reheating work can test here

Felder, Kofman and Linde showed that in a model with a field-dependent daughter mass, a sufficiently nonadiabatic first passage can create particles and later decays can redistribute their energy. Kofman, Linde and Starobinsky also studied amplification over multiple passages and the effect of expansion and backreaction. The present matter kinetic factor and the potential are fixed by different HDblast assumptions. The existence of a first passage motivates an explicit daughter calculation. It supplies neither an instant-preheating rate nor a constant decay width for a moving patch. [Instant preheating](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9812289) · [Reheating after inflation](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9704452)

In recent lattice work on trilinear couplings the transition to fluid equations was justified using the measured equations of state and small interaction energy near the end of the simulation. The authors then initialized the fluid evolution with measured, oscillation-averaged densities. Mukaida and Tsuji's 2026 preprint explicitly assumes a nearly homogeneous oscillation with a period short relative to the expansion and decay times. They treat the later thermalization of hard decay products using additional interactions. These are concrete conditions that a cosmological reheating calculation must examine. [Trilinear reheating, §V](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13465#S5) · [Reheating beyond instantaneous thermalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01480) · [Kinetic thermalization model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14054)

A review of reheating emphasizes the distinction between radiation-like bulk behavior and equilibrium. Occupation of a finite set of modes or the energy supplied by a prescribed pump says little about temperature without a scattering and number-changing model. The static calculation in this study has explicit in/out frequencies. The dynamical calculation instead declares a finite-dimensional canonical Hamiltonian and compares its instantaneous ground states. The two results have different operational meanings. A study of adiabatic bases in preheating shows explicitly how intermediate-time particle counts depend on the chosen modes. That dependence motivates publishing the field variable, state and homogeneous control alongside every dynamical count here. [Adiabatic basis dependence in preheating](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06111) · [2025 review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19980)
