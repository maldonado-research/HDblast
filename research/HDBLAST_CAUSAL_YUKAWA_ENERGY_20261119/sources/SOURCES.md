# Where does the energy in a fermion pair come from?

A late-time detector can count outgoing pairs and weight each pair by its energy. Identifying which degree of freedom supplied that energy calls for an additional calculation of the parent, the interaction and each time-dependent input to the Hamiltonian. The references below supply independent checks for this calculation.

## Pair spectrum and its causal response

Boyanovsky and Herring derive the one-loop self-energy for a scalar coupled to massless Dirac fermions in a spatially flat expanding universe. Their Eqs. (IV.1)–(IV.3) place the spectral density under the measure $d\omega/(2\pi)$. In the normalization used here, the resulting weight is $K_q(\omega)=(\omega^2-q^2)\Theta(\omega-q)/(8\pi^2)$. Both $q$ and $\omega$ are comoving. The threshold counts only pairs whose total four-momentum is timelike or null. The retarded response and emitted pairs use the same spectral weight. The paper also exhibits ultraviolet dependence associated with dressing the initially bare scalar, even though its late-time decay law has a finite interpretation. [Boyanovsky and Herring, Secs. IV–V](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12343#S4)

Fleming, Roura and Hu give the antisymmetric and symmetric kernels of a harmonic reservoir in Eqs. (4)–(6). Both follow from the single measure $\sum_j c_j^2\delta(\omega-\omega_j)/(2m_j\omega_j)$. Choosing unit oscillator mass and $c_j^2=2\omega_j K_q(\omega_j)\Delta\omega_j$ reproduces the regulated vacuum two-point measure of the fermion bilinear. Equality of these correlators suffices for second-order perturbation theory with a linear coupling to that bilinear. Beyond this order the true fermion field also has exchange correlations and occupation restrictions. Aurell's Eqs. (29)–(32) show in a driven classical oscillator model how varying the coupling adds work through the explicit time derivatives of the interaction and compensation term. Here the corresponding quantum identity is $d\langle H\rangle/ds=\langle\partial_sH\rangle$. Turning off the interaction at the endpoint still leaves the work accumulated during the switch. [Fleming, Roura and Hu, Sec. II](https://arxiv.org/html/1004.1603#S2) · [Aurell, Sec. IV](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.07811#S4)

## What a finite energy balance establishes

Del Río, Ferreiro, Navarro-Salas and Torrenti construct the renormalized Dirac stress and scalar bilinear for a homogeneous classical Yukawa background in four-dimensional FLRW spacetime. Their Eqs. (92)–(94) display scalar-potential and curvature-dependent terms, including $R\Phi^2$. This gives a concrete benchmark for a continuum stress tensor. A subtraction fixed by the static response of a finite oscillator reservoir leaves the derivative and curvature matching conditions to be determined for the shell theory. [Del Río et al., Secs. IV–V](https://arxiv.org/html/1703.00908#S5)

Herring and Boyanovsky revisit energy transfer in a scalar condensate at large fermion flavor number in Minkowski spacetime. Equation (III.82) expresses total energy through the condensate kinetic term, a potential including fermion vacuum contributions, and the positive energy-weighted fermion occupations. Using the fermion mode equations to differentiate the last term gives the backreaction in the condensate equation. Their analysis shows why a static effective potential misses dynamical field renormalization. The common test for HDblast is whether the independently evolved parent and the energy-weighted outgoing spectrum satisfy one work equation. The calculation here also has a prescribed expanding geometry, a varying parent mass and an explicit switching function. [Herring and Boyanovsky, revised February 2026, Eq. (III.82)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22465v2#S3.E82)

## Recent comparisons and competing mechanisms

- Logan, Stolarski and Yasin study production during coherent post-inflationary oscillations. They report a regime with an approximately half-filled Fermi sphere and a weak-coupling regime dominated by resonance peaks. Their Appendix F assesses the neglect of backreaction by estimating $\rho_\psi/\rho_\varphi$ for their chosen potential and couplings. Applying that test to the shell would require physical couplings and energy scales for the HDblast system. [October 2026 revision, Appendix F](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07326v3#A6)
- Araz, Forshaw and Jenkinson use tensor-network emulation of a proposed device to study one-dimensional spatial lattice Yukawa dynamics. In their finite models, the scalar decay width and scattering resonance position stabilize at different scalar oscillator occupation cutoffs. This supplies a concrete reason to check each observable under its own numerical refinement. Their cutoff limits local scalar occupation. The present study limits the total comoving energy of daughter pairs. [30 October 2026 preprint, Sec. V](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00201v1#S5)
- Cléry, Kachanovich and Tytgat calculate a different route to dark fermions during four-dimensional reheating. In dark QED the trace anomaly permits production via a real and a virtual dark photon, with the virtual photon converting to a fermion pair. A yield measurement alone would leave this and other energy sources to distinguish. This paper has a dark gauge sector and massive fermionic dark matter. Its relic-abundance estimates rely on those ingredients. [13 November 2026 preprint, Sec. IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18337v1#S4)
