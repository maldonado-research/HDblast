# Sources for driven scalar parents and directional daughter production

## What the latest HDblast archive already tests

The public HDblast head on 21 November 2026 is `dc99ff5c5894abcff3e11e02e4b97e19da31de00`. PR 191 drives an initially unexcited neutral field using a moving potential minimum. PR 192 joins this mechanism to a phenomenological decay and charged-fluid evolution, including energy transferred from the driver, redshift work, power lost by the parent and electric work on the daughters. It finds hot growth by a factor 2.89 for its selected reference parameters. A microscopic daughter model sees far more stringent supply constraints than the fluid law imposes. The open issue relevant to this calculation is whether a specified isotropic source can populate each direction at the requested rate and how repeated shell passages affect the available states. ## Which microscopic results carry over?

- **Birth directions and occupation.** Cho, Choi, Joh and Seto give the spin-summed scalar Yukawa matrix element in Eq. (7). At fixed scalar mass its dependence on masses alone makes the vacuum rest-frame differential width uniform in solid angle. Eqs. (5)–(12) give the differential collision term for opposite daughter momenta. For a highly occupied scalar and matched particle/antiparticle distributions the net factor is `(1−f)²−f² = 1−2f`. The first product accounts for the two outgoing vacancies and the second for the inverse process. A nonzero magnetic background can select different modes or matrix elements. The vacuum angular law is an input to a continuum test. [Cho et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08229v2)
- **A shell-crossing benchmark.** Moroi and Yin derive production by a homogeneous oscillating scalar with comoving momentum held fixed. Their Eqs. (2.5)–(2.13) give a delta-function rest-frame momentum and a crossing depth proportional to the inverse speed through the shell. An initially empty fermion state reaches `(1−exp(−2 f̄))/2` in their isotropic Markov description. The factor of two for two daughters, and whether the width counts one or all available internal channels, matter when using it as a numerical check. Cho et al. explicitly display a coherent resonant crossing for which the QFT occupation approaches one while the Markov prediction approaches one-half (Fig. 6 and Eqs. (73)–(76)). Pauli exclusion imposes one per individual state. [Moroi and Yin](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.09475v2) · [Cho et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08229v2)
- **Electromagnetic transport and bandwidth.** Aleksandrov, Kudlis and Klochai identify the instantaneous kinetic momentum as the conserved canonical momentum minus the charge times the homogeneous vector potential. Their spin-resolved kinetic equations also illustrate which information a scalar occupation can lose. They study vacuum production by an electric field in flat spacetime. Boyanovsky and Herring evolve a Yukawa-coupled scalar with a finite preparation time in expansion and find history-dependent corrections to a local exponential width. The bandwidth and angular source of one charged-wall model still require its interaction history and background. [Aleksandrov et al., Sec. II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17204v2) · [Boyanovsky and Herring](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12343v2)
- **Collisions and feedback.** Ala-Mattinen et al. evolve momentum distributions with elastic and inverse channels. Their comparison of exact and approximate relaxation terms shows why a fluid drag coefficient alone cannot specify how quickly a narrow angular or radial cell is cleared. Bhusal et al. compare perturbative scalar decay and Dirac evolution in a quartic reheating model and find parameter-dependent Pauli suppression and resonance. Herring and Boyanovsky derive an energy-conserving evolution for a Yukawa condensate and produced fermions in a large-species treatment. These studies identify how to test feedback and depletion. None evaluates the two archived HDblast histories. [Ala-Mattinen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.06456v2) · [Bhusal et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.16203v2) · [Herring and Boyanovsky](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.22465v2)
- **Parent excitation.** The excitation energy of a displaced harmonic oscillator is controlled by the Fourier component of the trap acceleration at its natural frequency. A cosmological example explicitly obtains coherent modulus motion from a time-dependent linear source when the minimum shifts rapidly. Garcia Garcia et al. compute scalar production from a field coupled linearly to a spherical bubble profile, using the ambient field’s spectrum and estimating backreaction. Those dimensionality and source choices differ from a neutral zero mode confined to a closed three-sphere. [Guéry-Odelin and Muga, App. B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.4957v2) · [Hagihara et al., Sec. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.05002v2) · [Garcia Garcia et al., Secs. 2 and 3.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.02729v1)

## Spin average for elastic scattering through an isolated scalar pole

The Particle Data Group gives the peak Breit–Wigner cross section as the product of the incident spin average, the partial-wave scale and the two branching fractions,

$$
\sigma_{i\to f}(E_0)=
\frac{2J+1}{(2s_1+1)(2s_2+1)}\frac{4\pi}{k^2} B_i B_f .
$$

Here $k$ is the incident center-of-mass momentum. For an unpolarized massive Dirac fermion and antifermion incident on a spin-zero pole, the prefactor is $1/4$. The peak elastic cross section for a specified channel $a$ is $\pi B_a^2/k^2$. Summing distinct outgoing channels replaces the second branching fraction by their sum. For the scalar Yukawa coupling, the pair has parity $(-1)^{L+1}=+1$ and total $J=0$, requiring $L=S=1$. The resulting $^3P_0$ partial wave has one total-angular-momentum projection. The PDG quark-model section gives this same two-spin classification explicitly for a fermion–antifermion pair. Using the peak at every incident energy yields a generous transport bound, since the Breit–Wigner factor is below one away from resonance. [Baer and Cahn, §51.1, Eq. (51.1)](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2025/reviews/rpp2025-rev-cross-section-formulae.pdf) · [Amsler, Crede and DeGrand, §15.3 and Table 15.2](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2025/reviews/rpp2025-rev-quark-model.pdf)
