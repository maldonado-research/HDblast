# Next calculations after the spectral checkpoint

The next priority is to establish the physical meaning of the chosen radial
phase space. The scalar operator and local kinetic calculations now have
reviewed proofs; those results alone do not identify every admissible global
Einstein–scalar perturbation.

1. **Covariant phase space at the regular cone.** Derive the presymplectic
   current from the doubled Einstein–scalar action including GHY and shell
   contributions. Evaluate its cone limit for finite-energy wave packets,
   and distinguish smooth extendible perturbations from externally prescribed
   cone data. A successful result must state which gauge transformations have
   zero charge and show whether the reduced radial norm exhausts that physical
   space. A nonzero flux or an additional charged exceptional mode would limit
   the physical stability interpretation without invalidating the specified
   self-adjoint radial theorem.
2. **Validated quantitative scalar mass bounds.** Enclose the background
   functionals `alpha_*`, `m_*`, `M_g` and `k_g` for selected exact detunings,
   using a validated background/Jacobi solver or analytic inequalities. The
   present theorem proves a positive bound exists at each fixed detuning; a
   floating-point eigenvalue estimate is not a substitute for these enclosures.
   Study the dimensionless small-detuning limit separately. A positive physical
   mass floor uniform as `H_b -> 0` is already excluded by the essential threshold.
3. **Dynamical relevance of distinct branches.** Specify a finite-energy initial
   value problem before asking whether the original unstable branch can evolve
   toward the regular branch. Energy accounting, constraints, shell matching and
   radiation at the cone must all be retained. Static coexistence supplies no
   transition mechanism or reheating prediction.
4. **Independent scientific evaluation and discriminating observables.** Seek
   expert assessment of the narrow model-specific contribution and its closest
   prior work. A cosmological-origin claim would additionally require a complete
   evolution, quantitative observable predictions and comparison with explicit
   four-dimensional alternatives. Neither a stable branch nor a spectral gap
   distinguishes a higher-dimensional origin by itself.

These are proposed calculations, not results achieved by this checkpoint.
