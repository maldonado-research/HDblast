# Next calculations after the cone-current checkpoint

1. **Specify a physical initial/boundary problem.** Choose either a smooth
   extension across the cone or an exterior characteristic-data problem.
   Define the actual Cauchy/characteristic data, energy norm, regularity,
   shell conditions and allowed gauge generators. A radial closed form is
   not sufficient to specify this problem; the flat comparator distinguishes
   radial from inertial energy even before gravitational constraints.
2. **Construct registered radiative solutions.** Establish local existence
   and constraint propagation with the doubled shell, then show whether
   nonzero leading characteristic data are realized by actual generic
   solutions satisfying the uniform remainder assumptions. The new current
   formula can evaluate their flux once existence and compatibility hold.
   It does not replace either proof.
3. **Determine the gauge quotient and completion.** Control reconstruction
   in the chosen physical norm and compute all boundary pairings, including
   finite null cuts, shell embeddings and potential/corner conventions.
   Prove which transformations have zero charge and whether exceptional
   sectors or additional boundary data survive. Compare completed spaces
   using bounded maps rather than pointwise smoothness of isolated modes.
4. **Quantify scalar gaps and dynamical relevance.** Validated numerical
   bounds for the existing gap functionals remain useful independently.
   A branch-transition calculation requires a constraint-consistent initial
   value problem and energy/radiation accounting. Neither positive reduced
   spectra nor the present flux identities predict a Big Bang or reheating.

These are proposed tasks, not results supplied by this capsule. The immediate
priority is a well-posed physical problem with a specified energy topology.
