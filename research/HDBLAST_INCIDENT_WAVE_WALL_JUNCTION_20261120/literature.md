# Which parts of the incident-wave test have precedents?

## Radiation reaching a cosmological wall

Gergely and Keresztes studied an asymmetric Friedmann brane absorbing outward radiation from a bulk black hole. They distinguish the push exerted by incoming light from the gravitational effect of energy added to the brane. Keresztes, Képíró and Gergely subsequently varied the transmitted fraction while neglecting reflection. These give concrete precedents for a wall gaining energy and for comparing absorption with transmission. The absorption fraction is a physical input. Junction conditions relate the specified energy flux to the change in wall energy. [Irradiated asymmetric Friedmann branes](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0601217) · [Semi-transparent brane-worlds](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0603223)

The January 20 HDblast working notes modeled a positive mass jump in an advanced Vaidya coordinate and treated bath formation separately. The present comparison specifies which side of the wall is kept. In an untrapped region, future-directed light that starts at a smaller radius and reaches the wall must move toward larger radius. On that interior side, positive outgoing flux uses retarded time and a *decreasing* bulk mass. A term proportional to bulk mass divided by the fourth power of the scale factor contributes to the expansion equation. A wall bath requires its own energy balance. Earlier HDblast work also found that an incident scalar pulse could reach the shell while the specified particle-production channel stayed inactive. [HDblast incident-pulse study](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24354840)

## Which wall is allowed to move?

Chamblin and Reall derived the metric and scalar matching conditions for a moving wall in a static dilatonic bulk. In the HDblast comparison, keep the metric, the scalar profile and the original tuned boundary coupling fixed. At the same finite cut, the moving normal multiplies the static normal derivative by $\sqrt{1+\dot b^2}$, where $b$ is the proper-distance position and the dot uses wall proper time. The prescribed scalar boundary source there is still the static one. Equality forces $\dot b=0$ wherever the original scalar gradient is nonzero. This conclusion depends on holding the bulk solution and coupling fixed. Other actions admit moving dilatonic walls. [Dynamic dilatonic domain walls](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9903225)

## What happens to waves at a warped horizon?

Chamblin and Gibbons constructed gravitational waves on smooth domain walls while preserving the scalar and warp profiles. Their wave equation includes a radially independent graviton branch. Their Kaigorodov example has radial dependence and divergent tidal forces. Brecher, Chamblin and Reall evaluated curvature in freely falling frames for waves on extremal branes. Together these motivate checking the tidal force an infaller encounters along the actual trajectory, including whether a finite-duration pulse has already passed. The HDblast calculation applies that test to a particular scalar-weighted Maxwell wave on its tuned background. [Supergravity on the brane](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9909130) · [AdS/CFT in the Infinite Momentum Frame](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0012076)

A June 2026 preprint by Danielsson, Panizo and Van Hemelryck constructs self-gravitating electromagnetic waves on a dark bubble. It matches two AdS wave geometries at a brane and studies transversely localized beams. Its bulk field content and boundary conditions differ from HDblast. It provides a concrete comparison for extending a plane-wave calculation to finite transverse beams. The studies above establish precedents for the gravitational mechanisms. The present tests address the narrower question of which mechanisms survive the specific HDblast assumptions. [Self-gravitating electromagnetic waves in the dark bubble model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16547)

## Can a pulse put the field into a state that bounces and later feeds radiation?

Tetradis showed that energy entering a brane from the bulk can accelerate expansion for certain bulk and brane equations of state. The radiation-dominated case in that analysis decelerates. Matsui, Takahashi and Terada found classical bounces in four-dimensional closed universes with a real scalar potential that flattens at large field values. They also describe how kinetic energy acquired during contraction can carry a field over a barrier. The HDblast test combines different ingredients. Its wall field starts with zero energy. A prescribed force supplies work, and the mass lost by two irradiating interiors is chosen to supply that work. The calculation tests whether the field reaches a plateau and the wall bounces before it reaches the emitting surface. These precedents establish both bulk influx and scalar-supported curvature bounces as existing research directions. [Cosmological Acceleration from Energy Influx](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0211200) · [Bouncing Universe from Nothing](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12312)

Kofman, Linde and Starobinsky related damping of a weakly coupled oscillating scalar to a perturbative particle-decay width. They also showed how parametric resonance can invalidate that description. Himemoto and Tanaka supplied a brane-localized dissipative interaction for a bulk scalar and recovered effective four-dimensional decay when the Hubble rate and scalar mass are small relative to the inverse AdS curvature length. In the HDblast exit test the scalar lives on the wall. A chosen damping coefficient transfers its kinetic energy to a fluid with radiation pressure. This tracks the energy transfer, while a particle spectrum, scattering rate and equilibrium temperature would require a specified interaction and particle state. [Towards the Theory of Reheating After Inflation](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9704452) · [Braneworld reheating in the bulk inflaton model](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0212114)
