# What physical mechanism can change the photon coupling?

## A time-dependent electromagnetic kinetic coefficient

Fujita and Namba explicitly use $-I(\chi)^2F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}/4$ with a spectator scalar $\chi$ and follow gauge amplification through inflation and its aftermath. Their equation (4) displays the extra interaction, and their analysis discusses backreaction and the effective electromagnetic coupling to charges. Membiela studies a scalar-dependent electromagnetic term through an ekpyrotic contraction and a nonsingular bounce, with different coupling histories and initial conditions. Both provide examples in which scalar evolution can transfer energy to an electromagnetic field. The expansion of a round four-dimensional universe with constant $I$ leaves free Maxwell conformal modes unexcited. The HDblast experiment supplies its own $\Delta\ln I$ and transition interval on two specified field descents. [Fujita and Namba, §§II–III](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05673) · [Membiela, §§II–III](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.2162)

The closed-space multiplicity matters when translating mixing into energy. Bezerra, Mostepanenko, Mota and Romero give the exact electromagnetic thermal energy on a round three-sphere, with photon frequencies $k/a$ and $2(k^2-1)$ oscillators in each integer shell $k\ge2$. Their equation (19) uses known thermal occupations. Here the in-vacuum evolves, and each final mean occupation follows from a Bogoliubov coefficient. Both calculations use the same spectrum. A temperature would require an additional account of the distribution and its equilibration. [Bezerra et al., §II](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.84.104025) · [Independent spectral check](checks/CHECKS.md)

An October 2026 preprint by Naidenko, Sobol and Vilchinskii compares regularized vacuum stress with horizon-based cutoffs for an inflaton-coupled gauge field. Their calculations illustrate the ultraviolet divergence of the unsubtracted energy during a varying coupling and how prescriptions for separating the contributions can give different electric and magnetic energies. Their inflationary cutoffs and coupling laws have different domains of application from a switch with constant in and out plateaus. Here the late energy above the specified out-vacuum can be obtained directly from occupations when the high-frequency sum converges. A local stress and scalar source suitable for driving the coupled geometry during the transition would need consistent renormalization. [Naidenko, Sobol and Vilchinskii, §§III–V, v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32599v1)

## Where might a four-dimensional coupling come from?

Csáki, Hubisz and Lee give an explicit warped five-dimensional example. Their equations (4.6)–(4.8) include a bulk gauge action and localized boundary kinetic terms. The reduced inverse coupling contains the proper interval length divided by $g_5^2$ plus the boundary contributions. Changing the modulus can therefore induce a tree-level gauge coupling if the bulk and boundary terms have the required dynamics. The classical Maxwell action confined to a strictly four-dimensional brane is insensitive to a universal Weyl factor in its induced metric. Specifying the location and kinetic term of the gauge field changes the conclusion. [Csáki, Hubisz and Lee, §4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.3844)

A noncompact extra dimension raises a different issue. Zhao, Liu and Zhong exhibit a thick-brane gauge model with a scalar-dependent kinetic weight $\chi(y)$. Their equation (24) requires a finite normalization integral $\int dy\,\chi(y)|\rho_0(y)|^2$ for the gauge zero mode. Finite gravitational normalization alone does not supply this gauge integral. Recovering an HDblast photon would require locating its gauge field, establishing its normalizable profile and calculating how its normalization changes with the rolling field. The published HDblast five-dimensional junction calculation independently shows why a background solution must satisfy both the bulk scalar equation and its boundary condition. [Zhao, Liu and Zhong, §III](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.6480) · [HDblast scalar junction study](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23111008)

Charged quantum matter can also break classical Weyl invariance. Benevides, Dabholkar and Kobayashi compute the anomaly-induced photon effective action on Weyl-flat backgrounds and find that its logarithmic dependence does not generate coherent classical magnetic fields in their weakly coupled regime. A quantum anomaly depends on the actual charged spectrum, running and quantum state. This work supplies no value for the HDblast tree-level profile. Separately, Garani, Redi and Tesi calculate production of conformal fields by metric inhomogeneities. That source can operate with constant gauge coupling on a distorted geometry, whereas the present round calculation isolates the response to $I(\Theta)$. [Benevides, Dabholkar and Kobayashi, §§2–5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.08237) · [Garani, Redi and Tesi, §§2–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12249v2)

## From wave production to radiation domination

Kofman, Linde and Starobinsky derived conditions under which perturbative damping of an oscillating inflaton can be related to a microscopic decay rate, and studied regimes where collective particle production changes the dynamics. Their account separates excitation of fields from subsequent scattering and thermal equilibration. The HDblast fluid test instead inserts a field-dependent transfer rate over part of a first descent and conserves the total energy of the scalar and radiation components. It tests what conversion history would be sufficient for equality while the geometry responds. Identifying that rate with the quantum photon channel would require matching its exchange history with backreaction included. [Kofman, Linde and Starobinsky, §§I and III](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9704452)
