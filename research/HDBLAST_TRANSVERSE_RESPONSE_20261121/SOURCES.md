# Comparing field growth on the wall with plasma experiments and simulations

In simulations of two colliding, symmetric, initially cold electron–positron plasmas, Bret et al. computed the spontaneous seed field, its amplification, and the time needed to reach saturation. That time provides a lower bound on the formation time of the shock in their model. The finite-time calculation on the HDblast wall gives each newly injected group its own age and allows the axial force and current to change throughout the run. A growing linear mode has to accumulate enough impulse within those ages to account for the measured spread. [Bret et al., *Collisionless shock formation, spontaneous electromagnetic fluctuations, and streaming instabilities*](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4798541)

Bret et al. later followed particles through filamentary fields during the collision of two initially unmagnetized pair-plasma shells. The reflected particles and the time they spent in the growing interaction region helped determine when a shock formed. Bret and Dieckmann found several competing linear instabilities when a flow-aligned guide field was present in cold counterstreaming pair plasmas. Those setups provide controls for idealized limits of the wall calculation. The archived wall maintains a guide field and continually creates new particles. [Bret et al., formation mechanism and timing](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.4144) · [Bret and Dieckmann, aligned-field calculation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07974)

In a laboratory experiment, Warwick et al. measured magnetic fields of at least one tesla by proton radiography after a quasi-neutral electron–positron beam passed through background electron–ion plasma. The reported persistence of these fields motivates asking whether the larger incident wave in HDblast could leave a transverse field in place before particles appear. The experiment involved a material background plasma and a measured terrestrial beam. Predicting persistence on the wall requires its own field evolution and boundary conditions. [Warwick et al., *Experimental Observation of a Current-Driven Instability in a Neutral Electron-Positron Beam*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.119.185002) · [Author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08162)
