# Primary comparison and contribution boundary

The scalar-level construction and scalar–metric cancellation use established
geometry. The current contribution is a constrained reconstruction and an exact,
conditional robustness estimate for the retained Einstein–scalar shell model.
This focused comparison establishes neither priority nor exhaustive coverage.

- M. Mars, *First and Second Order Perturbations of Hypersurfaces*, Class.
  Quantum Grav. **22** (2005) 3325–3348,
  [gr-qc/0507005](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0507005), Lemma 1, Proposition 3
  and section 7. Moving embeddings and ambient fields both contribute to induced
  geometry. The shared first-variation Hessian cancels when differencing two
  second variations; the current area formula still requires its own conventions
  and regularity argument.
- M. Giovannini, *Gauge-invariant fluctuations of scalar branes*, Phys. Rev. D
  **64**, 064023 (2001), [hep-th/0106041](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0106041),
  equations (3.17), (3.23) and (4.17)–(4.22). Its invariant scalar–metric
  combination has the same standard cancellation structure after matching
  conventions. Its smooth Lorentz-sliced branes and linear normal-mode problem
  differ from the present compact constrained mean and shell-volume estimate.
- M. Gasperini, G. Marozzi and G. Veneziano, *Gauge invariant averages for the
  cosmological backreaction*, JCAP **03** (2009) 011,
  [0901.1303](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.1303), section 3 and equation (3.23).
  Scalar-defined integration surfaces can be coordinate invariant while still
  depending on the scalar/surface chosen. Its induced-volume delta window includes
  the scalar-gradient factor. Here the level surface lies inside an already
  specified Cauchy slice; the construction does not select that physical slice.

  [earlier prepared-family result](../HDBLAST_PREPARED_SHELL_VOLUME_20261101/)
  supplies the absolute negative response and its retained analytic premises.
  Neither earlier result is modified by the new restricted class.
