# Primary context for a scalar-labelled initial geometry class

Checked 4 November 2026. This is a focused comparison with three primary sources, not an exhaustive literature review, a priority determination, or external specialist review. Primary arXiv metadata and the relevant PDF passages were read directly. The statements about the retained shell model below describe its proposed result and scope; the cited papers do not prove its numerical constants.

## Assessment

Defining a level set through a scalar, evaluating its induced geometry, and cancelling the scalar displacement against the metric perturbation are established constructions. The initial profile

\[
D_h(\varphi)=\frac{\Delta A^{(2)}(\varphi)}{A_0(\varphi)}
=\frac32J-3a\frac{w}{p}
\]

should therefore be presented as a model-specific scalar-labelled area perturbation, not as a new general invariance principle. Here the two families have the same background and first variation, the superscript denotes their declared second variation convention, \(p=\phi_0'\ne0\), and \(a\) is the retained logarithmic warp derivative. The genuinely additional work is the constrained reconstruction, its domain and endpoint conditions, the quantitative conversion of geometric control into the five-component initial norm, and the signed response bounds in this model.

A geometric restriction on admitted preparations is not a physical prescription selecting those preparations. Nothing in the three sources selects the retained compact mean, supplies the constant \(R\le2\times10^{33}\), proves the bound \(N\le R/2000\), or proves the retained signs of the volume and expansion coefficients. Those are separate claims requiring the model's own derivation and certificates.

## 1. Moving hypersurfaces and second variations

Marc Mars, *First and Second Order Perturbations of Hypersurfaces*, Class. Quantum Grav. **22** (2005) 3325–3348. [arXiv:gr-qc/0507005](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0507005), [DOI:10.1088/0264-9381/22/16/013](https://doi.org/10.1088/0264-9381/22/16/013). First submitted 1 July 2005; the primary page shows one version.

**Passages checked:** §3, Lemma 1, Eqs. (7)–(9); §6, Proposition 3, Eq. (40); §7, especially the discussion immediately before Proposition 6.

Lemma 1 differentiates a pullback by a moving embedding. With the paper's notation,

\[
\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\chi_\epsilon^*T_\epsilon
=\chi_\epsilon^*(\mathcal L_{Z_\epsilon}T_\epsilon+\partial_\epsilon T_\epsilon),
\]

\[
\frac{d^2}{d\epsilon^2}\chi_\epsilon^*T_\epsilon
=\chi_\epsilon^*\bigl(\mathcal L_{W_\epsilon}T_\epsilon+
\mathcal L_{Z_\epsilon}^2T_\epsilon+
2\mathcal L_{Z_\epsilon}\partial_\epsilon T_\epsilon+
\partial_\epsilon^2T_\epsilon\bigr).
\]

Proposition 3 applies this machinery to the induced metric and extrinsic curvature of a nondegenerate hypersurface in arbitrary dimension; the paper also states applicability to arbitrary metric signature. It gives the natural framework for including both a perturbed ambient metric and a perturbed scalar-level embedding. The determinant/area variation is a subsequent calculation from the induced metric; Mars does not state the retained shell profile or its quantitative theorem.

Section 7 distinguishes two freedoms: spacetime identification gauge, and the choice of coordinates/identification on the abstract hypersurface. The paper says the induced tensors are invariant under the former when the physical embedding is transformed consistently, while explaining the additional intrinsic reparametrization freedom. This supports careful treatment of both the moving scalar surface and the spatial coordinates. It does not identify physically different Cauchy surfaces.

**Application, not a quoted theorem:** when two families share the same first tangent and the same scalar-level definition, the quadratic terms in their common first variation cancel in the difference of their second area variations. This is also the ordinary second-derivative chain rule: the common Hessian term cancels, leaving the first derivative acting on the difference of second data. The retained coefficient \(\frac32J-3aw/p\) still requires its own convention and geometry calculation.

## 2. The standard scalar–metric invariant in a warped scalar background

Massimo Giovannini, *Gauge-invariant fluctuations of scalar branes*, Phys. Rev. D **64**, 064023 (2001). [arXiv:hep-th/0106041](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0106041), [DOI:10.1103/PhysRevD.64.064023](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.64.064023). First submitted 5 June 2001; the primary page shows one version. The arXiv identifier is verified.

**Passages checked:** §III.B, Eqs. (3.17), (3.21), (3.23)–(3.24); §IV.A, Eqs. (4.15), (4.17), (4.21)–(4.22).

For the conformally warped background, the paper uses a warp factor denoted \(a(w)\), \(\mathcal H=a'/a\), transverse scalar metric perturbation \(2\psi\eta_{\mu\nu}\), and bulk scalar perturbation \(\chi\). Under a radial gauge displacement \(\epsilon_w\),

\[
\widetilde\psi=\psi-\mathcal H\epsilon_w,
\qquad
\widetilde\chi=\chi-\phi'\epsilon_w.
\]

The canonical invariant is

\[
\mathcal G=a^{3/2}\chi-z\psi,
\qquad z=\frac{a^{3/2}\phi'}{\mathcal H},
\qquad
\mathcal G=a^{3/2}X-z\Psi.
\]

Consequently the relative scalar–metric combination

\[
\psi-\frac{\mathcal H}{\phi'}\chi
=-\frac{\mathcal H}{a^{3/2}\phi'}\mathcal G
\]

is invariant wherever these divisions are allowed. This is the familiar uniform-scalar/comoving-curvature type cancellation, here explicitly verified in a scalar-brane primary source.

After matching radial coordinate and metric normalizations, the retained area combination has this same structure: writing its transverse conformal metric difference as \(J=2\psi\) gives \(D=3(\psi-aw/p)\). This is a kinematic comparison, not an identification of the two dynamical problems. Giovannini studies linear fluctuations of smooth warped scalar branes with four-dimensional Lorentz slices and their normal modes. The retained S3 shell, scalar-dependent tension, constrained compact second data, initial-area reconstruction, and quantitative response estimates are different ingredients. A difference of second variations with common first variation transforms linearly in the corresponding difference, which is why the linear invariant structure is relevant; this does not make arbitrary individual second perturbations linear gauge invariants.

## 3. Relational level sets, induced volume, and the remaining slice choice

M. Gasperini, G. Marozzi and G. Veneziano, *Gauge invariant averages for the cosmological backreaction*, JCAP **03** (2009) 011. [arXiv:0901.1303](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.1303), [DOI:10.1088/1475-7516/2009/03/011](https://doi.org/10.1088/1475-7516/2009/03/011). First submitted 9 January 2009; v2 19 January, v3 8 March 2009.

**Passages checked:** §2, Eqs. (2.10)–(2.13); §3, Eqs. (3.2)–(3.7), the discussion after Eq. (3.19), and Eq. (3.23); §4, the discussion after Eq. (4.9).

The authors anchor the integration domain to scalar functions, with a hypersurface given by \(A(x)=A_0\). They emphasize that a finite boundary must also transform as a scalar-defined boundary. Their default Eq. (3.7) uses the full metric determinant. For the induced proper-volume measure they explicitly give the alternative covariant window

\[
\delta(A(x)-A_0)\sqrt{|g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu A\partial_\nu A|}
\tag{their Eq. 3.23}
\]

and explain that it replaces the full determinant weight by the induced hypersurface determinant. Thus the source supports the scalar-level induced-volume construction without confusing its two measure prescriptions.

Most relevant for interpretation, after Eq. (3.19) they say the prescription “strongly depends on the scalar observable A chosen to specify the hypersurface,” while for any fixed A it is independent of gauge. After Eq. (4.9) they again state that the result is insensitive to the gauge but not, in principle, to the reference hypersurface.

Their construction uses a scalar with timelike spacetime gradient to select spacelike hypersurfaces. In the retained initial-data application, the bulk scalar labels three-dimensional level surfaces **inside an already chosen four-dimensional Cauchy slice**. The direct analogue is the coarea expression

\[
A_\epsilon(\varphi_*)=
\int_{\Sigma_\epsilon}
\delta(\phi_\epsilon-\varphi_*)
|\nabla_{\gamma_\epsilon}\phi_\epsilon|\,dV_{\gamma_\epsilon},
\]

on a regular level set, with any needed boundaries specified geometrically. This is invariant under simultaneous spatial diffeomorphisms of the initial metric, scalar, and domain. It does not by itself choose \(\Sigma_\epsilon\), or establish independence under replacing it with another physical Cauchy slice. A fully spacetime-relational version would also require a physical rule selecting that initial slice and transforming it consistently.

## Consequences for the proposed class

- **Geometric meaning:** \(D\) compares actual scalar-labelled level-set areas on the declared physical initial slice. Its raw and conditioned formulas may be two coordinate representatives of that one quantity. The normalizations, scalar labels, and second-variation convention must be fixed explicitly.
- **Norm scope:** a bound on \(D\) and its derivative in a declared scalar label is invariant under spatial coordinate changes with scalar labels transformed as fields. An arbitrary relabelling \(\phi\mapsto f(\phi)\) changes a derivative norm unless the norm and weights are transformed too; it is not an additional established invariance.
- **Uniqueness scope:** injectivity of the constrained compact preparation map requires the retained first-order ODE and its endpoint/support conditions. Whole-packet \(D=0\) can establish whole-packet uniqueness only under those conditions. A profile known on the causal interval \([1,1.25]\) yields only the corresponding local uniqueness statement unless a separate continuation argument is proved.
- **Prediction scope:** the bounded class can support robust signs of the declared second coefficients. This does not produce a uniform finite-\(\epsilon\) window for every admitted nonlinear family; that would require uniform remainder control.
- **Selection scope:** the action and boundary conditions need not select the class merely because the class is geometrically formulated. A state, variational, regularity, preparation, or other physical selection rule remains additional work.

Suggested wording: “Using standard hypersurface perturbation and scalar–metric gauge-invariant constructions, we define a scalar-labelled initial area profile for the declared Cauchy slice. For the retained compact mean family, a constrained reconstruction and quantitative norm estimate turn bounded profile data into a class with robust signed second-response coefficients. The class is conditional on its geometric bounds and is not yet selected by a physical preparation principle.”
