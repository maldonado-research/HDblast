# Literature and relation to earlier HDblast work

We searched primary papers on time-dependent gauge fields, renormalization with an external rolling field, and what positive spectral weights allow one to infer from a finite number of response coefficients. These sources address distinct physical settings. Their relevance to the closed-universe first descent depends on which assumptions are shared.

**A recent test of electromagnetic backreaction.** Naidenko, Sobol and Vilchinskii consider an inflationary gauge-kinetic model with a power-law coupling in approximately de Sitter space. Their calculations compare estimates of electric and magnetic energy using different horizon prescriptions. Separately resolving the electric and magnetic pieces matters because their difference sources the inflaton. Agreement on the total alone can conceal disagreement on the source. Here we evolve each oscillator covariance and check the parent’s work alongside the total energy. Their continuum approximations have a different background and coupling history from the compact switch on a closed spatial slice. [Naidenko et al., October 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32599)

**Covariant stress and a dynamical source.** Elías and Mazzitelli work out the consequences of comoving and physical ultraviolet cutoffs for cosmological quantum fields. Their scalar examples show that a cutoff and the choice of bare conserved or noncovariant terms affect the stress equations. Del Río, Ferreiro, Navarro-Salas and Torrenti treat a quantized Dirac field with a Yukawa coupling to a classical scalar and construct simultaneous adiabatic subtractions for the stress tensor and the scalar source. For HDblast the corresponding consistency test would be to derive both quantities for the actual gauge kinetic operator and geometry, fix the finite terms, and verify the exchange identity. The scale-dependent pressure and scalar-work tests in this deposit check its finite prescription. A covariant five-dimensional matching calculation remains open. [Elías and Mazzitelli, 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.02993) · [del Río et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00908)

**A local ambiguity even at fixed spatial curvature.** Ichinose and Odintsov compute the four-dimensional trace anomaly of a vector field coupled to a background dilaton and find additional dilaton-dependent invariants. The trace alone leaves the state-dependent stress unresolved. A simple example shows why static observations also leave curvature-dependent local terms unresolved. Use signature $(-,+,+,+)$, cosmic time, $H=\dot a/a$ and $\ell=\log I$. Consider two illustrative Lagrangian terms, $-R(\nabla\ell)^2/6$ and $G^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\ell\nabla_\nu\ell/3$, with the same dimensional normalization. For a homogeneous $\ell$ on a unit-curvature closed Friedmann background, the respective coefficients of $\dot\ell^2$ are

$$\dot H+2H^2+a^{-2},\qquad H^2+a^{-2}.$$

These follow from $R=6(\dot H+2H^2+a^{-2})$ and $G_{00}=3(H^2+a^{-2})$. Every static-radius probe gives the same $a^{-2}$ from both terms, whereas the rolling coefficients differ by $\ddot a/a$. Matching the homogeneous force on a prescribed static metric therefore cannot determine what stress these terms contribute during expansion. Sushkov shows that general curvature-gradient couplings can give higher-order equations and that the Einstein-tensor combination has second-order equations. Neither example has been matched to the five-dimensional parent or used in the numerical evolutions here. [Ichinose and Odintsov, 1998](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9802043) · [Sushkov, 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.0980)

At the midpoint in field distance of the archived switch, the two geometric coefficients in units of $H_0^2$ are $(0.211373,0.937226)$ on the positive-minimum trajectory and $(0.718667,0.955878)$ on the negative-minimum trajectory. To reproduce them, load the archived branches with `gauge.load(prior)` and evaluate `gauge.Roll(branch, archive, 1001, limits=(0.5,0.8)).points["switch_start"]`. The lower sampling boundary now selects the midpoint of the original 0.2–0.8 switch. With the returned dimensionless `a`, `h`, and velocity $v$, the pair is $(2B-v^2/2,B)$ where $B=h^2+a^{-2}$, since the fixed trajectory satisfies $dh/d(H_0t)=a^{-2}-v^2/2$. These numbers evaluate curvature on the prescribed histories. The operator strengths, forces and stresses remain undetermined by this check. **What positive weights can determine.** Chiang and colleagues show how a spectrum with positive weights gives a convex set of moment sequences in a scattering problem. Our closed-sphere susceptibility has a related elementary representation obtained directly from its oscillator equations. Its first two moments restrict the range of responses below the lowest added resonance. The finite examples in this deposit have identical first and second calibration coefficients, different higher moments, and different outgoing energies. The bounds use the support and positivity derived for this particular system. [Chiang et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02862) · [Calculation and assumptions](completion/COMPLETION.md)

**A recent example involving charged matter.** Sharma, Kumar, Subramanian and Sanyal study helical gauge production near the end of low-scale inflation with a pseudoscalar coupling. A Schwinger current from charged particles can arrest amplification, and subsequent field estimates depend on the treatment of saturation. This illustrates an independent physical input needed to relate a produced gauge field to later matter and radiation. The couplings and cosmologies differ from those in this study, so the paper supplies no quantitative efficiency for HDblast. [Sharma et al., November 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19721)

**Earlier project checks.** The D-Blast 3 archive’s September 4 *Statistical and radiation second audit*, section 4, “Radiation conversion independently recalculated”, distinguishes a present-day, physically normalized density integral from a proxy and explains why emission time and later redshifting matter. The earlier HDblast gauge and backreaction calculations supply first-descent histories and finite-band mode energies used here. To compare this result with cosmic data one would still need a physically motivated matter/spectral coupling, a consistent stress prescription over the scales of interest, and evolution to a specified observable.
