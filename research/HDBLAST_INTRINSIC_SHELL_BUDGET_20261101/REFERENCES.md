# Primary references and prior-work boundary

- T. Shiromizu, K. Maeda and M. Sasaki, *The Einstein Equations on the 3-Brane
  World*, [gr-qc/9910076](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9910076). Its general
  Gauss/Codazzi equations supply standard hypersurface machinery. Its later
  constant-bulk/constant-tension matter reduction must not be applied unchanged
  to the present scalar and variable tension.
- T. Buchert, *On Average Properties of Inhomogeneous Fluids in General
  Relativity I: Dust Cosmologies*, [gr-qc/9906015](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9906015),
  equations (9f), (10c). The expansion-variance/shear combination and averaging
  commutator are established machinery. Here they are derived kinematically for
  intrinsic Gaussian observers; the paper's dust matter evolution is not assumed.
- T. Buchert, *On Average Properties of Inhomogeneous Fluids in General
  Relativity II: Perfect Fluid Cosmologies*,
  [gr-qc/0102049](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0102049), equation (13c) and section 3.
  Matter-flow lapse and pressure contributions in that framework reinforce the
  distinction between a chosen geodesic foliation and an assumed fluid flow.
- The October 16 [intrinsic scalar note](../HDBLAST_GEOMETRIC_BULK_SOURCES_20261016/shell-invariant/SHELL_INTRINSIC_SCALAR.md)
  already contains the scalar-gradient identity and its earlier zero brackets.
  This checkpoint does not claim that identity as new. Only its first old bracket
  fits the present exact-family time slab; the second is not transferred.
- The October 23 [proper-time volume specification](../HDBLAST_TOTAL_RESPONSE_PREFLIGHT_20261023/observable/SHELL_PROPER_TIME_OBSERVABLE.md)
  already defines anchored shell volume and accounts for clock/measure changes.
  Its scalar second data are not automatically those of the later selected family.

The primary Buchert PDFs and relevant equation passages were inspected directly
for this checkpoint; the known Gauss source and recent reheating literature were
inspected in the preceding continuation. This is a focused comparison, not an
exhaustive literature or novelty determination. The general normalized
scalar-curvature Hessian at a round Einstein metric is standard geometry and
is derived explicitly here rather than claimed as a new Yamabe result.
