# Focused methods comparison for the exact-packet initial-data experiment

31 October 2026. Two focused web searches were followed by retrieval of three
primary or author-written texts. This is a bounded methods check, not an
exhaustive literature search, a novelty assessment, or an external review of
HDBLAST. Raw retrievals are retained privately; only bibliographic metadata,
retrieval hashes and this comparison belong in the public research capsule.

## Preserving functional dependence in validated arithmetic

M. Neher, K. R. Jackson and N. S. Nedialkov, *On Taylor Model Based Integration
of ODEs*, author-hosted manuscript, 18 August 2006:
<http://www.cs.toronto.edu/pub/reports/na/Taylor_Models_ODES_2006.pdf>.
The retrieved 21-page PDF identifies all three authors. Sections 2.2–2.3
explain how ordinary interval evaluation loses dependence between repeated
occurrences of the same variable, and how polynomial-plus-remainder
representations can reduce the resulting overestimation. Example 2.3 also
shows that composition becomes invalid when the inner range is outside the
outer model's validated domain. These passages, the introduction and the
relevant composition example were inspected; no claim of a complete proof
audit of the paper is made.

The directly relevant principle is to preserve a proved common analytic
factor before bounding a difference. In the new displacement definition,
the exact packet and exponential factors are shared with the actual target.
Their approximation-difference channels can therefore be zero by functional
identity. Actual-versus-nominal background coefficient and separately
validated derivative-remainder channels remain. Two overlapping independent
interval boxes do not prove cancellation. This experiment uses its stated
paired jet arithmetic; it does not claim to implement the paper's entire
Taylor-model ODE integrator or to introduce a new general numerical method.

## The analytic reduction and its computer verification have different roles

Javier Gómez-Serrano, *Computer-assisted proofs in PDE: a survey*,
arXiv:1810.00745v1, 1 October 2018:
<https://arxiv.org/html/1810.00745v1>.
The title, author, version, abstract and Sections 1–1.1 were inspected.
The survey distinguishes an analytic reduction to sufficient conditions
from the rigorous numerical verification of those conditions, with examples
principally from incompressible fluid equations. It discusses arithmetic,
rounding and reproducibility issues that affect apparently simple checks.

For the present calculation the reduction is the retained all23 initial-data
and response-error argument. The new numerical work must establish its
specific hypotheses: denominator separation, complete interval coverage,
correct derivative scaling, a positive Gram norm, error rather than absolute
jet inputs, and required initial/boundary regularity. Agreement between
normal and optimized execution and rejection of deliberate corruptions
test implementation behavior. They do not substitute for that reduction,
prove every inherited analytic premise, or establish the cosmological model.

## A wave discretisation result has a different equation and boundary law

Lehel Banjai, Emmanuil H. Georgoulis and Brian Hennessy, *Space-time finite
element methods for nonlinear wave equations via elliptic regularisation*,
arXiv:2507.22757v1, 30 July 2025:
<https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22757v1>.
The author/version information, abstract and introductory model equation
were inspected. The paper studies a conforming space-time Galerkin method
for a defocusing semilinear scalar wave equation on a bounded polygonal or
polyhedral domain in spatial dimensions one to three, with homogeneous
Dirichlet boundary data. It gives stability/well-posedness results for that
discretisation and a priori error bounds for sufficiently smooth solutions.

Its local space-time resolution motivation is relevant to later numerical
design. Its theorems are not a validation of the present five-dimensional
Einstein–scalar system, the coupled 23-amplitude operator, or its physical
dynamic shell/Robin conditions. The exact-packet benchmark retains its
existing causal response operator and sources. No finite-element theorem
or new boundary-existence theorem is imported here.

## Consequence for this checkpoint

The useful method is a precisely defined candidate change with separate
absolute-candidate and target-error ledgers. A local comparison uses a
positive lower bound for the actual target at the same point. A smaller
global upper certificate does not by itself prove a smaller unknown global
true error. These established distinctions guide the predeclared gates;
none of the retrieved papers supplies evidence of a higher-dimensional
Big Bang origin. The 2026 retrieval dates are not publication dates.
