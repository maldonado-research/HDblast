J. D. Bondurant and S. A. Fulling, *The Dirichlet-to-Robin Transform*, J. Phys. A **38** (2005), 1505–1532, [arXiv:math-ph/0408054](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0408054), [primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/math-ph/0408054), DOI [10.1088/0305-4470/38/7/007](https://doi.org/10.1088/0305-4470/38/7/007). Sections II and III.2 give a classical delayed convolution for a flat half-line scalar Robin wave problem. This is the relevant precedent for retaining causal boundary feedback beyond a single image or first lift. The present constant-matrix Volterra reconstruction is elementary linear-system analysis; no discovery of the resolvent method is claimed. The source's scalar commuting-operator hypotheses are not invoked as a theorem for the coupled Einstein–scalar problem. The matrix construction and its error majorants are derived directly in this capsule without positivity, a matrix inverse or parity commutation.

The recent scalar-wave reconstruction and acoustic shape-derivative papers compared in the preceding note do not provide a pointwise C2 error certificate for this coupled shell system. Their finite-element energy estimates, boundary conditions and distance-from-boundary hypotheses differ from those used here. This checkpoint uses exact rational interval integration and finite-series tails, rather than a named Convolution Quadrature discretization or an imported numerical existence theorem.

The contribution of this checkpoint is the explicit application and reproducible conditional error ledger for the retained formal 23-mode model: a reconstruction satisfying its auxiliary actual affine boundary law, a tighter direct C1 upper certificate, and validated sample enclosures. It neither establishes originality against all published work nor resolves nonlinear existence, full constraint compatibility, journal significance or cosmological evidence.
