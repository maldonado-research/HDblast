# Do the fields left on the wall give the particles a thermal distribution?

The five-dimensional radial probe supplies a sideways electric field before the first charged particles form. We followed daughters born at different times and positions through that field. The incoming component, the axial wall expansion, and the mean axial field and particle production still follow their recorded histories. The simulation evolves the extra electromagnetic fields generated by the daughters, including their leakage at the wall.

At the instant when the reference model has exchanged the first 1% of its whole-run absolute electrical work, a zero transverse wave gives a transverse cumulative-count discrepancy very nearly 100 percentage points. After calibrating the strength of one wave at a time, the best sampled radial-probe examples give the following local particle results. The thermal transverse count uses the previous study’s temperature. Its value is invariant under a longitudinal boost. Energy and stress depend on the frame.

## Particle experiment

A local periodic patch represents an equal-amplitude combination of opposite helicities as an electric wave $E_y=e(t)\cos(k_c x)$ and magnetic wave $B_z=b(t)\sin(k_c x)$. The axial electric field points along $z$. The provided transfer amplitudes are physical field coefficients. The wavenumber $k_c=a_{\rm birth}k_{\rm physical}$ is comoving. The input multiplier $\alpha$ is the peak launch coefficient relative to the axial pulse in the radial-probe experiment. The cosine has spatial RMS $1/\sqrt2$ times its peak. The companion prescribed-wall experiment instead measures $\alpha$ relative to the recorded incoming characteristic at the wall. This local particle sampling approximation is particularly consequential for harmonics whose wavelength is comparable to the curvature radius. Propagation in the radial probe is calculated mode by mode on the curved background. [Radial-probe orbit runs](BULK.json) · [Prescribed-wall orbit runs](LOCAL.json)

Continuous creation is discretized in equal accepted-mass cohorts. Each cohort samples the inherited angle-dependent birth distribution, uniform azimuth, and uniform spatial phase. A conjugate negative particle has momentum $(p_x,-p_y,-p_z)$ at the positive particle's spatial phase. This is the benchmark’s projection along one gauge direction. All quoted stresses refer to that charge-paired sector. Adding three identical copies with mutually rotated axes can cancel an anisotropy in their total stress while each sector still misses its thermal distribution. This pair symmetry holds for the prescribed polarization and the self-consistent $E_y,E_z,B_y,B_z$ fields. X-directed charge current and local charge cancel. The test freezes the axial mean history and omits individual scattering events. The two benchmark names identify different reference histories and temperatures inherited from the two interaction models. Particle feedback includes the transverse and axial currents in the retained nonzero Fourier modes in the patch. It does not recalculate gravitational backreaction, the common axial field, the births, or the reflection of the new particle radiation from the outer collar. The number-fraction comparison uses the maximum absolute difference from the Maxwell–Jüttner transverse cumulative momentum distribution at the archived temperature $\theta=T/m$. We also evaluate the full charge-summed stress $T_{ij}\propto\sum wp_ip_j/\gamma$ in the neutral wall frame. A thermal gas at rest on the wall has three principal stresses of one third after division by the trace. Two streams boosted along the axial field have two equal transverse eigenvalues and a larger longitudinal eigenvalue. The sign-paired distribution has $T_{xy}=T_{xz}=0$; the calculation retains $T_{yz}$ and diagonalizes the remaining block. Its net $y,z$ momentum cancels exactly, and the computed net $x$ momentum is negligible in the selected standing waves. The wall-rest mean-energy comparator includes rest mass, with $\langle\gamma\rangle_{\mathrm{th}}=3\theta+K_1(1/\theta)/K_2(1/\theta)$.

For comparison with the original drifting-fluid closure, let $u$ be its recorded directed four-velocity along the guide field, $\Gamma=\sqrt{1+u^2}$ and $h$ its enthalpy per rest mass. Dividing energy and stress by the wall-frame number density, a pair of opposite identical thermal streams predicts $E=h\Gamma-\theta/\Gamma$, two transverse stresses $P_{\perp}=\theta/\Gamma$, and an axial stress $P_\parallel=P_{\perp}+h u^2/\Gamma$. We evaluate both the Maxwell–Jüttner enthalpy $h=K_3(1/\theta)/K_2(1/\theta)$ and the prior solver’s Taub–Mathews approximation. ## A lower bound independent of the imposed wave strength

Suppose that the extra imposed electric field points along $y$ and its magnetic field along $z$ throughout this patch. It can change transverse particle motion but contributes exactly zero to $E_z+(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)_z$. Its amplitude, wavenumber, and time dependence therefore do not enter the axial boost directly. A particle born at $b$ receives the axial boost $Q\int_b^T aX\,dt/a_T$ from the mean field. It has a small initial momentum and can additionally receive an axial impulse from its own plasma's fields. The sign of $X$ is constant in the tested birth intervals. Taking one charge sign, a necessary population-mean boost magnitude from the mean field is

$$ P_0=\frac{Q}{a_T}\int_{t_0}^T a(t)|X(t)|\frac{D(t)}{D_T}\,dt. $$

Here $D(t)$ is accepted comoving daughter rest, so $D(t)/D_T$ is the fraction already born at $t$. Particle birth can reduce the mean by at most $p_\star=\max_b\sqrt{R_b^2-1}$ in units of daughter mass ($a_b/a_T\le 1$). Taking the conjugate charge yields the same energy. We use the sign-specific axial momentum to bound mean energy by convexity, while the total neutral momentum can vanish.

For one Fourier harmonic, the Euclidean norm of its vector of source coefficients in conformal time is bounded above by $2 Q D/K$. Each particle has $|\mathbf v|\le1$ and $(\cos kx,\sin kx)$ has unit length. Summing with positive weights gives the bound for arbitrary locations and velocities, including strong clumps. Source-free propagation preserves the field norm, and outward electric leakage decreases it. The conformal axial force per charge from this harmonic obeys $|\delta\mathcal E_z+v_x\delta\mathcal B_y|\le\sqrt2\,\|\delta\mathcal F\|$. The two bounds and $d\eta=dt/a$ give the following upper bound on the population-mean absolute axial impulse per daughter-induced harmonic:

$$ C_1=\frac{2\sqrt2 Q^2}{Ka_T}\int_{t_0}^T \frac{dt}{a(t)}\frac{D(t)}{D_T}
                 \int_{t_0}^{t}\frac{D(s)}{a(s)}\,ds. $$

All induced fields vanish before the first birth. With at most $N$ real Fourier pairs and any imposed wave of the stated polarization, the continuum collisionless model must satisfy

$$ \langle\gamma\rangle\ \ge\ \sqrt{1+[P_0-p_\star-N C_1]_+^2}. $$

This holds without estimating the plasma's actual current-growth rate. For numerical evaluation we use one-sided cell bounds on the positive integrals and specify piecewise-linear interpolation of the recorded inputs. We also provide the separate endpoint bounds for the equal-number quantile orbit grids and a trapezoidal cross-check. Taking additional harmonics weakens the finite-mode guarantee. A component of the imposed force along $z$, collisions that exchange momentum, a different axial drive, or altered births changes the statement. At the 1% reference-work marker the halved-step lower mean impulses from the axial field are 23,667 and 28,383 $mc$. Each allowed daughter-driven harmonic can remove at most 412.2 and 724.0 $mc$ on average. Even with seven harmonics carrying favorable currents, the specific continuum mean energy must exceed 7.23 and 12.18 times the energy of a wall-rest thermal gas at the prescribed temperature on both time resolutions. The seven-harmonic floors are below the energy predicted when the archived counterstreams are boosted to the wall frame. The energy inequality therefore constrains wall-rest thermalization; the split in the two sideways stresses tests the stronger moving-stream comparison.

## Numerical checks

The orbit solver splits drifts, magnetic rotations, electric pushes, Fourier propagation and boundary leakage. The electric push uses a discrete energy-gradient velocity for its mode currents. Positive leakage and the loss of induced field energy balance the work done by those fields. Particle energy tracks injection, expansion, mean-field work, work by the supplied wave, and work by the induced wave as separate terms. The maximum relative particle-energy ledger residual across both broad scans is below $9\times10^{-13}$, and the induced-field residual in units of final particle energy is below $3\times10^{-17}$. The imposed wave is advanced in the upstream transfer solve. The signed work by that prescribed wave on the daughters need not equal a change in its own energy in this one-way coupling.
