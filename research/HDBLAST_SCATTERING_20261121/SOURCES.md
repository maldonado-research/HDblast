# Sources for the shell scattering test

## Starting population and comparison gas

The parent HDblast study specifies the particle populations, the two interaction histories, the comparison with a drifting thermal stream, and the 20 energy-and-direction bins on a common shell-time slice. Its bins allow the same counting experiment before and after collisions. The source budget and its early work marker belong to that simulated shell. [Polarization study at its merged revision](https://github.com/maldonado-research/HDblast/tree/173a1c13f9b4722b91fd2346d837d129c3836d4f/research/HDBLAST_POLARIZATION_20261121) · [Archived inputs and results](https://zenodo.org/records/24424913)

A separate HDblast study examines a neutral scalar resonance, three unpolarized Dirac flavors and limits on how rapidly they can relax a charge current. Its limits depend on the spin, available outgoing channels and definition of drag. Translating a drag rate into a count of binary encounters requires the angular interaction law and the population on which it acts. [Directional transport calculation, pinned working revision](https://github.com/maldonado-research/HDblast/blob/93b41992fece0db35de2eb51ed5289d1b31bec02/research/HDBLAST_DIRECTIONAL_TRANSPORT/README.md)

## Relativistic collision sampling

Peano, Marti, Silva and Coppa derive the collision probability for pairs counted in a specified frame. In units with light speed equal to one, their Eq. (2) gives the pair weight as the cross section times $g=\sqrt{|\mathbf v_1-\mathbf v_2|^2-|\mathbf v_1\times\mathbf v_2|^2}$. For a constant cross section, one can propose pairs uniformly and accept with probability $g/2$. For an isotropic differential cross section, a collision draws a uniform outgoing direction in the pair's center-of-momentum frame, preserves each momentum magnitude in that frame, and transforms back. The paper demonstrates that the choice of pair weight matters even when every accepted collision conserves total four-momentum. Cannoni explains the distinction between the frame-dependent flux factor $g$ and the invariant relative speed $g/(1-\mathbf v_1\cdot\mathbf v_2)$. [Peano et al., *Statistical kinetic treatment of relativistic binary collisions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.1762) · [Cannoni, *Lorentz invariant relative velocity and relativistic binary collisions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.00569)

## Pressure and the energy distribution

Bazow, Denicol, Heinz, Martinez and Noronha give an analytic solution for a classical massless gas with elastic collisions and a constant cross section in a homogeneous, isotropically expanding spacetime. The energy-momentum tensor and particle current have their equilibrium forms while the distribution retains an explicit non-equilibrium energy dependence. Their solution shows why the spectrum supplies an additional test after agreement on macroscopic moments. HDblast tests massive, initially streaming particles. [Bazow et al., *Analytic Solution of the Boltzmann Equation in an Expanding System*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.07834)

A November 2026 preprint by Tie-Huai Zhang and collaborators studies cumulative Coulomb scattering. For independent deflections at fixed relative speed, their Eqs. (1)–(2) give the angular moment of order $l$ as $\langle P_l\rangle=\exp[-N(1-\widetilde\sigma_l)]$, where $N$ is the expected collision count and $\widetilde\sigma_l$ is the angular average of $P_l(\cos\theta)$ for one encounter. A rate fitted to one angular moment leaves the others undetermined. Their screened Coulomb examples illustrate how a narrow angular core can coexist with rare large deflections. Applying that interaction to the HDblast shell would require a specified coupling and screening scale. [Zhang et al., *Analytic Solution to Cumulative Coulomb Scattering in Boltzmann Theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13793)
