# Can the energy in two particle streams make a hot gas?

At the early electrical-work marker of the preceding study, the two charge signs move in opposite directions close to light speed. This calculation takes the particle momenta at that instant, turns off production, fields and expansion, and lets the particles collide in a homogeneous volume. The simulated gas contains the two charge signs for the representative beam axis in the predecessor particle calculation. Rotated copies of this axis and explicit flavor-changing collisions are outside this box. Each collision conserves particle number, energy and three-momentum. A charge label stays with its particle. The exercise asks what distribution the existing energy can support if an interaction can provide enough scattering.

With both charge signs allowed to collide, random deflections in the two-body center-of-momentum frame converted directed motion into a nearly stationary hot gas. At eight collisions per particle on average, the three principal pressures divided by energy density were **0.3255, 0.3331, 0.3414** and **0.3281, 0.3342, 0.3377** in the two small-pulse histories. An ultrarelativistic isotropic gas has a value of one third in each direction. We also compared counts in 20 broad energy and direction bins, using the beam axis to test for residual directed motion in each charge sign. The maximum difference in total variation from the predicted common equilibrium was **1.43%** in the first history and **1.37%** in the second. The maximum uses the larger of the two sign-specific distances in each history. [Numerical histories](KINETIC.json)

## Deflection angle matters

An isotropic collision emits one partner uniformly over the full center-of-momentum sphere. We repeated the calculation with the same total cross section and a direction uniform in solid angle within 30 degrees of the incoming momentum. The average $1-\cos\chi$ for this cone is $(1-\cos 30^\circ)/2$, about 0.067. The comparison keeps the event cross section fixed while reducing the average amount of direction change per encounter.

At eight of these smaller collisions per particle, the positive sign still drifted at about 0.56 times light speed in both small-pulse histories. Each population differed from the common thermal counts along the beam axis by as much as 43.5%. By 32 collisions per particle, the three total pressures were close to one third, while the two signs retained speeds near 0.17 times light speed in opposite directions. The beam-axis count test still missed by about 13%. At 128 collisions the absolute residual drift of either sign was below 0.0032 times light speed and both beam-axis count distances were below 1.30% on the fine samples. ## Collision kernel and time

Use units with particle mass and light speed equal to one. For initial lab momenta $p_i,p_j$, energies $E_i=\sqrt{1+|p_i|^2}$, and velocities $v_i=p_i/E_i$, define $g=E_iE_j-p_i\cdot p_j$. The Møller flux is

$$v_M=\frac{\sqrt{g^2-1}}{E_iE_j}.$$

Each eligible unordered pair has rate $v_M/(N-1)$ per unit of $n\sigma c t$, where $N$ counts both charge signs in this cell, $n$ is their total physical number density, and $\sigma$ is a constant event cross section. A collision involving two particles increments the participation count by two. The all-sign case permits all unordered pairs, including same-sign pairs. The same-sign case removes the opposite-sign pairs. The unlike-only case draws one particle of each sign uniformly, proposes at rate $2N_+N_-/(N-1)$ per unit exposure and accepts with probability $v_M/2$. Its only eligible charge pairing is the one allowed by the vector pole in the separate rate calculation. These controlled boxes keep a constant cross section and cannot reproduce the momentum dependence of that pole. The cross section and angular law are prescribed sensitivities. The density, particle production and scattering amplitude appropriate during the evolving pulse enter the separate encounter calculations. [Relativistic collision sampling and the Jüttner distribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.1762)

To sample very rare collisions between almost parallel particles efficiently, fix a unit axis $h$ along the initial momentum of each sign and set $\eta_i=1-v_i\cdot h$. Then $v_M\le1-v_i\cdot v_j\le2(\eta_i+\eta_j)$. The last inequality follows by bounding each squared perpendicular velocity by $2\eta_i$ and using $2\sqrt{\eta_i\eta_j}\le\eta_i+\eta_j$. The mixed experiment uses $\eta=1$ throughout. In a group of $N_g$ eligible particles, select the first particle in proportion to $\eta_i$ and its distinct partner uniformly. Draw trials with rate $2(N_g-1)\sum_i\eta_i/(N-1)$ on an exponential clock. Accept the trial with probability $v_M/[2(\eta_i+\eta_j)]$. The two orderings give precisely the desired rate for every unordered pair. We update the weights after each collision and verify the majorant throughout.

The pair has invariant mass $\sqrt{s}=\sqrt{2(1+g)}$ and center-of-momentum magnitude $k=\sqrt{(g-1)/2}$. Outgoing momenta have this same magnitude in opposite directions in that frame. They are boosted back to the wall frame. The largest relative error in a single pair's energy was $1.1\times10^{-15}$ after rounding upward on the reference machine. The largest change in any component of the whole cell's mean four-momentum divided by the initial mean energy was below $4\times10^{-16}$ at recorded endpoints. Same-sign-only trials also check the separate populations. This is a spatially homogeneous molecular-chaos collision model with equal numerical particle weights. There is no spatial transport, ongoing acceleration, inelastic channel or changing gravitational geometry in the cell.

## Thermal comparisons and numerical checks

For a lab-slice number sample in relativistic classical equilibrium, let $e$ and $q$ be the conserved mean energy and momentum, $h(\theta)=K_3(1/\theta)/K_2(1/\theta)$ be the enthalpy per particle, $u$ the spatial four-velocity and $\Gamma=\sqrt{1+|u|^2}$. They obey $q=hu$ and $e=h\Gamma-\theta/\Gamma$. Solving the scalar equation determines the unique equilibrium parameters in each simulated case. With both signs allowed the fit uses their combined mean four-momentum. If only same-sign scattering is allowed, each sign has its own conserved mean. To compare with a moving thermal gas, the calculation includes the factor $1+\beta v'\mu$ in the momentum count density on the wall time slice. We evaluate both the angle to the fitted drift and the angle to the original beam axis in its fitted rest coordinates. This second measurement reveals sign-specific counterstreaming when the fitted common drift is tiny.

The samples contain 4,096, 8,192 or 16,384 equal-weight particles per sign from independently scrambled birth times. Another collision randomization at 4,096 per sign holds the source particles fixed. There are 52 source experiments. All 12 input populations reproduce their own collisionless 20-bin benchmark before any collision. The particle count and time are fixed at the preceding study's marker of 1% of full-run cumulative absolute electrical work. In each finite stochastic box, results are sampled at the specified mean number of collision participations. Exposure is read from the continuous event clock. Independently sampling a fitted equilibrium in the archived bins uses two scrambled sequences of 65,536 and 131,072 points per sign. A separate stationarity test starts two charge tags in the same known moving Jüttner gas. Through 32 isotropic collisions per particle the largest 20-bin difference from the fitted gas for either label was 2.15 percentage points or less at 8,192 particles per sign.

On the three different small-pulse birth samples, the fitted same-sign temperatures ranged from 0.82 to 1.12. The exposure to 32 same-sign encounters in the smaller samples varied appreciably with these source moments. Qualitative inheritance of directed motion persists across samples. Mixed isotropic trials at eight encounters per particle gave minimum and maximum principal pressures between 0.323 and 0.345 in units of energy density across both histories and all recorded samples. Agreement of these coarse count tests and stresses does not measure the complete distribution or demonstrate photon production, chemical equilibrium or a cosmological era.
