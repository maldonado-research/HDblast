# What an elastic collision test can establish

Primary literature and project records checked November 21, 2026.

## Choosing collision partners in a moving gas

For a spatially homogeneous cell of physical volume $V$, let each simulated particle represent $w$ physical particles. In the cell frame, the dilute Boltzmann event hazard for a specific, distinguishable pair is

$$
\lambda_{ij}=\frac{w}{V}\,\sigma(s)\,v_{\rm M},\qquad
v_{\rm M}=\frac{\sqrt{(p_i\cdot p_j)^2-m_i^2m_j^2}}{E_iE_j},\qquad s=(p_i+p_j)^2 .
$$

Units use $c=1$. The factor $v_{\rm M}$ accounts for incident flux. For two massless counterpropagating particles it is 2. The pair's invariant relative speed is a different quantity. Xu and Greiner derive the cell probability $\lambda_{ij}\Delta t$ for small steps and explain the normalization under subdivision into test particles. Peano et al. show that incorrect momentum-dependent sampling can produce a stationary distribution different from the relativistic Boltzmann equilibrium even when every accepted collision conserves four-momentum. [Xu and Greiner, §II and Eq. 10](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/0406278#S2.E10) · [Peano et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.79.025701)

In continuous time the probability of no event along a collision-free trajectory is $\exp[-\int dt\,\sum_{ij}\lambda_{ij}(t)]$. A draw with a constant exponential rate is exact when these rates are fixed until the next event. An applied force, expansion or injection can change them between elastic collisions. A rate-bound sampler can propose events at a higher rate and retain them in proportion to the instantaneous physical rate. The bound must cover the sampled pairs and their changing invariant energies. These timing facts concern the stochastic algorithm. They do not imply a particular mean free path for HDblast. [Gillespie, exact stochastic event simulation](https://doi.org/10.1021/j100540a008) · [Xu and Greiner](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/0406278)

## Scattering directions determine more than drag

For a pure parity-even vector exchanged in the $s$ channel, with equal point-Dirac masses and unpolarized partners, the center-of-momentum distribution in $u=\cos\theta$ is proportional to $A+Bu^2$, where $A=3-2\beta^2$, $B=\beta^4$ and $\beta^2=1-4m^2/s$. This follows by setting $G_E=G_M=1$ in Dbeyssi et al.'s one-vector-current result and taking equal incoming and outgoing speeds. The normalized distribution has $\langle u\rangle=0$ and $\langle u^2\rangle=(A/3+B/5)/(A+B/3)$. The second moment goes from $1/3$ at threshold to $2/5$ in the ultrarelativistic limit. An isotropic center-of-momentum sampler has the same vanishing first angular moment but a fixed second moment of $1/3$. Distinguishing those laws matters when measuring isotropization beyond a single mean drag. This comparison specifies an isolated channel and its angular shape. [Dbeyssi et al., Eq. 14](https://arxiv.org/html/1110.6722#S2.E14)

## Testing the shape of the distribution

For classical elastic scattering, a common-rest-frame equilibrium has $f_a(\mathbf p)\propto \exp(-E/T)$, with conserved species numbers setting the separate normalizations. Energy and number supply an equilibrium comparator. The pressure anisotropy, the momentum spectrum and independent higher moments test whether the sampled distribution resembles it. Equal pressures by themselves leave the radial shape undetermined. For a locally homogeneous sample drawn with the equal-wall-time measure $f\,d^3p$, a spectrum in a moving rest frame uses $d^3p'=(E'/E)d^3p$; the corresponding histogram weights are $E'/E$. An equivalent comparison can fit $f\propto\exp(-u\cdot p/T)$ directly on the original equal-time slice. A finite sample has fluctuations even at exact equilibrium. Matched thermal reference ensembles provide a noise level for the same histogram bins and sample size. Elastic redistribution preserves each particle species. [Peano et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.79.025701) · [Ruggieri et al., §IV](https://arxiv.org/html/1312.6060#S4)

The wall's Taub–Mathews equation of state takes equality in Taub's lower bound $(h-\Theta)(h-4\Theta)\ge1$. In an isotropic massive rest-frame gas, with energies in rest-mass units, the left side equals $\langle E\rangle\langle 1/E\rangle$. Equality requires a single energy magnitude. The approximation can reproduce bulk variables of a thermal gas with small error while leaving its spread of particle energies unresolved. A collision cross section sharply dependent on energy can be sensitive to this spread. [Mignone and McKinney, Eqs. 9, 11 and 13](https://arxiv.org/html/0704.1679#S2.E11)

A September 2026 preprint by Wei, Wang and Hu gives exact time-dependent relativistic Boltzmann solutions for homogeneous, isotropic, massless mixtures with energy-independent cross sections and additional constraints on the initial distributions. It provides a possible control for moment evolution under those assumptions. The HDblast massive resonant gas has a different collision kernel. [Wei et al., September 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26845)

A spatial plasma can bend particles before their first binary collision. Bret et al. found that magnetic filaments grew from the Weibel instability in simulations of two initially unmagnetized, counterstreaming relativistic pair-plasma shells. Their 2D and 3D calculations demonstrate a concrete particle-deflection mechanism that a homogeneous wall cannot resolve. The wall has a different background field, geometry and injection history. A growth calculation on that evolving background would be needed to decide whether transverse fields grow enough to broaden its injected beam. [Bret et al., particle trajectories and shock formation](https://arxiv.org/html/1406.4144v1#S4)

A guiding field also changes which instability grows. Bret and Dieckmann found four possible leading linear modes for their cold, identical counterstreaming pair plasmas with an aligned magnetic field. Their ordering varied with beam Lorentz factor and magnetization. The gauge sectors and sign currents used here differ from their two initially neutral plasmas. A follow-up can use the evolving *kinetic* distributions as input to a linearized field-and-particle calculation, and measure how much transverse impulse the growing modes could accumulate before the 1% marker. A positive instantaneous growth rate alone leaves the seed amplitude, available growth time and saturation open. [Bret and Dieckmann](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07974) · [Required impulse for this benchmark](ballistic/KICK.md)
