# Targeted source and state literature review

Five additional primary sources were retrieved on 1 October 2026 through completed Firecrawl metadata and in-body research calls. Calls completed in approximately 1–10 seconds. This focused review complements the first checkpoint's recent-paper survey; it is not an exhaustive literature or novelty search.

1. C. Molina-París, P. R. Anderson and S. A. Ramsey (1999), [One-loop lambda phi^4 theory in Robertson-Walker spacetimes: adiabatic regularization and analytic approximation](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9908037). This is the closest retrieved formal match: fluctuations have M²=m²+lambda phi²/2. Section III gives second-order field-square and fourth-order stress subtractions. Section V, equations 38–40, explicitly verifies exchange between the mean field and fluctuations, including the subtraction terms. Its local analytical approximation cannot describe nonlocal particle production and fails in the large-mass limit; distinguish it from the full renormalized mode formalism.

2. T. Markkanen and A. Tranberg (2013), [A Simple Method for One-Loop Renormalization in Curved Space-Time](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.0180). Explicit matter/gravitational counterterms retain control of finite parts and link the scalar equation to fourth-order stress. Section 5.2 identifies terms in the compared interacting-theory adiabatic prescription not obtainable by redefining only the original couplings. This warns about specific prescriptions and finite terms; it does not establish that all adiabatic regularization is inconsistent. Its adiabatic-vacuum calculation does not solve arbitrary nonequilibrium production.

3. J. Lindig (1998), [Not all adiabatic vacua are physical states](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9808133). Adiabatic order below four generically causes initial stress singularities for RW Klein–Gordon fields. Equation 2.11 specifies both amplitude and derivative, including the −W'/(2W) term. Iteration N corresponds to derivative order 2N; low-momentum completion is still state dependent and special cancellations can occur. Do not repeat this paper's historical claim that all adiabatic states are Hadamard: the following source corrects it.

4. J. Baacke and N. Kevlishvili (2009), [Initial time singularities and admissible initial states for a system of coupled scalar fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.1128). A naive initial mass-diagonal vacuum can give t log(t) behavior in fluctuations and a 1/t stress-trace singularity. A Bogoliubov change removes the singularity; ultraviolet conditions leave infinitely many admissible states. Section 5 explains why particle number alone does not reconstruct fluctuations: anomalous correlators contribute. The main derivation is Minkowski, with FRW modifications discussed. Its terminology for a naive adiabatic vacuum must not be confused with fourth-order WKB data.

5. W. Junker and E. Schrohe (2001), [Adiabatic vacuum states on general spacetime manifolds: Definition, construction, and physical properties](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0109010). Definition 3.2 and Lemma 3.3 distinguish finite-order Sobolev regularity from Hadamard behavior and expressly correct the earlier general Hadamard assertion. Hadamard states are the infinite-order subclass. Local quasiequivalence thresholds do not automatically give thresholds for every differentiated stress observable.

## Consequences for this experiment

The correct exchange test is rhodot+3H(rho+p)=J phidot; setting the right-hand side to zero while M(phi) changes is incorrect. Vary one common effective action to obtain stress, current and finite matching terms.

Same-operator Hadamard state differences cancel state-independent local terms. Their exchange identity still contains Delta J phidot. Our fixed finite-band calculation is a tractable relative diagnostic; its spline and finite-time state data do not establish a complete continuum renormalization.

Initial amplitudes and derivatives must be specified together. Moving the initial slice and choosing a fresh vacuum changes the state, not just the solver. Resolve interpolation and ultraviolet sensitivity before making asymptotic particle-production claims.

None of these papers supplies the HDBLAST shell junctions, its bulk exchange dynamics, or its finite brane matching coefficients. Those remain part of the model-specific derivation.
