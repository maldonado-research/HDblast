**Suggested title:** HDBLAST: finite-band quantum modes, coherent stress and scalar current on an archived expanding shell

**Author:** Ricardo Maldonado. **Date:** 1 October 2026.

We evolve a real minimally coupled spectator scalar on a previously archived, source-free expanding shell in a tuned five-dimensional Einstein–scalar model (delta=0.1). The prescribed scalar-dependent mass is m²/H0²=100²(phi−0.5)². Finite-time initial and final reference states, a fixed comoving momentum band and ten numerical variants were specified before the new integration.

For the declared primary setup, the integrated endpoint occupation is 1.9544% below the matched-band Gaussian estimate and 0.0503% below its previously corrected estimate. This agreement concerns one integrated count, not a general spectrum. The relative scalar current includes a coherent contribution 10.96% of the particle-only estimate. The relative pressure is negative, while the massive particle-gas estimate is small and positive. The relative stress is not a radiation fluid.

All ten registered numerical variants pass their controls. The largest sampled paired energy-work residual is 1.73e-7 against a 1e-5 threshold. Independently implemented RK4 modes reproduce all 32 endpoint occupations within 1.9e-11 and the relative endpoint energy, pressure and current within 6.1e-8 in the stated units. A post-registration check identifies strong finite-time particle-basis dependence in the small high-momentum tail. A separate one-loop operator audit identifies additional shell potential and intrinsic-curvature terms required by the proposed quantum extension.

The deposit candidate contains source, selected raw backgrounds, registration, full numerical report, curves, figures, independent checks, primary-literature notes and SHA-256 integrity manifest. Text and data are CC BY 4.0; code is MIT.

This is a finite-band prescribed-background relative-stress experiment. It does not establish a Hadamard state, absolute renormalized source, vacuum matching coefficients, thermalization, coupled backreaction, a radiation era or a hot Big Bang. AI tools assisted the work and internal checks; these are not external peer review. No external mathematical novelty is claimed.
