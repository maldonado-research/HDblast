# When can a neutral parent supply charged particles?

## A field-dependent threshold has a known microscopic origin

Freese, Sfakianakis, Stengel and Visinelli studied a scalar inflaton decaying to fermions whose effective mass depends on a Higgs condensate. Their Eq. (20) gives the tree-level vacuum rate for a neutral scalar of mass M into a Dirac fermion and its antiparticle. For one species with interaction −y X ψ̄ψ, in units with ℏ = c = 1, the parent at rest has width Γ = y²M[1 − 4m²/M²]³ᐟ²/(8π) above M = 2m. In their cosmology the daughter mass m changes. The same kinematic inequality can change sign if the parent mass M varies, as it does in this HDblast test. An instantaneous width also assumes slow variation on microscopic time scales. Their Eq. (20) and Fig. 2 give a physical precedent for delayed decay. The masses, coupling, reservoir and duration on the HDblast wall require independent values. The factor with power 3/2 belongs to this scalar Yukawa interaction. [Freese et al., Sec. III and Eq. (20)](https://arxiv.org/html/1712.03791#S3.E20)

A threshold constrains each individual decay. Producing N pairs costs at least 2mN at production in a local inertial frame. The available parent number, the time integral of its decay probability and the momentum distribution of the daughters are separate constraints. Here a varying parent mass exchanges energy with the wall scalar even before decay. For comoving parent number Nₓ, the reversible work in a mass increment is Nₓ dM. A consistent energy budget debits that contribution to the field controlling M.

## Finite radius and finite observation time

For the round unit three-sphere the two-component spatial Dirac operator has eigenvalues ±(n+3/2), with multiplicity (n+1)(n+2) per sign for n ≥ 0. For radius a, all eigenvalues scale as 1/a. With spatially uniform mass m and no gauge background, the positive instantaneous single-particle energies are therefore Eₙ = √[m² + (n+3/2)²/a²]. The lowest candidate free pair energy is 2E₀. A homogeneous Yukawa parent also has mode-selection rules and matrix elements. This free spectrum supplies a kinematic test for an assumed width. Exact resonances, finite duration, gauge fields and occupation numbers affect the actual probability. [Fang, Levitin and Vassiliev, Sec. 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1605.08589#S2)

Cortez and colleagues give the corresponding four-component cosmological field explicitly. Their construction has two independent pairs of dynamical spinor coefficients for each spatial harmonic label. In a count for one Dirac species, it is important to specify whether the tabulated number counts particles of one sign of electric charge, both signs, or particle–antiparticle pairs. Each populated pair requires one available particle state and one available antiparticle state. An extra independent factor for the antiparticle would double-count pairs. [Cortez et al., Secs. II and III](https://arxiv.org/html/1603.00037#S2)

For a stationary background, first-order perturbation theory over a time T gives the squared integral |∫₀ᵀ exp[i(2Eₙ−M)t] dt|² = T² sinc²[(2Eₙ−M)T/2], with sinc(x) = sin(x)/x. The first zero is at an energy mismatch of 2π/T. A background that changes during the measurement calls for the integrated phase ∫(2Eₙ−M)dt and the time-dependent vertex. Smooth switching provides a check on contributions from the endpoints. A mode above the sharp threshold of an infinite-time calculation can have a finite-duration response. That response alone does not give an irreversible decay width. Boyanovsky and Herring derive time-dependent survival and quasiparticle dressing in an expanding cosmology. They compute the massless-daughter case and discuss massive thresholds as an extension. [Boyanovsky and Herring](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12343)

## A recent test of fermionic reheating

Bhusal and colleagues compared a Boltzmann treatment with fermion mode dynamics and Pauli occupancy in a model with a quartic inflaton minimum. They find that perturbative decays into fermions alone cannot complete reheating in their scenario. Their calculation shows how large daughter phase-space occupation can invalidate an apparently fast rate computed in vacuum. For HDblast, comparing the desired abundance with the available compact states and distinguishing lasting occupation from temporary excitation directly tests the chosen mechanism. The numerical limits in their paper depend on their potential and couplings. [Bhusal et al., JCAP 06 (2026) 064](https://doi.org/10.1088/1475-7516/2026/06/064)

## Checks on an assumed scattering rate

For distinguishable particles, angular-momentum unitarity limits each elastic s wave to a cross section of 4π/k², with k the center-of-mass momentum. Flores and Petraki use this normalization in Eqs. (7)–(8) of their partial-wave analysis. The inequality for a single partial wave is an optimistic benchmark for the proposed short-range channel. Long-range forces can scatter through many angular momenta. For a weakly coupled relativistic gauge plasma, Arnold, Moore and Yaffe include screened two-body collisions and effective nearly collinear splitting and merging in the leading-order transport operator. Both the state of the medium and the type of interaction therefore matter when translating an elastic collision estimate into a current relaxation time. [Flores and Petraki, Sec. 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02222#S2.E7) · [Arnold, Moore and Yaffe, Sec. II](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0302165)

The earlier closed-roll release found that changing the imposed sphere radius by 10% could change the response of scalar daughters by nearly an order of magnitude under one prescribed 16-cycle drive. Its daughter field and prescribed source differ from a pair of massive charged fermions.
