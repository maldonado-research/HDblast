# When can the same probe prepare and measure a wave?

In an acoustic model studied by Borcea, Druskin, Mamonov and Zaslavsky, a transducer emits a pulse and measures the pressure back at its own location. Equations (19)–(22) give the even time extension of the signal as a sum of cosines. Each mode carries its squared overlap with the transducer, multiplied by the nonnegative Fourier transform of the chosen pulse and a common positive normalization. Equation (35) writes the data as an inner product with the prepared profile. This provides a precedent for using common-sign modal weights when the source and readout profiles coincide. The stationary acoustic cosine propagator has a different time dependence from the de Sitter shell. [Borcea et al., *Inverse Problems* (2018), §2.1](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6420/aabb16) · [Equations in the authors’ manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/1704.08375v2#S2.E21)

The relation between an emitter and a receiver can itself be tested. Andersen, Lunde and Kocbach made three transmission measurements with piezoelectric disks in air to infer the complex transmitting voltage response and open-circuit receiving sensitivity. Their formulas include the reciprocal transducer’s impedance, propagation distances and phase. They reported preliminary characterization near the first radial mode, with useful results in the 75–125 kHz band. Javaherian’s September 2026 preprint revision gives a related mathematical example. For the modeled pressure measurement on a receiver surface, the adjoint source is a time-reversed dipole surface source up to a constant. These results illustrate why a reciprocal source and readout require a specified observable, normalization and response band. Neither experiment nor numerical model calibrates a cosmological scalar. [Andersen et al., §§2, 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.02246v1) · [Javaherian, v25, Eqs. (64), (80)](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.04466v25#S6.E80)

There is a direct distinction between observing the prepared profile and observing a wave arriving from elsewhere. In Benakli’s flat compact-interval brane model, Eq. (42) multiplies each gravitational mode by the eigenfunction evaluated at the source brane and at the receiver brane. For the two different endpoints the overlap can change sign across modes. This gives an explicit higher-dimensional example in which reciprocity between two sites permits signed weights. A common-sign condition in the shell comparison therefore needs an independent physical reason to identify the source profile with the receiver. [Benakli, v1, §§II, IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09969v1#S4.E42)

Song, Du and Xu used GW170817 strain and electromagnetic information about sky position and distance to reduce parameter degeneracies in a compactified Kaluza–Klein binary-waveform model. Their separate Taiji result uses a simulated massive black-hole binary. The observed constraints refer to corrections to gravitational radiation from a binary. Applying them to HDBLAST would require a prediction mapping the shell packet to that strain and its independent source information. The present shell readout has no such map. [Song, Du and Xu, preprint v1, §§II–V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00980v1)
