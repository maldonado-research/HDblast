## Anninos, Arias and Galante, *Umbilic Observatories*

[arXiv:2610.40783v1, 29 October 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40783v1). Primary passages: sections 2–3, equations (2.2)–(2.6), (3.5)–(3.13); section 3.2; sections 4.4–4.5, especially (4.34); section 5.

Their concrete analysis uses four-dimensional vacuum Minkowski space with a three-dimensional de Sitter boundary. Conformal conditions fix its conformal class and mean curvature; Dirichlet conditions instead fix the induced metric. Umbilicity of the background decouples a physical boundary Weyl mode at linear order. Crucially, section 3.1 allows growth proportional to exp(|tau|), independent of angular momentum: absence of increasingly fast angular growth is not absence of all boundary growth. The positive inner-region bulk-master energy has amplification factor one in (4.34); it does not include arbitrary nonlinear Einstein–scalar jets. Section 3.2 studies restricted nonlinear boundary solutions and leaves general angular nonlinear evolution open.

For HDBLAST, the paper supplies a concrete reason to examine boundary degrees of freedom, gauge versus physical normal displacements, and angularly uniform boundary energy. It neither proves nor disproves stability of the different scalar-coupled, fully umbilic shell law. Its linear energy constant cannot replace L, P1, H, C, P2, an inverse-metric margin, or the finite-amplitude remainder. No coefficient bound transfers.

## Stevens and Valiente Kroon, *Wellposedness of the initial boundary value problem for the conformal field equations*

[arXiv:2604.24931v1, 27 April 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24931v1). Primary passages: section 1.1; section 3, Assumption 1 and Lemma 1; section 5.1, equation (33); section 5.2, Proposition 2; section 6, Proposition 3; section 7; section 8.

This is a four-dimensional vacuum formulation using extended conformal Einstein equations and a boundary-adapted conformal-geodesic gauge. Proposition 2 provides a local smooth solution with compatible smooth initial/corner data and a maximally dissipative Weyl relation, `phi_0-c conjugate(phi_4)=q`, `|c|<=1`. Here the phi symbols denote Weyl-spinor components, not HDBLAST's matter scalar. Constraint propagation uses a special reduction without transverse subsidiary modes. Section 7 establishes geometric compatibility for a restricted reflective subclass; arbitrary boundary choices are not covered.

The useful comparison is procedural: HDBLAST must justify the actual incoming modes, boundary gauge consistency, corner conditions, and subsidiary uniqueness of its own scalar-coupled mixed system. Intrinsic proper-time geodesics cannot be substituted for this conformal gauge construction. The local existence time is unspecified; the paper gives no HDBLAST full-shell parameter/spacetime suprema, state-ball radius, or amplitude bound. No direct theorem transfer is justified.

This is a review of established methods. Its boundary discussion explicitly requires a suitable boundary estimate; the whole-space argument alone does not prove a scalar-coupled shell theorem. The present note retains its own mixed-boundary uniqueness and actual-speed premises. These methods are standard, and the new contribution is the selected-family support/reset bookkeeping, not a claimed new propagation theorem.
