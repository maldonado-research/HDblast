The formal coefficient, its 287-dimensional angular section space, the physical shell law and the accepted contraction estimate are retained.

The bounded searches found useful recent reconstruction/error-estimator work and reconfirmed the current versions of the geometric-boundary papers. None of the six core papers below proves a validated numerical approximation for this five-dimensional Einstein–scalar material-shell problem. Their specific transferable methods are reconstruction, error decomposition, angular identities and geometric transport checks. These are not evidence for an invariant force, a nonlinear family, a cosmological origin or an external priority claim.

## A current-version warning that matters

The current [An–Anderson III, arXiv:2503.12599v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12599v3), revised 18 June 2026, explicitly weakens the original main linearized theorem after a gap. Theorem 1.1 proves injectivity and dense range at the stated uniformly bounded-geometry backgrounds; it does not give a general onto bounded solution operator. Discussion after Corollary 1.3 reports that general energy estimates fail, including at the flat round cylinder. Therefore the title or an earlier version cannot be used to import a robust numerical inverse for conformal-mean-curvature boundary data. This is a different boundary operator from the HDBLAST material shell; no failure of the latter is inferred. Its current abstract and body were freshly retrieved as a supplemental version check.

## Concrete consequences for the numerical stage

1. **Use the accepted contraction to certify the actual reconstructed field.** For the true registered equation `U=b+TU`, a continuous candidate `V` has `||U-V||X <= ||V-b-TV||X/(1-q)`, with accepted `q=0.002109`. No external FEM theorem is needed to replace that estimate. What is needed is a reproducible enclosure of this residual on complete causal cells, reflection transitions and shell traces. A point-sampled residual does not suffice.

2. **Build reconstruction before claiming a continuous error.** A polynomial time reconstruction can explicitly repair derivative jumps, as in Dong–Mascotto–Wang. Retain radial derivative enclosures and join conditions too: HDBLAST X controls values and scaled first x/r derivatives, whereas the external displacement bounds integrate over space. A C1 time reconstruction alone does not give a classical C2 space-time differential residual. Prefer the integral residual if the representation only has the regularity that it actually certifies.

3. **Separate input and discretization errors.** Keep the accepted budget's initial-profile, source, operator-coefficient and numerical-reconstruction contributions explicit. With approximate data, use the retained formula containing `e_free`, `eps_F`, `eps_G`, `eps_A,B,D,M` and a verified candidate `V_X`. Add a separate mesh-transfer contribution whenever changing representations, unless it is already enclosed in the continuous residual. Do not add a second truncation tail when the measured full residual already contains it.

4. **Certify actual derivative approximation errors.** The transformed first metric/scalar source needs the retained correlated background and scaled-C3 master/gauge approximation errors. Magnitude caps and a C0 comparator are not those errors. Reconstructing a field does not make its reconstruction error differentiable with the desired bound. Use differentiated integral equations or a proved derivative-error estimator for the actual master dynamic boundary and gauge mixed system.

5. **Check angular algebra in the correct norm.** Form the basis Gram matrix with probability-normalized sphere measure and the positive Frobenius tensor pairing. Check the exact projected bulk/boundary operators and source reconstruction against the full retained space. LTZ provides useful independent eigenvalue/divergence/trace checks, including TT channels, but a numerically small sampled defect is not a rigorous projection certificate. Retain the accepted universal pointwise conversion factors unless a separate explicit evaluation-kernel proof improves them; no sqrt(23) substitution follows from a feature count. This checkpoint separately proves the sharp selected-space constant sqrt(321/19), with its state/error closure hypotheses documented in angular/SELECTED_SPACE_CLOSURE.md and evaluation-bound/EVALUATION_BOUND.md.

6. **Choose an observation whose error follows from X.** The existing fixed-gauge shell scalar mean `J(U)=S^-1 <w(3,0),1>` has `|J(U)-J(V)| <= 3||U-V||X/S`. This is a legitimate declared numerical diagnostic. An invariant shell response needs a geometric clock/foliation and angular weighting, plus the full metric, scalar, embedding and measure variations. Mars identifies the two gauge freedoms that such a definition must address. A shell acceleration or second-normal-derivative functional requires another regularity/observation estimate.

7. **Monitor compatible boundary and subsidiary defects.** Ge–Herzog and An–Anderson motivate checks of momentum/scalar compatibility, initial constraints, harmonic subsidiary data and differentiated corners. At approximate first fields, any accepted exact elimination must either be accompanied by an error bound against the exact identities or retain the resulting first-row defect. These checks complement the full residual; they do not establish missing physical observables.
