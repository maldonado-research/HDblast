# Can a second pulse supply more particles?

## What two pulses can change

At leading order in the coupling, amplitudes from two intervals add. For the same initial state, let $A$ and $B$ be the emission amplitudes in one final-state bin, with the initial covariance included in the inner product. Its yield after the two pulses is $\|A\pm B\|^2=\|A\|^2+\|B\|^2\pm2\operatorname{Re}\langle A,B\rangle$. Averaging opposite signs cancels the cross term. A short gate also samples a wider range of frequencies, so fringe contrast at a single momentum need not survive integration over the emitted spectrum.

Akkermans and Dunne calculated interference in electron–positron creation by prescribed alternating electric pulses. The phase between production events controls peaks in the longitudinal momentum spectrum. Greene and Kofman derived a different recurrence for Yukawa-coupled fermions at successive zeros of the effective fermion mass in an oscillating inflaton background. It retains the inter-event phase and the fermionic occupation limit. Logan, Stolarski and Yasin showed in 2026 that high-momentum resonance peaks can dominate the integrated number for weak coupling in their quartic-inflaton model. HDblast varies the coupling to a parent field on prescribed shell histories. Neither electric-field reversal nor repeated fermion-mass zero crossings are required in its calculation. These papers motivate measuring spectra and integrated counts separately. ## Who pays the energy cost?

For a closed quantum system driven by a specified parameter $G(t)$, unitary evolution gives $d\langle H\rangle/dt=\langle\partial H/\partial t\rangle$. If $H$ contains $G(t)V$, its explicit switching work includes $\dot G\langle V\rangle$, together with contributions from any other time-dependent Hamiltonian terms. On an expanding shell the gravitational expansion work also enters the energy balance. Sriramkumar and Padmanabhan showed that switching over a finite interval can excite even an inertial detector in the Minkowski vacuum. This is a control for the interpretation of switching transients. An autonomous source must account for its own energy loss. Herring and Boyanovsky evolved a scalar condensate coupled to fermions and showed explicitly how the fermion backreaction term in the condensate equation is needed for conservation of total energy. In a quartic inflaton potential, Bhusal and colleagues found that Pauli blocking and fragmentation restricted reheating by fermions alone under their assumptions. A prescribed second pulse on the HDblast shell has to be connected to a physical energy source before its yield can be interpreted cosmologically.
