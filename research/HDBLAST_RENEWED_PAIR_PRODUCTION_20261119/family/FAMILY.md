# Can tuning two shorter pulses improve the particle count at fixed energy?

We kept the original gate and varied the amplitudes of two later unit-peak pulses independently. They occupy disjoint halves of the second window, so limiting both magnitudes to one also limits the total instantaneous magnitude to one. Our score is the ratio of the number of emitted particles to the number that would be present at chemical equilibrium at the same energy, divided by that ratio for the first gate alone. A score of one is the original result. The study registers this family as an exploratory test after seeing the hard spectrum of the second pulse. [Test specification](https://github.com/maldonado-research/HDblast/issues/157#issuecomment-7017538828)

At the cutoff 120, the best signed choice on the quiet history gives a relative improvement of approximately 0.102 parts per million. The fitted strengths of the early and late half-pulses are about +0.000405 and −0.00267. At cutoff 1920 the quiet improvement falls to approximately 0.00264 parts per million, with both strengths close to zero. None of the four tested histories gains as much as 0.16 parts per million at cutoff 120 or 0.003 parts per million at 1920 in this family. These precision statements compare solutions of the stored spectral matrices. Numerical source errors and physical modeling uncertainties limit an interpretation of the tiny positive gains. The broader waveform bound appears in the [chemical criteria](../criteria/CRITERIA.md).

For $v=(1,b_1,b_2)$ the matrices give $N(v)=v^T M_N v$ and $E(v)=v^T M_E v$. We normalize each quadratic by its value at the origin, obtaining $n$ and $e$. We seek the largest $n/e^{3/4}$. An optimization from the vertices and origin gives feasible coefficients. To check a proposed upper ratio $r$, bijectively map the coefficient square to $[0,1]^2$ and write the two quadratics in the Bernstein polynomial basis. Their extrema on any box lie between their smallest and largest basis coefficients. The verifier first bounds the quadratics separately. When that test is inconclusive, it checks all Bernstein coefficients of $n^4-r^4e^3$ and subdivides the box as needed. The stored floating-point entries of the symmetrized real matrices are converted to rational numbers for this sign test. On all 16 combinations of frequency cutoff, history and sign restriction, the certified upper ratio is within approximately $10^{-11}$ of a feasible value.
