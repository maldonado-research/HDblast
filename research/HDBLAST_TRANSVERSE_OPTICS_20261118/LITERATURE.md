# Photon surfaces, bulk shortcuts, and five-dimensional optics

Checked against primary sources on 2026-11-18 UTC. This targeted screen is not an exhaustive
literature survey or a determination of global novelty. Eight sources below were read directly
in versioned primary PDFs/HTML; canonical histories were checked independently of index dates.
The established geometric results must be distinguished from their evaluated application to
the current finite-regularity, codimension-one Einstein–scalar shell.

## What applies directly

**Local null confinement is established geometry.** Perlick's Proposition 3 says that a timelike
submanifold of dimension at least two is totally umbilic if and only if every initially tangent
null geodesic remains in it for a parameter interval around its initial point [1].
Thus K_ab=αh_ab, including variable α, makes a tangent intrinsic null geodesic an ambient null
geodesic locally: the normal acceleration K(k,k)=αh(k,k) vanishes.
This is not by itself a statement about all distant endpoints, earliest arrival, normal stability,
or a ray that leaves the shell with nonzero transverse momentum.
For the present piecewise geometry the argument applies on each regular one-sided bulk region;
smooth optical equations are not silently continued across a distributional shell.

**Normal stability requires a tidal coefficient.** Koga–Harada's Propositions 1–2 express local
normal stability through R(k,n,k,n), with positive sign restoring in their curvature convention,
and decompose it as C(k,n,k,n)+Ric(k,k)/(D−2) [2].
For a generic photon surface this is not fixed by umbilicity alone.
Their warning about the component missing from Gauss–Codazzi must, however, be qualified when
the bulk field equation independently supplies Ric(k,k).

In the present codimension-one scalar system, contracting Gauss with a shell-tangent null k
removes the quadratic K terms, giving, in the registered curvature convention,

R_5(k,n,k,n) = Ric_5(k,k) − Ric_h(k,k) = (kφ)^2 − Ric_h(k,k).

The second equality uses the actual Einstein–scalar equation, not the photon-surface theorem.
Codazzi also gives R_5(k,n,k,e)=0 for a tangent screen vector e perpendicular to k.
Since ∇_k n=αk, the class of n is parallel in the null screen quotient.
Consequently the normal optical coefficient can close from the separately known scalar Ricci
source and intrinsic shell geometry. This is a direct application calculation, not a new general
photon-surface theorem. It does not determine an observable before the particular solution,
initial optical data and measurement map have been specified.

**Five-dimensional bulk optics has three screen directions.** Ortaggio–Pravda–Pravdová use
L_ij=σ_ij+θδ_ij+A_ij with θ=tr(L)/(D−2), and give the general Sachs equations [5].
For total expansion Θ=tr(L), their Eq.(15b) becomes

dΘ/dλ = −Θ²/3 − σ_ijσ_ij + ω² − Ric_5(k,k)     (D=5).

Here ω² is their nonnegative twist norm; λ is affine.
For twist-free bundles the scalar field gives Ric_5(k,k)=(kφ)^2≥0, so it contributes focusing.
The analogous intrinsic four-dimensional shell screen has dimension two and coefficient 1/2,
not 1/3. A focusing inequality alone neither locates a caustic without initial/interval data
nor determines the individual shear components or a measured magnification.

The ordinary Jacobi equation is the linearization in both position and velocity; the generalized
Jacobi equation retains velocity nonlinearities while linearizing only position [6].
A true first variation of a differentiable ray family uses the ordinary equation.
Finite beam width, finite angular spread and higher variations require their own remainder control.
Away from caustics an invertible Jacobi matrix gives L=J'J^-1 and Θ=(log|det J|)'.
With initial data respecting the shell/normal splitting, the three-dimensional bulk optical
system separates into the intrinsic two-dimensional screen block and the scalar normal block
above. A three-dimensional beam volume, sometimes called a “hyperbeam,” is not automatically
a four-dimensional observer's angular-diameter distance, flux, or detector response.

## What bulk-shortcut papers do and do not imply

Ishihara explicitly separates a vacuum pure-tension brane from additional brane matter [3].
In his conventions the latter gives K(k,k)=−κ_5²T(k,k)/2; the tension part drops out.
His local concavity argument and selected cosmological examples therefore do not justify
assigning a matter-induced shortcut to every pure-tension shell.
A scalar-dependent tension still has K∝h here; it is not the anisotropic brane stress used in
that shortcut argument. The bulk scalar Ricci source instead enters the optical closure above.

Caldwell–Langlois calculate and compare bulk and brane causal horizons for a specified FRW brane
in five-dimensional anti-de Sitter space [4].
Their Eqs.(19)–(20) agree exactly for static or de Sitter expansion, but can differ for evolving
matter-supported expansion. These are model-dependent endpoint calculations.
Neither their shortcut examples nor their de Sitter equality is a general theorem for the
current perturbed Einstein–scalar shell. Global return, earliest arrival, and comparison with
a shell-confined signal require an actual endpoint/arrival problem.

## Recent results with additional hypotheses

Caponio–Germinario–Masiello's 2026 Theorem 3.5 identifies Killing-invariant photon hypersurfaces
in a standard stationary spacetime with totally geodesic hypersurfaces of a Fermat metric [7].
This is useful when those stationarity and invariance hypotheses hold.
It is not a stationary optical reduction of an arbitrary evolving scalar shell.
The paper's massive-particle, fixed-energy and Jacobi–Randers results are separate constructions.

Anninos–Arias–Galante's 2026 “Umbilic Observatories” studies a de Sitter **three-dimensional**
timelike hyperboloid in **four-dimensional Minkowski** space [8].
Its linearized conformal/Dirichlet boundary dynamics has no frequency growth increasing with
angular momentum; the conformal boundary Weyl mode decouples from the bulk at linear order.
The mode equation and bulk energy argument are inspected in §§3.1 and 4.5.
This is a useful recent boundary-stability comparison, not a proof for our five-dimensional
scalar system, a geodesic normal-stability theorem, or an observational lensing result.
