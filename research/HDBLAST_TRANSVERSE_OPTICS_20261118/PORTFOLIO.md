The D-Blast 3 registered-shell controls in folder 152 discuss a gapped continuum of bulk gravitons as a candidate thermal energy-loss channel and identify the extra assumptions needed to use its rate. The folder 150 referee report explicitly limits its numerical evolution to the homogeneous bulk sector. Those calculations concern field energy and background stability. They do not determine the normal thickness of a null pencil. The local copies of both reports agree byte for byte with their public counterparts. The author's separate faster-than-light project assumes a signal speed in each moving emitter's frame for a flat-spacetime two-observer test. Its return-time law uses different propagation assumptions from a null geodesic in this model. The September observations review already listed gravitational-wave amplitude damping, bulk shortcuts and GW170817. That review used search snippets. Two primary papers clarify the distinction. Pardo and colleagues compare gravitational-wave and electromagnetic distances under a specified amplitude-damping law, keeping the remaining waveform fixed. Lin, Yu and Liu compute gravitational and electromagnetic horizons for a brane in a five-dimensional de Sitter bulk; in their de Sitter universe case the bulk radius supplies no arrival-time shift. Applying either approach here would require emission physics and a propagation law for the detected field. The present normal Jacobi calculation concerns a neighboring ray's separation along one registered tangent pencil. It supplies neither a measured leakage rate nor a signal returning earlier than light confined to the shell.
