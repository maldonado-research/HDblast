# Can the local shell volume mimic a radiation era?

On the complete spatial cut of the registered shell at past proper duration $r=\rho_b q$, let $V_\epsilon(r)$ be its three-volume. Define $a_V=(V_\epsilon/2\pi^2)^{1/3}$ using the volume of the unit round three-sphere. This tests the expansion history that a closed homogeneous universe would need to reproduce. The actual perturbed cut can be anisotropic.

At zero amplitude $a_0(r)=\rho_b\cosh(r/\rho_b)$. The actual cut-volume map is twice continuously differentiable in amplitude into functions with three continuous clock derivatives on the compact registered interval. The sphere isometry that reverses the quadrupole sends the full finite-amplitude cut metric to the opposite-amplitude one by pullback. Integrating its volume density gives $V_{-\epsilon}=V_\epsilon$ at every clock. Thus $a_V=a_0+O(\epsilon^2)$ uniformly with its first two proper-time derivatives. For $H_V=\dot a_V/a_V$, dots in $r$, this gives
$$
\dot H_V+H_V^2=\frac{\ddot a_V}{a_V}=\rho_b^{-2}+O(\epsilon^2),\qquad
H_V^2+a_V^{-2}=\rho_b^{-2}+O(\epsilon^2).
$$
Both background identities follow directly from the hyperbolic cosine. Reversing the time orientation preserves these expressions. Consider four-dimensional general relativity with $k=+1$, constant $G>0$, a cosmological constant of density $\rho_\Lambda$, and homogeneous radiation with $p_r=\rho_r/3$, in units $c=1$. If its scale factor exactly equals $a_V$ with the same physical clock and curvature normalization, Friedmann's equations give $H_V^2+a_V^{-2}=(8\pi G/3)(\rho_r+\rho_\Lambda)$ and $\ddot a_V/a_V=(8\pi G/3)(\rho_\Lambda-\rho_r)$. Consequently its required fraction of the total density supplied by radiation is
$$
f_r=\frac{\rho_r}{\rho_r+\rho_\Lambda}
=\frac12\left[1-\frac{\ddot a_V/a_V}{H_V^2+a_V^{-2}}\right]=O(\epsilon^2).
$$
The denominator remains positive at the zero-expansion cut. This test excludes any fraction bounded below by a fixed positive threshold, such as one half, on the registered interval at sufficiently small amplitudes. An explicit amplitude radius and the sign of the second-order correction have not been evaluated. For a general volume history the displayed diagnostic need not define a physical fluid. [Particle Data Group, Big-Bang Cosmology, §§22.1.3 and 22.1.5.2, Friedmann equations and radiation](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2026/reviews/rpp2026-rev-bbang-cosmology.pdf)

**An exact history match imposes a stronger condition.** Throughout $0\leq q/\ell\leq19/40$ the actual nonlinear observer cuts agree exactly with their background, for all admitted amplitudes. On this interval the two geometric expressions above equal $\rho_b^{-2}$ exactly, forcing $\rho_r=0$ and $\Lambda=3/\rho_b^2$ in the specified comparison. Separately conserved radiation obeys $d(\rho_r a_V^4)/dr=0$. An exact match on a connected interval containing the quiet cut would therefore require zero radiation throughout it. This is a necessary condition on the proposed homogeneous comparison. Whether the perturbed post-contact volume is exactly the vacuum de Sitter volume remains open. A consistency test for an arbitrary positive $C^3$ volume radius $a(r)$ works even at $H=0$. Set $F=H^2+a^{-2}$, $A=\ddot a/a$, $\Lambda_{\rm req}=3(F+A)/2$ and $M_{\rm req}=3a^4(F-A)/(16\pi G)$. The identity $\dot F=2H(A-F)$ gives $\dot M_{\rm req}=-a^4\dot\Lambda_{\rm req}/(8\pi G)$. On a connected interval, $\dot\Lambda_{\rm req}=0$ and $M_{\rm req}\geq0$ are necessary and sufficient for this radius to solve the closed FLRW equations with constant $\Lambda$ and separately conserved nonnegative radiation. Set $\Lambda=\Lambda_{\rm req}$ and $\rho_r=M_{\rm req}/a^4$. For a history matching the quiet cut these constants are $3/\rho_b^2$ and zero, respectively. Uniqueness of $\ddot a=a/\rho_b^2$ with the quiet-cut radius and velocity then requires $a(r)=\rho_b\cosh(r/\rho_b)$ on the entire connected matching interval. This tests the volume history. Full spacetime isotropy is a separate condition.

The background’s hyperbolic-cosine radius was previously calculated from the intrinsic shell geometry. Inferring a thermal history from the cut-volume diagnostic would require additional physics. For a candidate production interval in forward physical time $t$, let $Q(t)$ be the net source in $d\rho_r/dt+4H\rho_r=Q$. Reaching an independently measured $\rho_{r,f}$ from zero requires $\int_{t_i}^{t_f}a(t)^4Q(t)\,dt=a(t_f)^4\rho_{r,f}$. Testing that energy budget requires independent bounds on production and losses and an independent chronology linking the proposed source to the thermal epoch. The quiet cut used in the local calculation supplies no such chronology. An earlier blast, such energy transfer, inhomogeneous stress and modified Friedmann equations lie outside this local two-component test. ### Can decay of a pressureless parent evade the quiet-interval condition?

Consider a closed homogeneous GR model containing constant vacuum energy, a pressureless parent of nonnegative density $\rho_X$, and radiation of nonnegative density $\rho_r$. They may exchange energy at any internal rate. Put $F=H^2+a^{-2}$ and $A=\ddot a/a$. Friedmann's equations now give $F-A=4\pi G(\rho_X+4\rho_r/3)$. With fractions relative to total density, the geometric diagnostic measures $f_r+3f_X/4=(F-A)/(2F)$. Thus both fractions must approach zero at least quadratically with perturbation amplitude on the matched local interval. The exact quiet cut has $F=A$, which forces both densities to vanish there.

Internal transfer cancels in the combined continuity equation. In future proper time let $Y=a^3(\rho_X+\rho_r)$. Then $\dot Y=-Ha^3\rho_r$ and $|\dot Y|\le |H|Y$. For locally absolutely continuous combined density and a regular positive scale factor, integration in either time direction from a zero of $Y$ gives $Y=0$ throughout the connected interval. This condition applies even to an arbitrary time-dependent decay rate and needs neither the sign nor the size of the transfer. A nonzero hot phase connected through regular evolution would require changing at least one of these physical assumptions. For example, energy could enter the matter sector from a dynamical reservoir with different pressure, or matching to the measured shell clock and volume could cease. The argument assigns no temperature to the shell.

### Checking the volume-history diagnostic

The exact-arithmetic replay evaluates the reconstruction on six analytic examples. At the turning point of closed radiation cosmology it recovers unit radiation fraction, while at the de Sitter minimum the fraction is zero. A radius varying linearly with proper time can give an instantaneous fraction of one half while the inferred vacuum term varies in time. The latter fails the constant-vacuum test. These examples check the diagnostic and its conventions. Applying the derivative test away from the quiet interval would require the actual perturbed volume to the required precision.
