# What can a source and receiver on the wall measure?

The intrinsic angular diameter of a source uses two independent angles in the observer's sky. A five-dimensional null pencil has a third angle, obtained by tilting the initial ray out of the material wall. Its Jacobi length predicts where the tilted ray would be in the bulk. Two wall-confined photons separated by an angle in the observer's sky measure the tangential Jacobi map. Inferring the normal length from that map using the field equations is a model-dependent inference. An independent measurement would require a probe with a specified response to bulk propagation. ## A tilted ray leaves the receiver surface

In a one-sided smooth Gaussian collar write $g=dr^2+h_{ab}(r,y)dy^a dy^b$, with the material wall at $r=0$ and outward $K=\partial_r h/2$. The reflected, pure-tension model gives $K(0,y)=\kappa(y)h(0,y)$, where $\kappa=\sigma(\Phi)/6$. This uses the one-copy reflection assumption. For independent copies the general junction fixes a sum of extrinsic curvatures. For an intrinsic null geodesic with tangent $k$, the ambient normal acceleration is proportional to $K(k,k)=0$; uniqueness gives the same tangent ray in the smooth one-sided geometry. Moreover, $\nabla_k n=\kappa k$, so the unit normal is parallel in the optical screen modulo the null generator. For an affinely parametrized bulk null ray launched into this collar, write $K(r,y)=\kappa(y)h(r,y)+rB(r,y)$, with $\kappa$ extended constantly in the normal coordinate. Smoothness makes $B$ bounded on compact subcollars. The normal equation and null constraint give

$$
r''=K_{ab}\dot y^a\dot y^b=-\kappa(\dot r)^2+rB_{ab}\dot y^a\dot y^b.
$$

Let $r(0)=0$, $v=\dot r(0)\ne0$. Along the ray choose bounds $A\geq|\kappa\dot r|$ and $C\geq|B_{ab}\dot y^a\dot y^b|$. For $\eta=A\lambda+C\lambda^2/2<1$, integration and $|r(s)|\leq s\sup|\dot r|$ give $\sup_{[0,\lambda]}|\dot r-v|\leq |v|\eta/(1-\eta)$. In particular, for $\eta\leq1/3$ the ray moves to signed normal distance of magnitude at least $|v|\lambda/2$ with the launch sign. An ideal zero-thickness receiver on the wall cannot catch a distinct obliquely launched free ray on this interval. A receiver of normal half-thickness $d$ can intercept such a ray only for $|v|\leq2d/\lambda$. Uniform short intervals follow for compact sets of initial directions with a fixed frequency and smooth metric bounds. This estimate gives no numerical recrossing time for the perturbed global spacetime.

The unperturbed solution has a stronger result wherever its regular warped chart applies. With $g_0=dy^2+\rho(y)^2 h_{\mathrm{dS}}$ and $dz=dy/\rho(y)$, it is conformal to $dz^2+h_{\mathrm{dS}}$. Thus any bulk causal path projects to a causal path in $h_{\mathrm{dS}}$ between its wall endpoints. A free null geodesic has affine-linear $z$ for the product representative, and a nonzero normal launch cannot return within that chart. These statements depend on the regular warped region. A continuation across a degeneracy, an additional reflecting surface or a change of the bulk geometry needs its own analysis. There is an explicit background acceptance bound in the archived collar. For the physical affine parameter, the warped normal equation integrates to $\rho(y)\dot y=\rho_b v$ exactly, since $K=(\rho_y/\rho)h$ on each background slice. The verified interval $129\leq\rho\leq131$ includes the wall and every slice out to $z=8\ell$. Until a ray exits that collar,

$$
\frac{129}{131}|v|\lambda\leq |y(\lambda)-y_b|\leq\frac{131}{129}|v|\lambda.
$$

Thus a receiver confined to normal distance $d$ can intercept only initial tilts $|v|\leq(131/129)d/\lambda$ there. With the emitted frequency set to one, $|v|$ is the sine of the emission angle away from the wall. The factor $131/129$ is a bound on background radial variation. ## A conditional two-station intensity test

Wall-confined apparatus could in principle couple to a wave that propagates in the bulk. Consider an additional massless probe in geometric optics. Require the source to emit a calibrated smooth pencil into a one-sided bulk, the receiving apparatus to sample its grazing-ray amplitude, and the interface law to preserve the assumed transport up to known factors. For a source vertex use physical affine length $\lambda$, normalized once there, and restrict to a segment before caustics. Let $I_5=|a_5|^2$ be the squared geometric-optics amplitude after correcting the detector response. For energy flux one must also account for photon frequency and the detector motion. Assume conserved wave action $\nabla_A(|a_5|^2 k^A)=0$. If the tangential angular Jacobi map is $D_2$ and the unit normal-angle Jacobi length is $J_n$, the full area per bulk solid angle on the tangent ray is $|\det D_2|\,|J_n|$. Codazzi and umbilicity decouple the normal and tangential screen equations. At two stations along this pencil the transport law gives

$$
\frac{I_5(\lambda_2)|\det D_2(\lambda_2)|}
     {I_5(\lambda_1)|\det D_2(\lambda_1)|}
=\frac{|J_n(\lambda_1)|}{|J_n(\lambda_2)|}.
$$

The independent tangential-area measurement and a common source angular intensity are essential. In the background $|\det D_2|=\lambda^2$ and $J_n=\lambda$; at $\lambda_2=2\lambda_1$ the ratio is $1/2$. For a conserved four-dimensional massless probe confined to the shell, its corresponding intensity times tangential area is constant and the ratio is one. This compares calibrated transport laws across scales. For observations specified on a receiver's past cone one must also fix the emission normalization and convert affine length and frequency consistently when interchanging the endpoints.

A round bulk pencil alone at fixed affine length has no first-order trace change in its total area for this family: its background optical tidal matrix vanishes, and the linear focusing trace is the first variation of $\mathrm{Ric}_5(k,k)=(k\phi)^2$, which vanishes because the background wall scalar is constant. Consequently $\delta\log |\det D_2|=-\delta\log |J_n|$. Direction-dependent emission, redshift selection, source motion and interface coupling can affect measured flux. The registered shell tension and scalar junction do not specify an emitting apparatus or a tangent-limit propagator, including any surface modes, reflection or diffraction at grazing incidence. The two-station calculation is a conditional probe design.

Even a measured bulk-like propagation law can have an effective four-dimensional description. In the flat massless Neumann half-space, the decaying radial solution for Euclidean tangential momentum $p>0$ gives unit wall-source response $1/p$. Four-dimensional massive propagators give the identical linear response, since $(2/\pi)\int_0^\infty dm/(p^2+m^2)=1/p$. This elementary example uses a continuum spectrum; implementing a particular curved, time-dependent material wall and its calibrated probes requires the appropriate interface and state. The experiment could discriminate the two conserved single-probe transport laws specified above. A broad claim about dimension would require additional restrictions on fields, sources and detectors.
