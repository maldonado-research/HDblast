# Where the moving-parent calculation fits

Two earlier calculations give independent checks on the spectrum. The HDblast question is whether the published rolling-shell histories produce distinguishable daughter counts, energies or angular stresses for specified incoming scalar states. The literature already gives the underlying two-particle threshold.

## Moving parents and finite interaction times

Boyanovsky and Herring integrate over a massless fermion and antifermion of fixed total comoving momentum magnitude $q$. In their Eqs. (IV.1)–(IV.3) the density is $\rho(\omega,q)=(\omega^2-q^2)\Theta(\omega-q)/(4\pi)$ with integration measure $d\omega/(2\pi)$. This reproduces the single-branch weight $(\omega^2-q^2)/(8\pi^2)$ for $\omega>q$. Spatial momentum conservation fixes the threshold even when the time integral extends over a finite interval. Their observable is the survival probability of an adiabatically prepared scalar. Here the prepared scalar mode and the switching history enter the Fourier amplitude, and counting daughter pairs also requires specifying the reference state. [Boyanovsky and Herring, Sec. IV and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12343)

Lankinen, Malmi and Vilja write the moving-parent amplitude in Eq. (35). The passage immediately below it obtains the second diagram by conjugating the scalar mode and reversing the scalar momentum in the spinor argument. They specialize to a parent at rest in Eqs. (36)–(37). This supports retaining both frequency branches when testing a finite interaction on an expanding shell. In the HDblast number-state calculation the second branch's *excess* weight comes from subtracting vacuum production from stimulated creation in the occupied mode. [Lankinen, Malmi and Vilja, Secs. II–III](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05084)

For the phase-sensitive comparison, Hsiang and Hu's Eqs. (89)–(93) give the occupation $n=|\beta|^2$ and the pair correlation $C=\alpha^*\beta^*$ for a pure two-mode squeezed vacuum. Since $|\alpha|^2-|\beta|^2=1$, the state saturates $|C|^2=n(n+1)$. The phase of $C$ remains free at fixed $n$. These relations justify the allowed states used to ask whether occupation alone determines the shell response. [Hsiang and Hu, Eqs. (89)–(93)](https://doi.org/10.3390/e23111544)

## What would establish shell-to-radiation energy transfer

For a Dirac field coupled to a classical scalar $\Phi$, del Río et al. give the exchange identity $\nabla_\mu\langle T_\psi^{\mu\nu}\rangle=g_Y\langle\bar\psi\psi\rangle\nabla^\nu\Phi$ in their conventions. They renormalize both the stress and the scalar source. Their Eqs. (6) and (10)–(12) show how this term cancels against the scalar equation in the combined balance. The quantized parent and externally prescribed switch used here require their own interaction stress and switch-work accounting before daughter energy can be attributed to loss from the shell. [Del Río et al., Secs. II and V](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00908)

A recent comparison by Bhusal et al. evolves Dirac modes before fragmentation in a quartic inflaton model and examines Pauli suppression of decays after fragmentation. Their figures 4–5 compare fermion distributions and energy densities against a kinetic calculation. A comparable check at larger occupancy would evolve the daughter distribution as it fills. Their quantitative coupling ranges depend on their inflaton potential and initial conditions. [Bhusal et al., Secs. 3–5, JCAP (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1475-7516/2026/06/064)
