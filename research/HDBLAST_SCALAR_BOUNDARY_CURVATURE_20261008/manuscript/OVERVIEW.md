# Controlled scalar boundary response of regular de Sitter branes

**Research draft for author and external mathematical review; not submitted.** Prepared 8 October 2026. The standalone source is `scalar-boundary-curvature.tex`. A compilation claim requires the companion compilation receipt.

The draft addresses one boundary-value problem: two identical regular five-dimensional Einstein–scalar interiors joined at a de Sitter brane. It contains two complementary results.

1. **A uniform small-detuning branch with a quantitative remainder.** A weighted inverse remains bounded as the regular interior becomes arbitrarily long. A nonlinear contraction and both junction conditions construct a branch, unique in a specified small tube. Explicit sufficient inequalities give

   \[
   \left|H^2-\frac{kf_0}{3}\delta-
   \left(\frac{f_0^2}{36}-\frac{kf_1^2}{24(w-2k)}\right)\delta^2\right|
   \le K_H\delta^3.
   \]

   Exact rational checks give conservative, readable bounds for the explicitly defined registered polynomial model: **0 < δ ≤ 10⁻⁷, K_H ≤ 335**. A distinct simple model, W = 3 + (5/2)η² and f = 1 + η, has **0 < δ ≤ 10⁻³, K_H ≤ 0.0236**. Neither statement certifies the uploaded registered-model sweep at δ = 0.0005–0.002. Exact decimal input conventions are specified; the registered instance is not silently identified with a rounded binary64 parameter.

2. **An exact quadratic susceptibility at every fixed positive detuning.** Holding the stationary-point tension fixed and varying scalar coupling ε gives a locally unique analytic branch, with

   \[
   H^2=H_0^2+C_2\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^3),\qquad
   C_2=-\frac{\delta^2g_1^2[4A(D+w)-D']}{48(D+w)^2}<0.
   \]

   The finite-detuning coefficient is exact at order ε². Its nonlinear coupling radius and cubic remainder constant are existential unless independently evaluated. The explicit regular mode F = cosh(ky) for w = 5k gives a closed-form benchmark.

The first result removes the earlier small-detuning branch assumption on explicitly controlled intervals. The second result provides a different, finite-detuning response theorem; its limit alone would not prove the first. Both use standard Einstein–scalar equations and junction conditions. Neither introduces a new law of gravity.

The uniform bounds also show strictly lower curvature than the separate scalar-independent boundary model throughout the stated small intervals. That comparison concerns two different boundary theories. A constant scalar generally fails the scalar junction in the original scalar-dependent theory.
