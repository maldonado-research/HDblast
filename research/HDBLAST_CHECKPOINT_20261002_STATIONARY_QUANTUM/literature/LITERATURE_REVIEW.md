# Stationary semiclassical de Sitter branes: focused primary literature assessment

Reviewed 2026-10-02 UTC. This bounded round extends the prior DESITTER and SMOOTH_FRW reviews. Five additional primary PDFs were retrieved from explicit arXiv versions, hashed, and read at the passages identified below; three have 2025 or 2026 versions. Their full texts, extracted text, and rendered pages remain private. Companion metadata records retrieval dates, exact URLs, hashes, passage scope, and the six bounded INSPIRE searches. This is not an exhaustive search, external review, or novelty assessment.

## Consequences for the declared stationary family

A self-consistent vacuum-supported de Sitter brane is an established type of semiclassical construction. The useful advance sought here is therefore the independently verified solution of this particular massive, minimally coupled, finite-matched source and radial junction problem. A small numerical residual establishes a stationary root within the specified effective equations. It does not establish causal stability, radiative control, quantum-state preparation, energy transfer, or heating.

The new literature most directly changes three interpretations:

1. **Quantum sources can produce additional stationary branches.** Continue the root connected to the classical solution as the loop coefficient tends to zero, and report additional roots separately. A root that only exists when loop and Einstein terms balance needs its own EFT and perturbation analysis. Neither numerical convergence nor a static Hessian determines physical branch selection.
2. **Bulk and brane scales must both be screened.** For the currently declared N=1 family, increasing gamma increases the five-dimensional gravitational coupling itself. A small brane Hubble scale cannot protect a bulk region whose curvature exceeds the five-dimensional Planck scale. The accompanying dimensional note gives a diagnostic independent of physical unit normalization; its result is conditional on the actual solved geometry, not a universal EFT cutoff.
3. **Stationary Euclidean matching and causal response are different calculations.** Keep the positive reference r fixed while varying the metric and scalar source. Lorentzian perturbations require the retarded response kernel and state variation. They cannot be inferred by differentiating a Euclidean one-point curve alone.

## Primary passages checked

**S. W. Hawking, T. Hertog and H. S. Reall, Brane New World, [hep-th/0003052v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0003052v3), 13 April 2000.** Printed p.8, Sec.3.1, Eqs.3.1–3.7 displays a common Euclidean action containing bulk Einstein, Gibbons–Hawking, brane tension, and the wall CFT effective action. Two AdS5 balls are joined across a spherical wall. The Israel equation requires an effective tension 3 coth(y0)/(4 pi G5 l), and the conformal anomaly provides the additional contribution. With R=l sinh(y0), Eq.3.7 is

(R^3/l^3) sqrt(1+R^2/l^2) = N_CFT^2 G5/(8 pi l^3) + R^4/l^4.

This page was also visually inspected. The paper finds one positive radius for this specific model; neither that uniqueness nor its CFT coefficient applies to the present mass law. Its N_CFT is a gauge-group rank, and the effective number of degrees of freedom scales as N_CFT^2. It must not be identified with a count of N free real scalars. The source is a strongly coupled conformal field theory, rather than a massive minimally coupled scalar.

Printed p.14, following Eq.3.43, explicitly recovers the radius equation by varying the unperturbed action. The authors choose their finite curvature-squared convention after this variation. Printed p.16, Eqs.3.52–3.53, explicitly says the renormalization length cannot yet be set to the unknown sphere radius when varying that radius. This supports the procedural requirement to hold the declared reference fixed during variation, although it does not prescribe the present finite matching conditions. Printed p.4, Eqs.2.1–2.2, and p.16, Eq.3.50, distinguish the classical bulk/string hierarchy from the large-wall four-dimensional limit. Their Euclidean regularity and analytic continuation are additional state/boundary choices, not a general replacement for Lorentzian response.

**Paul R. Anderson, Carmen Molina-París and Emil Mottola, Cosmological Horizon Modes and Linear Response in de Sitter Spacetime, [0907.0823v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.0823v2), 22 October 2009.** Printed pp.4–6, Eqs.1.2–1.3 and surrounding derivation identify two contributions to a stress variation: a causal stress-tensor commutator response to the perturbed metric, plus independent changes in the quantum state. The local contact terms and response use the same renormalized effective action as the self-consistent background. Their criterion concerns bounded gauge-invariant solutions of the linearized equations. It is more demanding than a stationary root or scalar-only derivative test.

Printed p.18, Eqs.3.10–3.11, excludes Planck-scale trace solutions under the paper's assumed low-curvature semiclassical regime. The assumption is essential: this is not a theorem permitting the deletion of any unwanted branch at strong gravitational coupling. Their explicit de Sitter calculation treats conformal matter; the result about metric-induced sub-Planckian perturbations and additional state-dependent horizon modes cannot be transferred as a stability verdict for the present massive minimally coupled brane. In particular, their large-N suppression of graviton fluctuations (p.5) is not available in a declared N=1 run merely because gamma is large.

**Bruno Valeixo Bento and João F. Melo, EFT & Species Scale: Friends or foes?, [2501.08230v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08230v3), 14 June 2025; JHEP 05 (2025) 212.** Printed pp.16–17 specifies four-dimensional, minimally coupled scalars and a Minkowski graviton expansion. Printed pp.27–30, Eq.3.39 and Secs.4.1–4.2, distinguishes the strong-coupling estimate inferred from the propagator from the scale suppressing independently matched higher-curvature operators. The latter requires Wilson coefficients/UV information; it is not fixed by the isolated matter loop.

For N identical massless scalars, the paper's particular running-coupling definition yields Lambda_strong=M_Pl sqrt[240 pi^2/(N+240 pi^2)] (Eq.4.9), recovering N^(-1/2) for sufficiently large N. The authors explicitly require the mass to be small compared with this inferred scale, not just small compared with M_Pl. Printed pp.30–32, Eqs.4.11–4.18, treat the complementary mass regime; increasing N at fixed nonzero mass can give N^(-1/4) behavior. Thus a rough N E^2/(16 pi^2 M4^2) screen is a dimensional loop proxy, not this paper's coefficient-level result or a general threshold prescription. It is especially inappropriate to invoke a large-N species limit as the explanation for the N=1 gamma scan. The computation also does not supply a localized five-dimensional brane propagator.

**Akihiro Ishibashi, Kengo Maeda and Takashi Okamura, Instability thresholds for de Sitter and Minkowski spacetimes in holographic semiclassical gravity, [2512.00503v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00503v3), 5 March 2026; JHEP 05 (2026) 308.** Printed p.7, Eqs.3.1–3.2, gives a self-consistent four-dimensional de Sitter equation with an anomaly stress from a strongly coupled theory with a gravity dual. With y=H^2=1/ell^2 and A=G4 L^3/(8G5), their equation becomes y(1-Ay)=Lambda4/3. Algebraically, where the discriminant is nonnegative, the roots are [1 +/- sqrt(1-4A Lambda4/3)]/(2A). The minus root has the usual classical limit as A tends to zero; the plus root is a separate anomaly-supported branch. This root decomposition is a direct algebraic consequence of their displayed equation, not a claimed derivation of the present massive-source branches.

Printed pp.8,10–12, Eqs.4.1–4.2 and 4.13–4.17, define their dimensionless gamma_d, combine scale dependence with matched higher-curvature coefficients, and perform an actual perturbation analysis. Their gamma_4=G4 L^3 H^2/(pi G5) differs from N kappa5^2/L^3 here. The critical gamma and unstable perturbation masses must not be copied into this model. Printed p.25–26 explicitly treats an attempted relation to finite-brane holography as future work and notes that pushing a brane to the boundary need not interpolate smoothly between the frameworks. This is particularly relevant: an AdS-boundary semiclassical equation is not automatically the finite-radius Israel boundary condition being solved here. Their rejection of higher-curvature-dominated roots also depends on their perturbative assumptions.

**Yuri Michinobu, Kaluza–Klein tower thresholds and scheme dependence of the species scale, [2607.04602v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04602v1), 6 July 2026.** Printed pp.6–8, Sec.3, Eqs.8–19, evaluates an infinite KK tower with proper-time cutoff profiles. Eq.14 separates a leading term proportional to the effective species number with a regulator-dependent coefficient from a logarithmic term stable within the specified cutoff class. Eqs.18–19 combine local loop terms and matching terms. The paper is explicit that a single local loop coefficient cannot determine a physical higher-derivative cutoff; parametric consistency requires assumptions about matching coefficients and absence of enhanced cancellation or dominance.

This adds a current primary reason to keep the finite action and its matching conditions visible. A large stationary correction, or cancellation at a declared reference point, is not by itself a scheme-independent radiative-stability test. This is a KK-tower calculation; no KK tower is part of the current N=1 family, so its detailed coefficients and tower counting are not imported.

## Mass-law feedback and what remains to be calculated

For a one-species stationary effective density W(x,H;r) normalized so that Q=2 partial_x W, the declared quadratic mass law is

z=1+b(eta-eta_ref)/2,  x=r z^2,

with b in {-1,0,1}, x_eta=r b z, and x_etaeta=r b^2/2. For b=+/-1 this is the zero-bare-mass quadratic matter form with eta_chi=eta_ref-2/b and coupling magnitude sqrt(r)/2 in the stated dimensionless convention. The source chain rule at fixed H,r is

partial_eta (N W) = (N r b z/2) Q,

partial_eta^2 (N W) = (N/2) [(r b^2/2)Q+(r b z)^2 partial_x Q].

At eta=eta_ref, x_eta=r b and x_etaeta=r b^2/2. The quadratic Hessian above is the one relevant to this declared family. These are algebraic checks within the declared finite convention, not a new literature formula. Every local matched term belongs in W before taking the derivatives. The complete coupled Jacobian also varies the metric and bulk fields; the displayed fixed-H derivative is insufficient for branch or dynamical stability. The b=0 source derivative vanishes, but the quantum stress can remain nonzero. The strength of interactions with a propagating source depends on that source's canonical kinetic normalization, so b in {-1,0,1} alone does not determine a perturbative matter-coupling bound.

For b=+/-1, x vanishes at eta=eta_chi. The positive-mass minimally coupled de Sitter determinant and state benchmark require x>0; the massless endpoint is outside that construction unless a separate infrared/state prescription is supplied. In particular, the factor x_eta=0 at the zero of x does not establish a finite or vanishing source when Q itself is singular. The previously checked S4 zero mode gives Q approximately 3H^4/(8 pi^2 x) at fixed H>0 as x tends to zero from above. Consequently x_eta Q/2 approximately 3b H^4/(16 pi^2 z), which is singular as z tends to zero. This conditional endpoint statement does not assume that a coupled branch actually approaches that point at fixed nonzero H. The reference r stays fixed at its declared positive baseline value. It must not be replaced by the future quantum solution's Hubble scale during variation.
The stationary free-field determinant can be exact at one matter loop for fixed background/source. That does not control graviton loops, loops of a dynamical source, higher-dimension operators, or causal response after those quantities are evolved. Fixed finite matching defines a model for the present exercise; it does not measure its Wilson coefficients or demonstrate radiative naturalness.

## Search boundaries and inherited evidence

The search consisted of six one-page INSPIRE queries (limits 30 each) and direct retrieval of the five explicit arXiv versions. INSPIRE date searches can match publication dates rather than first-submission dates; returned older preprints were labeled by their actual version dates. Recent nearby titles concerning canonical ADM backreaction, Wilsonian coarse graining, brane tension cosmology, black-hole perturbations, and string uplift were screened at metadata/abstract level only. They are not cited as equation evidence here.

Starobinsky's 1980 paper, DOI [10.1016/0370-2693(80)90670-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(80)90670-X), was identified in authoritative bibliographic metadata; its body was not downloaded/read in this five-paper round. The history and analogy discussed by Hawking–Hertog–Reall are distinguished from direct verification of Starobinsky's original equations. The earlier DESITTER and SMOOTH_FRW reviews remain the provenance for the matched massive de Sitter pair, positive-mass/zero-mode limitations, variable-mass exchange identity, and earlier real-time methods. No owner-repository re-audit was performed: this round introduced no claim relying on new owner-repository content, and the prior inventory is not independent replication.

The resulting interpretation is limited to stationary mathematical solutions of a declared common action and explicit radial junction problem. It does not establish a thermal population, a measured physical normalization, a hot Big Bang, or causal dynamical stability.
