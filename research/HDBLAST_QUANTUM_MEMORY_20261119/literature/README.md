# What a reduced oscillator bath can establish

## The choice of modes determines what the approximation preserves

Chin, Rivas, Huelga and Plenio use orthogonal polynomials to map an oscillator reservoir to a semi-infinite chain with nearest-neighbor couplings. The full transformation preserves the dynamics. De Vega, Schollwöck and Wolf show how Gaussian quadrature chooses a finite set of bath frequencies and positive coupling weights. Their real-time analysis depends on bandwidth, mode count, and whether the Hamiltonian is quadratic. Woods and Plenio give finite-time error bounds for bounded system observables, with explicit dependence on the Hamiltonian and the initial bath covariance. HDblast evolves an unbounded field and uses a different quadrature variable. A numerical error for shell expansion must therefore be determined separately. In a 2026 comparison, Chen and Franco varied both mode count and cutoff and observed a finite interval of agreement for explicit bath models. [Chain mapping, Sec. 3](https://arxiv.org/html/1006.4507#S3) · [Bath discretization, Sec. III](https://arxiv.org/html/1507.07468#S3) · [Observable bound, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/1508.07354#Thmtheorem1) · [Chen and Franco, conclusions](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13103#S5)

In this study the nodes match moments of inverse squared frequency on the band from 60 to 120. For a positive measure μ of mass μ₀, an M-node Gaussian rule integrates every polynomial of degree at most 2M−1 exactly. Consequently any such polynomial p gives the bound

$$
  \left|\int f\,d\mu-Q_M(f)\right| \le 2\mu_0\,\max |f-p|.
$$

To see this, subtract p from f in the integral and in the rule. Both have positive weights totaling μ₀. This argument applies to a scalar integrand for a prescribed drive. It explains why matching many static moments can still leave a substantial error for a rapidly oscillating drive. A sign for the error requires more information about the integrand. For instance, Gaussian and Gauss–Radau rules give two-sided bounds for certain positive resolvents. That sign argument does not extend to a general oscillating drive. [Gaussian rule and choice of weight](https://arxiv.org/html/1507.07468#S2.SS2) · [Resolvent bounds, Sec. 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21505#S5)

## The vacuum specifies noise as well as response

For a linear oscillator bath in equilibrium, the same positive spectrum weights the sine kernel controlling dissipation and the cosine kernel controlling symmetrized fluctuations. The latter has an additional thermal factor that tends to one in vacuum. An excitation probability uses the **ordered** two-time correlation. Interchanging the two times can change the probability. Louko and Satz give a switched-detector example with the ordering explicit in Eq. 2.3. In the HDblast pair channel the starting spectral density comes from the massless Yukawa two-fermion phase space at leading order. Harmonic modes can represent that correlation under the stated assumptions. Higher-order fermion occupations and Pauli blocking require a fermionic calculation. [Noise and dissipation, Eqs. 4–6](https://arxiv.org/html/1004.1603#S2.E4) · [Switched quantum probe, Eq. 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/0710.5671#S2.E3) · [Pair spectrum, Eq. IV.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12343#S4.E3)

Knowing the energy in each frequency bin leaves the relative phases undetermined. For an experimental example, Wasilewski and colleagues used spectral interference to reconstruct a photon's density matrix, including correlations between frequencies. The rank prediction in this package concerns the specified single linear parent and initially empty band. A centered pure Gaussian parent permits one occupied combination at this order. Purity alone would be insufficient for an arbitrary initial state. In a separate expanding-universe study, Bhusal and colleagues found that Pauli blocking suppresses fermion production in a quartic-inflaton model and that thermalization needs additional physics. For HDblast, energy retained by a few bath modes is a statement about energy transfer. A temperature or reheating efficiency requires occupation data, interactions and expansion dynamics appropriate to those quantities. [Spectral coherence measurement](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0702200#S3) · [Quartic-inflaton calculation, JCAP 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16203v2#S5)
