## Higher-dimensional magnetic Weyl and observer dependence

Sigbjørn Hervik, Marcello Ortaggio and Lode Wylleman, [*Minimal tensors and purely electric or magnetic spacetimes of arbitrary dimension*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.3563v2), *Classical and Quantum Gravity* **30** (2013), 165014, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/0264-9381/30/16/165014), supplies the closest dimensional comparison. The actual article body was read in §3.1, Definition 3.1 and Eqs. (19)–(26), §3.2, Definition 3.4, and selected §3.4 passages around Proposition 3.13.

Their electric/magnetic decomposition is relative to a unit timelike vector. Equation (21) collects the components with one timelike index, `C_uijk`; in five dimensions this magnetic sector has 16 independent components on the four-dimensional rest space. The familiar symmetric rank-two magnetic tensor and its equivalence to the complete magnetic sector are explicitly special to four spacetime dimensions, Eqs. (25)–(26). The present symmetric tensor instead selects an oriented three-dimensional screen with an additional radial vector. It is a declared projection of the five-dimensional magnetic sector, not its full reconstruction.

This distinction prevents importing a four-dimensional scalar/FLRW magnetic-vanishing statement as a proof of the five-dimensional screen identity. The local complete `H1=0` identity must come from its own all-point calculation. A change of physical observer is also distinct from a perturbative gauge transformation: even where `C0=0`, a first observer change can act on nonzero `C1` in the second response. The cited paper's PE/PM classification and observer-uniqueness results do not assert that every magnetic projection is independent of the observer, nor do they identify this diagnostic as radiation. No classification theorem from it is needed by the numerical bound.

## The precise second-order gauge condition

Marco Bruni, Sabino Matarrese, Silvia Mollerach and Sebastiano Sonego, [*Perturbations of spacetime: gauge transformations and gauge invariance at second order and beyond*](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9609040v2), *Classical and Quantum Gravity* **14** (1997), 2585–2606, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/0264-9381/14/9/014), was checked in the primary body, §3, Eqs. (3.2)–(3.4), §4.1, Proposition 1 (p.12), and §4.2, Proposition 2, Eq. (4.9) (p.13). Its general framework is not restricted to four dimensions; its cosmological example is.

For a smooth geometrical tensor family with `H2=H''(0)`, their transformation law gives

```
H2^Y - H2^X = (L_xi2 + L_xi1^2) H0 + 2 L_xi1 H1^X.
```

Thus complete `H0=H1=0` gives a sufficient second-order Stewart–Walker type condition. Proposition 1 states the more general Lie-derivative criterion; lower-order vanishing is not the only possible sufficient case. The vanishing condition must concern the complete tensor family to which the law is applied. Vanishing of one TT projection alone does not establish the premise unless the projector and the other transformation terms have also been controlled. The original 1974 Stewart–Walker paper was not retrieved; the cited second-order result was checked in this primary generalization.

For the present application, `U`, `n`, the screen projector, orientation, evaluation surface and angular/frame prescription must form the same covariantly transforming geometric construction in both gauges. Reimposing coordinate normals in a new chart without transporting that construction does not establish this premise. A future clock/shell/radial transport prescription must also preserve the intended background observer and have a controlled first variation. A first observer change acting on full `C1` is not erased merely by the chosen screen `H1=0`. Bruni et al.'s §6 magnetic-Weyl example (p.22) is a four-dimensional scalar cosmological example, not the missing five-dimensional screen proof. The present checkpoint therefore makes no gauge-invariance claim.

## The new localized-source comparator

Geoffrey Compère and Sk Jahanur Hoque, [*Cosmological perturbations from localized sources: a gauge-invariant multipolar theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26623v1), v1 posted 19 October 2026, was checked from the locally cached API record and PDF. The abstract, introduction, §8.2, selected §10.3 passages and §11 were read. This is a selected-section comparison, not an audit of the paper's entire construction.

Their setting is the retarded linearized metric and induced cosmic-fluid response of localized sources in four-dimensional, spatially flat FLRW, with arbitrary smooth expansion history and specified past/source data. In §8.2, Eqs. (8.14)–(8.15), gauge-invariant combinations are obtained from explicit gauge transformation laws. The spatial-frame discussion at the end of §8.2 (p.55) separately fixes the multipole origin and Cartesian axes: background translations and rotations act on multipoles even when perturbative gauge invariance holds. This supports keeping gauge, physical anchor and frame conventions separate here.

Section 10.3, Eqs. (10.13)–(10.17), gives a compact-source example in which whole-space scalar/tensor projections differ outside the source while their exterior curvature contributions cancel. The paper explains that exterior measurements agree for corresponding observer worldlines and frames when the exterior geometries agree. Its global inverse-Laplacian SVT projection is a different operation from the present compact-sphere harmonic calculation; the example does not invalidate the latter. It does show why a tensor label alone cannot replace a complete local curvature or measurement calculation.

The introduction (p.5) and discussion (§11, pp.69–70) identify additional matter evolution, nonlinear couplings and specified observer motion needed for signatures or lasting imprints, and explicitly restrict the derived results to linearized perturbations. Their equations do not transfer directly to this five-dimensional Einstein–scalar model with a timelike shell and a second parameter response. No existence, uniqueness, tail or gauge theorem from that paper is used to certify this checkpoint.
