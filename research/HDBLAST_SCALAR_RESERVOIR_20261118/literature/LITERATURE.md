# Particle-production energy sources and the B1 reservoir

Primary-source check: 2026-11-18 UTC. This targeted classic/recent screen addresses
the energy source and feasibility of a field-dependent-mass particle cohort.
The papers describe mechanisms, not observations of HDBLAST or evidence for a cosmic origin.
Four versioned primary bodies were read directly; no global novelty claim is made.

## Instant preheating supplies a mechanism and a source-accounting requirement

Felder–Kofman–Linde [1] use a scalar interaction −g²φ²χ²/2 and a Yukawa decay channel.
Near a rapid mass crossing, |dot(m_chi)| comparable to m_chi² signals loss of adiabaticity.
Their §III Eqs.(3)–(4) give an occupation spectrum and number density with exponential
suppression by the bare mass relative to g|dot(phi_0)|.
This is a local crossing approximation; expansion is neglected only because the production
interval is short in their regime. It cannot be applied from a mass function alone.

After production, m_chi=g|φ| grows as the scalar moves.
Section II explicitly identifies the rolling scalar as the energy feeding that mass growth.
Eq.(10) gives the allowed fermionic decay rate Γ=h²m_chi/(8π), with kinematic restrictions.
Decay can preserve the transferred energy in daughter particles before the scalar reverses.
Without suitable decay, energy can return as the mass falls; §V discusses the possibility
that particle backreaction stops or reverses the scalar motion.
Neither a growing assigned particle mass nor an imposed decay rate is a source of free energy.

For a schematic isotropic cohort balance, distinguish three terms:

dot(rho_chi)+3H(rho_chi+P_chi) = P_production + W_mass − Q_decay,
dot(rho_rad)+4H rho_rad = Q_decay.

On adiabatic kinetic segments, W_mass=m_chi dot(m_chi)∫f(p)/E(p) d³p/(2π)³,
reducing to dot(m_chi)n_chi for a nonrelativistic cohort.
Near a nonadiabatic crossing the full mode-energy calculation is needed;
mass times number can miss the particles' creation momentum/energy.
These are bookkeeping identities for the stated approximation, not a new particle model.
The source-field/bulk/boundary balance must supply the opposite production and mass-work terms.
Pressure and cosmological dilution are the separate expansion-work terms on the left.
A nonzero nonadiabatic source Q cannot be inferred merely from pressure or acceleration.

Thus the prior B1 radiation-gain/source-integral check tests its decay-to-radiation ledger.
It does not alone close the creation-energy, mass-growth, and source-field backreaction ledger.

## A directly relevant 2026 development: conserve the interacting energy

Herring–Boyanovsky [2] derive renormalized scalar-condensate/fermion equations in a
Hamiltonian treatment. Section III.7, especially Eqs.(III.146)–(III.150), couples fermion
mode evolution to the condensate and exhibits the conserved total renormalized energy.
This supports solving the source and produced sector self-consistently, rather than
replacing dynamics by a static effective potential plus a separately imposed loss term.

Its scope is zero-temperature Minkowski space, a large-N_f Yukawa framework and
self-consistent one-loop corrections. The introduction explicitly leaves cosmological
extension, higher loops and numerical integration to future work.
The emergent entropy discussed is entanglement entropy, not a demonstrated thermal bath.
Consequently it supplies a conservation methodology, not B1 couplings, a five-dimensional
expanding solution, a calibrated temperature, or a thermalization result.

## Bulk-to-brane transfer must respect the action and junction laws

Maeda–Wands [3] derive scalar/tension/matter brane equations from a five-dimensional action.
Their Eq.(2.15) shows that matter–scalar coupling enters the scalar junction condition.
Codazzi Eq.(2.26) and the following equation give, in their conformal-coupling conventions,

D_nu tau_mu^nu = −(Omega_phi/Omega) tau D_mu phi.

The varying tension contribution cancels from this ordinary-matter divergence.
For constant Omega, ordinary matter is separately conserved in this model even when
the tension depends on the scalar. This particular trace coupling is not a universal
particle-production law; it illustrates why the selected matter action fixes the exchange.
The paper also distinguishes induced scalar/Weyl geometry from actual brane matter.

Accordingly, adding a field-dependent-mass matter sector to a pure-tension shell generally
requires rederiving its scalar source and gravitational junction rows.
One cannot assume both the original matter-free boundary law and an independent new
matter source without checking that the augmented action makes them compatible.
Normal orientations and action normalizations must be converted before copying coefficients.

Tanaka–Himemoto [4] make the channel distinction explicit.
In a Randall–Sundrum infinite-bulk, low-energy setting, a quasi-localized scalar mode
can lose energy by escaping away from the brane even without direct matter interactions.
Eqs.(18)–(19) put that loss into an effective dark-radiation component.
This is not automatically a population of photons, neutrinos or other thermal brane particles.
For explicit dissipation, Eq.(9) introduces separate brane and bulk decay terms;
Eq.(33) is the brane-matter energy-source equation and Eq.(34) carries the corresponding
opposite transfer term in the effective bulk sector.
The near-brane oscillatory description assumes mℓ≪1, Hℓ≪1 and the specified oscillatory
mode regime, not an arbitrary scalar shell. Its phenomenological decay rates are not
a microscopic derivation of the B1 interaction.

## Consequence for feasibility

The useful next check is one action-consistent balance for the actual B1 source:
identify the scalar/tension/bulk reservoir, include creation and mass-growth work,
include the daughters and any bulk escape, and show that total exchange closes with
the corresponding field and boundary backreaction.
Only then do decay efficiency, expansion, thermalization and a radiation-dominated
history become separate physical tests. None of these references assigns an observed
temperature, entropy, reheating efficiency or cosmological origin to the present model.

No additional Zenodo/GitHub/D-Blast review was warranted by this bounded source check.
The previously verified B1 radiation-transfer note remains a limited numerical consistency
result; it was not promoted to an interacting total-energy or thermal-history proof.
