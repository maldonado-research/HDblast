# What can a shell trace tell us about the source?

## Bulk data can enter a four-dimensional equation

Mukohyama eliminated the bulk master field for linear scalar perturbations of a spatially flat, positive-tension brane in an anti-de Sitter bulk. The resulting brane equation contains a term set by the incoming bulk solution and an integral over earlier brane data. Its kernel is the retarded bulk Green function evaluated on the brane. The published erratum adds the boundary condition $r\partial_\perp(G_R/r)=0$ and removes a factor of two from the integrals in Eqs. (47)–(49). An independent assumption on incoming waves changes what the brane observer can predict. Computing the corresponding kernel for the registered spherical shell would require its own bulk operator and junction laws. [Mukohyama, §IV, Phys. Rev. D 64, 064006 (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.64.064006) · [Published erratum, Phys. Rev. D 66, 049902 (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.66.049902)

Minamitsuji, Himemoto and Sasaki studied a de Sitter brane with a scalar field in the bulk. Their Eq. (8) imposes homogeneous Neumann data on the scalar. Equations (10)–(12) show how the projected five-dimensional Weyl tensor enters the intrinsic metric equation. With a tachyonic bulk inflaton, long wavelengths and a low-energy expansion, they found leading brane perturbations equivalent to those of a rescaled four-dimensional Einstein–scalar theory. This gives a physical example of similar intrinsic dynamics arising in two descriptions. Its boundary condition, expansion and wavelength regime differ from those of the HDBLAST packet. In particular, a free source in a comparison scalar equation cannot be identified with the Weyl tensor simply from the trace it produces. [Minamitsuji, Himemoto and Sasaki, §§II, IV–V, Phys. Rev. D 68, 024016 (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.68.024016)

## What could restrict the comparison source?

Kian, Liu and Yamamoto study recovery of a source from partial interior observations for evolution equations. Their Problem 1.1 restricts the source to a product of a temporal function and a spatial profile. Their uniqueness results depend on the equation, observation region, measurement time and assumptions on the factors. A signed ratio of two time averages on one spherical harmonic supplies different data. Applying their conclusions would require a new inverse problem with the shell’s actual measurements. [Kian, Liu and Yamamoto, §1, arXiv:2105.11987v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.11987v1)

Benakli’s June 2026 preprint illustrates a different measurement design. For a compact extra dimension and two branes, Eqs. (22)–(27) resolve a gravitational retarded propagator into four-dimensional massive modes. Each term has specified source and receiver overlaps and becomes propagating above its mass threshold. This provides an example of a response law tested across frequencies using the same couplings. The threshold and overlaps in that model do not determine the spherical shell’s scalar response. [Benakli, §III, arXiv:2606.09969v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09969v1)

The Firecrawl search examined primary work on projected brane equations, effective four-dimensional dynamics, retarded transfer and inverse source problems. A date-restricted query covered 1 January 2025 to 18 November 2026. These theoretical studies supply comparison models and assumptions. None of the cited studies measures either HDBLAST time window or establishes the physical origin of the prescribed packet.
