# Can one particle interaction heat an expanding HDblast wall?

The previous model began with a closed three-dimensional space already present and its particle population empty. A disturbance traveling through the fifth dimension reached that space, drove an oscillating field and supplied the conditions for charged particles to form. An electric field accelerated the particles. The model then assumed a constant rate at which collisions converted the ordered motion into heat. Here we replace that rate, from the first births onward, with the momentum transfer calculated for one fixed scattering interaction. [Previous calculation](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24407984)

**With one resonance shared by three particle species, the radius grew 2.068-fold while at least 90% of the added surface energy was in random particle motion.** Giving each species its own identical elastic channel produced 2.156-fold growth. With elastic scattering turned off, that share peaked at 55.65%. These numbers use the same pulse, masses and rule for producing charged particles. This is a numerical example within a thermal-fluid description. Each stream is assumed to have an equilibrium-shaped momentum distribution in its own rest frame. Establishing whether the newly born particles develop that distribution is an open kinetic problem. [Full evolution and controls](boost/RESULTS.md)

**The expansion eventually stopped, and the amount of hot matter is far too small for a direct cosmological interpretation.** Even converting the entire wall's added energy at turnaround into equilibrium radiation with 106.75 effective particle degrees of freedom leaves a shortfall by a factor of at least $6.4\times10^{43}$ in the dedicated-channel run compared with the photon entropy in one present-day Hubble sphere. A scenario connecting this wall to those photons would need to account quantitatively for the missing entropy and the subsequent expansion. [Entropy budget and assumptions](cosmology/BUDGET.md)

## Three checks on the particle model

1. **Where can a pair form?** Quantum exclusion limits how many identical fermions can occupy a given state. The scalar source in this model tries to emit pairs in every direction. A count averaged over directions missed short episodes where the requested direction was crowded. In the heavier reference case the direction-by-direction count blocked about 29 per million of all requested births over the measured interval. At the worst instant, immediately to one side of an electric-field reversal, it blocked about one in four. Tracking returns to previously occupied states on that fixed trajectory roughly doubled the integrated deficit. The live fluid runs separately feed the direction-dependent energy and momentum of accepted pairs back into the wall, using fresh-state capacity or a smooth alternative for joint vacancies. A full interacting occupation history remains open. [Directional calculation](science/RESULTS.md) · [Independent check](audit/AUDIT.md) · [Repeated states](theory/HISTORY.md) · [Live sources](feedback/FEEDBACK.md)
2. **When does scattering matter?** Fitting a rate only once the gas is already hot can be misleading. Turning off collisions at the start of the hot phase in the reference run barely changed its growth. A single broad spin-one resonance, evaluated at the actual temperatures and relative speeds in the live run, kept the gas hot as the radius more than doubled. At the radius doubling, random particle motion held 97.26% of the added wall energy. The microscopic parameters were held fixed during each run. Particle creation, possible inelastic reactions and equilibration have not all been derived from a single underlying theory. [Late-time comparison](scattering/RESONANCE.md) · [All-speed evolution](boost/RESULTS.md)
3. **Does hot expansion supply the photons we see?** The closed geometry and positive bulk mass limit how much added energy can reside on the wall at a given expansion rate. That sets a maximum equilibrium entropy for a specified radiation content. We compared it with the measured microwave background using both the fixed normalization of this example and a separate temperature-based test matched to measured gravity. A successful heating calculation alone still leaves this cosmological deficit. [Measured inputs and literature](LITERATURE.md) · [Computation](cosmology/BUDGET.json)

## Reproduce the calculation

From this directory, use Python 3.12, a C++17 compiler available as `g++`, NumPy and SciPy. The checked-in release manifest pins the source, reference inputs and numerical products. To verify the records or run the complete calculation into an empty directory, use

```sh
python -I -B -S study.py
python -I -B study.py --verify --output-dir /tmp/hdblast-directional-replay
```

This study tests energy transfer on a particular finite, closed geometry. A test against the early universe would also need a viable expansion and entropy history, particle content, and a predicted observation that distinguishes the proposed extra-dimensional source from alternatives. The calculations here do not identify such a signal in data. [Prospective directional question](https://github.com/maldonado-research/HDblast/issues/157#issuecomment-7054044578)
