# Births at a fixed emission angle

In the more massive example of study #192, pooling all outgoing directions gave enough empty momentum states for the entire tested source. At the same mass, charge, action and source history, directions with spare states coexist with directions that would be overfilled. Requiring an isotropic, sharp, collisionless source at each instant leaves about **29 out of every million requested daughters** without a newly crossed state during the measured interval. The calculation includes oppositely charged daughters born in back-to-back pairs. ## The direction that a pair occupies

In the local continuum approximation, let the magnitude of the comoving birth momentum be $k=a\mu\sqrt{R^2-1}$ for a parent at rest, with $\mu=mL$ and $R=M/(2m)$. A coordinate convention for the positive charge is $\mathbf K_+=k\mathbf n-b\mathbf e$, where $\dot b=\mu Q a X$ carries the sign of the electric field along the fixed axis $\mathbf e$. The negative daughter has opposite physical momentum and charge, giving $\mathbf K_-=-\mathbf K_+$. Their outward surface normals have opposite signs, and the two normal speeds agree. Each pair requires one state of each charge. Converting both the requested and available counts to pairs divides both by two. Spin selection rules and prior occupation could remove further states.

For the positive daughter define $u$ relative to the instantaneous positive electric force and set $c=\dot k$, $d=|\dot b|$. The favorable speed of empty labels crossing the birth sphere is $|c-du|$. The integral over $u$ uses $du/2$ on $[-1,1]$. In the established six-population normalization the requested total rate in wall proper time is $P=2\pi^2 S_0 \dot D_c/\mu$, with $D_c$ the comoving daughter rest energy. Dividing by the shell area and its degeneracy gives a requested normal speed $s=P/(12k^2)$. The accepted fraction in direction $u$ is $\min(1,|c-du|/s)$, giving the mean instantaneous loss
$$
\ell=\frac12\int_{-1}^{1}\left[1-\frac{|c-du|}{s}\right]_+du .
$$
Study #192 first averaged the speed over angle and then clipped the total. Clipping each direction limits a strictly isotropic source at its proposed rate. Both comparisons grant every crossing access to a vacant state on either side of the moving shell. At a second crossing the same state could already be occupied. These calculations condition on a single sharp birth momentum, the local continuum approximation, and collisionless injection with no angular redistribution.

The loss integrand is a triangle as a function of $u$. Where $d>0$ its center is $c/d$ and its half-width is $s/d$, clipped to the physical interval. Whenever $d>|c|+s$, the entire triangle fits and $\ell=s/(2d)$. A zero-speed grazing angle therefore creates a deficit on a finite angular band for any nonzero isotropic request. The missing fraction can be very small.

At $4\times10^{12}$ the clipped model admits every direction at the exact electric reversal near $\tau=6.61835$, where $|c|/s=1.2154$. The peak loss occurs on either side of the reversal. With $h=|c|/s$ held fixed within a step, the maximal loss over $d$ is $1-h$ at $d=0$ for $h\le1/2$. At $h>1/2$ it occurs at $d/s=\sqrt{(1+h)^2-2}$ and is $1/(1+h+\sqrt{(1+h)^2-2})$. These expressions follow by integrating the truncated triangle. The code maximizes over the actual range of the signed field on each step. Near the heavy-case peak $d/s\simeq1.7053$ and the grazing direction has $u\simeq0.7127$. On both time grids the peak is interpolated about $6.08\times10^{-8}$ wall time from the zero. This offset is much shorter than a native step and depends on smooth interpolation of the signed field. The native and halved grids give 25.50596% and 25.50571% for the peak, and 0.00289920% and 0.00289926% for the integrated loss. An analysis restricted to native step endpoints can miss these events.

Across the full measured interval, about 75.9% of the denied births in the heavier example fall within $|u|<0.01$, a band spanning 1% of the emission solid angle. At the smaller mass the corresponding fraction is 39.9%. The eight stored bins use direction relative to the instantaneous electric force, so their orientation follows field reversals. The positive-daughter number-weighted mean $u$ of the admitted population is $-1.5260\times10^{-6}$ in the higher-mass example. The negative daughter mirrors that direction in the same coordinate frame. The per-axis accepted angular moments are $\langle P_2\rangle=1.33586\times10^{-5}$ and $\langle P_4\rangle=-9.68689\times10^{-6}$. With equal emission on three orthogonal axes, symmetry cancels the quadrupole of the summed population while $\langle n_x^4+n_y^4+n_z^4-3/5\rangle=(2/5)\langle P_4\rangle$ survives. These are number moments of newborn particles. Late-time stresses depend on subsequent transport and on the momentum weight.

## Numerical method

The readout adds a binary write of accepted native steps to the pinned phase-averaged solver. The steps retain wall proper time, cumulative particle rest at both endpoints, birth radius in momentum space, signed electric acceleration and the position of the sphere center. Within a step $\dot k$ is the endpoint difference divided by its duration, the requested rate follows the actual change in rest, and $\dot b$ is interpolated linearly. The angular integral splits at its support, apex and histogram edges. Three-point Gauss quadrature integrates the linear tent times the angular polynomials through fourth order exactly on each angular segment. Time quadrature resolves sign and threshold crossings; wide steps near a reversal also use geometric panels across the rational tails. Native and halved time steps, interval coverage, bin sums and the prior angle-integrated results are checked.
