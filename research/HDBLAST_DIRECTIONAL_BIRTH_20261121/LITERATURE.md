# What a directional state count tests

Primary sources checked on November 21, 2026. The calculations below distinguish three physical questions. Can the decay reach a state? Is that state already occupied? Can scattering carry a particle out of it fast enough?

## A pair requires two compatible vacancies

A spin-zero parent at rest can emit a fermion and an antifermion back to back. Each pair uses one state of each charge in compatible spin channels. In a local homogeneous electric field, suppose the birth shell of the positive charge is centered at the signed comoving shift $-b\hat z$. For kinetic birth direction $\mathbf n$ and comoving radius $k$, the canonical momenta are $\mathbf K_+=k\mathbf n-b\hat z$ and $\mathbf K_-=-\mathbf K_+$. Both the shell center and the birth direction reverse for the partner, so the two partners have the same absolute normal sweep speed $|\dot k-\dot b\,\mathbf n\cdot\hat z|$. A sum over all directions can count open states in directions the assumed source does not populate. This local continuum construction also assumes the spin and angular selection rules permit the transition. Cho et al. derive opposite momenta for scalar decay and evolve a pair correlator alongside particle occupations. Aleksandrov et al. derive the momentum and spin dependence for the separate process of vacuum production in a spatially uniform electric field. [Cho et al., §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08229#S3) · [Aleksandrov et al., Eq. 106](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17204#S2.E106)

If the occupations of the partnered states are independent, their common vacancy is $(1-f_+)(1-f_-)$. For correlated states the appropriate quantity is $\langle(1-\hat n_+)(1-\hat n_-)\rangle$. For example, an isolated spin-resolved channel in a superposition of an empty state and a pair has common vacancy $1-f$ when either member has occupation $f$. Knowing only the two mean occupations bounds the common vacancy between $\max(0,1-f_+-f_-)$ and $1-\max(f_+,f_-)$. A net production formula can also contain inverse transitions or coherent interference. In a 2026 preprint Bernal and Fong explicitly evolved the momentum distribution of right-handed neutrinos as expansion opened states for further production. Their cosmology prescribes its own particles and interaction rates. [Cho et al., Eqs. 42–48](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08229#S3.E42) · [Bernal and Fong, September 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17681)

## Finite duration gives an energy distribution

At weak coupling, with constant daughter energies and slowly varying vertex, a source observed through a time window $w(t)$ gives amplitude proportional to $\int w(t)e^{i(2E-M)t}\,dt$. For a rectangular window of duration $T$, the probability profile is proportional to $T^2\operatorname{sinc}^2[(2E-M)T/2]$, with $\operatorname{sinc} x=\sin x/x$. The first zero occurs at energy detuning $|2E-M|=2\pi/T$. Near a threshold the relation between energy detuning and momentum is nonlinear. A shaped window changes the tails, and a moving field changes the phase accumulated by each pair. An artificially widened shell at unit transition probability would grant every newly counted state the full weight of a resonant state. Ramsey et al. derive a time-nonlocal Yukawa response. Logan et al. show explicitly that narrow higher-momentum resonances or a broad approximately half-filled region can dominate in different regimes of their quartic model. The width, phase and normalization of a proposed source must therefore be specified together. [Ramsey et al., §III](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/9709267#S3) · [Logan, Stolarski and Yasin, revised October 2026](https://doi.org/10.1103/p5hl-gkxv)

## Scattering must change the relevant occupations

For distinguishable partners in a single elastic s-wave the unitarity ceiling is $4\pi/k_{\rm cm}^2$ in units with $\hbar=1$. Which particles can approach this ceiling depends on their channel and relative momentum. A dilute scattering rate averages the cross section times relative flux over the partner distribution. A degenerate gas also supplies vacancy factors for both final particles. Relaxation of a current requires weighting collisions by the momentum they transfer. Clearance of a selected birth state requires following scattering into and out of that state. Gehm et al. explicitly integrated the energy-dependent unitarity cross section with Pauli factors for a trapped, two-component, nonrelativistic Fermi gas. Their result illustrates the necessary distribution dependence. For two relativistic fluids in relative motion, Asano, Iwamoto and Takahara evaluated thermal energy and momentum transfer separately using a Coulomb collision kernel. The mediator, temperatures, densities and relative drift determine the transfer in the present model. [Gehm et al., Eqs. 8–9](https://arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0304633#S2.E9) · [Partial-wave channel and symmetry conventions](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02222#S3) · [Asano et al.](https://doi.org/10.1086/509762)

## Measured photons and the early-universe clock

Fixsen combined microwave-background temperature measurements to obtain $T_\gamma=2.72548\pm0.00057$ K. The Planck 2018 cosmological-parameters analysis inferred $H_0=67.4\pm0.5$ km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ assuming spatially flat base ΛCDM. For a two-polarization blackbody the measured temperature gives $s_\gamma/k_B=(4\pi^2/45)(k_BT_\gamma/\hbar c)^3$. Multiplying by $(4\pi/3)(c/H_0)^3$ counts photons' entropy in one present Hubble sphere. That sphere is a defined comparison volume. The full particle horizon and a whole closed three-sphere have different volumes. This gives $s_\gamma/k_B=1.4792\times10^9$ m⁻³ and $S_\gamma/k_B=1.6020\times10^{88}$ in the Hubble sphere. Egan and Lineweaver independently reported $1.478(3)\times10^9$ m⁻³ and integrated over a larger observable volume. [Fixsen](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.1955) · [Planck Collaboration](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06209) · [Egan and Lineweaver, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/0909.3983#S2.T1)

For the ideal Standard Model gas with all species relativistic and in thermal equilibrium, 28 bosonic and 90 fermionic degrees give $g_{*\rho}=g_{*s}=28+(7/8)90=106.75$. At lower temperatures the species content changes, and interactions affect even the high-temperature equation of state. An absolute wall temperature or entropy estimate needs its own assumptions about species, energy sharing and volume. In standard radiation-dominated expansion, weak reactions changing neutrons into protons and vice versa fall out of equilibrium around 1 MeV, at an age of order 1 s. Efficient assembly of nuclei starts later, near 0.1 MeV in the first few minutes, when thermal photons cease to destroy deuterium faster than it can accumulate. Those temperatures and ages apply to the standard thermal history. Matching a four-dimensional expansion coefficient at one time does not calculate neutron, deuterium or helium abundances on the wall. [Husdal, ideal-gas degree counts](https://arxiv.org/html/1609.04979) · [Saikawa and Shirai, interacting Standard Model equation of state](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.01038#S3) · [Particle Data Group, *Big Bang Nucleosynthesis*, §24.1](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2024/reviews/rpp2024-rev-bbang-nucleosynthesis.pdf)

A parallel HDblast study also used the archived heavy trajectory and found about 29 unavailable births per million from the instantaneous directional constraint, with a peak shortfall of 25.5%. Its repeated-state calculation reports a lower estimate near 48 per million. The two studies share the same underlying fluid history. The separate implementation cross-checks the angular calculation. It adds no independent observational evidence.
