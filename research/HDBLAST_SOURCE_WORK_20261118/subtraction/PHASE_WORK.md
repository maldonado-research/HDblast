## Same number spectrum, different source

Set $V=a^3$, $q=\chi$, $p=V\dot\chi$, $K=k/a$ and $\omega^2=K^2+m^2$. A dot denotes $d/ds$. At the cut define $x=\sqrt{V\omega}\,q$ and $y=p/\sqrt{V\omega}$. For unit-Wronskian pure Gaussian modes define

$$
n=\tfrac12(|x|^2+|y|^2-1),\quad c=\tfrac12(|x|^2-|y|^2),\quad d=\mathrm{Re}(x^*y),\quad c^2+d^2=n(n+1).
$$

The quarter turn $(x,y)\mapsto(y,-x)$ is generated by the instantaneous oscillator Hamiltonian. Its action on each number eigenstate is a phase, preserving the entire number probability distribution and energy spectrum. It sends $(c,d)$ to $(-c,-d)$. The classical mixture with equal weights for these two Gaussian states has vanishing anomalous quadratic correlations. It agrees on all subsequent quadratic observables with the state averaged over a full uniform phase rotation, since the probe dynamics are linear. Higher correlations need not agree.

Write $d\nu=k^2dk/(2\pi^2)$ and $u=\phi-0.5$. The comoving scalar source conjugate to $\phi$ is

$$
VJ = \int d\nu\,\frac{100^2 u}{\omega}\left(n+\tfrac12+c\right).
$$

The sign here gives the work going into the probe when multiplied by $\dot\phi$. A dynamical source would feel the opposite force. In the fine-grid near-positive case, the band number at the cut is 1.5468145 and the comoving sources are 798.041 (original), 171.164 (quarter turn) and 484.603 (phase averaged). Their values relative to the instantaneous ground-state source 354.645 are respectively +443.396, −183.480 and +129.958. The original and rotated final instantaneous band numbers are respectively 3.55696 and 0.92326. These are defined by the physical Hamiltonian at the endpoint. The previous release's order-two endpoint analyzer is a different finite-time measurement.

## Sharp range over the unknown phases

Let $S(s)$ be the real two-column Hamiltonian transfer from the normalized cut coordinates $(x,y)$ to $(q,p)$, with rows $b(s)$ and $e(s)$. For a state with symmetric initial covariance $C$, the field variance is $bCb^T$. Define the symmetric two-by-two matrix for future scalar mass work of a single mode by

$$
L_W=\frac12\int_{s_c}^{s_f} V\,\dot m^2\, b^Tb\,ds,\qquad \dot m^2\equiv d(m^2)/ds .
$$

Its contribution to the work is $\mathrm{tr}(L_W C)$. Pressure work uses $L_P=-(3/2)\int h[e^Te/V-V(K^2/3+m^2)b^Tb]ds$, with $h=\dot a/a$. The endpoint energy form is $L_E=(e^Te/V+V\omega^2 b^Tb)/2$ at the endpoint, minus its value at the cut. The field equations give the matrix identity $L_E=L_W+L_P$, so the energy budget holds for every initial phase.

For any one such matrix $L$, $C=(n+1/2)I+\left(\begin{smallmatrix}c&d\\d&-c\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The possible integrated results at fixed $n(k)$ have midpoint $M$ and half-width $D$ given by

$$
M=\int d\nu\,(n+1/2)\mathrm{tr}L,\qquad
D=\int d\nu\,\sqrt{n(n+1)}\sqrt{(L_{11}-L_{22})^2+4L_{12}^2}.
$$

Each mode attains its extreme by aligning $(c,d)$ parallel or antiparallel to $(L_{11}-L_{22},2L_{12})$. These choices preserve its number distribution among phase rotations of the original centered pure Gaussian. Modes may choose their phases independently. Intermediate values follow by continuous phase rotation. Thus this interval is exact for the prescribed backgrounds and phase family when the integrals are exact. Individual extrema of different forms generally require different phases. Apply the same formula to a weighted sum of the forms to obtain its maximum. Values below are numerical quadratures and distinguishable from a validated enclosure of a continuum simulation.

## Subtraction, energy conservation and comparison across geometries

Per mode the comoving probe energy is $E=(|p|^2/V+V\omega^2|q|^2)/2=\omega(n+1/2)$. The comoving pressure is $Vp_\chi=[|p|^2/V - V(K^2/3+m^2)|q|^2]/2$. The integrated balance is

$$
\Delta\int E\,d\nu = W+P,\quad
W=\int V\dot\phi J\,ds,\quad P=-\int 3hVp_\chi\,ds .
$$

Subtracting the instantaneous ground entries $E_0=\omega/2$, $Vp_0=K^2/(6\omega)$ and $VJ_0=100^2u/(2\omega)$ gives another exact balance, since $d(\omega/2)/ds=-3hVp_0+\dot\phi VJ_0$. The excitation energy $E-E_0=\omega n$ is nonnegative at each time. Its derivative and its mass-work part can have either sign. This is a finite comoving band. Subtracting the lowest-energy value of the instantaneous Hamiltonian does not specify covariant ultraviolet renormalization.

A finite source-potential convention illustrates the difference between subtractions on one history and on distinct histories. Add an energy density $\lambda C(\phi)$, pressure $-\lambda C(\phi)$ and source $\lambda C'(\phi)$ to the reported probe ledger, with opposite reassignment to the source sector in the combined theory. Then $\Delta(V\lambda C)=\int\lambda V C'\dot\phi\,ds+\int3h\lambda VC\,ds$. Choose the even polynomial $C=[(\phi-0.5)^2-(\phi_i-0.5)^2]^2$ at the common initial field $\phi_i$ of the evolving state. Both $C$ and $C'$ vanish initially. On the nearby positive history the raw scalar-work excess over quiet from $s=0.80$ to $2.10$ is −0.35753. In the instantaneous-ground convention it is −0.36153. Adding this finite potential to the raw probe ledger shifts that excess by about $5.8552\times10^{-6}\lambda$. The corresponding coefficients are $2.2438\times10^{-5}$ for the nearby negative history and $1.4508\times10^{-5}$ for the distant positive history. Matching the initial offset and satisfying conservation on every history therefore do not by themselves fix the allocation of energy between source and probe. In a specified renormalized theory, independent measurements or physical matching conditions would fix the admissible finite terms. The complete coupled energy budget must use one consistent convention.

## Reproduction

The program reads the compact rolling shell histories in the preceding Zenodo edition. It integrates the complex modes in the canonical $(\chi,\Pi)$ variables and integrates the mass and pressure works from their derivatives. A second real transfer evolution computes the full quadratic work forms. The fine result uses 48 Gauss–Legendre momentum nodes and DOP853 with relative tolerance $4\times10^{-11}$. The comparisons include 32 nodes, a looser tolerance, and both archived bulk grid resolutions. Across the 12 histories and settings the complex-mode normalization error is below $5\times10^{-12}$. The pointwise matrix error in $L_E-L_W-L_P$ stays below $7\times10^{-7}$ at the quadrature nodes. This is a test-field calculation on supplied backgrounds.
