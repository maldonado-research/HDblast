# What the project had already established about the energy source

The November particle experiment supplies matched rolling B1 histories with and without an incoming pulse. It follows the native gas and, separately, an undecayed quantum field on the saved shell. The quiet solution already crosses the mass zero. The new question is how the scalar work and expansion work change on these particular near- and far-compensated trajectories, and how the answer changes if the quantum source acts back on the shell. Several earlier HDblast studies supply parts of that calculation. ## From an external pulse to an evolving shell

- **6 September.** The D-Blast 3 note *An exactly calculable quantum production channel* solved an external hyperbolic-secant mass pulse. Its Eq. (13) identifies the work term involving the time derivative of the squared mass and the field variance. The note explicitly asks for a brane-derived trajectory and reciprocal energy accounting. The local archive path and SHA-256 are recorded in the catalog. [Archive provenance](CATALOG.json)
- **22 September.** The D-Blast 3 folder 153 archive contains *A matter-production extension with a signed energy budget*. Varying one matter action on the shell and two gravitational interiors gives all three junctions. With the note’s outward normal, the matter contributions to $nA,nB,n\phi$ are $+\kappa_5^2\rho/6$, $-\kappa_5^2(2\rho+3p)/6$, and $-\kappa_5^2 j/2$. The matter balance is $\dot\rho+3H(\rho+p)=j\dot\phi$. The archive member is byte-identical to the preserved public derivation. The run reported an endpoint current difference of $84.61252H_0^4$, including $8.35882H_0^4$ from coherence beyond its particle-only estimate. The prior junction review makes two distinctions relevant to this calculation. A local potential placed both in the shell tension and in the quantum source can satisfy a conservation check while counting the same physics twice. An absolute continuum source also requires finite potential and intrinsic-curvature matching information. The prior one-loop analysis identifies the operators $M^4$, $M^2R$ and curvature-squared terms. A finite-band subtraction on a particular trajectory cannot by itself fix their coefficients.
