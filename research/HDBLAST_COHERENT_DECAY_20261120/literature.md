# When can a coherent neutral field supply charged fermions?

## Energy loss near a turning point

A neutral field driven from zero is a coherent oscillator with a phase. Its stored energy can be almost entirely potential when it reverses direction. Suppose a homogeneous field χ with potential V(χ,ψ) obeys χ̈ + (3H + Γ)χ̇ + ∂χV = 0. Multiplying by χ̇ shows that its energy density satisfies
$$
\dot\rho_\chi+3H\dot\chi^2=(\partial_\psi V)\dot\psi-\Gamma\dot\chi^2.
$$
Here the first term on the right is work from the rolling field, and the second is energy given to the modeled daughters. At an exact turn the local damping power vanishes even if the parent stores substantial energy. Ramsey, Hu and Stylianopoulos derive a time-nonlocal dissipation kernel for Yukawa-coupled fermions. A local friction law requires an approximation to this underlying response. [Ramsey et al., Sec. III](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/9709267#S3.E72)

One check can be done analytically. Take χ = A cos(Mt+δ) at fixed mass M, with negligible expansion, drive and depletion during a short measurement. The oscillator energy density is ρ = M²A²/2. For a constant weak width over duration T, integrating Γχ̇² gives a ratio to ΓρT of
$$
1-\cos(2\delta+MT)\,\frac{\sin(MT)}{MT}.
$$
A gate that starts at the maximum displacement gives $1-\sin(2MT)/(2MT)$, approximately $2(MT)^2/3$ for a short gate. Averaging over many oscillations recovers the familiar result. The gate's timing matters when the scalar reverses over a substantial part of the conversion interval. This identity tests the local model. A microscopic calculation can also retain the phase of the produced fermions.

## Threshold, available states and coherence

For a scalar quantum at rest with vacuum interaction −yχ f̄f and a single Dirac species, the lowest-order continuum width is $y^2M(1-4m_f^2/M^2)^{3/2}/(8\pi)$ when $M>2m_f$. The near-threshold suppression makes a narrow width compatible with a large coupling. Coupling strength and the number of available final states each need a separate check. On a round three-sphere of radius a the first free fermion momentum is $3/(2a)$. Even the lowest on-shell pair then requires $M\ge2\sqrt{m_f^2+[3/(2a)]^2}$ in the absence of a gauge background. Finite measurement time broadens stationary resonances. [Freese et al., Eq. (20)](https://arxiv.org/html/1712.03791#S3.E20) · [Fang, Levitin and Vassiliev, Sec. 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1605.08589#S2)

Pauli exclusion allows at most one fermion per individual quantum state. An incoherent kinetic equation for a decay uses final-state factors $(1-f)(1-\bar f)$ and has a separate inverse process. A coherent pair amplitude can also interfere with amplitude from an earlier passage. Greene and Kofman found bursts and phase-dependent fermion occupation when the *daughter effective mass* approached zero. That event differs physically from a turn of the *parent field* or a crossing of $M=2m_f$. Cho and colleagues explicitly compare fermion occupation from coherent quantum equations with Boltzmann decay and blocking. Logan, Stolarski and Yasin's 2026 calculation further shows that a broad occupied region and narrow higher-momentum resonances can dominate in different coupling ranges of their quartic model. Establishing a persistent abundance in the present wall therefore calls for mode occupations and a test of their backreaction. [Greene and Kofman](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0003018) · [Cho et al., Sec. 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08229#S3) · [Logan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07326)

## Earlier HDblast work and an observational check

The September D-Blast 3 shell report distinguished a zero-mass endpoint without oscillations from an interior minimum with an oscillating scalar. Its temperature estimates used a decay width with an undetermined prefactor. The later HDblast driven-parent calculation followed a neutral field from zero and reproduced a target abundance with conversion switched off. The TOE changing-mass study supplies a direct control for the fermion sector. For a mode with Hamiltonian $p\sigma_x+m_{\rm eff}\sigma_z$, the instantaneous basis rotates at half the rate of the angle $\operatorname{atan2}(p,m_{\rm eff})$. In a prescribed expanding geometry $p=k/a$, so the off-diagonal coupling has magnitude $|m_{\rm eff}\dot p-p\dot m_{\rm eff}|/[2(p^2+m_{\rm eff}^2)]$. A control with constant daughter mass on the same history measures the part from geometry. Counting final excitations also requires specifying the initial state and final measurement basis. A classical field initially at zero can also be displaced by an explicit source. These mechanisms draw on different inputs. The present test asks whether the driven field and its daughters can be evolved together while preserving their energy balance. Whether charged daughters can later represent the observed hot plasma depends on their physical masses, species, scattering and expansion history. In a specific low-reheating cosmology Barbieri and colleagues inferred $T_{\rm RH}>5.96$ MeV at 95% confidence. An October 2026 preprint by Akita and colleagues finds that changing which particles receive the energy can alter the predicted primordial neutron fraction. An internal-energy fraction on a dimensionless wall alone cannot be mapped onto either observational result. [Barbieri et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01369) · [Akita et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36062)
