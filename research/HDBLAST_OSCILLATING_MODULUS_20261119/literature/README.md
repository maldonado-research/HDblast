# Can an oscillating shell modulus make radiation?

## Which particles can receive its energy?

A brane field that sees a metric $g_J=A^2(\sigma)g_E$ couples at first order to the stress tensor trace through $(\alpha/M_P)\,\delta\sigma\,T^\mu{}_\mu$, up to the sign convention for the stress tensor. Here $\sigma$ is canonical, $M_P=(8\pi G)^{-1/2}$ and $\alpha=M_P\,d\ln A/d\sigma$ at the minimum. Brax, van de Bruck, Davis and Rhodes obtain the logarithmic derivatives of the two induced metrics in their low-energy two-brane action. They neglect higher-derivative matter couplings. Their analysis establishes how such a coupling arises in a brane model and leaves the HDblast potential and matter content to be specified. [Brane matter action and trace sources, Eqs. 47–55](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0209158#S3)

In the locally flat, weakly coupled, long-duration limit, one real massless scalar $X$ of curvature coupling $-\xi R X^2/2$ gives $\Gamma_{XX}=\alpha^2(1-6\xi)^2m_\sigma^3/(32\pi M_P^2)$. One species with $\xi=0$ gives a coefficient $1/(32\pi)$ multiplying the scaling estimate in folder 146. One with $\xi=1/6$ gives zero at this order. For the reference scalaron, $\alpha^2=1/6$, so the minimal width becomes $m_\sigma^3/(192\pi M_P^2)$. Arbuzova, Dolgov and Rudenko derive the real-scalar coefficient by two methods and use an *unreduced* Planck mass in Eqs. 53 and 69. Each independent real scalar counts once. One massive Dirac species instead gives $\Gamma_{\bar\psi\psi}=\alpha^2m_\sigma m_\psi^2(1-4m_\psi^2/m_\sigma^2)^{3/2}/(8\pi M_P^2)$ above threshold. The massless limit vanishes. Additional direct Yukawa or other interactions require their own couplings. [Independent scalar-width calculations, §§2.1–2.2 and §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.11288) · [Bernal, Rubio and Veermäe, §3 and §4.1 Eq. 40, with opposite sign convention for the curvature coupling](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.02442)

An interacting gauge theory can carry a trace anomaly, with gauge term $[\beta(g)/(2g)]F^a_{\mu\nu}F^{a\mu\nu}$ in a canonical gauge-field normalization. The resulting rate depends on the species, coupling, masses and matching across thresholds. Kamada shows how heavy thresholds decouple in a sequestered scalaron model. Shtanov's 2026 calculation combines massive loops with the anomaly in Standard Model photon production. Below all charged thresholds it gives a two-photon rate proportional to $m_\sigma^7$. Assigning a generic fixed gauge width to this brane modulus would require additional matter physics. [Kamada, §§2–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05209) · [Shtanov, JHEP 08 (2026) 095, §§3.4 and 4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09510#S4.SS4)

## Expansion, positive curvature and a finite particle measurement

For rapid coherent oscillations in $V\propto|\sigma|^n$, Turner obtains $\rho_\sigma\propto a^{-6n/(n+2)}$. A nearly harmonic minimum gives mean pressure zero when oscillations are fast compared with expansion and damping. Early asymmetric motion, the first passage through the minimum and individual oscillations can depart from this average. For a positive-curvature Friedmann universe $H^2=(\rho_\sigma+\rho_r)/(3M_P^2)-1/a^2$ in units where the curvature index is one. The curvature term dilutes more slowly than either dust or radiation. Turnaround can precede radiation equality. Kanekar, Sahni and Shtanov give the closed scalar Friedmann equation explicitly. Saha and Nandy's October 2026 scalaron preprint studies radiation build-up in a *flat* geometry in its Eq. 15. Its radiation-dominated solution supplies a comparison for depletion, while the curvature and turnaround require a separate calculation. Equality describes energy fractions. Equilibration requires particle interactions on the available timescale. [Turner, Phys. Rev. D 28, 1243 (1983)](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/5593758) · [Closed scalar dynamics, Eq. 8](https://arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/0101448#S2.E8) · [Scalaron depletion with radiation, §III](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01407#S3)

On an expanding spacetime a choice of positive-frequency modes enters a finite-time particle count. Habib, Molina-París and Mottola construct an adiabatic number basis and a renormalized stress tensor on Friedmann geometries. Their Eq. 21 gives discrete harmonics on a closed three-sphere. Dabrowski and Dunne explicitly compare finite-time particle numbers in different bases. A massless, conformally coupled scalar in the conformal vacuum supplies a zero-production control. A pulse with static before-and-after metrics admits unambiguous in/out frequencies for nonzero momentum. That controlled limit leaves the state, discrete spectrum, and stress tensor on the actual expanding closed trajectory as separate physical questions. [Friedmann harmonics and adiabatic basis, §§II–IV](https://arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/9906120#S2) · [Dependence on the adiabatic basis, §II.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1405.0302#S2.SS2)

Tomberg's 2026 preprint calculates the distribution of exit times for a stochastic field on a quadratic hilltop. Its Eq. 29 obtains the noise from the field modes at the chosen coarse-graining scale. The familiar amplitude $H/(2\pi)$ follows in the slow-roll limit. Its choice of boundary conditions also affects the exit-time distribution. Here $H_{\rm top}/(2\pi M_{\rm Pl})$ identifies the archive's reference displacement. Estimating a probability for a tuned closed bounce would require its own quantum state, smoothing scale and evolution on the closed spatial slices. [Tomberg, Eqs. 29–30](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09690#S3.E29)
