## Continuous ODE validation

Ayala, Blanco, Cakoni, Hovsepyan and Vogelius, [*On the potential lack of response in a model of second-harmonic generation. A computer-assisted proof*, 2609.23217v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23217v1), submitted 19 September 2026. PDF pp.7–8, Theorem 2.2, states an approximate-inverse radii-polynomial criterion. For Frechet-differentiable `F:X→Y` on a ball in Banach spaces and an **injective bounded** operator `A:Y→X`, one must enclose:

`||AF(p)||≤Y0`, `||I−AF′(p)||≤Z1`, and `||A(F′(x)−F′(x̃))||≤Z2||x−x̃||`.

If `(Z2/2)r²−(1−Z1)r+Y0<0` for a radius inside the declared domain, the theorem supplies a unique zero near the approximation. Theorem 3.11, PDF pp.22–23, replaces those bounds by suprema over a parameter interval. Sec.3 uses weighted Chebyshev sequence spaces and a controlled infinite tail; matching finitely many nodes is not its proof. The paper's reported optical eigenvalue certificate is not a cosmological certificate, and its particular constants do not transfer. Its practical lesson is to save a residual, an inverse-defect bound, the complete function-space/tail specification and a strict acceptance inequality.

For the present linear initial-value problem, an explicit fundamental-matrix residual bound may be simpler than constructing a nonlinear radii-polynomial proof. `RESIDUAL_AND_HALFSPACE_FORMULAS.md` derives that elementary bound and an oscillator-weighted variant. These are calculations supplied in this review, not a theorem discovered in the source or a certificate that the actual archived inputs meet its prerequisites. A frequency scale, integration precision, Wronskian check or higher-order interpolation agreement by itself supplies no continuous residual enclosure.

## A new action-based bulk dissipation construction, with a decisive caveat

Besharat, Lehner and Radkovski, [*A bulk model for dissipation and fixing EFT equations on its boundary*, 2602.13431v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13431v2), submitted 13 February, revised 6 October 2026; checked metadata: *Phys. Rev. D* 114, 076006 (2026), DOI `10.1103/wk9q-srg4`. Sec.2.1, Eqs.(2.5)–(2.10), PDF pp.5–6, derives local damping from a conservative auxiliary half-line field with action proportional to `∫[(∂tχ)²−(∂zχ)²]` and `χ(0,t,x)=φ(t,x)`. The outgoing condition `χ(z,t,x)=φ(t−z,x)` turns the action's boundary variation into `−τ∂tφ`, giving positive damping for `τ>0`.

The source **omits transverse spatial gradients and the bulk mass**, explicitly because those would gap reservoir modes and reflect low frequencies. The extra coordinate is a representation of auxiliary strings/bath modes; it is not evidence that physical spacetime has another dimension. This derivation supports retaining action boundary terms and declaring incoming/outgoing conditions. It does not supply the Lorentz-covariant massive half-space square-root kernel used in the prospective toy model.

The higher-derivative selective-damping variant has a stronger limitation. Appendix A, PDF pp.16–18, Eq.(A.16) and the following paragraph, says its chosen spectral density is not positive definite for `ω²>k²`, which precludes a physical interpretation of that bath. Its noise weight has growing directions. Its formal SK fluctuation/dissipation relation therefore cannot be imported as a positive quantum noise covariance or a unitary microscopic completion. Sec.2's gravity outline also leaves gauge fixing, constraint propagation and nonlinear well-posedness unanalysed. The stable positive Robin scalar testbed should be assessed using its own action, not certified by this selective-damping example.

## A new continuum-bath equivalence in a restricted gravity sector

Calzada and Bargueno, [*Homogeneous and Isotropic Linearized Gravity as a Caldeira–Leggett System*, 2607.28209v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28209v2), submitted 30 July, revised 16 September 2026. PDF pp.2–4, Eqs.(12)–(19), obtain a reservoir Hamiltonian by quadratic expansion and canonical diagonalization. The premises include a homogeneous isotropic nondegenerate minimum for the environmental Hamiltonian, a compatible Minkowski background, an ADM formulation, and no metric-time-derivative terms in the environmental action at the retained perturbative order. The resulting homogeneous metric mode has a **negative gravitational kinetic coefficient**; its stability is conditional and is not the positive scalar toy model's stability proof.

Eqs.(6)–(8) express friction and the initial force through the same reservoir spectral density and initial variables. Eq.(24), PDF p.4, displays the thermal quantum force correlator: its symmetrized part contains `coth(ℏω/(2kBT))cos(ωΔt)`, and its antisymmetric part contains the sine term. Quantum fluctuations persist at zero temperature; the earlier classical statement that thermal noise vanishes there does not apply to this quantum correlator.

The transferable point is specific: a quadratic reduced kernel compresses the environment into spectral data and state preparation. A matched response/noise law is compatible with a collection or continuum of four-dimensional degrees of freedom. This source's homogeneous-gravity equivalence is not a theorem about all backgrounds or all nonlinear observables, and it supplies no HDBLAST dimensional inference. The exact four-dimensional continuum replica for the half-space scalar is derived separately from its Robin modes.

## A new rigorous approximation result for non-Markovian kernels

Huang, Ding, Wang, Kaye, Li and Lin, [*Provably Efficient Long-Time Exponential Decompositions of Non-Markovian Gaussian Baths*, 2603.25708v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25708v2), submitted 26 March, revised 5 April 2026. Main-text PDF pp.2–3 and supplement pp.7–9 assume nonnegative spectral functions that are compactly supported or decay exponentially at high frequency. Each analytic segment must admit a holomorphic extension into a specified lower-half-plane semi-ellipse; endpoint singularities have a declared order `α>−1` and constants bounded in that complex region.

Supplement Theorem 1 bounds the pointwise error of a contour quadrature represented as a finite sum of complex exponentials. Corollary 2 then gives the number of terms needed for a specified `L1[0,T]` error: for positive endpoint order the asymptotic complexity can be independent of `T`; logarithmic/step and divergent endpoints have explicit polylogarithmic `T` factors. The constants, analytic domains and ultraviolet truncation error still need verification for an implemented model. Complex damped exponentials are a finite approximation; they are not an exact realization by finitely many closed free four-dimensional fields.

The unregulated Robin half-space noise spectrum decreases only as `1/|ω|` at high frequency, and hence does **not** meet the source's compact-support/exponential-decay premise. A declared regulator or an independently proved smeared ultraviolet tail is required before using this theorem. The source provides a useful future approximation design, not a ready certificate for the current toy model or stored trajectory.

## Foundational exact passages

Dvali, Gabadadze and Porrati, [*4D Gravity on a Brane in 5D Minkowski Space*, hep-th/0005016v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0005016v2), PDF pp.5–6, Eqs.(6)–(11), writes the scalar prototype action and Green-function equation. Its Euclidean resolvent is `exp(−p|y|)/(MP²p²+2M5³p)`. This is a concrete action-derived square-root/nonanalytic boundary contribution. The factor two comes from the two sides of the full line and does not transfer unchanged to a single half-space. Analytic continuation to a retarded problem must retain its causal branch. The gravity tensor sector and cosmological background have additional assumptions; this scalar formula does not supply them.

Nesterov and Barvinsky, [*Duality of the Dirichlet and Neumann problems in braneworld physics*, hep-th/0211081v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0211081v1), PDF pp.2–7, constructs a tree-level quadratic brane action from normal derivatives of the Dirichlet Green function, Eqs.(8)–(10), or from the inverse boundary-restricted Neumann Green function, Eq.(17). Eq.(18) states `[W GD W]=−GN^{-1}` with the paper's Wronskian/normal conventions; Sec.3 derives the relation for a nondegenerate second-order operator on a manifold with boundaries. The gravitational example uses a specified two-brane background and the transverse-traceless sector. This supports deriving the boundary kernel by solving the bulk problem. Its formal in-out action is not by itself a chosen retarded influence functional or noise state; one must supply causal and state prescriptions separately.

Langlois, Sorbo and Rodriguez-Martinez, [*Cosmology of a brane radiating gravitons into the extra dimension*, hep-th/0206146v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0206146v1), PDF pp.4–8, derives the signed brane continuity law `ρ̇+3H(ρ+p)=−2σ` from its Vaidya bulk and junction conditions, Eq.(10). The factor two is radiation into two copies of a Z2 bulk. Eqs.(12)–(18) then obtain a KK coupling and a thermal collision-rate energy loss, rather than assigning flux by hand. Premises include radially escaping null radiation, the `f>0` patch, thermal relativistic brane matter, `H≪T≪M5` and `T≫μ` for the stated KK approximation. The old source's dark-radiation estimate is not a current observational result and is not imported into HDBLAST. The present one-sided flat scalar toy model has a different energy ledger and no derived reheating history.

Fleming, Roura and Hu, [*Exact analytical solutions to the master equation of quantum Brownian motion for a general environment*, 1004.1603v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.1603v2), PDF pp.4–5, Eqs.(1)–(15), begins with a bilinear oscillator/bath Lagrangian. The same `I(ω)=Σcn²/(2mnωn)δ(ω−ωn)` gives the sine dissipation kernel and `coth(ω/(2T))` cosine noise kernel. It treats initially uncorrelated system/bath states, discusses a smooth switch-on and retains the initial-slip term when rewriting the memory kernel. The paper's phase-space propagator and initial terms cannot be replaced by an arbitrary local damped ODE without an approximation bound. Its frequency-renormalization and cutoff discussion reinforces that white noise, thermalization and preparation are additional physical choices. Matching the deterministic mean alone does not match the reduced probability law.
