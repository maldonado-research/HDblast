## Established constraint and energy framework

H.-O. Kreiss, O. Reula, O. Sarbach and J. Winicour,
[*Well-posed initial-boundary value problem for the harmonic Einstein
equations using energy estimates*, arXiv:0707.4188v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.4188v2),
15 October 2007. The primary introduction, Theorems 1–3 and discussion
establish energy estimates and a strongly well-posed harmonic problem for
the specified boundary class. Theorem 3 includes conditions on the boundary
matrices; the argument is not a theorem for arbitrary boundary laws.

The same authors,
[*Boundary conditions for coupled quasilinear wave equations with application
to isolated systems*, arXiv:0807.3207v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.3207v1),
21 July 2008. The primary main theorem treats its specified coupled wave
system and boundary structure, with sufficiently small smooth compatible
data. Its constraint applications make the role of homogeneous subsidiary
equations and initial/boundary constraint data explicit. The energy and
boundary assumptions must be established for any proposed application.
A later date printed by a rendered page does not make this a 2026 paper.

**Use here:** the distinction between an algebraic source identity and an
initial-boundary existence/uniqueness argument is established prior theory.
The present eight-mode wave and source checks bind a particular current
implementation to that architecture. They do not replace the retained
regularity, physical shell or subsidiary uniqueness arguments with a general
vacuum boundary theorem.

## Recent gauge study

Fan Zhang and Lee Lindblom,
[*Improved Gauge Conditions for Large Expansion Cosmological Models*,
arXiv:2610.36123v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36123v1),
26 October 2026. The primary abstract and Section II describe a generalized
harmonic numerical gauge for four-dimensional cosmological evolutions on
three exemplar spatial topologies. They report evolutions with spatial
volumes increasing by more than a million. This is a numerical gauge result
in that setting, not observational evidence for a cosmological origin.

Section II explicitly distinguishes lapse growth and coordinate conditioning
from spatial expansion, and describes metric-dependent gauge source
functions. This suggests a useful later diagnostic: test numerical gauge
conditioning separately from physical or gauge-invariant observables during
large expansion. Its dimension, background, numerical problem and boundary
conditions differ from the current five-dimensional scalar/Israel shell.

Adopting a metric-dependent gauge source would change its first and second
variations, C1 and QC, and the associated reduced operator/source formulas.
The current verified formulas cannot simply be carried over to that choice.
This checkpoint retains the present gauge and does not claim that the recent
gauge improves its errors or extends its nonlinear evolution.

## Recent reconstruction study

David A. Lowe, Yiru Wang and Juanyi Yang,
[*Gravitational Reconstruction in AdS4*, arXiv:2610.38887v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38887v1),
28 October 2026. The primary introduction and source-domain passages concern
linearized vacuum perturbations of exact AdS4 with a fixed conformally flat
conformal-boundary metric. Conservation and tracelessness, Fourier/source
restrictions, normalizability and convergence are separate requirements.
Section III explicitly identifies restrictions beyond the Ward identities.

The useful methodological comparison is to keep a reconstruction's source
class, boundary condition, gauge restoration and convergence assumptions
explicit. Its conformal boundary and vacuum AdS4 equations are different
from a finite physical scalar-dependent timelike shell in five dimensions.
Its kernels or convergence results therefore do not close the current
Einstein–scalar shell problem, and no such inference is used here.
