# Focused sources for the matched linear stress response

Prepared 2 October 2026 in America/Los_Angeles. Retrieval and verification
timestamps in the metadata are explicitly UTC. This round reread relevant
passages in five previously downloaded primary PDFs, checked their hashes,
and refreshed authoritative bibliographic/version records. Six bounded
topical INSPIRE searches screened recent work. No newly found 2025/2026
paper was sufficiently close to replace the selected sources. The 2026
Lai–Ota paper below is a useful inherited source, not a new discovery here.

The proposed task fits established real-time response and adiabatic
renormalization methods. Its useful deliverable is an independently checked
stress/current benchmark in the already declared finite prescription, at
one exact massive minimally coupled reference. This source audit provides
context and checks on interpretation. It is not an external peer review,
an exhaustive survey, a priority assessment, or a numerical stress result.
Raw responses, PDFs, extracted text, and rendered pages are excluded from
the public payload.

## Five primary passage checks

**Carmen Molina-París, Paul R. Anderson and Stephen A. Ramsey, One-loop
lambda phi^4 theory in Robertson-Walker spacetimes: adiabatic
regularization and analytic approximation,
[gr-qc/9908037v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9908037v1), 11 August 1999.**
Printed p.6, Eqs.3.10–3.11 and surrounding discussion, assign the mean-field
mass shift lambda phi²/2 to second adiabatic order while retaining a fixed
positive zeroth-order mass. Variance subtraction stops at order two and
stress subtraction at order four. Printed p.11, Eqs.5.2–5.4, separately
display the source exchange of the bare quantum stress and of its adiabatic
subtraction. Their subtraction therefore obeys the same exchange identity,
and the classical and quantum contributions exchange equal and opposite
energy. Printed p.11 was visually inspected in this round.

For x=m²+lambda phi²/2, their quantum exchange translates to
rho'+3(a'/a)(rho+p)=x' Q/2. This is the most direct structural precedent
for the proposed variable-mass Ward check. It does not say that the
prescribed quantum sector has zero divergence, or that one may omit the
external source work. The paper's self-consistent mean-field theory,
local analytic approximations, and finite choices differ from the present
fixed-r external-source calibration. Its equations support the counting
and exchange structure; the exact local coefficients and finite-band
identities still require the project's displayed calculations.

**Antonio Ferreiro, Samuel Monin and Francisco Torrenti, Physical scale
adiabatic regularization in cosmological spacetimes,
[2311.08986v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08986v2), 24 February 2024.**
Printed pp.8–9, Eqs.29–37, introduce a positive reference frequency and
explicitly repair the naive fourth-order stress subtraction under their
adiabatic assignment: Eqs.36–37 contain extra fourth-order field-square
terms needed for conservation. This is a concrete warning against choosing
density and pressure counterterms merely by UV convergence or by copying
only a variance subtraction. Printed pp.13–14, Eqs.66–69, give the BD
Hankel modes and separate minimal density and pressure spectra.

At xi=0 and m²=2H² their mode equation has Hankel index 1/2. The canonical
frequency is then k, but the minimal stress still contains scale-factor
derivatives and the physical mass. This elementary specialization supplies
no reason to use conformally improved stress. The paper's final physical
scale prescription and its explicit adiabatic counting differ from the
inherited split x=r+h with fixed positive r. Its conservation repair must
not be imported as an extra contact on top of an already matched action.
Instead check the complete inherited expansion and its Ward identity.

**Tommi Markkanen and Arttu Rajantie, Massive scalar field evolution in de
Sitter, [1607.00334v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00334v3), 26 January 2017.**
Printed pp.9–12, Eqs.3.16–3.22, discuss cutoff boundary terms, combining
mode-by-mode adiabatic subtractions before integration, the BD equation of
state, and subtraction of the equilibrium expectation value. These passages
were already checked in the de Sitter checkpoint and were read again.
Printed p.13, Eqs.4.8–4.9, was additionally checked here: both density and
pressure contain coherent terms proportional to Re(C1 C2*), separately
from terms proportional to |C2|². The coherent stress can be linear in a
small Bogoliubov perturbation while a particle occupation starts at second
order. Their explicit formulas include the coupling dependence and cannot
be reduced to a positive particle-energy density without further assumptions.

Their cutoff discussion initially uses a fixed physical momentum cutoff.
The proposed finite-band test holds a comoving K fixed, so the associated
time derivative has different endpoint terms. Neither finite-band result
may be replaced by the removed-cutoff equation of state. Their eventual
equilibrium-difference prescription does not fix the absolute finite
reference variance or vacuum stress used in the present common action.
Their equilibration/backreaction conclusions are not a stability verdict
for the HDblast shell.

**Paul R. Anderson, Carmen Molina-París and Emil Mottola, Cosmological
Horizon Modes and Linear Response in de Sitter Spacetime,

Printed pp.4–6, Eqs.1.2–1.3, were read again. The stress response is a

The response and background use the same renormalized action and specified

Their actual perturbation is a metric perturbation, and their explicit de

coefficient for the massive minimal reference. In particular the missing

be replaced by the homogeneous variance or stress test. The prior causal
review remains the provenance for this paper's Appendix A finite-part

**Han Lai and Atsuhisa Ota, Ultraviolet Structure of Real-time Gravitational

opposite signs in density and pressure. The closed formulas written in
terms of delta Q then redistribute terms using the forced-mode equation;
the pressure coefficient −Q0 delta x/6 does not replace the bare explicit

generated when delta Q'' is reduced by that equation.

contacts are required. This is an exact algebra check under the displayed
prescription, not a new stress integration or a verification of a numerical
implementation. The trace and density/pressure contacts also agree in the
continuum, and the stationary derivative is a separate past-infinite

Neither the literature nor the passed variance calibration supplies a

responses and distinguish quadrature, mode, cutoff, and cancellation
errors. Constructing pressure from the same Ward identity later called a
test would only check bookkeeping; the direct stress and an independently
evaluated ledger are needed. No cited result establishes linear stress as
particle yield, positive radiation energy, heating, thermalization, or
coupled shell stability.

## Search outcome and limits

Six one-page INSPIRE topical queries and five targeted bibliographic lookups
were performed, followed by five arXiv version-history checks. The broad
linear-response query returned 54 total records; only its first 25 were
screened. Other topical searches returned at most 21 records. A September
2026 photon-gas paper was screened at abstract level and excluded: it uses
Boltzmann relaxation-time transport rather than vacuum scalar stress
renormalization. Modified-dispersion adiabatic renormalization, spinor
vacuum stress, compact-dimension stress, AdS energy conditions, and graviton
Hadamard renormalization were likewise outside the bounded benchmark or
already inherited pointers; they were not used as primary equation evidence.

Date filters sometimes retrieved older preprints by publication date. The
search does not establish an absence of closer work, a new discovery, or
priority. All metadata requests succeeded and the five reused PDFs matched
their earlier recorded hashes. No new PDF download, external referee
review, private-repository audit, or physical numerical evaluation was
performed. There is no access blocker for the selected primary evidence.
