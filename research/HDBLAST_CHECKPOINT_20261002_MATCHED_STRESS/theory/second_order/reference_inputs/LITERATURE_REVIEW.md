# Focused literature check for the smooth curved-source control

Eight primary arXiv PDFs were downloaded over verified HTTPS and their relevant methods sections inspected. Two variable-mass action papers received a separate independent reading of their derivations and rendered equation pages. Four bounded arXiv metadata searches screened recent methods; subsequent timeout and rate-limit failures were retained. This is a targeted methods review, not an exhaustive survey, external referee report, or priority assessment.

## Primary passages checked in this round

**Antonio Ferreiro, Samuel Monin and Francisco Torrenti, Physical scale adiabatic regularization in cosmological spacetimes, [2311.08986v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08986v2), 2024; first submitted 2023.** Section IIIA, pp. 8–10, Eqs. 29–37 introduces a reference frequency and explicitly corrects a nonconserved naive stress expansion under its assignment that explicit m² and mu² are order two. Eqs. 36–37 add the required fourth-order mode-square pieces. Eqs. 48–53 separate reference scales at different derivative orders. Section IV, pp. 11–12, Eqs. 54–64 gives finite coupling conditions, local geometric shifts, and a matched trace.

That bookkeeping caveat matters: this checkpoint splits x=r+h with r at order zero and h at order two, retaining the r-dependent quadratic-mode terms in its fourth-order stress. Its graded Ward identities and action bridge must be checked directly; the paper does not say every naive reference-mass expansion conserves stress. The final PSAR prescription also fixes its zeroth-order reference to a constant physical mass to impose zero Minkowski vacuum stress. This differs from keeping r positive through a variable-x zero crossing and declaring a finite matched potential. The paper does not supply this checkpoint's complete variable-x source pair.

**Antonio Ferreiro and Jose Navarro-Salas, Running couplings from adiabatic regularization, [1812.05564v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05564v2), 2019; first submitted 2018.** Section II, pp. 2–3, Eqs. 7–10 and the following text assign m²−mu² order two while the reference frequency is zeroth order. Section IV, pp. 3–4, Eqs. 15–24 relates reference-scale changes to finite local gravitational/electromagnetic stress and matched couplings. Eq. 20 says local ambiguity coefficients are not fixed by axioms. Its complex charged scalar, electric current, and normalization differ from the real scalar and x-current Q/2 here; its coefficients cannot be copied without translating conventions.

**C. Molina-París, P. R. Anderson and S. A. Ramsey, One-loop lambda phi^4 field theory in Robertson-Walker spacetimes: adiabatic regularization, [gr-qc/9908037v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9908037v1), 1999.** Section III, pp. 6–7, Eqs. 3.10–3.12 uses fixed positive m² and assigns lambda phi²/2 order two. Variance subtraction stops at order two and stress at four. Section V, p. 11, Eqs. 5.2–5.4 gives the bare and subtraction quantum exchange and its opposite mean-field term. With x=m²+lambda phi²/2, the quantum sector obeys rho'+3L(rho+p)=x'Q/2. A prescribed x therefore has source work; only a consistently evolved combined system is closed and conserved.

This supports the counting/source structure, not literally the present h≤0 zero-crossing path. Extending to arbitrary external x is an independent spurion/algebra calculation. The paper's local analytical approximation is also distinct from nonlocal particle production and from a uniform exact-state heavy-regulator remainder theorem.

**T. Markkanen and A. Tranberg, A Simple Method for One-Loop Renormalization in Curved Space-Time, [1303.0180v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.0180v2), 2013.** Section 2 specifies separate finite matter and gravitational conditions. Section 5.1, Eq. 5.6 gives the local V/F coefficients; Appendix C, especially Eqs. C.2–C.3 and C.8–C.14, confirms the F(x)R and R² metric variations after translating signs. Its action/mode agreement is directly demonstrated through two derivatives, Eq. 5.10; higher-order agreement is described as plausible and requires further calculation. The fourth-order bridge in this checkpoint rests on its displayed direct calculation, not a proof borrowed from that paper.

Section 5.2, pp. 16–17, Eqs. 5.11–5.12 critiques instantaneous-M² order-zero interacting-theory subtraction, whose log(M²) and inverse-M² finite terms cannot be obtained by shifting only the constants of its original action. That is a different finite prescription from the present fixed-r, h-order-two subtraction. Cite the distinction rather than asserting unrestricted action/adiabatic equivalence.

**Han Lai and Atsuhisa Ota, Ultraviolet Structure of Real-time Gravitational Wave Linear Response in a Resonant Scalar Field, [2606.16296v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16296v1), June 2026.** Section 5, pp. 15–17, Eqs. 5.11–5.15 varies a time-dependent c2 R term and obtains rho=0 but p=2c2ddot on Minkowski space. Pressure matching can therefore fail while a flat energy ledger passes. Eq. 5.18 gives an off-shell linear-response/tadpole mismatch in its resonant toy; Eqs. 5.20–5.23 suggest a covariant dynamical completion, whose full analysis is left to future work. That different GW-response observable is not a completed coupled solution or a contradiction of this external-x one-point exchange calculation.

**Cristian Armendariz-Picon and Alberto Diez-Tejedor, Cosmic Energy Density: Particles, Fields and the Vacuum, [2305.16293v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16293v1), 2023.** Section 2.3, pp. 23–25, Table 1 and Eqs. 2.41–2.44 gives the adiabatic, frequency and production conditions needed for particle approximations. Eq. 2.43 includes occupation and coherent UV terms; Eq. 2.44 distinguishes the combined source stress from its basis-dependent components. Section 3.4, pp. 40–41, Eq. 3.23 gives beta~k^-2 after a sharp transition and the associated logarithmically divergent excitation energy. This supports retaining coherent stress and addressing nonsmooth geometry/state artifacts separately from local matching. It does not measure HDBLAST radiation or determine shell couplings.

## Two newly screened methods leads

**Guillermo Ballesteros, Jesús Gambín Egea and Flavio Riccardi, Background-independent one-loop renormalization of tensor and scalar primordial spectra, [2607.06042v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06042v1), July 2026.** Section 2, pp. 5–8, Eqs. 6–12 and Section 2.3 isolates a UV WKB region and analytically matches poles/local finite terms before numerical integration. Its assumptions include positive-frequency asymptotic data and effective-mass derivatives small compared with the corresponding powers of k. The intermediate UV/IR split scale cancels in the combined result. This is a possible later method for analytic tail separation or perturbation calculations; the paper's actual observables are one-loop two-point spectra. It supplies neither the present one-point source nor a certified finite-K error bound.

**Leda Gao, Paul R. Anderson and Robert S. Link, Backreaction and order reduction in initially contracting models of the universe, [2308.11040v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11040v1), August 2023.** Section III, pp. 11–14 compares four treatments of higher-derivative semiclassical evolution. Only their full iterative M4 supports fourth-order or higher initial states among the tested methods. Simplified M1–M3 use special conformal/derivative structure and need substantial modification for nonconformal fields. This is a future backreaction design lead; no such method was implemented in this prescribed-background checkpoint. Its closed RW geometry, conformal coupling and state-dependent bounce/collapse results do not transfer to the minimally coupled HDBLAST shell.

## Implications and limits

Stress, pressure and the changing-mass source need one declared finite action. Conservation and a flat limit cannot determine independent Einstein or curvature-squared finite coefficients. Exact physical modes must be separated from positive-reference subtraction and from any heavy/local approximation at x=0. None of the inspected papers proves this checkpoint's uniform exact-state heavy-field bound; its smoothness, static-preparation, gap and compact-interval assumptions remain conditional.

No direct contradiction was found in the displayed local F/R² variations, V/F signs or exchange convention after translating the papers' conventions. The fourth-order bridge and numerical controls stand on the checkpoint's independent calculations and their stated limits. Existing literature does not convert a prescribed-background calculation, or any later numerical pass, into a source-consuming shell, thermal radiation bath, hot Big Bang, global state on the nonsmooth archive, or external novelty claim.
