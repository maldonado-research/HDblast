# Could an event in a higher dimension have helped start the hot Big Bang?

## A very simple guide to HDBLAST Zenodo v20

**By Ricardo Maldonado, Independent Researcher**  
**Public research record:** [HDBLAST Zenodo v20, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21367209](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21367209)

## The shortest honest answer

HDBLAST asks a very big question:

> Could a powerful event in an extra direction of space have transferred energy into our Universe and helped create its hot beginning?

This is a **scientific hypothesis**. That means it is an idea being turned into equations, computer tests, and possible predictions.

It is **not a proven discovery**.

HDBLAST has not proved that an extra dimension exists. It has not proved what caused the Big Bang. It has not completed a full five-dimensional collision. It has not calculated the total gravitational-wave energy from such an event. It has not detected a signal.

What the project has done is still useful:

- it found and publicly corrected an earlier mistake;
- it identified a narrower mathematical route that may allow a higher-dimensional gravity effect;
- it built reproducible tests;
- and it showed exactly where the present computer work still fails.

That is real research progress. It is not confirmation of the main idea.

## First: what does the Big Bang mean?

The standard Big Bang model says that the early Universe was extremely hot and dense. Space expanded. The Universe cooled. Matter later formed atoms, stars, galaxies, planets, and life.

There is strong evidence for this hot, expanding history. HDBLAST does not try to erase it.

There are still deeper questions. What produced the earliest hot state? What powered the earliest expansion? Was there a physical stage before the part we can currently describe? NASA's public account says scientists still do not know what came before cosmic inflation or what powered it. HDBLAST asks whether a higher-dimensional event could be part of that deeper story. [NASA: Cosmic History](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/overview/)

In simple terms, the standard Big Bang model describes much of **what happened after the Universe became hot and began expanding**. HDBLAST asks a speculative question about **what might have helped create that starting state**.

## What is an extra dimension?

We experience three directions in space:

- left and right;
- forward and backward;
- up and down.

An extra dimension would be another direction that is difficult or impossible for us to see directly.

Imagine tiny creatures that live only on a sheet of paper. They know the directions across the sheet, but they do not understand the space above it. A person outside the sheet could touch or shake it. The creatures might see waves travel through their world even though the cause came from a direction they could not see.

HDBLAST uses a somewhat similar picture. Our visible Universe might behave like a surface inside a larger space. A violent event in that larger space might send energy into our Universe.

This is only an analogy. The real work uses equations, not an actual sheet of paper.

Extra-dimensional and brane-world models are real subjects in theoretical physics. For example, published work by Lisa Randall and Raman Sundrum studied gravity in a warped five-dimensional space and its Kaluza–Klein gravity modes. Other physicists derived effective gravity equations for a four-dimensional brane inside a five-dimensional spacetime. These established papers show that the **mathematical ingredients are legitimate research topics**. They do not prove HDBLAST. [Randall–Sundrum paper 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9905221), [Randall–Sundrum paper 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9906064), [Shiromizu–Maeda–Sasaki brane equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9910076)

Physicists also search experimentally for signs of extra dimensions. CERN describes possible tests involving Kaluza–Klein states and missing energy. A large 2024 CMS search found no statistically significant excess in the channel it examined and instead placed stronger limits on certain extra-dimensional models. This means extra dimensions are seriously testable ideas, but there is no accepted detection supporting HDBLAST. [CERN: how extra dimensions might be tested](https://home.cern/science/physics/extra-dimensions-gravitons-and-tiny-black-holes/), [CMS 2024 search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09320)

## What does “higher-dimensional blast” mean?

The word **blast** does not mean an ordinary bomb.

It means a fast, powerful release or transfer of energy in a model with an extra spatial dimension.

The hypothesis asks whether fields or structures in that larger space could interact and transfer some energy into our Universe. In principle, that energy might:

- help create a hot state;
- disturb gravity;
- create particles;
- affect expansion;
- or leave a pattern that could later be tested.

The hard part is not imagining this. The hard part is proving that one complete, self-consistent event works in the equations and then finding a unique sign that nature actually did it.

## What did the project actually find?

### Finding 1: an earlier radiation claim was not valid

An earlier calculation produced a nonzero gravitational tensor pattern. It was interpreted as emitted radiation.

A later Fourier-support audit found a serious problem: the calculation looked in a wave direction that the declared source did not actually produce.

Think of checking a radio frequency where no station was transmitting. A pattern in the receiver is not proof of a broadcast if the source never sent that frequency.

Because of this correction:

- the earlier PHYS-M309 result is **not certified as emitted gravitational radiation**;
- the earlier `1.33765%` number is **not a same-event higher-dimensional energy fraction**;
- that number is now only an imposed mathematical window or profile diagnostic.

This correction is the strongest established result in the current release.

Publishing a correction is not a weakness in science. It is what honest science is supposed to do when a better test finds a problem.

### Finding 2: a narrower mathematical door remains open

The correction did not show that every possible higher-dimensional radiation route is impossible.

The work found that two coordinated wave components in the **same field**, crossing at an angle, can select a massive Kaluza–Klein gravity mode within the stated model.

In very basic terms, a Kaluza–Klein mode is a gravity vibration that can involve the extra dimension and appear “heavy” when viewed from our Universe.

The calculation shows that this route is mathematically allowed and has a nonzero coupling in the retained thick-brane model.

It does **not** show:

- how much total energy a real event would emit;
- that the event happened in nature;
- that the mode has been observed;
- or that the mode created the Big Bang.

Finding an unlocked door is not the same as showing that nature walked through it.

### Finding 3: the computer pipeline stops before a physical event

The project is building tools intended to simulate a complete event in four spatial dimensions plus time.

The latest registered pipeline passes checks A01 through A09. It fails at A10. A11 and A12 therefore remain false.

This does **not** mean that the hypothesis is “75% true.” The checks measure whether parts of a calculation are ready. They do not measure the probability that nature uses the hypothesis.

The failed A10 gate means the project does not yet have:

- production-ready gravitational starting data;
- a physical generalized-harmonic checkpoint;
- a complete nonlinear five-dimensional event;
- a closed account of where all energy goes;
- an absolute ordinary-gravity energy;
- an absolute Kaluza–Klein energy;
- a present-day gravitational-wave spectrum;
- or a detection.

Stopping at the first failed gate is intentional. It prevents an unfinished calculation from being advertised as a physical result. Generalized-harmonic evolution and constraint-preserving boundaries are established numerical-relativity methods, but HDBLAST still has to implement and validate a full physical event successfully. [Lindblom and collaborators on generalized-harmonic evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0512093)

Five-dimensional numerical-relativity simulations have been performed in other research settings, including published convergence tests of five-dimensional black-hole and gravitational-wave spacetimes. That shows the general computational field is real. It does not validate the HDBLAST event or solver. [Yoshino and Shibata: Higher-dimensional numerical relativity](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.2760)

## Is HDBLAST legitimate?

The fairest answer is:

> **HDBLAST is legitimate as a speculative, testable research hypothesis. It is not legitimate to present it as an established theory, a discovery, or proof of the origin of the Universe.**

The project uses real subjects and tools:

- general relativity;
- scalar fields;
- Fourier analysis;
- extra-dimensional and brane-world models;
- Kaluza–Klein gravity;
- numerical relativity;
- convergence tests;
- public code and reproducibility checks.

Using real scientific tools does not guarantee that the proposed answer is correct.

A scientific hypothesis must be allowed to fail. HDBLAST stays on scientific ground only if it continues to:

- make clear, testable claims;
- report failed tests;
- correct errors;
- compare its results with known physics and observations;
- let independent experts inspect the work;
- and reject or change the idea if the evidence goes against it.

The v20 correction and fail-closed gate are good examples of those habits. They do not prove the central hypothesis.

## Is it an accepted scientific theory?

No.

In everyday speech, people often use “theory” to mean any idea. In science, an established theory is a broad explanation supported by strong evidence and repeated successful testing.

HDBLAST has not reached that level. The most accurate labels today are:

- **hypothesis**;
- **research program**;
- **theory-development project**;
- **computational and mathematical work in progress**.

## Is the Zenodo release peer reviewed?

No. This Zenodo software deposit is not, by itself, a peer-reviewed journal paper.

Zenodo preserves files, metadata, versions, and a permanent DOI. Its official documentation says any user can create a record, and explains that the DOI makes the record citable and helps reproducibility and attribution. That is useful, but it is different from scientific approval. [Zenodo: About records](https://help.zenodo.org/docs/deposit/about-records/)

The DOI proves that a particular public record existed at a particular time. It does not prove that its scientific claims are true.

## Is it serious enough for scientists to examine?

The **question** can be scientifically serious. The **answer** is not yet established.

Scientists could reasonably examine the equations, code, correction, numerical tests, and proposed predictions. They could also find errors, prior work, or physical problems that cause them to reject parts or all of the proposal. That is normal.

It would be inaccurate to say that the scientific community has accepted HDBLAST. There is not yet independent community confirmation of the central claim.

The best route toward serious scientific consideration is:

1. turn the main work into a focused paper;
2. obtain expert review from researchers in general relativity, numerical relativity, cosmology, and extra-dimensional gravity;
3. submit it to a suitable peer-reviewed journal;
4. publish exact predictions before looking for confirming data;
5. obtain independent reproduction;
6. and compare those predictions with real observations.

## How important are the findings today?

The current importance is **real but limited**.

Today, the work matters because it:

- publicly corrected an earlier overinterpretation;
- clarified that a nonzero tensor at an arbitrary wave direction is not automatically emitted radiation;
- retained a narrower mathematical possibility;
- published reproducibility material;
- and clearly identified the next failed numerical gate.

These may become useful specialist contributions after expert review. Their wider importance is still unknown.

They do not currently justify changing schoolbooks, history books, or the accepted account of the Big Bang.

## Could the work ever change history or textbooks?

Possibly—but only after much stronger evidence.

There are several very different levels:

### Level 1: where the project is now

A public hypothesis, a correction, an allowed mathematical channel, and an incomplete computer pipeline.

**Textbook change:** none.

### Level 2: independent mathematical and computer confirmation

Outside experts confirm the equations, reproduce the work, and complete a stable five-dimensional event.

**Possible effect:** a specialist advance in higher-dimensional gravity or numerical cosmology. Still not proof that nature used the mechanism.

### Level 3: a unique prediction agrees with observation

HDBLAST predicts a clear pattern that competing explanations do not naturally predict, and independent observations find it.

**Possible effect:** major evidence for the mechanism and perhaps for extra-dimensional physics.

### Level 4: many independent confirmations

Different teams, methods, instruments, and data sets agree; alternatives fail; the result survives years of criticism.

**Possible effect:** a foundational change in cosmology.

If that happened, textbooks might add a new chapter explaining a physical event that produced or prepared the hot Big Bang state.

It would probably **extend** the standard Big Bang story, not erase it. The well-tested history of expansion, cooling, element formation, the cosmic microwave background, stars, and galaxies would still need to remain correct.

The change would be like discovering what lit the fuse—not denying what happened after the fuse was lit.

## What would verification mean for Ricardo Maldonado?

If Ricardo Maldonado documented a genuinely original mechanism and clear prediction **before** it was confirmed, and independent researchers later verified it, the public record could help establish his contribution and its date.

Possible results could include:

- recognition as an originator or early developer of the verified idea;
- citations and scientific collaborations;
- invitations to present the work;
- peer-reviewed publications;
- a place in the historical account of the discovery;
- and, in an extraordinary case, major professional honors.

None of this is automatic, and awards should never be promised.

Scientific credit would depend on:

- genuine originality compared with earlier literature;
- correct equations and code;
- clear predictions made in advance;
- peer review;
- independent reproduction;
- observational confirmation;
- and the contributions of the teams that perform the simulations and measurements.

Credit would probably be shared. Major discoveries usually require proposers, theorists, numerical researchers, experimenters, and independent confirming teams.

The correct statement today is:

> Ricardo Maldonado has a dated public record of a developing hypothesis, its calculations, a major correction, and its current failed gates. This establishes documentation and priority evidence for the specific material recorded there. It does not establish scientific truth or exclusive credit.

A formal college degree is not what makes a claim true or false. Evidence does. However, advanced cosmology and numerical relativity are extremely technical. Collaboration with trained specialists would greatly improve the chance that errors are found early and that any valid contribution is understood and credited properly.

## What if HDBLAST is wrong?

Then the work can still have value.

A failed hypothesis can:

- expose a mathematical mistake;
- improve computer tests;
- rule out a possible mechanism;
- clarify what evidence is missing;
- and help others avoid repeating the same error.

Science advances by learning what fails as well as what succeeds.

## What must happen next?

Before the central hypothesis could be considered credible, the project would need to:

1. produce starting data that satisfy all required gravity and matter constraints;
2. run a stable, convergent, nonlinear five-dimensional event;
3. show that improving the numerical resolution gives the same physical answer;
4. track all relevant energy from the beginning to the end;
5. separate real radiation from boundary and finite-box artifacts;
6. calculate a unique, quantitative prediction;
7. compare it with existing cosmological and particle-physics limits;
8. have outside experts reproduce the result;
9. pass peer review;
10. and obtain independent observational confirmation.

The central hypothesis may fail at any one of these steps. That possibility must remain open.

## One-paragraph public summary

HDBLAST is a developing scientific hypothesis that asks whether an event in an extra dimension could have helped create the hot beginning described by the Big Bang model. The project uses real ideas from gravity, fields, extra-dimensional physics, and computer simulation, but it has not proved that the event happened. Its strongest current result is a correction: an earlier radiation interpretation was withdrawn because the declared source did not actually contain the required wave component. A narrower mathematical route involving a massive higher-dimensional gravity mode remains possible, but no complete five-dimensional event, absolute radiation energy, observable signal, or detection has been obtained. HDBLAST is therefore a speculative and testable research program—not an accepted theory or discovery.

Could our Universe have been sparked by an event in an extra dimension? HDBLAST is testing that possibility with equations and computer simulations. It has not proved the idea. ## Bottom line

HDBLAST asks a bold question using scientific tools.

Its current results do not rewrite history.

They show that one earlier interpretation was wrong, that a narrower mathematical possibility remains open, and that a clear sequence of difficult tests remains unfinished.

If those tests fail, the hypothesis must be changed or rejected.

If they succeed, are reproduced by independent experts, and lead to a unique confirmed observation, the consequences could be historic.

The correct description today is:

> **HDBLAST is a speculative, falsifiable, publicly documented scientific hypothesis—not a proven origin of the Universe.**
