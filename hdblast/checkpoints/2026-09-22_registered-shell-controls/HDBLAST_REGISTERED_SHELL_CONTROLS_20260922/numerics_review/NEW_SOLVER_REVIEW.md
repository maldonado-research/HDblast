# Independent review of the replacement registered-detuning solver

22 September 2026. Target: `../solver/registered_solver.py`. Source hash is recorded in `NEW_SOLVER_CHECKS.json`. This review did not edit that solver or run an evolution.

## Result

No algebraic error was found in the new wave equations, their linearization, stable potential subtraction, Hamiltonian expansion, stretched-coordinate chain rule, or coupled junction terms. Nineteen independent bounded controls pass. The saved near-target eigenvalues converge toward `1.6571937`; that is numerical evidence concerning the expected physical mode, not a proof that the full discrete evolution has no additional instability.

The initially puzzling growth of the weighted constraint diagnostic on the finest saved eigenvectors has two identifiable numerical causes: differentiated eigenvector velocity noise, and the quadratic incompatibility of a linear eigenmode with the full nonlinear junction. It is not evidence of a sign error in the new PDE system.

## Checked implementation details

- With computational coordinate increasing toward the shell, the analytic map has `z'>0` and `z''=-alpha*z'<0`. Both signs and the second-derivative chain rule are correct.
- The quintic Hermite extension reproduces monomials of degree 0 through 5 at all three ghosts. Constant, linear, and quadratic functions of physical `z` pass mapped derivative tests through the shell.
- The boundary derivatives correctly linearize to `ga=rb(sigma*b+sigma'*f)/6` and `gf=-rb(sigma'*b+sigma''*f)/2`. The sparse coupled derivative blocks contain the right source-field columns.
- A new real centered directional difference of the nonlinear right-hand side agrees with each assembled sparse Jacobian to the stated tolerance at all four saved registered resolutions. This complements the producer's complex-step test.
- The shifted scalar `psi=phi+1` avoids loss of the small regular-cone initial displacement. The shifted expressions for `U` and `U'`, the cone series, and the static sampling map are consistent with the original definitions.
- The Hamiltonian diagnostic uses the correct exact-background subtraction. Its one-sided velocity derivative at the endpoint is independent of the imposed momentum junction identity. This is an improvement over V2's invalid zero-slope monitor.

The spectral matrix represents the nondissipative operator. It does not include KO terms if a caller constructs a solver with nonzero `ko`; current spectral runs construct it with `ko=0`, so there is no mismatch in those runs.

## Amplitude and resolution controls

For a saved real eigenmode normalized to `f_b=1`, evaluate constraints at `+epsilon` and `-epsilon`, with `epsilon=1e-8`. The odd combination divides out the linear amplitude and separates it from the leading quadratic junction mismatch. Values below are therefore per unit shell scalar amplitude; they should not be confused with the very small absolute residuals of a small-amplitude run.

The independent Hamiltonian uses one-sided derivatives of the stored fields rather than ghost values generated by junction data. Its larger error is compatible with the large coefficients of those independent stencils, and it converges rapidly. It must not be presented as equally small as the original ghost-based monitor.

The “diagnostic velocity projection” in the last column sets `(pa,pb,pf)=lambda*(a,b,f)` solely to inspect the saved eigenvector. The original archived modes and evolutions were not changed. The sparse first-order eigenproblem requires this relation, but the returned eigenvectors have small absolute defects, reaching about `1.54e-7` per unit normalized mode in the finest case. Taking a one-sided derivative amplifies the local defect. With this source of eigenvector noise removed, the shell momentum diagnostic shows approximately fourth-order convergence as well. Projection is a diagnostic, not a substitute for recording raw residuals or independently verifying a corrected seed.

The quadratic Hamiltonian contribution explains the finite-amplitude weighted diagnostic increasing at finer spacing. Write the endpoint mode values as `b=B epsilon`, `f=F epsilon`. The nonlinear Neumann data have the expansion

\[
g_A=g_A^{(1)}\epsilon+g_A^{(2)}\epsilon^2+O(\epsilon^3),\qquad
g_A^{(2)}=\frac{\rho_b}{6}
\left(\frac{\sigma B^2}{2}+\sigma'BF+2\phi_b F^2\right).
\]

The stored linear eigenmode only satisfies the first-order boundary condition. Applying the full nonlinear ghost data introduces a second-derivative term in the Hamiltonian with leading coefficient `-6*g2_b*g_A^(2)`, where `g2_b` is the solver's coefficient multiplying the Neumann derivative in `D2` at the shell. This term scales approximately as `epsilon^2/h` at fixed mode shape.

At amplitude `1e-7`, these coefficients generate absolute residuals about `1e-8` and `2e-8`, matching the saved trend. Taking amplitude to zero at fixed grid and refining a fixed finite-amplitude, only linearly compatible seed are different limits. A full nonlinear initial-data claim needs second-order or fully nonlinear compatibility; the current modes are valid candidates for a bounded linear-limit test.

## Qualifications for interpreting new runs

1. A near-shift eigensolve targets the expected scalar rate. The other returned eigenvalues may be gauge, constraint, boundary, or poorly converged numerical modes; their comparatively large residuals do not support physical mode counting.
2. The static shifted-tension seed has the documented target-junction mismatch. The linear-mode seed has full nonlinear junction error at second order. Neither should be called exact nonlinear constraint-compatible initial data.
3. The shell scalar perturbation is a useful physical growth probe because the static scalar at the fixed shell has no background time derivative. Metric coordinate modes can still contaminate the full state. Growth agreement should be accompanied by constraint convergence, amplitude checks, and appropriate boundary/domain checks.
4. The new smooth taper is a quintic smoothstep with finite differentiability, not a mathematical C-infinity function. Its transition occupies approximately `[-0.99L,-0.85L]`; the closest seed-taper disturbance can reach the shell after about `0.85L` coordinate time. Current short runs must stay inside that causal window when relying on isolation.
5. The normalized constraint denominator is a positive background scale; it is a useful convention, not a bound on the relative error of every perturbed equation. Publish the convention, the raw residuals, and amplitude-scaled linear diagnostics together.
6. The fixed refined region and conformal lapse remain limitations for nonlinear roll-off and late-time fate. Passing the linear test is progress toward those calculations, not their completion.
