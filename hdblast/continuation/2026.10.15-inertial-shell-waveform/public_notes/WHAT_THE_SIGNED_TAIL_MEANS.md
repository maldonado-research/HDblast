# What the signed shell tail adds

The new calculation distinguishes a small, nonzero response after the main
pulse from an interval that merely allows such a response. For one specified
wider quadrupole perturbation, the normalized linear trace is strictly
positive throughout the certified post-pulse window. The physical scalar's
quadrupole coefficient has the opposite sign because its background gain is
negative. The quadrupole itself changes sign across the sphere, so this is
not an everywhere-negative scalar field or an isotropic signal.

The calculation retains the boundary's inertial term. In the comparison
problem, the boundary has a causal memory of the incoming pulse. A separate
estimate bounds the difference between that comparison problem and the
actual coupled linear equation, including the bulk potential and changing
boundary coefficient. That error is small enough to certify the tail's sign.
The proof covers every time in the exported cells; the drawn midpoints are
visual guides.

The width is a newly specified model state, ell=10^-6. The earlier completed
waveform used ell=10^-10. Its bounds remain valid for that earlier state.
The response integral here was calculated afresh; its coefficient and time
axis have no observational calibration. The two opposite-sign witness pairs
also establish at least one zero in each stated interval, without proving
that either zero is unique.

This result advances the quantitative first variation. Earlier conditional
constructions of a complete second Taylor coefficient and exact families
retain their assumptions. The next quantitative calculation is the wider
state's full second-order forcing and shell observable, including metric,
scalar, material-pullback and proper-time corrections. A controlled nonlinear
remainder is needed to assign an effective finite perturbation amplitude.

The calculations and internal reviews support conditional mathematics for
the chosen model. They do not establish that a higher-dimensional blast
caused the Big Bang. This checkpoint is published on GitHub; it makes no new
Zenodo version or APS submission.
